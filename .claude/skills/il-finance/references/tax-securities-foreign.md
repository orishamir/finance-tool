## Summary

- Gains on foreign securities and foreign funds are taxed like Israeli ones: up to 25% of the real capital gain, as the top slice of income (TXF-01, TXF-02).
- For a foreign-currency security held by an individual, the "real" gain is measured against the exchange rate, not the CPI, automatically (TXF-03). A foreign fund registered on TASE uses the exchange rate only if its prospectus commits to 90% main-currency assets, otherwise the CPI: so says the Tax Authority's 2019 letter, read only in a copy (TXF-04, open question 10).
- Foreign dividends: 25% in Israel (TXF-08). Foreign tax on them is credited, capped at the Israeli tax on that income, per income-type basket, with a 5-year carry-forward (TXF-17 to TXF-20).
- US withholding on US dividends to an Israeli resident is capped at 25% by the treaty with a W-8BEN; 30% without (TXF-12, TXF-13). With 25% US and 25% Israeli, the credit leaves no further Israeli tax in principle (my computation from TXF-08, TXF-12, TXF-18). A US ETF organised as a trust (VOO, IVV and ACWI are all series of Delaware statutory trusts, TXF-37) is a US corporation for the treaty if US tax treats it as one, as it does a RIC; its dividends bear at most 25% (the 1993 protocol's Art. 12(3)(a)) and Israel credits that tax; a RIC's capital gain dividends may reach the holder with no US tax at all (TXF-41). How Israel classes such a fund's distribution, and its capital gain dividend, is open (Q19, Q27).
- Tax suffered inside a foreign fund (for example US withholding on an Irish fund) is not creditable in Israel (TXF-16, a reading no source states in words; for TASE-registered foreign funds the 2019 letter, read only in a copy and so a lead, allows credit only for tax on the fund's own distributions; TXF-06 is open).
- No Israeli rule was found that taxes a non-controlling holder on a listed foreign fund's undistributed income; the CFC regime needs Israeli control (TXF-11). The Tax Authority's 2019 letter on TASE-registered foreign funds taxes the holder only on a sale and on distributions, which supports the sale-only reading by implication; an older circular (7/2003 s.4.4) pointed the other way. Since the 2005 Amendment 147, the Ordinance's trust chapter does not apply to "קרן להשקעות משותפות מחוץ לישראל" (TXF-36), and no Israeli text distinguishes a fund by legal form or compares a US fund with an Irish one (TXF-37). Q1 and Q19 are narrowed, not closed: there is still no official sentence about an accumulating foreign fund. Since 2026-11-04 the strongest support is the funds' own annexes: managers of accumulating UCITS classes registered on TASE report Tax Authority rulings under which only a sale and a distribution are taxed (institution strength; the rulings are unpublished). A currency that fell gives no negative inflationary amount, and the currency part of a shekel loss is not allowable (TXF-03, Q5 closed 2026-10-26).
- Through an Israeli bank or TASE member a foreign fund is not self-reported, on whichever exchange it trades: the bank withholds 25% of the real gain on a sale (TXF-29) and 25% of a dividend (TXF-30). An accumulating fund changes only the timing: nothing to withhold until the sale (TXF-31). Each year the holder files nothing because of the fund, unless a general filing trigger applies, and keeps the form 867 (TXF-32).
- At a foreign broker nothing is withheld in Israel: semi-annual report and advance on gains, advance on dividends by 30 April, annual return (TXF-20 to TXF-22).
- More than USD 60,000 of US-situs assets (including shares of US corporations, so US-domiciled ETFs) creates a US estate tax filing duty; no US-Israel estate treaty is listed. Irish-domiciled fund shares are not US-situs (TXF-27).

## Rules

### TXF-01 Israeli residents are taxed on gains from foreign securities
- Rule: An Israeli resident is liable to tax on capital gains from selling an asset both in Israel and outside Israel. The Ordinance's "asset" covers property "בין שהם בישראל ובין שהם מחוץ לישראל", and "security" includes "נייר ערך שהונפק מחוץ לישראל".
- Conditions / exceptions: Olim and veteran returning residents have a 10-year exemption on foreign assets (TXF-26). A loss on an asset whose gain would have been exempt is not an allowable loss (TXF-23).

### TXF-02 Rate on the real capital gain
- Rule: An individual pays tax on real capital gain at a rate "שלא יעלה על 25%", and the capital gain is treated as the highest slice of taxable income (s.91(b)(1)). The Ordinance sets no separate rate for foreign assets.
- Conditions / exceptions: Substantial shareholder 30%; age 60+ starting rates; assets bought before 2012; unlinked debt at 15%; the 3% + 2% surtax: all in `tax-securities-israeli.md` and `tax-general.md`. The Tax Authority's 2026 table of special-rate income lists "רווח הון במכירת ניירות ערך" at 15%, 20%, 25%, 30%. *2026-10-25 rule-text pass:* quote made verbatim ("במכירת", not "ממכירת") and the printed guide page removed from the text (the Source line carries the file page).
- Figures: 25%, fixed in law (s.91(b)(1)); in force for 2026.

### TXF-03 Real gain on a foreign-currency security is measured against the exchange rate (automatic)
- Rule: For a security held by an individual that is denominated in a foreign currency or whose value is linked to one, the exchange rate is treated as the "index" for computing the inflationary amount, so the taxable real gain is the gain in that currency. Text of the s.88 definition of "מדד": "על אף האמור, לענין נייר ערך בידי יחיד, הנקוב במטבע חוץ או שערכו צמוד למטבע חוץ, יראו את שער המטבע כמדד".
- Conditions / exceptions: This is mandatory ("יראו"), not an election. The only election in the definition is for a person who bought an asset in foreign currency while a foreign resident: "מי שבהיותו תושב חוץ רכש נכס במטבע חוץ כדין, רשאי לבקש שיראו את שער המטבע שבו נרכש הנכס כמדד". A foreign fund registered on TASE is not covered by this rule: its index is open since 2026-11-07 (TXF-04). If the currency fell over the holding period, the inflationary amount is zero, not negative: the whole shekel nominal gain is the real gain, and tax never exceeds the rate times that gain. If the sale ends in a shekel loss, the part of the loss caused by the currency's fall cannot be offset; only the rest is allowable. The Supreme Court so held for an individual's foreign-currency securities (ע"א 3555/15 מוזס, 12.9.2016), as the Tax Authority's circular 10/2025 states it: the court held that the assessing officer's position, to separate the inflationary amount and not allow it as an offset against the gain, "היא בדין ותואמת את הוראות הפקודה ותכליתה"; and it quotes the judgment: an inflationary amount, "בין אם מקורו בשינוי ערך מדד או בשינוי שער חליפין, אינו בר קיזוז כהפסד" (appendix, p.9). The circular applies it in two steps: "בשלב הראשון - יש לחשב את הפסד ההון בערכים נומינליים", then "בשלב השני - יש לבודד את סכום הפסד ההון שאילו היה רווח היה חייב במס" (appendix, p.9; examples 3 and 4, p.11). A family company uses the CPI, not the exchange rate (ע"א 1038/17 אור שרה, the circular's note 13). TXS-02 states the same for the CPI.
- Figures: none.

### TXF-04 Foreign fund registered on TASE ("קרן חוץ"): exchange rate or CPI as the index
- Rule: The Tax Authority's 2019 letter, read only in a copy (open question 10), states: on a sale of a "קרן חוץ" unit, the index for the inflationary amount is the change in the exchange rate, provided the fund's prospectus states and commits that at least 90% of its investments in each tax year are in assets whose interest is paid by, or whose inflationary amount is computed by, the fund's main currency rate, and the fund notified TASE and committed to comply and report any change. "Main currency rate" is the foreign currency the fund chose in its prospectus. Otherwise the CPI is the index. The currency in which the fund is denominated is irrelevant.
- Conditions / exceptions: The source is a letter of the Tax Authority's capital-market taxation department to TASE, the Banks Association and the non-bank members' association, dated 4 July 2019, issued before foreign funds were first offered on TASE. Whether it is still in force was not confirmed (Q10). This differs from TXF-03, which applies automatically to a foreign-currency security held directly. The letter is read only in a copy on capitax.co.il; no official copy was found. *2026-10-25 rule-text pass:* searched again for an official copy (Wayback index of gov.il/BlobFolder filtered by the letter date and by foreign-fund words; web search for the title): none found, so the rule keeps the labelled copy. **[2026-11-06, foreign-fund pass]** Still no official copy (open question 10). The letter applies its test "בדומה לקרנות נאמנות וההנחיות הרלוונטיות להן". The test's words are the 2003 regulations' definition of an Israeli "קרן נאמנות להשקעות חוץ": 90% of investments in each tax year in assets whose interest is paid by, or whose inflationary amount is computed by, the main currency rate (TXS-14). So the wording is the official test for an Israeli fund, and only its extension to a "קרן חוץ" rests on the letter; whether the 2003 regulations still operate is itself open (TXS-14). The funds' own annexes repeat that extension (for example Invesco FTSE All-World, [p.19](https://mayafiles.tase.co.il/rpdf/1609001-1610000/P1609964-00.pdf#page=19), "בדומה לקרנות נאמנות"; institution). Inferred, no official text says it: without the letter, the index would follow s.88's definition of "מדד", the exchange rate for a security held by an individual "הנקוב במטבע חוץ" (TXF-03) and the CPI otherwise; nothing read says whether a TASE-listed foreign fund's unit is so denominated. **[2026-11-07, official status]** The 4.7.2019 text is the capital-market taxation department's general guidance, which TASE members apply, and was never published officially; each fund manager's own taxation ruling on it is private and not among the published rulings. The funds' annexes that repeat it and the capitax copy are leads only, so nothing in this rule changes on their strength; only the department itself, asked directly, can settle it (open question 10).
- Figures: none. The 90% threshold of the letter rests only on the copy and is not a figure of this rule; the same 90% in an official text is the 2003 regulations' test for an Israeli foreign-investment fund, TXS-14's figure `foreign_investment_fund_threshold`.

### TXF-05 "קרן חוץ" on TASE: Israeli withholding as for a traded security
- Rule: The Tax Authority's 2019 letter, read only in a copy (open question 10), states: because a "קרן חוץ" is a "unit" under the Joint Investment Trust Law after its Amendment 23, the withholding provisions that apply to traded securities apply to it. On purchase or sale, capital-gain withholding follows the Income Tax Regulations (withholding from consideration, payment or capital gain on sale of a security, a fund unit or a future transaction), 2002. On a distribution to an individual, tax is withheld at the s.125B(1) rate (25%); to a company, at the s.126(a) rate; a foreign-resident unit holder is exempt from withholding.
- Conditions / exceptions: Under those regulations a payer ("חייב", "אדם המשלם למוכר תמורה או תשלום, בעד מכירת נייר ערך") paying an individual seller withholds "בשיעור של 25% מרווח ההון הריאלי" (reg. 2(a)) and offsets losses on securities it manages in the same tax year (reg. 9(a)). 2026-10-26 (scope settling): Bank Leumi's ETF page, which repeated these rules, was dropped as a source. **[2026-11-06, foreign-fund pass]** Without the letter, official text still carries two parts. The withholding on a sale follows from the Ordinance and the regulations for any foreign security sold through a bank or TASE member (TXF-29). The 25% on a distribution of a fund organised as a company follows from reg. 2(ד) (TXF-30). These parts rest on the copy alone: that a "קרן חוץ" is a "unit"; the company rate on its distributions; the exemption from withholding for a foreign-resident holder; and the distribution of a fund that is an arrangement rather than a company (open question 19). Read: the Ordinance takes "יחידה" from the Joint Investment Trust Law (s.88). That law defines "יחידות של קרן חוץ" apart from a fund's units (s.113א, [file p.75](https://www.new.isa.gov.il/images/Fittings/isa/asset_library_pic/al_lobby/al_lobby-628ccfb552dac/Hashka01.pdf#page=75)), and it treats a foreign fund's unit "כאילו היו יחידה, קרן ומנהל קרן" only for the nine sections s.113ד(א) lists, none of them about tax; the last is s.83(א), a fund manager's liability to a unit holder ([file pp.76-77](https://www.new.isa.gov.il/images/Fittings/isa/asset_library_pic/al_lobby/al_lobby-628ccfb552dac/Hashka01.pdf#page=76)). **[2026-11-07, official status]** The 4.7.2019 text is the capital-market taxation department's general guidance, which TASE members apply, and was never published officially; each fund manager's own taxation ruling on it is private and not among the published rulings. The funds' annexes that repeat it and the capitax copy are leads only, so nothing in this rule changes on their strength; only the department itself, asked directly, can settle it (open question 10).
- Figures: 25% of real gain (reg. 2(a)); 25% on distributions to individuals (s.125B(1)).

### TXF-06 "קרן חוץ" on TASE: the foreign tax credit is open (a 2019 letter limits it to tax on the fund's distributions, read only in a copy)

### TXF-10 Israeli intermediary that receives a foreign dividend for an Israeli resident withholds 25%
- Rule: "המקבל מתושב חוץ דיבידנד על מניות בשל תושב ישראל או לזכותו, ינכה ממנו מס בשיעורים אלה: (1) שולם הדיבידנד ליחיד – 25%; (2) שולם הדיבידנד לחבר־בני־האדם – בשיעור המס המרבי."

### TXF-11 A non-controlling holder of a listed foreign fund is taxed on sale and distributions only (by implication; no deemed-distribution rule)
- Rule: An individual who is not a controlling holder of a listed foreign fund is taxed on two events: a sale or redemption of the unit (capital gain, TXF-01 to TXF-03) and a distribution (dividend, TXF-08); income the fund reinvests reaches the tax base only through the sale price. No Tax Authority sentence says this in words for an accumulating fund; it follows from three texts. (1) For a foreign fund registered on TASE, the Tax Authority's capital-market department wrote to TASE and the banks on 4.7.2019 that "קרן חוץ הינה יחידה כמשמעותה בחוק", set withholding on a sale ("ברכישה או במכירה של קרן חוץ יחולו הוראות ניכוי מס במקור מרווח הון") and on distributions only, and allowed credit only for foreign tax "ששולם בגין חלוקת רווחים בלבד מקרן החוץ" (TXF-05, TXF-06). Accumulating Irish UCITS ETFs are among the funds listed on TASE (example: BlackRock's factsheet for iShares CSPX, "Use of Income : Accumulating", "This product is also listed on: Tel Aviv Stock Exchange"). (2) Since Amendment 147 (2005) the s.88 definition of a security includes a unit and "נייר ערך שהונפק מחוץ לישראל". (3) No attribution rule reaches a non-controlling holder: the deemed-dividend rule for foreign entities (s.75B, "חברה נשלטת זרה") applies only to a "בעל שליטה" (an Israeli resident holding, directly or indirectly, alone or with another, at least 10% of a means of control) in a foreign company that meets all of these conditions ("שמתקיימים בו כל אלה"): its shares are not listed for trading on an exchange, or under 30% were offered to the public or listed ("פחות מ־30% מהמניות או מהזכויות"); most of its income or profits are passive; the foreign tax on its passive income does not exceed 15%; and more than 50% of a means of control is held by Israeli residents (or the other control tests in the same paragraph). A listed foreign ETF held by a retail investor does not fit these conditions on their face. (4) Foreign ETF managers that registered accumulating classes on TASE report Tax Authority rulings given to their funds and list only these two events. iShares II plc, for its "USD (Acc) Share Class": "ביום 1 ביולי 2019 התקבלה בידי הקרן החלטת המיסוי מהמחלקה המקצועית של רשות המיסים", then "לפי הדין הקיים כיום, ועל פי החלטת המיסוי, חלות על היחידות המוצעות על פי נספח זה הסדרי המס המתוארים בתמצית להלן": a real capital gain "ממכירת היחידות המוצעות על ידי יחיד", and a distribution "ככל שתתבצע חלוקת רווחים", with the note that the units "שייכות לסוג יחידות "צובר", כלומר, שאינו מכריז על חלוקת דיבידנדים, אלא, צובר ומשקיע מחדש הכנסה ורווחים אחרים". The Lyxor S&P 500 annex (Multi Units Luxembourg, ruling of 24.1.2021) and the Invesco Markets II annex (ruling of 9.2.2022, "החלטה זאת תואמת את המתואר בפרק זה") say the same for accumulating classes. None of them names a charge before a sale.

### TXF-12 US withholding on US dividends paid to an Israeli resident: treaty cap 25%
- Rule: Under the US-Israel income tax treaty, US tax on dividends from US sources paid to an Israeli resident may not exceed "(a) 25 percent of the gross amount of the dividend paid"; 12.5% applies only when the recipient is a corporation owning at least 10% of the voting stock and the payer meets a passive-income test (Art. 12(2)). The IRS treaty table shows Israel: dividends paid by US corporations, general, 25 (footnote w: the column-6 rate applies to dividends paid by a regulated investment company, RIC, or a REIT, the latter only for an individual holding under 10%); direct dividend rate 12½; no-treaty rate ("Other Countries") 30.
- Conditions / exceptions: The treaty rate requires the payee's foreign status and treaty residence to be documented to the withholding agent (TXF-13). A US-domiciled ETF is a RIC for this purpose per footnote w; whether a given ETF is a RIC is a fund fact. The treaty itself says so since the Second Protocol (1993): "Subparagraph (a) shall apply in the case of dividends paid by a Regulated Investment Company" (Art. 12(3)(a), IRS text file p.50); a fund organised as a trust counts as a US corporation for the treaty if US tax treats it as one (TXF-41, 2026-11-07). The treaty gives capital gains to the state of residence (TXF-14).
- Figures: 25% (Art. 12(2)(a)); 12.5% (Art. 12(2)(b)); 30% no-treaty. General effective date 1 January 1995 (Art. 31, per the IRS text); IRS table rev. May 2023.

### TXF-14 The US does not tax an Israeli resident's capital gains on US securities (fewer than 183 days in the US)
- Rule: For a nonresident alien present in the US fewer than 183 days in the tax year, US-source capital gains not effectively connected with a US trade or business are generally not taxed; at 183 days or more the net gain is taxed at 30% or a lower treaty rate. The US-Israel treaty exempts a resident of one state from tax by the other on gains from capital assets, unless (among other exceptions) the individual is present in the other state 183 days or more in the taxable year.

### TXF-16 Tax suffered inside a foreign fund is a fund-level cost, not creditable in Israel
- Rule: The Ordinance defines creditable "מסי־חוץ" as "מסים המשתלמים על ידי תושב ישראל לרשויות המס של מדינה מחוץ לישראל, על הכנסה שהופקה או שנצמחה באותה המדינה", computed as a percentage of income, excluding municipal taxes. Tax withheld from a foreign fund on its underlying holdings is paid by the fund, not by the Israeli resident. For a fund domiciled in Ireland, the US treaty rate on dividends from US corporations paid to an Irish resident is 15% (IRS table, Ireland, general column, footnote mm), against 25% for an Israeli resident holding the US shares directly (TXF-12).
- Conditions / exceptions: For TASE-registered foreign funds, the Tax Authority's 2019 letter, read only in a copy (open question 10), says this in words: credit only for tax on the fund's own distributions, in its state of origin (TXF-06). For a foreign-domiciled fund bought abroad, the non-creditability is my reading of the s.199 definition. The US-Ireland treaty treats an Irish collective investment undertaking as an Irish resident ("in the case of Ireland, a Collective Investment Undertaking", Art. 4(1)(d)) and caps US tax on dividends paid to it at "15 percent of the gross amount of the dividends in all other cases" (Art. 10(2)(b)), if it passes the limitation-on-benefits article (Art. 23: for example, its principal class of shares "is substantially and regularly traded on one or more recognized stock exchanges", which include the Irish Stock Exchange, London and Amsterdam). Whether a given Irish fund qualifies, and what US tax it actually suffered, is that fund's fact (its annual report), not a general rule (open question 7, narrowed 2026-10-26). Ireland deducts nothing from an Irish ETF's distributions to an Israeli resident (TXF-28; Q6 closed 2026-10-26).

### TXF-17 Foreign tax credit: what qualifies

### TXF-18 Foreign tax credit cap: by income source ("basket"), not by country
- Rule: The credit for foreign income "ממקור פלוני" (foreign income classified by the sources in s.2, Part E or Part E3, net of allowed expenses and offset losses) is capped separately per source. The Tax Authority describes this as the "basket method" ("בשיטת סלים") and says excess credit cannot be moved between two baskets.
- Conditions / exceptions: The limit is per source of income, not per country; since tax year 2021 the return must name the country the income came from (TXF-24).

### TXF-23 Losses on foreign securities
- Rule: A capital loss on an asset outside Israel, which would have been taxable if a gain, is offset first against capital gains outside Israel, then as other capital losses (s.92(a)(3)); a carried-forward foreign loss is likewise offset first against foreign capital gains (s.92(b)). ### TXF-27 US estate tax exposure of a non-US person holding US-situs assets
- Rule: The executor of a nonresident non-citizen must file Form 706-NA when "the fair market value at death of the decedent's U.S.-situated assets exceeds $60,000". "Stock of corporations organized in or under U.S. law is property located in the United States", no matter where the certificates are; "all other corporate stock is property located outside the United States". The maximum unified credit for such an estate is in general $13,000. The look-through that treated part of RIC (US fund) stock as non-US applied only to decedents who died after 2004 and before 2012. Bank accounts not connected with a US business, securities generating portfolio interest and certain life insurance proceeds are not US-situs.
- Conditions / exceptions: Estate tax treaties can change this; the IRS list of US estate and gift tax treaties (15 countries, including Ireland for estate tax) does not include Israel. So an Israeli resident holding US-domiciled ETFs has this exposure; shares of an Irish-domiciled fund are stock of a non-US corporation. **A US fund organised as a trust** (read in the US Code as the Law Revision Counsel prints it, current through Public Law 119-138, and in the eCFR, 2026-11-12): s.2104(a) makes shares held by a nonresident non-citizen US property "only if issued by a domestic corporation"; the definitions of s.7701(a) apply "When used in this title" (so to the estate tax too): "The term “corporation” includes associations", "domestic" means "created or organized in the United States or under the law of the United States or of any State", and "The term “stock” includes shares in an association". A regulated investment company is by definition a "domestic corporation" (s.851(a), whose opening "For purposes of this subtitle" limits it to income tax), and where such a company has more than one fund, "each fund of such regulated investment company shall be treated as a separate corporation for purposes of this title" (s.851(g)(1), "this title" being the whole Internal Revenue Code). The Treasury's classification rules say how a trust becomes a corporation for "federal tax purposes": an investment trust "will not be classified as a trust if there is a power under the trust agreement to vary the investment of the certificate holders" (26 CFR 301.7701-4(c)(1)); such a business entity can elect to be "an association (and thus a corporation ...)", and with no election a domestic one with two or more members is "A partnership" (301.7701-3(a), (b)(1)); the regulations deem the election only for a real estate investment trust, not for a regulated investment company. The look-through of s.2105(d), the one carve-out for RIC stock, "shall not apply to estates of decedents dying after December 31, 2011" (s.2105(d)(3)); the IRS says of the past window: "For an NRNC decedent who died after 2004 and before 2012, a portion of stock in a RIC is treated as property located outside the United States" (Form 706-NA instructions, library 2f8d7423a5ec12ca). Reading, with its gaps stated: the quoted words say that a regulated investment company is a "domestic corporation" (s.851(a), for the income-tax subtitle) and that each fund of a multi-fund one "shall be treated as a separate corporation for purposes of this title" (s.851(g)(1)). The step from there to a trust-form fund being a corporation for the estate tax is not in any text read: under 301.7701-3 a domestic business entity with two or more members is a partnership unless it elects to be an association, and the only deemed election quoted is for a real estate investment trust, not a regulated investment company; that a trust-form fund which is a regulated investment company has been classified as a corporation is inferred from s.851(a) admitting only a corporation, not read. If the step holds, its shares are "stock ... issued by a domestic corporation" under s.2104(a), so US-situs. No IRS sentence naming trust-form ETFs was read. Nor is it shown that VOO, IVV or ACWI is in fact a regulated investment company or made an election: IVV's statement of additional information says the fund "intends to qualify for treatment as a separate RIC", Vanguard Index Funds' 2014 statement says each fund "expects to qualify", and nothing was read for ACWI (TXF-37). A US exchange-traded product that is not a regulated investment company (for example a commodity grantor trust) is not covered (Q11). The tax: Form 706-NA computes the tentative tax with "Table A—Unified Rate Schedule in the version of the Instructions for Form 706 that corresponds to the decedent's date of death"; Table A (07/2026) runs from 18% on the first USD 10,000 to 40% above USD 1,000,000, and the tax on USD 60,000 is USD 13,000, the same as the maximum unified credit, so the credit offsets the tax on about the first USD 60,000 (computed from the table, not stated by the IRS).

### TXF-28 Ireland deducts no tax from an Irish ETF's distributions or redemptions for an Israeli resident
- Rule: An Irish fund ("investment undertaking") deducts Irish exit tax on a "chargeable event", which includes "the making of a regular payment by a fund to a unit holder other than on the cancellation, redemption of units or repurchase of a unit (e.g. an annual dividend)". Irish Revenue's manual says "any transaction in relation to, or in respect of, units which are held in a recognised clearing system does not require the deduction of exit tax", which "would be the case for an ETF where the units/shares in the underlying fund are bought and sold between investors on the stock market and are cleared through a recognised clearing system"; only an Irish resident unit holder must then self-assess. For units not held in a clearing system, "Exit tax is not required to be deducted in respect of the following non-resident unit holders provided that the fund is in possession of an appropriate non-resident declaration prior to the chargeable event" (or Revenue-approved equivalent measures). So an Israeli resident holding an Irish UCITS ETF receives its distributions without Irish tax, and Israel taxes them at 25% with no Irish tax to credit (TXF-08, TXF-17).
- Conditions / exceptions: This is Irish tax on the payment to the holder. Tax suffered inside the Irish fund on its own holdings (for example US withholding at the US-Ireland treaty rate) is a fund-level cost and is not creditable in Israel (TXF-16). The Israel-Ireland treaty was not read; with no Irish deduction it does not change the result. Which clearing systems Revenue recognises is in its manual Part 08-03-04 (not read).

### TXF-30 Dividend of a foreign fund through an Israeli bank or TASE member: 25% withheld; no 30 April advance
- Rule: When an Israeli bank or TASE member receives a foreign fund's dividend for an Israeli resident individual, it withholds 25% (TXF-10), and the holder has no advance to pay on it. The advance of 30 April (TXF-21) is for foreign dividends not withheld in Israel.

### TXF-32 What the holder does each year when an Israeli bank or TASE member withholds
- Rule: Holding a foreign fund through a withholder adds no filing of its own. No half-yearly report (TXF-29), no 30 April advance (TXF-30), and no annual return if the person is otherwise exempt: traded-security proceeds up to the ceiling with full tax withheld, foreign income fully withheld up to its ceiling, and none of the triggers that force a return (TXG-17, TXG-18). - Conditions / exceptions: The form: the withholder issues form 0867 for the previous tax year by 20 March (reg. 14); tax withheld is credited only against that certificate (reg. 15). ### TXF-34 Foreign tax above the treaty rate: the holder may bear more than the Israeli maximum, and the excess is not credited
- Rule: Two Tax Authority documents cap the foreign tax allowed as a credit at the lower of the tax actually paid and the rate in the treaty or the foreign domestic law. The 2019 letter on foreign funds says for a TASE-listed foreign fund: the foreign tax must have been withheld lawfully, paid to the foreign authority, and be such that it cannot be returned to the payer ("ולא יתכן שיוחזרו למשלם במדינה הזרה בדרך כלשהי"), and "מסי חוץ המותרים בזיכוי יהיו בשיעור שלא יעלה על שיעור המס הקבוע בדין הפנימי או באמנה (לעניין דיבידנד), הנמוך מבניהם". Ruling 5916/20 applies the same cap twice: at the withholder (s.3.2.2.2.2) and when the shareholder files an annual return (s.3.3.3.2), where the tax counts only if it is "מס סופי אשר לא הוחזר או יוחזר". So where the foreign country withholds at more than the treaty rate, the excess is neither credited by the withholder nor, in those two cases, at the annual return, and the total withheld can exceed the Israeli rate.
- Conditions / exceptions: The Ordinance's own words are narrower than that: s.204(ב) caps the credit on special-rate income at the Israeli tax on that income (TXF-18), s.205א carries an excess forward five years for the same source (TXF-19), and the guide allows a credit only for a final compulsory payment (TXF-17). Neither section names a treaty rate. The treaty cap is written only in the letter (its own funds) and the ruling (its own case, a US REIT), so a general statement rests on those two by extension, and on the wording of two banks. **[2026-11-07]** A third published ruling states the same cap at assessment: ruling 5257/26 (an Israeli individual whose US private investment funds are held through a family company) allows the credit only up to "סכום מיסי החוץ שהיו משולמים ע"י יחיד תושב ישראל על הכנסות החברה בהתאם להוראות האמנה", for example 25% on dividends from a US company, and none for US tax on gains from securities whose value is not mainly US real estate (s.3.2); it says it applies also to similar cases of entities transparent for Israeli tax (s.4). It is still a ruling on its own facts, not a directive to withholders. Without the 2019 letter, read only in a copy (open question 10), the cap rests on rulings 5916/20 and 5257/26. Discount and Mercantile tell customers that foreign tax withheld on foreign securities "יכול שיהיה בשיעור המס המרבי", possibly above the treaty rate, that "בהתאם להוראות רשות המסים בישראל" the Israeli top-up is measured against the treaty or domestic rate, the lower, and that the total withheld may exceed the Israeli maximum rate (each bank's own statement). The instruction they cite was not read: no general Tax Authority directive to withholders was found (TXF-10, open question 3). Whether the holder can recover the excess abroad, and how, was not found in any Israeli source read; nothing here says the excess is lost for good. Whether a carry-forward under s.205א applies to tax above the treaty rate, as opposed to tax within it that exceeds the Israeli tax, was not found either.

### TXF-35 A loss is offset before the foreign tax credit; a high foreign rate does not limit the loss
- Rule: Circular 10/2025 says the duty to offset losses, capital losses included, comes before the right to credit foreign tax against the Israeli tax on foreign income: "חובת הקיזוז של הפסדים בכלל והפסדי הון בפרט, קודמת לזכות הזיכוי של מסי חוץ" (s.6.3). Taxable income is fixed first, offsets included, and only then is the tax and the credit worked out. Separately, a loss abroad on which a gain would have borne no Israeli tax only because the source country's rate is above the Israeli rate "לא יוגבל בקיזוז רק בשל כך", since s.92 has nothing like s.29(3) (s.2.6).
- Conditions / exceptions: Derived: a loss that absorbs foreign income leaves less Israeli tax for the foreign tax to be credited against, so more of that foreign tax falls under the cap and the carry-forward (TXF-18, TXF-19). The sources named in s.6.3, position 28/2016 and the Amot decision, were not read.

### TXF-36 The Ordinance's trust chapter does not apply to a fund for joint investments outside Israel
- Rule: Section 75יז, the limit on the chapter "פרק רביעי 2: נאמנויות", says "הוראות פרק זה לא יחולו על כל אחד מאלה: (1) קרן נאמנות כהגדרתה בסעיף 88, וכן קרן להשקעות משותפות מחוץ לישראל". The Tax Authority's trust circular repeats it: "סעיף 75יז לפקודה קובע כי הוראות פרק הנאמנויות לא יחולו על כל אחד מאלה", naming the same two kinds of fund (s.12.1). The words date from Amendment 147 (2005) and stand unchanged in the current text.
- Conditions / exceptions: The chapter defines a trust as "הסדר שעל פיו מחזיק נאמן בנכסי הנאמן לטובת נהנה, שנעשה בישראל או מחוץ לישראל", so without this limit its words could reach a foreign fund organised as a trust (my reading). The term "קרן להשקעות משותפות מחוץ לישראל" appears once in the Ordinance and is not defined there (word search of two full copies); the bill's note on s.75יז ("מוצע לקבוע כי הוראות הפרק לא יחולו על קרן נאמנות") and the circular do not explain it. The nearest definition is in another law: "קרן חוץ" in s.113א of the Joint Investment Trust Law, "הסדר או תאגיד, שמטרתו השקעה משותפת בניירות ערך והפקת רווחים משותפת מהחזקתם ומכל עסקה בהם, שהוקם במדינת חוץ על פי דיניה". That a listed foreign ETF falls within the Ordinance's term is therefore a reading from the words, stated by no official text for such a fund. ### TXF-39 US tax withheld above what is due: a nonresident claims it back from the IRS on Form 1040-NR
- Rule: The IRS instructions for Form 1040-NR (2025) say that a nonresident who does not otherwise have to file "should file one if you can get money back", for example "You're eligible to get a refund of any federal income tax withheld", and that a filer "claiming a refund of U.S. tax withheld at source under chapter 3 or tax withheld under chapter 4" "must attach a copy of the Form 1042-S that shows the income and the amount of U.S. tax withheld". On one of the dividend payments, the withholding agent incorrectly withheld at a rate of 30% (instead of 15%). Alex is eligible to use the simplified procedure." The procedure is open to a nonresident with no US trade or business whose US tax was "fully satisfied through withholding of tax at the source" and who files "solely to claim a refund".
- Conditions / exceptions: The claim rests on a Form 1042-S that reports the income to the holder; the IRS example says "reported to Alex on Form(s) 1042-S". Deadlines, costs and how long a refund takes were not read. A bank's own term on reclaims: TXF-40.

### TXF-41 A US ETF organised as a trust: for the treaty it is a US corporation; its dividends bear at most 25% US tax, which Israel credits
- Rule: The US-Israel treaty defines a "United States corporation" as "a corporation (or any unincorporated entity treated as a corporation for United States tax purposes) which is created or organized under the laws of the United States or any state thereof" (Art. 2(1)(f)(i)). US income tax law defines a regulated investment company (RIC) as "any domestic corporation" registered under the Investment Company Act of 1940 "as a management company or unit investment trust" (26 USC 851(a)). So a US fund that is a RIC is a US corporation for the treaty, whatever its form under state law, and its dividends are US-source income ("only if paid by a corporation of that Contracting State", Art. 4(1)). The Second Protocol (1993) added to the dividends article: "Subparagraph (a) shall apply in the case of dividends paid by a Regulated Investment Company" (Art. 12(3)(a)), that is the 25% cap of Art. 12(2)(a) (TXF-12), never the 12.5% rate. The Treasury's technical explanation, "an official guide to the Convention", says: "Dividends paid by RICs are denied the 12.5 percent direct dividend rate and subjected to the 25 percent portfolio dividend rate". Israel "shall allow to a resident of Israel as a credit against Israeli tax the appropriate amount of income taxes paid or accrued to the United States", capped at the share of Israeli tax that the US-source net income bears to the whole (Art. 26(3)), and the Ordinance's credit applies with its cap per kind of income (TXF-17, TXF-18). So the US tax withheld on an ordinary distribution of such a fund, and its credit in Israel, are those of a dividend of a US company. The treaty's test is how US tax treats the fund, not whether state law calls it a trust or a company.
- Conditions / exceptions: Not every distribution of a RIC bears US tax. (1) Capital gains: "A capital gain dividend shall be treated by the shareholders as a gain from the sale or exchange of a capital asset held for more than 1 year" (26 USC 852(b)(3)(B)); the US taxes a nonresident alien individual's capital gains only "In the case of a nonresident alien individual present in the United States for a period or periods aggregating 183 days or more during the taxable year" (871(a)(2); TXF-14); and "A regulated investment company or intermediary may elect to not withhold on a distribution representing a capital gain dividend" (26 CFR 1.1441-3(c)(2)(i)(D)), so whether anything is withheld from one is the withholding agent's choice. (2) Interest-related and short-term capital gain dividends: "no tax shall be imposed" on them under the 30% tax (871(k)(1), (2); the second not for someone present 183 days or more), and "The mutual fund will designate in writing which dividends are interest-related dividends" (IRS Pub. 519). Inferred, no text states it: where no US tax was withheld there is nothing to credit, so the Israeli tax on that distribution falls in full. Credit cap: the Tax Authority's published ruling 5257/26 (an Israeli individual's US private investment funds held through a family company; it decides its own case and similar transparent entities) caps the US tax that counts at what an Israeli individual would pay under the treaty: "אם מדובר בהכנסות מדיבידנד שמקורם בחברה בארה"ב מיסי החוץ לא יעלו על 25%", and no credit for US tax on gains from securities whose value is not mainly US real estate (s.3.2; TXF-34). Not settled on the Israeli side: the treaty leaves a term it does not define to "the meaning which it has under the laws of the Contracting State whose tax is being determined" (Art. 2(2)), so its definition does not by itself classify the fund for Israeli tax. The Ordinance does not define a dividend; its source rule speaks of "מקום מושבו של חבר בני האדם משלם הדיבידנד" (s.4א(א)(10)), and a "חבר בני אדם" includes any company, partnership or association "בין שהן מואגדות ובין שאינן מואגדות" (s.1). Whether Israel taxes the fund's ordinary distribution as a dividend at the s.125B(1) rate (TXF-08), whether an Israeli bank withholds on it under reg. 2(ד), which says "דיבידנד על מניות" (TXF-30, open question 19), how Israel taxes a capital gain dividend (open question 27), and whether the unit is a security (TXF-37) are not stated in anything read. Whether a given fund is a RIC is that fund's own fact; VOO, IVV and ACWI are each a series, or a class of a series, of a Delaware statutory trust that says it intends to qualify (TXF-37). US estate tax on these funds: TXF-27.

25. **Reporting a final redemption held at a foreign broker (TXF-22, TXF-38).** s.91(ד)(2ג) has the half-yearly report for a security "הרשום למסחר בבורסה"; iShares says its funds are delisted "on or around" the redemption date. Whether such a redemption goes in the half-yearly report or in the 30-day report of s.91(ד)(1) is not stated. If the fund is a company in liquidation, s.91(ד)(2ד) adds a notice "בתוך 30 ימים מיום שהוחל בפירוק" and a report within 30 days of receiving the asset; whether a sub-fund's end is a liquidation of that kind is not stated. No Israeli text read puts a duty on the foreign broker, and what its statement shows was not read. Source: [the Ordinance](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/legalinfo/law_pkudat_mas_hachnasa/he/LegalInformation_kesher_%D7%A4%D7%A7%D7%95%D7%93%D7%AA%20%D7%9E%D7%A1%20%D7%94%D7%9B%D7%A0%D7%A1%D7%94%20%5B%D7%A0%D7%95%D7%A1%D7%97%20%D7%97%D7%93%D7%A9%5D%20-%20%D7%9C%D7%90%20%D7%9E%D7%A8%D7%95%D7%91%D7%93.pdf#page=152), file pp.152-153. Would settle it: a Tax Authority answer. Added 2026-11-01.
26. **A bank's holding commission on foreign fund units: does it reduce the gain?** Bank Hapoalim's general terms (09.23, p.28, s.22.8) let the bank receive, "החל מיום בו יותר על פי דין", a "עמלת קרן חוץ" from foreign fund managers or distributors for advice on or holding of foreign fund units, at "שיעור או סכום עמלת קרן החוץ המירבית המותרת על פי הדין", for each day a unit is held, and ends by applying "הוראות הסעיפים לעיל" to it "בשינויים המתחייבים". One of those sections, s.22.7, has the bank collect from the customer ("הבנק יגבה מאיתנו"), for each day units are held, the maximum distribution fee allowed from the manager, where the fund's manager has no distribution agreement with the bank (both on PDF p.28; [link](https://www.bankhapoalim.co.il/api/media/rvvhcfel/account-opening-request-adult-0923.pdf#page=28), library 5164629834e3d870, checked 2026-11-06, institution). Whether a holder of foreign fund units is ever charged under this is a reading, not stated. Tax reading: where the manager pays, the holder pays nothing that could be deducted, and the cost sits inside the fund (as TXF-16 for tax inside a fund). Where the holder is charged, whether the charge is an "הוצאות" item of the computation regulations (reg. 1: "דמי ניהול ודמי משמורת ... בעד פעולות בניירות ערך", TXS-06) is not stated in anything read. Would settle: the Tax Authority on which periodic charges count as expenses, or the approval under reg. 8 of the withholding regulations (TXS-06, TXS Q12). The commission itself and the law that caps it are product rules, for the domain-specialist (`mutual-funds-and-money-market-funds.md`, `etfs-and-index-funds.md`), reported 2026-11-06.
27. **How Israel taxes a US fund's capital gain dividend (TXF-41).** US law has the shareholder treat a RIC's capital gain dividend as a long-term capital gain (26 USC 852(b)(3)(B)), so an Israeli resident present in the US under 183 days bears no US tax on it (871(a)(2)), and the fund or the intermediary may choose not to withhold (26 CFR 1.1441-3(c)(2)(i)(D)). Whether Israel taxes the same payment as a dividend (25% of the nominal amount, s.125B(1)) or as a capital gain, and whether a bank withholds on it as a dividend, is not stated in anything read. It matters only in a year such a fund pays one. Would settle it: a Tax Authority ruling or circular on a US fund's distribution of capital gains. Added 2026-11-07.
