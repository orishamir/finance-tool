## Summary

- Gains on foreign securities and foreign funds are taxed like Israeli ones: up to 25% of the real capital gain, as the top slice of income (TXF-01, TXF-02).
- For a foreign-currency security held by an individual, the "real" gain is measured against the exchange rate, not the CPI, automatically (TXF-03). A foreign fund registered on TASE uses the exchange rate only if its prospectus commits to 90% main-currency assets; otherwise the CPI (TXF-04).
- Foreign dividends: 25% in Israel (TXF-08). Foreign tax on them is credited, capped at the Israeli tax on that income, per income-type basket, with a 5-year carry-forward (TXF-17 to TXF-20).
- US withholding on US dividends to an Israeli resident is capped at 25% by the treaty with a W-8BEN; 30% without (TXF-12, TXF-13). With 25% US and 25% Israeli, the credit leaves no further Israeli tax in principle (my computation from TXF-08, TXF-12, TXF-18).
- Tax suffered inside a foreign fund (for example US withholding on an Irish fund) is not creditable in Israel (TXF-16; explicit for TASE-registered foreign funds, TXF-06).
- No Israeli rule was found that taxes a non-controlling holder on a listed foreign fund's undistributed income; the CFC regime needs Israeli control (TXF-11). The Tax Authority's 2019 letter on TASE-registered foreign funds taxes the holder only on a sale and on distributions, which supports the sale-only reading by implication; an older circular (7/2003 s.4.4) pointed the other way. Since the 2005 Amendment 147, the Ordinance's trust chapter does not apply to "קרן להשקעות משותפות מחוץ לישראל" (TXF-36), and no Israeli text distinguishes a fund by legal form or compares a US fund with an Irish one (TXF-37). Q1 and Q19 are narrowed, not closed: there is still no official sentence about an accumulating foreign fund. A currency that fell gives no negative inflationary amount, and the currency part of a shekel loss is not allowable (TXF-03, Q5 closed 2026-10-26).
- Through an Israeli bank or TASE member a foreign fund is not self-reported, on whichever exchange it trades: the bank withholds 25% of the real gain on a sale (TXF-29) and 25% of a dividend (TXF-30). An accumulating fund changes only the timing: nothing to withhold until the sale (TXF-31). Each year the holder files nothing because of the fund, unless a general filing trigger applies, and keeps the form 867 (TXF-32).
- At a foreign broker nothing is withheld in Israel: semi-annual report and advance on gains, advance on dividends by 30 April, annual return (TXF-20 to TXF-22).
- More than USD 60,000 of US-situs assets (including shares of US corporations, so US-domiciled ETFs) creates a US estate tax filing duty; no US-Israel estate treaty is listed. Irish-domiciled fund shares are not US-situs (TXF-27).

## Rules

### TXF-01 Israeli residents are taxed on gains from foreign securities
- Rule: An Israeli resident is liable to tax on capital gains from selling an asset both in Israel and outside Israel. The Ordinance's "asset" covers property "בין שהם בישראל ובין שהם מחוץ לישראל", and "security" includes "נייר ערך שהונפק מחוץ לישראל".
- Conditions / exceptions: Olim and veteran returning residents have a 10-year exemption on foreign assets (TXF-26). A loss on an asset whose gain would have been exempt is not an allowable loss (TXF-23).

### TXF-02 Rate on the real capital gain
- Rule: An individual pays tax on real capital gain at a rate "שלא יעלה על 25%", and the capital gain is treated as the highest slice of taxable income (s.91(b)(1)). The Ordinance sets no separate rate for foreign assets.
- Conditions / exceptions: Substantial shareholder 30%; age 60+ starting rates; assets bought before 2012; unlinked debt at 15%; the 3% + 2% surtax: all in `tax-securities-israeli.md` and `tax-general.md`. The Tax Authority's 2026 table of special-rate income lists "רווח הון במכירת ניירות ערך" at 15%, 20%, 25%, 30%. *2026-10-25 rule-text pass:* quote made verbatim ("במכירת", not "ממכירת") and the printed guide page removed from the text (the Source line carries the file page).
- Figures: 25%, fixed in law (s.91(b)(1)); in force for 2026.

### TXF-03 Real gain on a foreign-currency security is measured against the exchange rate (automatic)
- Rule: For a security held by an individual that is denominated in a foreign currency or whose value is linked to one, the exchange rate is treated as the "index" for computing the inflationary amount, so the taxable real gain is the gain in that currency. Text of the s.88 definition of "מדד": "על אף האמור, לענין נייר ערך בידי יחיד, הנקוב במטבע חוץ או שערכו צמוד למטבע חוץ, יראו את שער המטבע כמדד".
- Conditions / exceptions: This is mandatory ("יראו"), not an election. The only election in the definition is for a person who bought an asset in foreign currency while a foreign resident: "מי שבהיותו תושב חוץ רכש נכס במטבע חוץ כדין, רשאי לבקש שיראו את שער המטבע שבו נרכש הנכס כמדד". A foreign fund registered on TASE follows a different test (TXF-04). If the currency fell over the holding period, the inflationary amount is zero, not negative: the whole shekel nominal gain is the real gain, and tax never exceeds the rate times that gain. If the sale ends in a shekel loss, the part of the loss caused by the currency's fall cannot be offset; only the rest is allowable. The Supreme Court so held for an individual's foreign-currency securities (ע"א 3555/15 מוזס, 12.9.2016, para 26: "בעסקה זו אין למעשה סכום אינפלציוני כהגדרתו בפקודה", and a loss "הנובע מהשינוי בשער החליפין לא היה חייב במס לו היה רווח"), and the Tax Authority's circular 10/2025 applies it in two steps: "בשלב הראשון - יש לחשב את הפסד ההון בערכים נומינליים", then "בשלב השני - יש לבודד את סכום הפסד ההון שאילו היה רווח היה חייב במס" (appendix, p.9; examples 3 and 4, p.11). A family company uses the CPI, not the exchange rate (ע"א 1038/17 אור שרה, the circular's note 13). Open question 5 closed 2026-10-26 (TXS-02 states the same for the CPI).
- Figures: none.

### TXF-04 Foreign fund registered on TASE ("קרן חוץ"): exchange rate or CPI as the index
- Rule: On a sale of a "קרן חוץ" unit, the index for the inflationary amount is the change in the exchange rate, provided the fund's prospectus states and commits that at least 90% of its investments in each tax year are in assets whose interest is paid by, or whose inflationary amount is computed by, the fund's main currency rate, and the fund notified TASE and committed to comply and report any change. "Main currency rate" is the foreign currency the fund chose in its prospectus. Otherwise the CPI is the index. The currency in which the fund is denominated is irrelevant.
- Conditions / exceptions: The source is a letter of the Tax Authority's capital-market taxation department to TASE, the Banks Association and the non-bank members' association, dated 4 July 2019, issued before foreign funds were first offered on TASE. Whether it is still in force was not confirmed (Q10). This differs from TXF-03, which applies automatically to a foreign-currency security held directly. The letter is read only in a copy on capitax.co.il; no official copy was found. *2026-10-25 rule-text pass:* searched again for an official copy (Wayback index of gov.il/BlobFolder filtered by the letter date and by foreign-fund words; web search for the title): none found, so the rule keeps the labelled copy.
- Figures: 90% of investments, each tax year (2019 letter).
- Source: [רשות המסים, מחלקת מיסוי שוק ההון: ניכוי מס במקור – קרנות חוץ (Foreign Funds), 4.7.2019 (עותק באתר capitax)](https://www.capitax.co.il/Attachments/04072019.pdf), pp.2-3, section 4.1.5 (starts on p.2; the 90% condition is on p.3, read on the rendered page, since the text layer prints digits through a broken font) · checked 2026-10-26 · primary (copy: no gov.il copy found)
- Scope: general
- Dimensions: `real-vs-nominal`, `currency`, `tax-at-exit`, `foreign-withholding-domicile`

### TXF-05 "קרן חוץ" on TASE: Israeli withholding as for a traded security
- Rule: Because a "קרן חוץ" is a "unit" under the Joint Investment Trust Law after its Amendment 23, the withholding provisions that apply to traded securities apply to it. On purchase or sale, capital-gain withholding follows the Income Tax Regulations (withholding from consideration, payment or capital gain on sale of a security, a fund unit or a future transaction), 2002. On a distribution to an individual, tax is withheld at the s.125B(1) rate (25%); to a company, at the s.126(a) rate; a foreign-resident unit holder is exempt from withholding.
- Conditions / exceptions: Under those regulations a payer ("חייב", "אדם המשלם למוכר תמורה או תשלום, בעד מכירת נייר ערך") paying an individual seller withholds "בשיעור של 25% מרווח ההון הריאלי" (reg. 2(a)) and offsets losses on securities it manages in the same tax year (reg. 9(a)). 2026-10-26 (scope settling): Bank Leumi's ETF page, which repeated these rules, was dropped as a source.
- Figures: 25% of real gain (reg. 2(a)); 25% on distributions to individuals (s.125B(1)).
- Source: [רשות המסים, מחלקת מיסוי שוק ההון: ניכוי מס במקור – קרנות חוץ (Foreign Funds), 4.7.2019 (עותק באתר capitax)](https://www.capitax.co.il/Attachments/04072019.pdf), p.2, sections 4, 4.1.1, 4.1.2 · checked 2026-10-25 · primary (copy: no gov.il copy found). ### TXF-06 "קרן חוץ" on TASE: foreign tax credit only for tax on the fund's own distributions
- Rule: An Israeli-resident unit holder in a "קרן חוץ" is entitled to a credit for foreign tax "ששולם בגין חלוקת רווחים בלבד מקרן החוץ", subject to ss.199-210, provided the foreign tax on the distribution was lawfully withheld in the foreign state, actually paid to that state's tax authority and cannot be refunded to the payer in any way. The creditable foreign tax is capped at the lower of the domestic-law rate and the treaty rate (for dividends). The "foreign state" is the fund's state of origin as stated in the annex to the fund's prospectus.
- Conditions / exceptions: Tax suffered inside the fund on its underlying holdings is outside this credit (it is not tax on a distribution from the fund).
- Figures: none.
- Source: [Tax Authority letter 4.7.2019](http://www.capitax.co.il/Attachments/04072019.pdf), section 4.1.4 · checked 2026-10-22 · primary.
- Dimensions: `foreign-withholding-domicile`, `distribution-policy`, `tax-during`
- Likely to change: yes (administrative letter).

### TXF-07 "קרן חוץ" distributions: reported on form 867ג; stated as not offsettable against securities losses
- Rule: Payments a "קרן חוץ" distributes and the tax withheld on them are listed on form 867ג. The letter adds: "הכנסה זו אינה ניתנת לקיזוז מהפסדי הון מניירות ערך".
- Conditions / exceptions: This appears to conflict with s.92(a)(4), which lets a securities loss of the year offset dividends on securities taxed at no more than 25% (TXF-23). Whether the letter means only the withholder's automatic offset or also the annual return is open (Q9).

### TXF-08 Dividends from foreign companies and foreign funds: 25%
- Rule: Dividend income of an individual is taxed at 25% (30% for a substantial shareholder in the paying company), notwithstanding ss.121 and 126. The Tax Authority's 2026 list of special-rate income shows "דיבידנד מחברות זרות- 25%, 30%".
- Conditions / exceptions: Foreign tax on the dividend is credited (TXF-17 to TXF-19). Dividends on a foreign security can absorb a securities loss of the same year (TXF-23).
- Figures: 25% / 30%, fixed in law (s.125B); listed for tax year 2026.
- Source: [פקודת מס הכנסה [נוסח חדש], נוסח רשות המסים](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/legalinfo/law_pkudat_mas_hachnasa/he/LegalInformation_kesher_%D7%A4%D7%A7%D7%95%D7%93%D7%AA%20%D7%9E%D7%A1%20%D7%94%D7%9B%D7%A0%D7%A1%D7%94%20%5B%D7%A0%D7%95%D7%A1%D7%97%20%D7%97%D7%93%D7%A9%5D%20-%20%D7%9C%D7%90%20%D7%9E%D7%A8%D7%95%D7%91%D7%93.pdf), p.203, s.125ב(1), (2) ("דיבידנד בידי יחיד – 25%") · checked 2026-10-25 · primary. ### TXF-09 Interest on foreign securities: up to 25%
- Rule: "ריבית המשתלמת על ניירות ערך זרים חייבת במס בשיעור שלא יעלה על 25%" (reported in section 15 of form 1301, fields 357/257/157). Older accrual periods (before 2012) have their own rates.
- Conditions / exceptions: The text concerns interest paid on foreign securities (for example foreign bonds). Whether interest-like distributions of a foreign bond fund are treated as interest or as dividend was not checked (Q14).
- Figures: 25% (interest accrued from 1.1.2012), per the 2025 guide.

### TXF-10 Israeli intermediary that receives a foreign dividend for an Israeli resident withholds 25%
- Rule: "המקבל מתושב חוץ דיבידנד על מניות בשל תושב ישראל או לזכותו, ינכה ממנו מס בשיעורים אלה: (1) שולם הדיבידנד ליחיד – 25%; (2) שולם הדיבידנד לחבר־בני־האדם – בשיעור המס המרבי."
- Conditions / exceptions: The regulation sets 25% on the gross dividend and does not mention foreign tax already withheld abroad. In practice the withholder credits the foreign tax withheld, up to the lower of the tax actually paid and the treaty (or foreign domestic) rate, and withholds only the difference. The Tax Authority's published ruling 5916/20 (a US REIT listed on TASE; a ruling made in agreement, published 8.9.2020) says the TASE member withholds "מסכום ההכנסה ברוטו - לפני זיכוי בגין מסי חוץ" and "יהא רשאי לתת זיכוי בגין סכום מס זר ששולם בפועל לשלטונות המס בארה"ב", capped at what the treaty allows; the 2019 letter on foreign funds caps creditable foreign tax the same way ("בשיעור שלא יעלה על שיעור המס הקבוע בדין הפנימי או באמנה (לעניין דיבידנד), הנמוך מבניהם", TXF-06). Not binding as a general instruction: a ruling decides its own case, and the rulings database says the public may expect the same treatment only "בכפוף לקיום מערכת נסיבות דומה"; no general directive to withholders was found. ### TXF-11 A non-controlling holder of a listed foreign fund is taxed on sale and distributions only (by implication; no deemed-distribution rule)
- Rule: An individual who is not a controlling holder of a listed foreign fund is taxed on two events: a sale or redemption of the unit (capital gain, TXF-01 to TXF-03) and a distribution (dividend, TXF-08); income the fund reinvests reaches the tax base only through the sale price. ### TXF-12 US withholding on US dividends paid to an Israeli resident: treaty cap 25%
- Rule: Under the US-Israel income tax treaty, US tax on dividends from US sources paid to an Israeli resident may not exceed "(a) 25 percent of the gross amount of the dividend paid"; 12.5% applies only when the recipient is a corporation owning at least 10% of the voting stock and the payer meets a passive-income test (Art. 12(2)). ### TXF-16 Tax suffered inside a foreign fund is a fund-level cost, not creditable in Israel
- Rule: The Ordinance defines creditable "מסי־חוץ" as "מסים המשתלמים על ידי תושב ישראל לרשויות המס של מדינה מחוץ לישראל, על הכנסה שהופקה או שנצמחה באותה המדינה", computed as a percentage of income, excluding municipal taxes. Tax withheld from a foreign fund on its underlying holdings is paid by the fund, not by the Israeli resident. For a fund domiciled in Ireland, the US treaty rate on dividends from US corporations paid to an Irish resident is 15% (IRS table, Ireland, general column, footnote mm), against 25% for an Israeli resident holding the US shares directly (TXF-12).
- Conditions / exceptions: For TASE-registered foreign funds, the Tax Authority says this explicitly: credit only for tax on the fund's own distributions, in its state of origin (TXF-06). For a foreign-domiciled fund bought abroad, the non-creditability is my reading of the s.199 definition. The US-Ireland treaty treats an Irish collective investment undertaking as an Irish resident ("in the case of Ireland, a Collective Investment Undertaking", Art. 4(1)(d)) and caps US tax on dividends paid to it at "15 percent of the gross amount of the dividends in all other cases" (Art. 10(2)(b)), if it passes the limitation-on-benefits article (Art. 23: for example, its principal class of shares "is substantially and regularly traded on one or more recognized stock exchanges", which include the Irish Stock Exchange, London and Amsterdam). Whether a given Irish fund qualifies, and what US tax it actually suffered, is that fund's fact (its annual report), not a general rule (open question 7, narrowed 2026-10-26). ### TXF-18 Foreign tax credit cap: by income source ("basket"), not by country

### TXF-23 Losses on foreign securities

### TXF-27 US estate tax exposure of a non-US person holding US-situs assets

### TXF-28 Ireland deducts no tax from an Irish ETF's distributions or redemptions for an Israeli resident
- Rule: An Irish fund ("investment undertaking") deducts Irish exit tax on a "chargeable event", which includes "the making of a regular payment by a fund to a unit holder other than on the cancellation, redemption of units or repurchase of a unit (e.g. an annual dividend)". Irish Revenue's manual says "any transaction in relation to, or in respect of, units which are held in a recognised clearing system does not require the deduction of exit tax", which "would be the case for an ETF where the units/shares in the underlying fund are bought and sold between investors on the stock market and are cleared through a recognised clearing system"; only an Irish resident unit holder must then self-assess. For units not held in a clearing system, "Exit tax is not required to be deducted in respect of the following non-resident unit holders provided that the fund is in possession of an appropriate non-resident declaration prior to the chargeable event" (or Revenue-approved equivalent measures). So an Israeli resident holding an Irish UCITS ETF receives its distributions without Irish tax, and Israel taxes them at 25% with no Irish tax to credit (TXF-08, TXF-17).
- Conditions / exceptions: This is Irish tax on the payment to the holder. Tax suffered inside the Irish fund on its own holdings (for example US withholding at the US-Ireland treaty rate) is a fund-level cost and is not creditable in Israel (TXF-16). The Israel-Ireland treaty was not read; with no Irish deduction it does not change the result. Which clearing systems Revenue recognises is in its manual Part 08-03-04 (not read).
- Figures: none.

### TXF-32 What the holder does each year when an Israeli bank or TASE member withholds
- Rule: Holding a foreign fund through a withholder adds no filing of its own. ### TXF-34 Foreign tax above the treaty rate: the holder may bear more than the Israeli maximum, and the excess is not credited
- Rule: Two Tax Authority documents cap the foreign tax allowed as a credit at the lower of the tax actually paid and the rate in the treaty or the foreign domestic law. - Conditions / exceptions: The Ordinance's own words are narrower than that: s.204(ב) caps the credit on special-rate income at the Israeli tax on that income (TXF-18), s.205א carries an excess forward five years for the same source (TXF-19), and the guide allows a credit only for a final compulsory payment (TXF-17). ### TXF-35 A loss is offset before the foreign tax credit; a high foreign rate does not limit the loss
- Rule: Circular 10/2025 says the duty to offset losses, capital losses included, comes before the right to credit foreign tax against the Israeli tax on foreign income: "חובת הקיזוז של הפסדים בכלל והפסדי הון בפרט, קודמת לזכות הזיכוי של מסי חוץ" (s.6.3). ### TXF-36 The Ordinance's trust chapter does not apply to a fund for joint investments outside Israel
- Rule: Section 75יז, the limit on the chapter "פרק רביעי 2: נאמנויות", says "הוראות פרק זה לא יחולו על כל אחד מאלה: (1) קרן נאמנות כהגדרתה בסעיף 88, וכן קרן להשקעות משותפות מחוץ לישראל". The Tax Authority's trust circular repeats it: "סעיף 75יז לפקודה קובע כי הוראות פרק הנאמנויות לא יחולו על כל אחד מאלה", naming the same two kinds of fund (s.12.1). The words date from Amendment 147 (2005) and stand unchanged in the current text.
- Conditions / exceptions: The chapter defines a trust as "הסדר שעל פיו מחזיק נאמן בנכסי הנאמן לטובת נהנה, שנעשה בישראל או מחוץ לישראל", so without this limit its words could reach a foreign fund organised as a trust (my reading). The term "קרן להשקעות משותפות מחוץ לישראל" appears once in the Ordinance and is not defined there (word search of two full copies); the bill's note on s.75יז ("מוצע לקבוע כי הוראות הפרק לא יחולו על קרן נאמנות") and the circular do not explain it. The nearest definition is in another law: "קרן חוץ" in s.113א of the Joint Investment Trust Law, "הסדר או תאגיד, שמטרתו השקעה משותפת בניירות ערך והפקת רווחים משותפת מהחזקתם ומכל עסקה בהם, שהוקם במדינת חוץ על פי דיניה". That a listed foreign ETF falls within the Ordinance's term is therefore a reading from the words, stated by no official text for such a fund. The section says what does not apply; it does not say how the holder is taxed (TXF-11, open question 23).

### TXF-37 A foreign fund's legal form: the 2019 letter covers an arrangement or a corporation alike; no official comparison of a US and an Irish fund was found
- Rule: The Tax Authority's 2019 letter quotes the definition of "קרן חוץ" as "הסדר או תאגיד", and gives its withholding, credit and index provisions for a "קרן חוץ" without any distinction by legal form. So for a fund registered on TASE, being a trust or a corporation does not change those provisions (TXF-04 to TXF-07). No official Israeli text was found that compares a US-domiciled fund in trust form, a regulated investment company (RIC), with an Irish-domiciled fund, or that taxes a holder differently because of the fund's legal form or domicile.
- Conditions / exceptions: Not stated anywhere read: how a US fund in trust form bought at a foreign broker, and not registered on TASE, is classified in Israel; and whether a distribution on a unit of a trust is "דיבידנד על מניות" for reg. 2(ד) (open question 19). Inferred, no official text says it: such a fund is taxed like any foreign security, because the s.88 definition takes in "נייר ערך שהונפק מחוץ לישראל" with no test of form, the trust chapter is closed to it (TXF-36), and the controlled-foreign-company rule needs control (TXF-11). Ruling 5916/20 on a US REIT is an analogy, not a statement on funds (TXF-11). The differences between a US and an Irish fund that were read are not Israeli classification rules: US withholding on the fund's own distributions (TXF-12) against fund-level US tax that is not creditable (TXF-16), and US estate tax (TXF-27). Example of the facts: BlackRock says iShares Trust "was organized as a Delaware statutory trust" and that IVV intends "to qualify as a regulated investment company". US law defines a RIC, for that subtitle, as "any domestic corporation"; that is US classification and does not bind Israel. The legal form of VOO and ACWI was not read in their own documents.

Rules that rest only on the guide are tagged primary as a Tax Authority publication but should be checked against the law where possible.
