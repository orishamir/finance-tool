## Summary

- Kupat gemel lehashkaa: no deduction or credit on deposits; gains inside the fund are not taxed; switching tracks or moving to another kupat gemel lehashkaa is not a withdrawal. A lump sum at or below the deposits carries no taxable gain, so nothing is taxed or withheld; whether the shortfall offsets anything is open (TXK-30, open question 10).
- A lump-sum withdrawal at any age is taxed: the law treats the gains withdrawn as interest income, taxed at up to 25% (s.3(ה4)(3), s.125C). The part of the gains equal to linkage differentials is exempt (s.9(13), which reaches a provident-fund account since Amendment 190 deleted the exclusion from 1.1.2012), so the tax is up to 25% of the real gain, as the 2016 bill's notes say; the manager withholds (TXK-07). The manager withholds 25% on the gains and nothing on the principal with its linkage, by the Tax Authority's withholder file; the regulation's text reads 20% (TXK-26).
- What changes at 60: from early retirement age (60) the balance may be moved to a provident fund for annuity, only to receive an annuity. Because the person's own deposits count as "exempt payments", the resulting "recognized annuity" is fully exempt from 60 (s.9A(b1)). A lump sum taken straight from the kupat gemel lehashkaa after 60 is taxed the same as before 60.
- A special exemption in s.9(18ב) (5 years, age 60, no apartment purchase) is not the general age-60 rule. It belonged to the 2016 multiple-apartments chapter and its status is unresolved.
- IRA: in law an IRA is a provident fund "in personal management". No IRA-specific tax rule was found, so it follows the wrapper it sits in.
- Child savings: the deposits, the parental addition and the grants are not income. Gains are taxed on withdrawal; National Insurance says "capital gains tax" at "25% of the gains" for both routes, and no Tax Authority text names child savings. Provident route: the law puts the money in a kupat gemel lehashkaa, and the Capital Market Authority's circular and the Ministry of Finance's tender committee (with the Commissioner's approval) say every rule of a kupat gemel lehashkaa applies to it, so a withdrawal is taxed as one: up to 25% of the real gain, withheld by the managing company (TXK-23). Bank route: the Ministry of Finance says the existing tax rules apply; by them 15% on the unlinked tracks and up to 25% of the real interest on the linked one, the rates still a reading (TXK-29). A medical withdrawal before 18 is not taxed at 35% (TXK-24). The annuity route is stated by National Insurance and the Minister of Finance, but its statutory basis is still unclear (TXK-25).

## Rules

### TXK-01 No deduction or credit on deposits to a kupat gemel lehashkaa
- Rule: The pension credit (s.45A(b), 35%) and deduction (s.47(b)) apply only to sums paid "לקופת גמל לקצבה". No provision gives a credit or deduction for deposits to a kupat gemel lehashkaa. The Ordinance lists sums an individual pays for himself into a kupat gemel lehashkaa as "תשלומים פטורים" (non-benefited "exempt payments", s.9A(a) para (4)), with no condition about credits. Para (3), for annuity funds, carries that condition.
- Conditions / exceptions: Deposits are made from income already taxed. The study-fund deduction for the self-employed (s.17(5A)) is a separate wrapper (`tax-hishtalmut.md`). A Knesset private bill's explanatory notes (2023, פ/3539/25, p.3) describe the product as savings "בלא הטבות מס בהפקדה". That is not law, but it agrees; the rule rests on the Ordinance, and the bill is no longer cited for it (2026-11-07).

### TXK-03 No tax on growth inside the fund
- Rule: The income of a provident fund is exempt unless it comes from a business the fund carries on, or from a company in a business the fund controls or holds a material holding in (s.9(2)(a)). "קופת גמל" here excludes an insurance fund (s.9(2)(b)). A kupat gemel lehashkaa is a provident fund (Provident Funds Law s.1, s.13(a)).
- Conditions / exceptions: Amendment 287 of the Ordinance (Economic Efficiency Law 2026, ספר החוקים 3511, 31.3.2026, p.415, s.4) replaced the definition of "החזקה מהותית" in s.9(2)(b) and widened the "שליטה" carve-out from a high-technology fund to any body of persons; the exemption itself stands. The business-income exception exists; how it would apply to a fund, or to an IRA (TXK-17), was not researched. Tax arises only when the member withdraws (TXK-05).

### TXK-04 Track switches and transfers between kupot gemel lehashkaa, whole or part, are not taxed
- Rule: The tax charge in s.3(ה4)(3) applies to sums an individual "משך" (withdrew) from a kupat gemel lehashkaa. Switching tracks inside the fund, or transferring the balance "מקופת גמל להשקעה לקופת גמל להשקעה אחרת", is not a withdrawal. The fund's own sales are exempt fund income (TXK-03). Whether the payment dates carry over on such a transfer is not stated: reg. 3(ב)(1) of the transfer regulations carries them over for transfers "למעט לפי - (1) פסקאות (8) עד (12) שבה", and route (11א), added in 2020 between (11) and (12), falls inside that range by its numbering (SW-39, SW open question 17; corrected 2026-10-26, scope settling).
- Conditions / exceptions: The legal text has no explicit sentence that says "no tax event" for a kupat gemel lehashkaa switch. That conclusion rests on the withdrawal wording plus the fund-level exemption, and the Ministry of Finance's team on short- and medium-term savings states it for every kupat gemel lehashkaa: "מעבר בין מסלולי השקעה שונים בתוך הקופה, וכן מעבר בין קופות גמל להשקעה אינה נחשבת 'אירוע מס' (דחיית מס)" (interim report for public comment, Feb 2025, p.14; a government report, not binding, describing the law in force). 2026-10-26 (scope settling): Harel's flyer ("ללא אירוע מס"), which only repeated this, was dropped as a source. A partial transfer (KGL-10) is inferred to be the same: it is not a withdrawal either, though neither the Ordinance nor the report speaks of part separately (2026-11-02, issue #165). A transfer to an annuity fund is covered in TXK-10. A transfer out to a different wrapper (for example a brokerage account) is a withdrawal.

### TXK-05 Lump-sum withdrawal: gains are treated as interest income
- Rule: "ריבית ורווחים אחרים ... שמשך יחיד מקופת גמל להשקעה, יראו אותם כהכנסה מריבית". Gains equal to the linkage differentials computed on the money are treated as income from linkage differentials. A partial withdrawal is treated as coming proportionally from principal, interest and linkage ("באופן יחסי").
- Conditions / exceptions: No age, holding-period or purpose condition appears in s.3(ה4)(3). A withdrawal at 30 and a withdrawal at 65 are classified the same way. Only the deposited principal comes out untaxed. The provision says "יחיד" (an individual) and does not distinguish who deposited. This is relevant to child savings (TXK-23).
- Figures: none.
- Source: [פקודת מס הכנסה [נוסח חדש], נוסח רשות המסים](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/legalinfo/law_pkudat_mas_hachnasa/he/LegalInformation_kesher_%D7%A4%D7%A7%D7%95%D7%93%D7%AA%20%D7%9E%D7%A1%20%D7%94%D7%9B%D7%A0%D7%A1%D7%94%20%5B%D7%A0%D7%95%D7%A1%D7%97%20%D7%97%D7%93%D7%A9%5D%20-%20%D7%9C%D7%90%20%D7%9E%D7%A8%D7%95%D7%91%D7%93.pdf), p.21, s.3(ה4)(3) "יראו אותם כהכנסה מריבית" · checked 2026-10-25 · primary.

### TXK-06 Rate on that interest: up to 25%, as the top slice of income
- Rule: s.125C applies to interest deemed under s.3(ה4)(3), even though it otherwise excludes payments from provident funds (s.125C(e)). Under s.125C(b) an individual pays "בשיעור שלא יעלה על 25%" on interest income, and that income is treated as the highest step of his taxable income. s.125C(c)(1) sets 15% where the interest is paid on an asset that is not CPI-linked, or only partly linked.
- Conditions / exceptions: s.125C(d) moves interest to marginal rates in listed cases (business income, interest expense claimed, substantial shareholder, and others). None names a kupat gemel lehashkaa. The 15% limb does not reach these gains: s.3(ה4)(3) carves the linkage on the money out as linkage income, exempt under s.9(13), and the explanatory notes to the 2016 bill say the gains "יהיו חייבים במס רווחי הון בשיעור 25% מהרווח הריאלי" (TXK-07). Corrected 2026-10-25 (tax-bases research): the record said no official source stated whether 25% on the real gain or 15% on the nominal gain applied. Surtax (s.121B) may apply on top (`tax-general.md`). A person aged 60 or older can pay less than 25%: s.125C(b) is a maximum rate, which lets lower brackets apply first (Tax Authority guide 2025, p.40), and s.121(ב)(1) gives the reduced brackets (10%, 14%, 20%, 31%) to all taxable income of an individual aged 60 or over. Because the gains are the top slice of income, this helps only where other income leaves those brackets unused (TXI-19, TXG-08). Corrected 2026-10-26 (open-questions audit, open question 12): the record said this was open.
- Figures: 25% maximum; 15% for non-linked assets. In force 2026. Fixed in law; the Minister may change the 15% by order (s.125C(c)(2)).

### TXK-07 Lump sum: the linkage part is exempt, the rest is taxed at up to 25% (the real gain); the manager withholds
- Rule: Gains an individual withdraws from a kupat gemel lehashkaa are interest income, "ואולם רווחים שקיבל כאמור בסכום הפרשי הצמדה שחושבו על הכספים כאמור יראו אותם כהכנסה מהפרשי הצמדה" (Ordinance s.3(ה4)(3); TXK-05). Linkage differentials an individual receives on an asset are exempt, "ובלבד שהתקיימו כל אלה": they are not partial linkage differentials, no interest or linkage expense was claimed for the asset, and they are not business income (s.9(13)). The sentence that kept a provident-fund account out of that exemption ("הוראות פסקה זו לא יחולו על נכס שהוא חשבון בקופת גמל", added by Amendment 147 in 2005) was deleted by Amendment 190 s.4(2), in force 1.1.2012. s.125C applies to these gains (s.125C(e)), so the rest is taxed at up to 25%, as the top slice of income (s.125C(b); TXK-06). The legislator said it in so many words: the gains "יהיו חייבים במס רווחי הון בשיעור 25% מהרווח הריאלי" (explanatory notes to the 2016 bill that added kupat gemel lehashkaa to s.3(ה4)(3)). The managing company withholds at source: the 2005 withholding regulations define "קופת גמל" as a fund (other than a study fund) whose payments s.125C governs, and their "ריבית" excludes linkage differentials that are exempt by law, so the withholding base is the real gain too.
- Conditions / exceptions: The 2005 bill's notes give the same mechanism for provident and policy gains: "מרכיב הפרשי ההצמדה ברווחים אלה זכאי לפטור ממס בידי יחיד, לפי סעיף 9(13) לפקודה, אם אינם הכנסה מעסק בידיו"; the 2012 bill's notes describe the s.3(ה4)(3) route as taxing the gains "למעט הפרשי ההצמדה". The Tax Authority's 2025 guide still prints the deleted sentence in its s.9(13) row (p.89); that line is the pre-2012 law and is not cited (the Tax Authority's own consolidated Ordinance, published 15.1.2023, and Nevo, updated 27.9.2026, no longer have it). s.3(ה4)(3) names no index; the s.1 definition of linkage differentials for an exemption covers linkage to the CPI or an exchange rate; that managers compute against the CPI is their practice. The capital-gains "inflationary amount" (s.88) is not the route, since the gains are interest income. The withholding rate is 25% on the gains and none on the principal with its linkage, by the Tax Authority's withholder file, whose exempt type is "קרן כולל הפרשי הצמדה על הקרן (פטור)" (TXK-26, updated 2026-11-06; reg. 3(א)'s text reads 20%); withholding is an advance, and the final tax is the rate above. Corrected 2026-10-25 (tax-bases research): the record said the base and the 25% rested on a manager's document and that the exempting provision was not found. Split on 2026-10-25: the withholding rate is TXK-26.

### TXK-30 A lump sum below what was deposited: nothing is taxed or withheld; no text makes the shortfall a loss, and an offset is open
- Rule: Read: what a withdrawal from a kupat gemel lehashkaa brings into income is "ריבית ורווחים אחרים ... שמשך יחיד מקופת גמל להשקעה", and the part of those profits equal to the linkage differentials on the money is linkage income (s.3(ה4)(3); TXK-05), which s.9(13) exempts (TXK-07); a partial withdrawal is split "באופן יחסי" between principal, interest and linkage (TXK-13). The Tax Authority's withholder file has two payment types for such a withdrawal: "קרן כולל הפרשי הצמדה על הקרן (פטור)" (type 87) and "ריבית ורווחים אחרים" at 25% (type 88). The 2016 bill that added the kupat gemel lehashkaa to s.3(ה4)(3) explains it as applying "אם הכספים שהצטברו בקופת הגמל להשקעה נשאו רווחים". Inferred, no text says it in one sentence: a lump sum that returns no more than the deposits carries no interest or other profit, so no tax falls on it and the managing company withholds nothing; one that returns more than the deposits but no more than the deposits with their linkage carries linkage income only, which is exempt (TXK-07). Whether the shortfall below the deposits is a loss that reduces tax on anything else is not stated in any text read (open question 10).
- Conditions / exceptions: Read versus inferred, as TXI-26 records for a bank deposit's lost principal. No provision read lets the shortfall be deducted or offset: "לא יותרו ניכויים או קיזוזים, ולא יינתנו פטורים, הנחות ממס או הקלות אחרות, אלא אם נקבעו במפורש בחוק" (s.245(א)). The only express route would be a capital loss under s.92, but the Ordinance classes what is withdrawn as interest, not as a capital gain (s.3(ה4)(3)), and no text read says whether a member's right in the fund is an asset whose redemption can give a capital loss under s.88. Even as a capital loss, a loss that is not from securities offsets only capital gains or betterment ("יהיה ניתן לקיזוז כנגד רווח הון או שבח בלבד", circular 10/2025 s.3), and only through an annual return. In the nearest decided case, a linked bank deposit whose linkage turned negative, the loss on that passive source was not offset against the interest; the district court found no provision for it, and the Supreme Court left open whether any route exists (TXI-25). The other direction, an outside securities loss against the fund's gains, is barred by the reading of circular 10/2025 s.3.7 recorded in open question 10. The Ministry of Finance team's report of July 2026 recommends, for a planned regime, no offset between such a fund's gains and capital losses outside an investment account; a recommendation, not law (TXK-21). These rules reach child savings in a fund (TXK-23) and a self-managed kupat gemel lehashkaa (TXK-17), which follow the kupat gemel lehashkaa. ### TXK-09 Recognized annuity is exempt from age 60
- Rule: "(ב1) הקצבה המוכרת שמקבל אחד מאלה פטורה ממס: (1) יחיד שמלאו לו 60 שנים; (2) יחיד שפרש פרישה מוקדמת כאמור בפסקה (2) להגדרה 'גיל פרישה'". The definition covers early retirement because of a stable disability of 75% or more.
- Conditions / exceptions: s.9A(b1) states no ceiling and no income test, unlike the qualifying-annuity exemption in s.9A(b) (`tax-pension-gemel-lekitzba.md`). The cap is for new and future savings: a saver whose kupat gemel lehashkaa is worth more than the cap on the day the change starts gets no further favoured deposits, but "יוכל מגיל 60 למשוך חיסכון זה כקצבה בפטור ממס רווחי הון גם על הכספים המוטבים וגם על כספים לא מוטבים" (report p.16, s.12.4.2), so under the report an existing fund above the cap keeps the age-60 annuity exemption on all of it. A record that says "no ceiling" should say both, with that transition. The exemption covers the whole recognized annuity, principal and gains. NII and health contributions on such an annuity were not researched (open question 9).

### TXK-10 The route to the exemption: transfer to a provident fund for annuity from age 60

### TXK-11 Capitalizing a recognized annuity: 15% on the "relative profit component"
- Rule: "על סכום שהתקבל עקב היוון קצבה מוכרת, אשר אינו פטור ממס לפי פסקה (1), יוטל מס רק בשל מרכיב הרווח היחסי, בשיעור של 15%" (s.9A(e)(3)). The relative profit component is the capitalized amount times (accumulated balance minus exempt payments), divided by the accumulated balance. The accumulated balance is the money from exempt payments in the person's annuity-fund account, up to the profit calculation before the entitlement age.

Whether money that came from a kupat gemel lehashkaa may be capitalized at all is open (TXK-10). ### TXK-12 The founder's age-60 example: what changes at 60 and what does not
- Rule (composite of TXK-01 to TXK-11; it adds no new law): The claim "a provident fund doesn't pay the 25% tax if you withdraw after 60" is true only for one route.
  - **Unchanged at 60:** A lump sum taken straight from the kupat gemel lehashkaa is still deemed interest and taxed at up to 25% (TXK-05, TXK-06; managers apply 25% on the real gain, TXK-07). Before and after 60, growth is untaxed and track switches and moves between kupot gemel lehashkaa are not taxed (TXK-03, TXK-04). Deposits never got a deduction (TXK-01).
  - **New at 60:** The balance may be moved to a provident fund for annuity, into an annuitants' track, for an annuity only (TXK-10). The person's own deposits are exempt payments (TXK-08), so the annuity they produce is a recognized annuity, fully exempt from 60 (TXK-09), principal and gains, with no ceiling in the statute. If such an annuity is capitalized (permission open), 15% applies to the nominal profit share (TXK-11).
  - **Not the general rule:** s.9(18ב) (five years, age 60, no apartment bought) covered only a closed 2016-2017 deposit window and its status is unresolved (TXK-15).
- Conditions / exceptions: The age-60 benefit means trading a lump sum for an annuity. The comparison with an ETF in a brokerage account therefore depends on whether the person wants an annuity. The exemption may be narrowed (TXK-21).
- Figures: 25% (lump sum), 0% (recognized annuity from 60), 15% (capitalized recognized annuity). 2026, fixed in law.
- Source: as in TXK-01 to TXK-11 · checked 2026-10-22 · primary for the law; institution for the 25%-on-real-gain practice · links from the cited rules: [Income Tax Ordinance, National Insurance Institute's consolidated copy, file of 21.6.2026](https://www.btl.gov.il/Laws1/00_0103_000000.pdf), [2](<https://www.nevo.co.il/law_html/law01/255_001.htm>), [3](<https://fs.knesset.gov.il/25/law/25_lst_2708087.pdf>), [4: Provident Funds Law s.22, gazette ס״ח תשס״ה p.898, file p.10](https://fs.knesset.gov.il/16/law/16_lsr_299984.pdf#page=10) ([unofficial consolidated text, the only copy found of the current wording](<https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_הפיקוח_על_שירותים_פיננסיים_(קופות_גמל)>)), [5](<https://media.harel-group.co.il/media/eh3fyryd/תקרות-הפקדה-בקופות-הגמל-ובקרנות-השתלמות-2026.pdf>), [6: transfer regulations, gazette ק״ת תשס״ח p.626, reg. 2 file p.4](https://free-justice.openapi.gov.il/free/moj/portal/rest/searchpredefinedapi/v1/SearchPredefinedApi/Documents/dDKkjTfMz0H3iRxP7u8fpoeVB94OXb~VXxIiNxEmuFE=#page=4) ([unofficial consolidated text, the only copy found of the current wording](<https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/תקנות_הפיקוח_על_שירותים_פיננסיים_(קופות_גמל)_(העברת_כספים_בין_קופות_גמל)>))

### TXK-18 IRA and other provident funds: foreign dividends and withholding inside the account
- Rule: In Israel, dividends and interest a provident fund receives are exempt fund income (s.9(2)(a)), with no Israeli withholding owed by the member while the money stays in the fund. Foreign tax withheld at source is a cost inside the fund. No Israeli source found says whether it can be reduced or recovered.
- Conditions / exceptions: IRS Table 1 (rev. May 2023) lists, for Israel: dividends paid by US corporations 25% general, 12.5% qualifying for the direct dividend rate, interest 17.5%, pensions and annuities 0%. It has no separate entry for dividends paid to an Israeli pension or provident fund, and it warns that it is not a full guide to eligibility. Whether a US or other treaty exempts or reduces withholding on dividends paid to an Israeli provident fund or IRA is open (open question 7). ### TXK-23 Child savings, provident track: taxed as a lump sum from a kupat gemel lehashkaa, up to 25% of the real gain, withheld by the managing company
- Rule: The law holds the provident-track money "בקופת גמל להשקעה" (National Insurance Law s.74ב(א)(2א); TXK-22). The Capital Market Authority's circular on the fund says it "היא קופת גמל להשקעה כהגדרתה בחוק קופות גמל, ויחולו עליה כל ההוראות החלות על קופת גמל להשקעה בהתאם להוראות הדין", unless the National Insurance Law, its regulations or the circular say otherwise, and neither the law nor the regulations sets a tax rule of their own (TXK-20). The Ministry of Finance's tender committee, in answers the Capital Market Commissioner approved, said the same before the program began. Asked about the gains after 21, a fund's managers noting that in a kupat gemel lehashkaa gains withdrawn other than as an annuity are taxed, it answered "על קופת חסכון לכל ילד חלות כל הוראות הדין לעניין קופת גמל להשקעה" (question 246). Asked what makes the fund a kupat gemel lehashkaa and whether withdrawals pay capital-gains tax of 25%, it answered "קופת חיסכון לילד הינה קופת גמל להשקעה לכל דבר ועניין וחלות עליה כל ההוראות הרגולטוריות החלות על קופת גמל להשקעה (ובנוסף להן ההוראות הייחודיות לחיסכון לכל ילד) לרבות לעניין תקרת ההפקדות השנתית" and "משיכת כספים מקופת חיסכון לכל ילד תמוסה במס רווחי הון" (question 215); the first sentence speaks of regulatory provisions, so question 246 is the stronger support for tax. So the general rule for a lump sum from a kupat gemel lehashkaa applies: the gains the child withdraws are interest income (s.3(ה4)(3); TXK-05), the linkage part is exempt and the rest is taxed at up to 25%, that is, on the real gain (TXK-07, TXK-06), and the managing company withholds 25% of the gains, principal with its linkage exempt (TXK-26).
- Conditions / exceptions: What is read and what is applied: the fund's status comes from the law, the regulator's circular and the Ministry of Finance; the tax comes from the Ordinance's rule for every kupat gemel lehashkaa; no Tax Authority text names child savings. Two premises the reading rests on: s.74ב(ו) makes the accumulated sums not income, but on its words not the gains on them (TXK-19), so the gains stay taxable; and the gains are read as the child's income, the money being the child's, though the law keeps it "בחשבון נאמנות לטובתו" (s.74ב(ב)), and whether the Ordinance's trust chapter (s.75ג) reaches that statutory trust was not studied, for this route as for the bank route (TXK-29, open question 1). Asked whether withdrawals pay "מס רווח הון של 25%", the tender committee answered that they are taxed without giving a figure; asked whether the managing company withholds from a withdrawal, it answered "כן. משיכה חייבת במס הון" (question 245). National Insurance's circular says 25% of the gains (TXK-20), the law's rate; the law's base is the real gain. ### TXK-25 Child savings: the annuity route, stated by NII and the Minister of Finance but without a clear statutory basis

### TXK-27 Lump sum from a kupat gemel lehashkaa: no National Insurance or health contributions on the gains
- Rule: The gains an individual withdraws from a kupat gemel lehashkaa are interest income to which s.125C applies (s.125ג(ה); TXK-05, TXK-07), so they are "הכנסה החייבת במס לפי הוראות סעיף 125ג(ב) או (ג) לפקודת מס הכנסה", which the National Insurance Law does not count as income for contributions (s.350(א)(6)). Health contributions are charged on the National Insurance base (National Health Insurance Law s.2, `tax-general.md` TXG-34), so they do not apply either.
- Conditions / exceptions: Where s.125C(d) moves interest to marginal rates (for example business income), the s.350(א)(6) wording does not cover it (TXG-27). This rule does not cover a recognized annuity from kupat gemel lehashkaa money (TXK-09): s.350(א)(7) takes out tax-exempt non-work income "למעט הכנסה מפנסיה מוקדמת כהגדרתה בסעיף 345ב(א)", and whether such an annuity taken before retirement age is an early pension is open (open question 9). Added 2026-10-26 (open-questions audit).
