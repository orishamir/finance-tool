## Summary

- Kupat gemel lehashkaa: no deduction or credit on deposits; gains inside the fund are not taxed; switching tracks or moving to another kupat gemel lehashkaa is not a withdrawal.
- A lump-sum withdrawal at any age is taxed: the law treats the gains withdrawn as interest income, taxed at up to 25% (s.3(ה4)(3), s.125C). The part of the gains equal to linkage differentials is exempt (s.9(13), which reaches a provident-fund account since Amendment 190 deleted the exclusion from 1.1.2012), so the tax is up to 25% of the real gain, as the 2016 bill's notes say; the manager withholds (TXK-07). The withholding regulation says 20% (TXK-26); Harel says it withholds 25%, which is not settled (TXK-28).
- What changes at 60: from early retirement age (60) the balance may be moved to a provident fund for annuity, only to receive an annuity. Because the person's own deposits count as "exempt payments", the resulting "recognized annuity" is fully exempt from 60 (s.9A(b1)). A lump sum taken straight from the kupat gemel lehashkaa after 60 is taxed the same as before 60.
- A special exemption in s.9(18ב) (5 years, age 60, no apartment purchase) is not the general age-60 rule. It belonged to the 2016 multiple-apartments chapter and its status is unresolved.
- IRA: in law an IRA is a provident fund "in personal management". No IRA-specific tax rule was found, so it follows the wrapper it sits in.
- Child savings: the deposits, the parental addition and the grants are not income. Gains are taxed on withdrawal (National Insurance says "capital gains tax"), but no official source gives the rate. The tax on the annuity route has no clear statutory basis.

## Rules

### TXK-01 No deduction or credit on deposits to a kupat gemel lehashkaa
- Rule: The pension credit (s.45A(b), 35%) and deduction (s.47(b)) apply only to sums paid "לקופת גמל לקצבה". No provision gives a credit or deduction for deposits to a kupat gemel lehashkaa. The Ordinance lists sums an individual pays for himself into a kupat gemel lehashkaa as "תשלומים פטורים" (non-benefited "exempt payments", s.9A(a) para (4)), with no condition about credits. Para (3), for annuity funds, carries that condition.
- Conditions / exceptions: Deposits are made from income already taxed. The study-fund deduction for the self-employed (s.17(5A)) is a separate wrapper (`tax-hishtalmut.md`). A Knesset private bill's explanatory notes (2023) describe the product as savings "בלא הטבות מס בהפקדה". That is not law, but it agrees.

### TXK-03 No tax on growth inside the fund
- Rule: The income of a provident fund is exempt unless it comes from a business the fund carries on, or from a company in a business the fund controls or holds a material holding in (s.9(2)(a)). "קופת גמל" here excludes an insurance fund (s.9(2)(b)). A kupat gemel lehashkaa is a provident fund (Provident Funds Law s.1, s.13(a)).
- Conditions / exceptions: Amendment 287 of the Ordinance (Economic Efficiency Law 2026, ספר החוקים 3511, 31.3.2026, p.415, s.4) replaced the definition of "החזקה מהותית" in s.9(2)(b) and widened the "שליטה" carve-out from a high-technology fund to any body of persons; the exemption itself stands. The business-income exception exists; how it would apply to a fund, or to an IRA (TXK-17), was not researched. Tax arises only when the member withdraws (TXK-05).

### TXK-04 Track switches and transfers between kupot gemel lehashkaa are not taxed
- Rule: The tax charge in s.3(ה4)(3) applies to sums an individual "משך" (withdrew) from a kupat gemel lehashkaa. Switching tracks inside the fund, or transferring the balance "מקופת גמל להשקעה לקופת גמל להשקעה אחרת", is not a withdrawal. The fund's own sales are exempt fund income (TXK-03). Whether the payment dates carry over on such a transfer is not stated: reg. 3(ב)(1) of the transfer regulations carries them over for transfers "למעט לפי - (1) פסקאות (8) עד (12) שבה", and route (11א), added in 2020 between (11) and (12), falls inside that range by its numbering (SW-39, SW open question 17; corrected 2026-10-26, scope settling).
- Conditions / exceptions: The legal text has no explicit sentence that says "no tax event" for a kupat gemel lehashkaa switch. That conclusion rests on the withdrawal wording plus the fund-level exemption, and the Ministry of Finance's team on short- and medium-term savings states it for every kupat gemel lehashkaa: "מעבר בין מסלולי השקעה שונים בתוך הקופה, וכן מעבר בין קופות גמל להשקעה אינה נחשבת 'אירוע מס' (דחיית מס)" (interim report for public comment, Feb 2025, p.14; a government report, not binding, describing the law in force). 2026-10-26 (scope settling): Harel's flyer ("ללא אירוע מס"), which only repeated this, was dropped as a source. A transfer to an annuity fund is covered in TXK-10. A transfer out to a different wrapper (for example a brokerage account) is a withdrawal.

### TXK-05 Lump-sum withdrawal: gains are treated as interest income
- Rule: "ריבית ורווחים אחרים ... שמשך יחיד מקופת גמל להשקעה, יראו אותם כהכנסה מריבית". Gains equal to the linkage differentials computed on the money are treated as income from linkage differentials. A partial withdrawal is treated as coming proportionally from principal, interest and linkage ("באופן יחסי").
- Conditions / exceptions: No age, holding-period or purpose condition appears in s.3(ה4)(3). A withdrawal at 30 and a withdrawal at 65 are classified the same way. Only the deposited principal comes out untaxed. The provision says "יחיד" (an individual) and does not distinguish who deposited. This is relevant to child savings (TXK-23).
- Figures: none.
- Source: [פקודת מס הכנסה [נוסח חדש], נוסח רשות המסים](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/legalinfo/law_pkudat_mas_hachnasa/he/LegalInformation_kesher_%D7%A4%D7%A7%D7%95%D7%93%D7%AA%20%D7%9E%D7%A1%20%D7%94%D7%9B%D7%A0%D7%A1%D7%94%20%5B%D7%A0%D7%95%D7%A1%D7%97%20%D7%97%D7%93%D7%A9%5D%20-%20%D7%9C%D7%90%20%D7%9E%D7%A8%D7%95%D7%91%D7%93.pdf), p.21, s.3(ה4)(3) "יראו אותם כהכנסה מריבית" · checked 2026-10-25 · primary.

### TXK-06 Rate on that interest: up to 25%, as the top slice of income
- Rule: s.125C applies to interest deemed under s.3(ה4)(3), even though it otherwise excludes payments from provident funds (s.125C(e)). Under s.125C(b) an individual pays "בשיעור שלא יעלה על 25%" on interest income, and that income is treated as the highest step of his taxable income. s.125C(c)(1) sets 15% where the interest is paid on an asset that is not CPI-linked, or only partly linked.
- Conditions / exceptions: s.125C(d) moves interest to marginal rates in listed cases (business income, interest expense claimed, substantial shareholder, and others). None names a kupat gemel lehashkaa. The 15% limb does not reach these gains: s.3(ה4)(3) carves the linkage on the money out as linkage income, exempt under s.9(13), and the explanatory notes to the 2016 bill say the gains "יהיו חייבים במס רווחי הון בשיעור 25% מהרווח הריאלי" (TXK-07). Corrected 2026-10-25 (tax-bases research): the record said no official source stated whether 25% on the real gain or 15% on the nominal gain applied. Surtax (s.121B) may apply on top (`tax-general.md`). A person aged 60 or older can pay less than 25%: s.125C(b) is a maximum rate, which lets lower brackets apply first (Tax Authority guide 2025, p.40), and s.121(ב)(1) gives the reduced brackets (10%, 14%, 20%, 31%) to all taxable income of an individual aged 60 or over. Because the gains are the top slice of income, this helps only where other income leaves those brackets unused (TXI-19, TXG-08). Corrected 2026-10-26 (open-questions audit, open question 12): the record said this was open.
- Figures: 25% maximum; 15% for non-linked assets. In force 2026. Fixed in law; the Minister may change the 15% by order (s.125C(c)(2)).

### TXK-07 Lump sum: the linkage part is exempt, the rest is taxed at up to 25% (the real gain); the manager withholds
- Rule: Gains an individual withdraws from a kupat gemel lehashkaa are interest income, "ואולם רווחים שקיבל כאמור בסכום הפרשי הצמדה שחושבו על הכספים כאמור יראו אותם כהכנסה מהפרשי הצמדה" (Ordinance s.3(ה4)(3); TXK-05). Linkage differentials an individual receives on an asset are exempt, "ובלבד שהתקיימו כל אלה": they are not partial linkage differentials, no interest or linkage expense was claimed for the asset, and they are not business income (s.9(13)). The sentence that kept a provident-fund account out of that exemption ("הוראות פסקה זו לא יחולו על נכס שהוא חשבון בקופת גמל", added by Amendment 147 in 2005) was deleted by Amendment 190 s.4(2), in force 1.1.2012. s.125C applies to these gains (s.125C(e)), so the rest is taxed at up to 25%, as the top slice of income (s.125C(b); TXK-06). The legislator said it in so many words: the gains "יהיו חייבים במס רווחי הון בשיעור 25% מהרווח הריאלי" (explanatory notes to the 2016 bill that added kupat gemel lehashkaa to s.3(ה4)(3)). The managing company withholds at source: the 2005 withholding regulations define "קופת גמל" as a fund (other than a study fund) whose payments s.125C governs, and their "ריבית" excludes linkage differentials that are exempt by law, so the withholding base is the real gain too.
- Conditions / exceptions: The 2005 bill's notes give the same mechanism for provident and policy gains: "מרכיב הפרשי ההצמדה ברווחים אלה זכאי לפטור ממס בידי יחיד, לפי סעיף 9(13) לפקודה, אם אינם הכנסה מעסק בידיו"; the 2012 bill's notes describe the s.3(ה4)(3) route as taxing the gains "למעט הפרשי ההצמדה". The Tax Authority's 2025 guide still prints the deleted sentence in its s.9(13) row (p.89); that line is the pre-2012 law and is not cited (the Tax Authority's own consolidated Ordinance, published 15.1.2023, and Nevo, updated 27.9.2026, no longer have it). s.3(ה4)(3) names no index; the s.1 definition of linkage differentials for an exemption covers linkage to the CPI or an exchange rate; that managers compute against the CPI is their practice. The capital-gains "inflationary amount" (s.88) is not the route, since the gains are interest income. The withholding rate: the regulation's text found says 20% (TXK-26), Harel says 25% (TXK-28, not settled); withholding is an advance, and the final tax is the rate above. Corrected 2026-10-25 (tax-bases research): the record said the base and the 25% rested on a manager's document and that the exempting provision was not found. Split on 2026-10-25: the withholding rate is TXK-26.

### TXK-11 Capitalizing a recognized annuity: 15% on the "relative profit component"

### TXK-12 The founder's age-60 example: what changes at 60 and what does not
- Rule (composite of TXK-01 to TXK-11; it adds no new law): The claim "a provident fund doesn't pay the 25% tax if you withdraw after 60" is true only for one route.
  - **Unchanged at 60:** A lump sum taken straight from the kupat gemel lehashkaa is still deemed interest and taxed at up to 25% (TXK-05, TXK-06; managers apply 25% on the real gain, TXK-07). Before and after 60, growth is untaxed and track switches and moves between kupot gemel lehashkaa are not taxed (TXK-03, TXK-04). Deposits never got a deduction (TXK-01).
  - **New at 60:** The balance may be moved to a provident fund for annuity, into an annuitants' track, for an annuity only (TXK-10). The person's own deposits are exempt payments (TXK-08), so the annuity they produce is a recognized annuity, fully exempt from 60 (TXK-09), principal and gains, with no ceiling in the statute. If such an annuity is capitalized (permission open), 15% applies to the nominal profit share (TXK-11).
  - **Not the general rule:** s.9(18ב) (five years, age 60, no apartment bought) covered only a closed 2016-2017 deposit window and its status is unresolved (TXK-15).

### TXK-21 Proposed changes (not law): the Finance Ministry team's final report, July 2026
- Rule: None of the following is law. The team on gaps between short and medium term savings products (set up by the Minister of Finance on 25.2.2024; press name "the Arbitrage Committee") published its final report on 1.7.2026. It recommends:
  - a tax-favoured "investment account" run by non-bank TASE members and small and medium banks, all banks after three years unless the minister delays it (p.5; pp.11-12, s.2.1), holding securities, mutual funds, kupot gemel lehashkaa and savings policies (p.11, s.1);
  - one cap on the tax benefit: 200,000 NIS in total per Israeli resident, inside and outside the account together, with no yearly limit, indexed to the CPI each 1 January (p.14, s.5.1 and s.5.2);
  - up to the cap: no tax event on moves between accounts, products, managers and tracks, and an exemption from capital gains tax on money drawn as an annuity after age 60 (p.10; p.13, s.4.1; p.14, s.6.1). A kupat gemel lehashkaa or savings policy bought outside the account keeps these too, but its gains cannot be offset against capital losses (p.10; p.14, s.4.5);
  - above the cap: all of today's benefits end, including deferral on moves and the age-60 annuity exemption (p.14, s.5.3); every move is a tax event (p.11; p.13, s.4.2);
  - a mutual fund held directly, outside the account: every action is a tax event even under the cap (p.13, s.4.2);
  - transition for money already held: savings up to the cap may enter the account without a tax event, at historic cost (p.16, ss.12.1-12.2). A kupat gemel lehashkaa above the cap on the start date gets no further favoured deposits; its money is split at the cap, moves are taxable only on the part above it, and from age 60 the whole of it may be drawn as an annuity free of capital gains tax (p.16, s.12.4). A holder of both a fund and a policy picks the favoured one within 12 months; the default is the fund (p.16, s.12.5). The report says these will be examined in depth at legislation (p.17);
  - a minor's investment account stays locked until 18, unless a court approves (p.17, s.13.1). Inheritance was not discussed (p.17, s.13.2);
  - the Securities Authority would run an information system of favoured deposits and set rules on account fees (p.10; p.12, s.2.1).
- Conditions / exceptions: The benefits come only after the Ordinance and the Provident Funds Law are amended and the account and information system exist (p.17, s.13.6). Study funds are not touched (p.17); deposits and study funds were left out of the team's work (p.4, note 4). Taxation stays under Part E of the Ordinance, with no change in how income is classified (p.17, s.13.4). The Capital Market Authority added a dissenting position: the plan does not level the field, because mutual funds keep loss offsets, and existing savers need transition rules that spare them (pp.21-23). What the 2026-10-22 press article predicted and the report does not say: a later age for the annuity exemption, a uniform 25% tax, a higher yearly cap. A 2023 private bill proposed an "investment account" with a capital-gains exemption on deposits up to the kupat gemel lehashkaa ceiling; its status was not checked.
- Status: no bill or draft law was found in two searches on 2026-10-31, which does not prove there is none. An article of 2.7.2026 calls these theoretical recommendations needing complex legislation, with the next arrangements law as the next chance ([Ice](https://www.ice.co.il/finance/news/article/1119346), secondary).

### TXK-22 Child savings: the provident track is a kupat gemel lehashkaa

### TXK-25 Child savings: the annuity route, stated by NII but without a clear statutory basis
- Rule: National Insurance says that if the savings stay in the kupat gemel lehashkaa until retirement, the child can take them as an annuity after retirement without capital-gains tax ("הילד יוכל לבחור לקבל את כספי החיסכון לאחר הפרישה כקצבה").

### TXK-27 Lump sum from a kupat gemel lehashkaa: no National Insurance or health contributions on the gains
- Rule: The gains an individual withdraws from a kupat gemel lehashkaa are interest income to which s.125C applies (s.125ג(ה); TXK-05, TXK-07), so they are "הכנסה החייבת במס לפי הוראות סעיף 125ג(ב) או (ג) לפקודת מס הכנסה", which the National Insurance Law does not count as income for contributions (s.350(א)(6)). Health contributions are charged on the National Insurance base (National Health Insurance Law s.2, `tax-general.md` TXG-34), so they do not apply either.
- Conditions / exceptions: Where s.125C(d) moves interest to marginal rates (for example business income), the s.350(א)(6) wording does not cover it (TXG-27). This rule does not cover a recognized annuity from kupat gemel lehashkaa money (TXK-09): s.350(א)(7) takes out tax-exempt non-work income "למעט הכנסה מפנסיה מוקדמת כהגדרתה בסעיף 345ב(א)", and whether such an annuity taken before retirement age is an early pension is open (open question 9). Added 2026-10-26 (open-questions audit).
