## Summary

- A fixed-rate bond pays fixed shekels. When market rates rise its price falls, and when they fall it rises: a buyer compares it with what new money earns (BND-01). Held to redemption it pays its face value; sold before, it fetches the market price of the day (BND-02).
- The yield to maturity turns a price into one yearly rate. For paper with no coupon (makam) it is exactly the return to redemption; for a coupon bond it assumes the coupons are reinvested at the same rate (BND-03).
- The longer a bond's duration (מח"מ), the more its price moves for the same change in rates; Israeli law defines duration and modified duration by formula (BND-04, BND-05).
- The central bank's rate moves short market rates directly; longer rates depend in part on what the market expects short rates to do (BND-06).
- The ISA names the risks apart: interest-rate, credit, market, liquidity and inflation risk (BND-07); reinvestment risk is stated only by foreign regulators (BND-08).
- Inflation erodes fixed shekels; a CPI-linked bond adjusts (BND-09). The Bank of Israel derives the inflation the market expects from the gap between unlinked and linked government-bond yields and publishes it (BND-10). Unexpected inflation moves wealth from savers to borrowers (BND-11).
- Bonds that mature at different times (a ladder) spread the risk of renewing all the money when rates are low (BND-12). A bond fund's price moves with rates by its bonds' duration, which its annual report must state (BND-13). Foreign regulators and the ISA tie the choice to how long the money can stay invested (BND-14).

## Rules

### BND-01 A bond's market price moves opposite to market interest rates
- Rule: The ISA states that a change in the rate of interest in the economy usually brings an opposite change in the price of a bond: "שינוי בשיעור הריבית במשק יגרור אחריו, בדרך כלל, שינוי הפוך במחיר האג"ח"; it names interest-rate risk "הסיכון ששווי השוק של האג"ח יירד כתוצאה מעלייה בריבית בנק ישראל". Why: an investor prices a bond by discounting its expected payments (ECB: "An investor calculates the price of a bond by discounting the expected future cash flows"), and when rates rise, bonds whose coupon is lower than what new bonds pay become less attractive (ASIC: "If interest rates rise, bonds offering lower coupon payment rates become less attractive investments"; OSC: "If the rate on your bond is higher than general interest rates, then your bond is still likely to be attractive to investors").
- Conditions / exceptions: The ISA text sits in its guide to mutual funds and speaks of the bonds in a fund (BND-13); the sentence on the inverse move is general. The comparison with new bonds is said in those words only by the foreign regulators (ASIC, OSC); no Israeli official text read says it (open question 1). The payments being fixed is the fixed-rate bond's definition: the Debt Unit's glossary defines "אג"ח בריבית קבועה" as a bond "שההחזרים בגינה (סכום הקרן ותשלומי הריבית) אינם צמודים" to an index, currency or other asset (p.41); a floating-rate or CPI-linked bond's payments change (GOV-11, GOV-14). The ISA guide is dated January 2017 and still linked by the ISA as its full guide (MF-41).

### BND-02 Held to redemption the bond pays its face value; sold before, the market price
- Rule: ASIC: "If you hold the bond until maturity, you get back the face value (or principal) of the bond. If you sell a bond before maturity, you’ll get the market value. This could be lower than the face value." OSC: "You can lose money on a bond if you sell it before the maturity date for less than you paid or if the issuer defaults on their payments."
- Conditions / exceptions: What an Israeli government bond pays at redemption is in its regulations: face value with the last coupon for a fixed-rate bond, linked to the CPI for a CPI-linked one (GOV-11, GOV-13); makam pays its face value (GOV-03). Selling before redemption is possible only for a tradable bond, on TASE or over the counter (GOV-17), at the price of the day (proposed dimension `market-price-exit`, GOV file). "If the issuer defaults" is credit risk (BND-07); the state stands behind government bonds (GOV-02). The Israeli official texts read say the price moves (BND-01) but do not put the hold-or-sell contrast in these words (open question 1).

### BND-03 Yield to maturity (תשואה לפדיון): one yearly rate from the price, and what it assumes
- Rule: ASIC: "YTM calculates the average annual return of a bond from when you buy it (at market value) until maturity. It assumes that you reinvest coupon payments in the bond at the same interest rate the bond is earning." For a bond with no coupon the price and the yield fix each other: the Bank of Israel's makam convention is I = ((100/P)^(365/N) − 1) × 100 (GOV-06), and the ECB says "The zero coupon curve represents the yield to maturity of hypothetical zero coupon bonds". So a makam bought at price P and held N days to redemption earns exactly the yield at purchase (derived from GOV-06: makam pays 100 at redemption, GOV-03); the Bank of Israel calls makam an investment "with a yield known in advance" (GOV-22).
- Conditions / exceptions: For a coupon bond, the realised return to redemption equals the yield at purchase only under the reinvestment assumption ASIC states; if the coupons are reinvested at other rates, the realised return differs (derived from ASIC's assumption; reinvestment risk, BND-08). All of this is nominal: a CPI-linked bond's yield is a real yield, the linkage on top (BND-09, BND-10). The CMA investment rules use the term in their duration formula ("Rt – שיעור התשואה לפדיון לתקופה t", BND-05) without defining it. No Israeli official definition of תשואה לפדיון for a coupon bond was read (open question 2). Whether TASE and bank screens compute secondary-market yields with GOV-06's convention is GOV open question 7.

### BND-04 The longer the duration (מח"מ), the more the price moves; a bond with no coupon moves more
- Rule: The ISA: "ככל שהמח"מ (משך חיים ממוצע) של איגרות החוב ארוך יותר, ההשפעה של שינויים בריבית על ערך האג"ח גדולה יותר"; its glossary: "ככל שמשך החיים הממוצע ארוך יותר, האיגרת תנודתית יותר ולפיכך מסוכנת יותר". The fund annual-report regulations make every bond fund print, under its bond table: "ככל שהמח"מ ארוך יותר, כך רגישות מחיר האיגרת לשינויים בשערי הריבית גבוהה יותר ולפיכך גם הסיכון הכרוך בהשקעה בה עולה" (reg. 17(ג)(5)(ד), BND-13). The rule of thumb, from the OSC: "a bond with a five-year duration will decrease 5% in price with each 1% increase in interest rates. The same bond will increase 5% in price with each 1% decrease in interest rates." On the coupon, the OSC says of strip bonds, which pay no interest along the way: "For this reason, strip bonds also tend to be affected more by changes in interest rates than regular bonds."
- Conditions / exceptions: The general statement that a smaller (not zero) coupon raises sensitivity was not found in any official text read (open question 3); it follows from the duration formula in BND-05, where coupons paid earlier pull the weighted time down (derived). The OSC's 5%-for-1% is a rule of thumb, not a formula; the approximation "price change ≈ − modified duration × change in yield" was not found written as a formula in an official text (open question 4), though the Debt Unit describes modified duration as the measure of how the interest rate affects market value (BND-05). The ISA guide's glossary defines מח"מ as the bond's average life "הנמדד בשנים ומשקלל את תשלומי הריבית התקופתיים של האיגרת לאורך חייה עד הפדיון הסופי" (p.30), which is what the formula in BND-05 computes.

### BND-05 Duration and modified duration: the official definition and formula
- Rule: The CMA's investment rules for institutional bodies define "מחמ"מ" (משך חיים ממוצע מתוקנן) as "תקופה, בשנים, ממועד החישוב ועד לפירעון הנכסים, לפי משך חיים ממוצע (מח"מ) בשנים לפירעון הנכסים", computed by the formulas in their schedule ("חישוב מח"מ, ומח"מ מתוקנן"). There, with C_t the cash flow at time t (in years), R_t the yield to maturity for period t and N the years left to redemption: duration D = Σ[C_t·t/(1+R_t)^t] / Σ[C_t/(1+R_t)^t], summed over t = 1 to N; modified duration MD = D/(1+R). The Debt Unit's glossary: "המח"מ (משך חיים ממוצע) המתוקנן (Modified Duration) - מדד המסייע בבחינת ההשפעה של שיעור הריבית על שווי השוק של החוב".
- Conditions / exceptions: The definition serves the CMA's investment limits for provident funds and insurers, not a saver's statement; it is cited here as the official text of the measure. The regulations were read as published in 2012; whether later amendments changed the definition or the schedule was not checked (open question 5). The schedule writes R without an index in MD; which yield it means is not said. For the makam, which has a single payment, D equals the time to redemption (derived from the formula); its price-yield formula is GOV-06. The Debt Unit reports the government debt's modified duration each year (about 6.5 in 2024, 6.2 in 2025); that is a figure about the state's debt, not a rule (lead for `data/` below).

### BND-06 How the policy rate reaches market yields, and the yield curve (עקום התשואות)
- Rule: The ECB: "The change in the official interest rates affects directly money-market interest rates and, indirectly, lending and deposit rates"; "Expectations of future official interest-rate changes affect medium and long-term interest rates. In particular, longer-term interest rates depend in part on market expectations about the future course of short-term rates." The yield curve is "the relationship between market remuneration (interest) rates and the remaining time to maturity of debt securities", and "When buying and selling bonds, investors include their expectations of future inflation, real interest rates and their assessment of risks." The Bank of Israel: "עקום התשואות הנגזר מאיגרות חוב ממשלתיות הנסחרות בשוק ההון הוא כלי מרכזי לתמחור נכסים ולניתוחים כלכליים, ובכלל זה לגזירה של ציפיות המשקיעים לגבי התפתחות הריבית והאינפלציה". The OSC: "Under normal conditions, the yield curve slopes upward to the right. This is because long-term bonds pay higher yields than short-term bonds."
- Conditions / exceptions: In Israel, the ISA ties bond prices to the Bank of Israel's rate (BND-01), and a daily money-market fund's yield follows the Bank of Israel's rate less costs (MF-41). No Bank of Israel explanatory text on how its rate passes to market yields was read: its HTML pages answer scripts with a bot check, and its discussion papers state their authors' views (open question 6). Extra pay for lending longer (a term premium) is not named in the texts read: the ECB says long rates depend "in part" on expected short rates and that investors include "their assessment of risks" (open question 6). The OSC also describes flat and inverted curves and warns "there's no guarantee that a yield curve — and therefore bond prices — will move the way you expect." The Bank of Israel publishes nominal and real zero-coupon curves by term (dataflow ZCM, BND-10) and yields to maturity (GOV-19).

### BND-07 The kinds of risk, as the ISA names them
- Rule: The ISA lists, apart: credit risk, "בעיקר בקרנות המשקיעות באג"חים שהונפקו על ידי חברות", the risk that the issuing company will not repay ("חדלות פירעון"), more real for bonds below investment grade; interest-rate risk (BND-01, BND-04); market risk, losses from market swings, among whose causes are "שינויים בשערי הריבית, בשערי החליפין ובשיעור האינפלציה"; liquidity risk, "האפשרות שמנהל הקרן לא יוכל למכור את הנכסים שהקרן מחזיקה עקב התפתחויות לא צפויות, או ייאלץ לבצע פעולות מהירות למימוש נכסים, עד כדי מחירי הפסד"; and inflation risk (BND-09). The OSC adds that a rating change moves the price: "If an issuer’s rating goes up, the price of its bonds will rise. If the rating goes down, the price will drop." ASIC lists what moves the market value: "interest rate movements", "credit risk of the issuer", "level of liquidity" and "when the bond is due to be paid back".
- Conditions / exceptions: The ISA writes these as risks of a mutual fund's holdings; MF-41 already summarises the ISA's general fund risks and is not repeated. What is specific to a bond held directly: credit risk depends on the issuer, and the state stands behind government bonds (GOV-02); ratings, the trustee and default are `corporate-bonds.md` (CORP-07, CORP-08, CORP-13 to CORP-15); liquidity on the exchange is CORP-19 and GOV-17; currency risk applies only to foreign-currency bonds (left out). Reinvestment risk is not in the ISA list (BND-08).

### BND-08 Reinvestment risk: money coming back is reinvested at the rates of its day
- Rule: The OSC, after an example where rates fall: "If you don’t sell, you’ll keep getting interest payments. However, if you reinvest that money, you’ll make less interest on it."; after rates rise: "If you reinvest that money, you’ll make more interest on it." ASIC's yield to maturity assumes coupons reinvested "at the same interest rate the bond is earning" (BND-03).
- Conditions / exceptions: No Israeli official text read names reinvestment risk (open question 7). ### BND-09 Nominal against real: inflation erodes fixed shekels; a CPI-linked bond adjusts
- Rule: The ISA: "קיים סיכון שהערך הריאלי של ההשקעה יישחק ויהיה קטן יותר מערכה בשעה שנעשתה. התופעה מהווה סיכון מהותי שיש לקחת בחשבון בעת השקעה במכשירים שאינם צמודים (ואינם שומרים על ערך הכסף), ובעיקר בחיסכון לטווח ארוך." The OSC's example: "if you hold a bond paying 3% interest and inflation reaches 5%, your return is actually negative (-2%), when adjusted for inflation"; and "Inflation risk increases the longer you hold a bond." The Debt Unit defines a CPI-linked bond as one whose repayments "צמודים לאורך חיי האג"ח, לשיעור השינוי במדד המחירים לצרכן". The Bank of Israel relates the two yields: 1 + π = (1 + i)/(1 + r), with i the nominal yield on unlinked bonds, r the real yield on linked bonds and π the expected inflation (BND-10).
- Conditions / exceptions: The OSC's −2% is its own simple subtraction; the exact real return is (1 + nominal)/(1 + inflation) − 1, by the same relation the Bank of Israel uses (derived). What a CPI-linked government bond pays, and that its linkage works both ways with no floor in the regulation text, is GOV-11 and GOV-12; the fixed-rate bond is GOV-13. Tax on the linkage and on the inflationary amount is the tax files' (TXI, TXS).

### BND-10 The inflation the bond market expects: what the Bank of Israel derives and where it publishes it
- Rule: The Bank of Israel derives inflation expectations from the capital market by the yields of tradable fixed-rate government bonds: 1 + π = (1 + i)/(1 + r), where i is "שער ריבית נומינלי" (nominal yield, unlinked bonds), r is "שער ריבית ריאלי" (real yield, CPI-linked bonds) and π is "הציפיות לאינפלציה". Its curve note says it uses "ציפיות השוק לגבי משתנים אלה, כפי שמגולמות במחירי אגרות החוב הממשלתיות הנומינליות ואגרות החוב הממשלתיות הצמודות למדד המחירים לצרכן". It publishes the results in its statistics service EDGE, dataflow ZCM ("ציפיות לאינפלציה ועקום 0", "Inflation expectations and zero yield curve"): monthly averages of 5-year and 10-year "inflation expectations derived from the capital market", forward expectations for the 2nd year, the 3rd year, 3–5 and 5–10 years, expectations from forward contracts, from banks' internal rates and from forecasters, and the nominal and real zero-coupon yields from 1 to 20 years; each series "Free (free for publication)".
- Conditions / exceptions: The Bank lists the measure's weaknesses: "הבדלים בסחירות", "מיעוט איגרות חוב הצמודות", "הטווח לפדיון של איגרות החוב", "שיטת ההצמדה של אגח צמודות" and "פרמיית סיכון אינפלציונית" (deck p.7), so the gap is not a pure forecast. The source is a slide deck of the Bank's Information and Statistics Department (March 2023). Read with BND-09: if inflation over the term turns out above π, the CPI-linked bond of the same term earns more than the unlinked one, and below π less (derived from the relation; no official text read says it, open question 8). The Bank's monthly release "הציפיות לאינפלציה מהמקורות השונים" was not read: the pages answer scripts with a bot check.
- Figures: none here; the series are data (lead for `data/` below).

### BND-11 Unexpected inflation moves wealth from savers to borrowers
- Rule: The ECB: "if there is an unanticipated increase in inflation, the value of savings goes down and the value of debt goes down, which transfers wealth from savers to borrowers."

### BND-12 Renewing short paper against locking a long bond; a ladder
- Rule: The OSC: "One way of reducing interest rate risk is to buy bonds that mature at different times. This is known as creating a ladder or laddering." "Laddering can help reduce the risk that all your bonds will mature at a time when interest rates are low. It also frees up cash at different times, which you can choose to reinvest or use as income." And: "All other things being equal, longer-term bonds tend to have higher returns and higher risk than shorter-term bonds. That’s because the longer you hold a bond, the more it could be affected by changes in interest rates, inflation and market declines."
- Conditions / exceptions: The trade-off as the texts give it: short paper renewed often carries little price risk (BND-04) and full reinvestment risk (BND-08); a long bond fixes its yield to redemption (BND-03) and carries price risk if sold early (BND-02). No Israeli official text read describes a saver's ladder (open question 7). The state runs one for its own debt: the Debt Unit plans its issues by a "מודל המדרגות" with "עקום פירעונות מדורג - הימצאות במבנה פירעונות רב שנתי מדורג", to reduce its own refinancing risk; that is the borrower's side, not advice to a saver.

### BND-13 A bond fund: its price moves with rates by its bonds' duration, which its annual report must state

## Open questions

1. **An Israeli official sentence that a bond's price falls because new bonds pay more, and that holding to redemption avoids the price move.** Tried: the ISA mutual-fund guide (2017), the Debt Unit's 2024 and 2025 glossaries, two web searches for an ISA or TASE bond guide (only brokers and the press came back), and the Bank of Israel's pages (bot check). Would settle it: a Bank of Israel consumer page on government bonds ("אג"ח ממשלתיות"; GOV open question 9) read in a browser, or an ISA investor page on bonds.
2. **An Israeli official definition of תשואה לפדיון for a coupon bond.** The CMA rules use the term undefined (BND-05); the BoI statistics page "אג"ח ומק"מ - תשואות לפדיון" answers scripts with a bot check. Would settle it: that BoI page or a TASE methodology document.
3. **That a smaller coupon makes the price move more.** Found only for no-coupon strip bonds (OSC, BND-04); otherwise derived from the duration formula. Would settle it: an official investor text that says it.
4. **The approximation "price change ≈ − modified duration × change in yield" as a written formula.** The CMA rules give D and MD (BND-05), the Debt Unit says MD measures the effect of the rate on value, the OSC gives 5%-per-1% for a five-year duration; none writes the formula. The SEC's bulletin on interest-rate risk on investor.gov, a likely place, answered 403 to automated reading. Would settle it: that bulletin read in a browser, or a BoI or ISA text.
5. **Whether the CMA investment rules' definition of מחמ"מ and its schedule (ק"ת 7125, 2012) still stand as published**, and whether the annual-report regulations' reg. 17(ג) (ק"ת 7646, 2016) was amended. Not checked against later booklets. Would settle it: the Knesset's secondary-legislation record of amendments to each, read for those clauses.
6. **A Bank of Israel explanatory text on how its rate reaches market yields, and a named term premium.** The ECB's pages state the mechanism (BND-06); the BoI's page "פעילות בשוק אגרות החוב" and its monetary-policy explanations answer with a bot check; its discussion papers are research. Would settle it: those BoI pages read in a browser.
7. **Reinvestment risk and a saver's ladder in an Israeli official text.** Only the OSC and ASIC say them (BND-08, BND-12). Would settle it: an ISA or BoI investor page.
8. **An official statement that the CPI-linked bond does better when inflation exceeds the break-even, and worse below.** Derived in BND-10 from the BoI's relation; not stated. Would settle it: a BoI explanatory text on break-even inflation.
9. **Unexpected deflation or lower inflation moving wealth from borrowers to savers.** The ECB states only the inflation side (BND-11). Would settle it: an ECB, BoI or other central-bank text stating the reverse.
10. **A bond fund's lack of a redemption date and constant renewal of holdings; whether duration must be published more often than yearly.** Not found in an official text; the SEC bulletin that likely says it was unreachable (403). Would settle it: an ISA directive on fund reports or fund pages, or an official investor text on bond funds.
11. **An Israeli official text that a saver should match the term of a bond to when the money is needed.** The ISA names the horizon as a factor only (BND-14). Would settle it: a BoI, ISA or CMA investor page.

## Left out and why

- **Tax** on coupons, discount, linkage and gains: the tax files (TXI, TXS). Nothing tax was found here that changes them.
- **Product terms** of government bonds, makam, money-market and fixed-date funds, ETFs and corporate bonds: their files, pointed to by id.
- **Historical figures** (rate paths, how far bonds fell in a year, the government debt's duration by year): not rules; leads for `data/` are listed in the report of 2026-11-15.
- **Foreign bonds and foreign products**: out of the brief. Currency risk is named in BND-07 only to say it applies to foreign-currency bonds.
- **Convexity, spreads, call risk, credit-spread duration**: not asked; no official Israeli text was looked for.
- **FINRA's investor pages** (seen in searches on duration): FINRA is a self-regulatory body, not a regulator or central bank, so it was not used; the OSC and ASIC, both statutory regulators, were.
- **Bank of Israel discussion papers** on what moves long yields: research stating its authors' views, not the Bank's explanation; not cited.

## Proposed dimensions

- `rate-sensitivity`: how far a holding's market value moves when market interest rates move (duration). Applies to every tradable fixed-rate bond, bond funds and ETFs, and much less to makam and money-market funds (BND-04, BND-13, MF-30).
- `market-price-exit` is proposed in `government-bonds-and-makam.md` and used here as there.
