## Summary

A study fund is taxed at three points. **Deposit:** for an employee, the employer's deposit up to 7.5% of a "determining salary" capped at 15,712 NIS a month (188,544 a year in 2026) is not taxed when deposited. Anything the employer pays above that is taxed as salary in the month it is paid. The employee's own deposit comes from net salary and gets no deduction. A self-employed person deducts up to 4.5% of income, with income capped at 293,397 NIS (2024 to 2027). **During:** gains are not taxed while they stay in the fund, and changing the asset mix is not a tax event. **Exit:** after 6 years from the first payment (3 years at retirement age or for study), everything withdrawn is exempt, except gains on deposits above the ceilings. Those gains are "interest income" taxed at up to 25%, and the fund withholds 25%. An earlier withdrawal is taxed at marginal rates on the employer's benefited deposits and on the gains, and the fund withholds 47% (2026). Heirs withdraw tax-free. As of 2026-10-22 the law is unchanged. The Finance Ministry's chief economist recommended narrowing the benefit (October 2025), and the press reports it is being considered again for 2027. No bill was found, so Q18 stays open.

## Rules

### TXH-01 Employer deposit within the limits is not taxed at deposit (employee)
- Rule: Amounts an employer pays to a study fund for an employee within the limits are treated as the employee's work income only "when he received them" (`בעת שקיבל אותם`), not when paid to the fund. The limit is the collective-agreement rate, capped at 7.5% of the determining salary (8.4% for a teacher, `עובד הוראה`).
- Conditions / exceptions: The limits are those set in a collective agreement, or in an agreement for a similar employee where none applies. The 8.4% applies only to members of three named teachers' funds. The 2025 Chief Economist paper adds that employer deposits within the ceiling are also exempt from National Insurance and health tax (`פטור ממס הכנסה, הפרשות לביטוח לאומי ומס בריאות על הפקדות המעסיק`). The NII statute itself was not read; `tax-general.md` should confirm it.
- Figures: 7.5% (8.4% teachers) of determining salary up to 15,712 NIS/month, which is 188,544 NIS/year (2026). The Chief Economist paper gives the resulting exempt employer deposit as 14,141 NIS/year "as of 2025". For 2026, 7.5% × 188,544 = 14,140.80 NIS/year. That is computed here, not a published 2026 figure. See TXH-02 for how the salary cap is updated.

### TXH-02 Determining salary (`משכורת קובעת`): definition and update
- Rule: The determining salary is work income excluding expense reimbursements, overtime and similar special payments, "but not more than twice the amount that is the ceiling for paying the cost-of-living allowance, as set from time to time in the agreement between the coordination bureau of the economic organisations and the Histadrut" (`אך לא יותר מכפל הסכום המהווה תקרה לענין תשלום תוספת היוקר כפי שהוא נקבע מעת לעת בהסכם בין לשכת התיאום של הארגונים הכלכליים לבין ההסתדרות הכללית של העובדים בארץ ישראל`). The Ordinance names no amount. The amount in force is the Tax Authority's: its 2026 deductions booklet gives 188,544 NIS a year (15,712 × 12).
- Conditions / exceptions: The cap moves only with the cost-of-living-allowance ceiling set in that agreement. It is not CPI-linked: s.3(ה) is not among the amounts that s.120א lists for yearly indexation under s.120ב. Since when the current amount has stood is not stated in the official texts read. *2026-10-25 rule-text pass:* removed "the law text lists 15,400 until 2004; from March 2004, 15,712" (a Wikisource editor note; s.3(ה) in the consolidated Ordinance has no amount). The amount now rests on the 2026 booklet, and the March 2004 start date was dropped for lack of an official source.
- Figures: 15,712 NIS/month, 188,544 NIS/year (deductions booklet 2026, tax year 2026). Updated only if the cost-of-living ceiling in the agreement changes; start date of the current amount not sourced.

### TXH-03 Employer deposit above the limits is taxed as salary when paid
- Rule: Amounts an employer pays above the limits are the employee's work income "when they were paid to the fund" (`בעת ששולמו לקרן`). They are taxed with the month's salary, not at withdrawal.
- Conditions / exceptions: The deposit itself is not a "benefited deposit" (`הפקדה מוטבת`, TXH-08), so gains on it are taxable at exit (TXH-11). The Chief Economist paper says the same: deposits above the ceiling are allowed, but they are taxed.
- Figures: none of its own; the limit is TXH-01.
- Source: [פקודת מס הכנסה [נוסח חדש], נוסח רשות המסים](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/legalinfo/law_pkudat_mas_hachnasa/he/LegalInformation_kesher_%D7%A4%D7%A7%D7%95%D7%93%D7%AA%20%D7%9E%D7%A1%20%D7%94%D7%9B%D7%A0%D7%A1%D7%94%20%5B%D7%A0%D7%95%D7%A1%D7%97%20%D7%97%D7%93%D7%A9%5D%20-%20%D7%9C%D7%90%20%D7%9E%D7%A8%D7%95%D7%91%D7%93.pdf), p.19, s.3(ה) "בעת ששולמו לקרן" · checked 2026-10-25 · primary. ### TXH-04 Employee's own deposit: no deduction or credit
- Rule: The employee's deposit is computed on gross salary but paid from net salary, after income tax, National Insurance and health tax. It gets no deduction or credit at deposit. It counts as a benefited deposit, so gains on it are exempt at exit, up to TXH-08's limit.
- Conditions / exceptions: s.17(5A) (deduction) covers only the self-employed fund. No employee study-fund deduction or credit appears in the Tax Authority's 2025 table of exemptions, deductions and credits (pp.86-95), which supports the reading.
- Figures: The benefited employee deposit is 2.5% of determining salary (TXH-08). The Chief Economist paper gives about 4,713 NIS/year for 2025. For 2026, 2.5% × 188,544 = 4,713.60, computed here.
- Source: [Chief Economist paper Oct 2025](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/dynamiccollectorresultitem/review-15102025/he/reviews-and-publishes_review-15102025.pdf), p.5, note 9 "ההפקדה בפועל נעשית מתוך שכרו נטו, קרי לאחר ששילם מיסי הכנסה, בריאות ודמי ביטוח לאומי" · checked 2026-10-25 · primary. [Tax Authority guide 2025](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/generalpage/income-tax-guide-knowyourright/he/Guides_IncomeTax_da-2025.pdf), pp.88-97 (table of exemptions, deductions and credits) · checked 2026-10-25 · primary.
- Dimensions: `tax-at-deposit`
- Likely to change: no

### TXH-05 Self-employed deduction (s.17(5A))
- Rule: An individual with business or professional income may deduct amounts paid to a self-employed study fund (`קרן השתלמות לעצמאים`), up to 4.5% of "determining income" (`הכנסה קובעת`). Determining income is taxable business or professional income before this deduction, capped at a ceiling.
- Conditions / exceptions: The Tax Authority guide describes the deduction for 2025 as 4.5% of income, up to a ceiling of 13,203 NIS (4.5% × 293,397). Someone who is both self-employed and an employee has a reduced ceiling (TXH-06). *2026-10-25 rule-text pass:* the yearly ceilings are no longer attributed to "the law" (283,905 and 293,397 were Wikisource editor notes). The Ordinance holds the base amount and its indexation; the 2025 and 2026 amounts rest on the Tax Authority guide and booklet.
- Figures: The Ordinance's text caps determining income at 156,000 NIS a year (s.17(5א)). That amount is one of the "social reliefs" of s.120א, updated each 1 January by the rise in the CPI (s.120ב(א)), with no update on 1 January of 2025 to 2027 (s.120ב(ה), added in 2024). The Tax Authority gives 293,397 for 2026 (deductions booklet 2026, `תקרת הכנסה קובעת בקרן השתלמות לעצמאיים 17(5א)`) and for 2025 (guide), so the maximum deduction is 13,203 NIS in both years (stated by the guide for 2025; computed here for 2026). The amount for 2028 is not known.

### TXH-06 Self-employed who is also an employee: reduced deduction ceiling
- Rule: For someone who is both self-employed and an employee, the deduction applies only to the part of business income within the "ceiling amount" (`סכום התקרה`). That is the lower of (1) business or professional income and (2) 293,397 minus the determining salary on which the employer paid to a study fund.
- Conditions / exceptions: In the guide's worked example, the employer-funded determining salary is capped at 188,544 (`משכורת קובעת מירבית 188,544`).
- Figures: 293,397 and 188,544 (2025 guide; both unchanged in the 2026 booklet).
- Source: [Tax Authority guide 2025](<https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/generalpage/income-tax-guide-knowyourright/he/Guides_IncomeTax_da-2025.pdf>), p.61 (sections 51-52, worked example) and p.92 · checked 2026-10-22 · primary. The [Ordinance](<https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/פקודת_מס_הכנסה>) text of s.17(5A), as returned by the fetch tool, did not show this reduction. Its statutory basis was not located (open question 7).
- Dimensions: `tax-at-deposit`, `deposit-ceiling`, `eligibility`
- Likely to change: with TXH-05

### TXH-07 Self-employed early withdrawal is business income; deposits not deducted are excluded
- Rule: Amounts an individual receives from a self-employed study fund, including interest, linkage differences and other gains, are business or professional income when received. Deposits that were not allowed as a deduction under s.17(5A) are excluded. Interest, linkage and other gains received on the dates in s.9(16B), that is, after the holding period, are interest income under s.2(4).
- Conditions / exceptions: Exempt withdrawals under TXH-09 to TXH-11 take precedence. This rule decides what is taxed when the exemption does not apply.
- Figures: none
- Source: [Ordinance](<https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/פקודת_מס_הכנסה>) s.3(e2) (`סעיף 3(ה2)`), Wikisource · checked 2026-10-22 · primary (mirror).
- Dimensions: `tax-at-exit`, `liquidity-lockup`
- Likely to change: no

### TXH-08 What counts as a "benefited deposit" (`הפקדה מוטבת`)
- Rule: Only gains that come from a benefited deposit are exempt at exit. For an employee (s.9(16A)(c)), a benefited deposit is (1) an employer's payment up to the amount or rate that s.3(e) does not treat as income when paid, and (2) the employee's payment up to one of: one third of the employer's payment at the s.3(e) rate; 2.5% of the determining salary; or another rate set for employee payments in a collective agreement approved before 12 June 2002. For the self-employed (s.9(16B)), it is a fixed annual amount.
- Conditions / exceptions: A kibbutz members' fund (s.58A) has its own amount, equal to the self-employed figure. Deposits above these amounts are allowed but not benefited (TXH-03, TXH-11). *2026-10-25 rule-text pass:* removed "the law lists 19,920 (2023), 20,520 (2024) and 20,566" (Wikisource editor notes; s.9(16ב) holds 14,140). The yearly amount now rests on the Tax Authority booklets; the 2024 and 2025 booklets carry a correction dated 30.4.2025 that sets the s.9(16א)-(16ב) amount at 20,566 a year, which closes open question 3.
- Figures: The Ordinance's text names 14,140 NIS a year for the self-employed benefited deposit (s.9(16ב)). That amount is one of the "social reliefs" of s.120א, updated each 1 January by the CPI (s.120ב(א)), with no update on 1 January of 2025 to 2027 (s.120ב(ה)). The Tax Authority's booklets give 20,566 a year for 2026 (`הפקדה מוטבת 9(16א) ו-(16ב)`), for 2025 and for 2024; the 2024 and 2025 booklets say the amount was updated to 20,566 on 30.4.2025. Under the freeze the 2027 amount is the same (computed from s.120ב(ה)). The Chief Economist paper gives "about 20,538" for 2025, which disagrees with the booklets; the booklets govern. For the self-employed, deposits between the deduction ceiling (13,203) and 20,566 are not deductible, but their gains are exempt. The Chief Economist paper (note 11) says the same.

### TXH-09 Exempt withdrawal after 6 years
- Rule: Amounts withdrawn from the account are exempt if 6 years have passed since the first payment date of that account. This includes deposits, linkage differences, and interest and other gains that come from the benefited deposit. The same applies to employee funds (s.9(16A)) and self-employed funds (s.9(16B)).
- Conditions / exceptions: The first payment date (`מועד התשלום הראשון`) is the earlier of (1) the end of the month in which the first payment was paid and (2) the end of the month for which it was paid, but not before the start of the tax year in which it was paid. The exemption covers all principal, but gains only on benefited deposits; gains on other deposits fall under TXH-11. The exemption is conditional: "if the employee withdrew any amount from his account, the account will be closed to further payments" (`ייסגר החשבון לתשלומים נוספים`), with the exception in TXH-10. The Minister may set rules on account closure and on when several accounts count as one (TXH-19).
- Figures: 6 years, fixed in law.

### TXH-10 Exempt withdrawal after 3 years: retirement age, or used for study
- Rule: The period is 3 years from the first payment date for (a) an employee or self-employed individual who has reached retirement age (`שהגיע לגיל הפרישה`), and (b) amounts that were used for the member's study (`ששימשו ... לצורך השתלמותו`). For the self-employed, the study must be "intended to maintain his professional level in his field of business or profession". The Tax Authority Director may set rules on entitlement to withdraw for study.
- Conditions / exceptions: A withdrawal for study in Israel of no more than one third of the balance at the time does not close the account, provided there was no other such withdrawal in the preceding 12 months (s.9(16A)(b)). "Retirement age" (`גיל הפרישה`) is defined in s.1 of the Ordinance as the retirement age under the Retirement Age Law 2004. That law gives the name "גיל הפרישה" only to its s.3 age (67 for a man, 65 for a woman, with a schedule for women born up to 1969); the early age (60) is "גיל הפרישה המוקדמת" (s.5) and the mandatory age "גיל פרישת חובה" (s.4), so the 3-year route opens at the s.3 age (KH-12; open question 5, closed 2026-10-26). The evidence a fund requires for study use was not sourced (open question 6).
- Figures: 3 years; one third of the balance; 12 months. All fixed in law.

### TXH-11 Gains on non-benefited deposits: interest income, up to 25% of the real gain
- Rule: Interest and other gains accrued from 1.1.2003 on deposits above the ceiling, withdrawn after the holding period, are taxed at up to 25%. Gains from the benefited deposit, up to the ceiling, are exempt on withdrawal. In law, interest and other gains received from a study fund on the s.9(16A)/(16B) dates are "interest income". The part of the gains equal to linkage differences on the payments is "linkage income" (`הכנסה מהפרשי הצמדה`). The base is the real gain: the Ministry of Finance Chief Economist paper describes the study-fund exemption as an exemption from capital gains tax "בגובה 25% מהרווחים הריאליים", real gains being nominal gains less the cumulative rise in the CPI (its note 7), and its simulation of a lower ceiling would tax at 25% "על כל רווח ריאלי שייווצר על ההפקדות מעל התקרה החדשה" (p.20).
- Conditions / exceptions: The 25% cap is the individual interest rate of s.125C(b), "as the top slice of income". The fund withholds 25% on "taxable interest amounts" (`סכומי ריבית חייבת`, TXH-15). The guide says to report the income in form 1301 section 15, field 157/257. The Tax Authority guide states no base. The real base follows from the law: s.3(ה4)(1) makes the part equal to linkage on the payments "linkage income", and s.9(13) exempts it; the sentence that kept a provident-fund account out of s.9(13) was deleted by Amendment 190 s.4(2) from 1.1.2012 (tax-bases research 2026-10-25; TXK-07). The 2025 guide's s.9(13) row (p.89) still prints the deleted sentence (`הוראות אלה לא יחולו על חשבון בקופת גמל`) and is no longer cited. The 15% of s.125C(c) (unlinked assets) does not reach these gains: the linkage part is carved out as linkage income, and the Chief Economist paper puts the tax at 25% of the real gain (open question 1, closed 2026-10-26; the same reading as TXK-06 for a kupat gemel lehashkaa). *2026-10-25 scope pass:* the figure `real_base` now cites the Chief Economist paper (primary); the two managers' pages (Mor, Clal) that said the same were dropped as sources.
- Figures: up to 25% (s.125C(b), in force 2026); withholding 25%; the base is the real gain (Chief Economist paper, October 2025).

### TXH-13 Early withdrawal by an employee: what is taxed and at what rate
- Rule: Before the period in TXH-09/TXH-10 has passed, the employer's benefited deposits are taxed as work income when received (s.3(e), TXH-01). Interest and gains are taxed at the ordinary marginal rates (s.121), because the reduced interest rate does not apply to "interest paid from a study fund before the said periods have passed" (s.125C(d)(5)).
- Conditions / exceptions: The base subject to withholding at the maximum rate (TXH-15) excludes amounts the employee deposited, amounts already taxed at deposit under s.3(e), and self-employed deposits not allowed as a deduction (1997 Order, s.1). So the employee's own deposits and the above-ceiling employer deposits are not taxed again. The order says this for withholding. The final-tax conclusion is inferred from s.3(e) and the withholding order, and the Ministry of Finance's 2024 memorandum describes it in the same terms: "an early withdrawal ... requires paying tax at the marginal rate on the money withdrawn". No reduced early-withdrawal rate is in force (TXH-23). **[2026-10-22 browser pass 3: the gains are interest income (TXH-11) and not personal-effort income, so the s.121 schedule for them starts at 31% for someone under 60 (TXG-03).]**

### TXH-15 Withholding by the fund
- Rule: A study fund paying a member "amounts from a study fund" (`סכומים מקרן השתלמות`, the non-exempt early amounts defined in the 1997 Order) withholds tax at the maximum rate, the highest rate in s.121. A fund paying "taxable interest amounts" (`סכומי ריבית חייבת`: interest and gains paid after the periods, excluding those from a benefited deposit) withholds 25%.
- Conditions / exceptions: Withholding is not the final tax. Someone whose marginal rate is lower than the maximum would recover the difference through a return (filing rules in `tax-general.md`). The 25% on taxable interest comes from a 2012 amendment that replaced "20%" with "25%" in reg. 2(ב) from 1.1.2012 and applies it to taxable interest arising from payments made to the fund from that date (`והן יחולו על סכומי ריבית חייבת שהופקו או שנצמחו בשל תשלומים ששולמו לקרן השתלמות ביום התחילה ואילך`). *2026-10-25 rule-text pass:* added the 2012 application rule, read in the amending regulations (official gazette).

### TXH-16 The fund keeps benefited and taxable money apart
- Rule: A provident fund (including a study fund) keeps separate records per member: employer and employee components, and within them, payments up to the cut-off date (for a self-employed study fund, 30 November 2002) and later payments split into benefited payments (`תשלומי ההפקדה המוטבת`) and taxable payments (`התשלומים החייבים`). Profits and expenses are allocated to the components at least monthly. On a transfer to another fund, all annual records pass to the receiving fund.
- Conditions / exceptions: The text read is a Wikisource copy whose latest visible amendment is from 2013-2014. Current status was not confirmed (open question 12). During the 2020 temporary order the Tax Authority set benefited money as the default source of a withdrawal, unless the member asked otherwise (TXH-23). Whether a member can choose the order in an ordinary withdrawal was not sourced (open question 10).

### TXH-19 Transfer to another study fund keeps the account's history
- Rule: Money may be moved from one study fund to another (transfer regulations, reg. 3(a)(4)). Where an employee's account at one fund is closed to further payments and payments continue at a second fund, the two accounts are treated as one if both funds were notified and no tax-exempt withdrawal was made from the first account. The first fund must give the second the first payment date of the closed account. When rights are transferred, all component records pass to the receiving fund (TXH-16).
- Conditions / exceptions: The transfer regulations bar a partial transfer from a study fund (reg. 9(c), as replaced in 2014). That is a product rule for `keren-hishtalmut.md` and `switching-and-transfers.md`. A transfer from a study fund to a provident fund for annuity is allowed only if the member was entitled to withdraw the money tax-free (reg. 2(a)(4)). Tax: reg. 3(ב)(1) of the transfer regulations says that a transfer between study funds (reg. 3(א)(4)) is made to an account in the member's name at the receiving fund, "ויראו לעניין דיני המס, את כל מועדי התשלום לקופה המעבירה כמועדי התשלום לקופה המקבלת". The regulation does not use the words "withdrawal" or "tax event"; it keeps every payment date for tax law. The Chief Economist paper (note 6) is consistent: savings policies are exempt "only on a switch between tracks, not between managing companies". *2026-10-25 rule-text pass:* the tax side now rests on reg. 3(ב)(1) (primary) instead of the Chief Economist footnote; open question 11 is closed for payment dates.

### TXH-22 Proposals to narrow the benefit (not law), including the 2027 budget (Q18)
- Rule: No bill or memorandum to narrow the study-fund benefit for 2027 was found. Status on 2026-10-22: proposals under discussion inside the Ministry of Finance.
- Conditions / exceptions:
  - **Ministry of Finance, Chief Economist, October 2025 (official policy paper):** recommends "significantly narrowing" the benefit, preferably by lowering the salary ceiling (`מומלץ לצמצם באופן משמעותי את הטבת המס בקרנות ההשתלמות, עם עדיפות להפחתת תקרת ההכנסה המזכה בהפקדה`). It simulates three options: (1) lower the monthly salary ceiling by 25%, from 15,712 to 11,784 NIS; (2) abolish the capital-gains exemption, keeping the deposit benefit; (3) limit the capital-gains exemption to 6 years, so that real gains accrued more than 6 years after the first deposit are taxed at withdrawal. Estimated revenue: about 1.7 billion NIS/year for options 1 and 3 and 2.2 billion for option 2. The paper notes that real gains above the new ceiling would be taxed at 25% under option 1. It is analysis, not a decision.
  - **Globes, republished by N12, 18.06.2026 (press):** the Treasury is expected again to recommend abolishing the exemption, as part of a broad 2027 Arrangements Law being prepared by the Budget Department. The article describes plans, not decisions.
  - **Calcalist, 12.10.2026 (press):** an internal committee (Tax Authority director, Budget Department head, Chief Economist) still thinks the exemption should be narrowed. The option on the table is limiting the capital-gains exemption "to the end of the sixth year only". The teams have not reached conclusions, and elections are pending.
  - **Earlier attempts:** the Calcalist 12.10.2026 report calls it a revival of an earlier idea. Per TXH-21, none was enacted.
- Figures: options above (2025 paper); none in force.

### TXH-23 Past temporary relief for early withdrawal: not in force
- Rule: No reduced tax rate for early withdrawal is in force. Two past measures are why websites still mention one:

## Left out and why

- Kibbutz and moshav members' funds (s.58A, s.9(16A)(c)(3)): small population; the benefited amount equals the self-employed figure (TXH-08).
- Controlling shareholders' study-fund payments (s.32(9)(b)(2), 4.5% cap): company tax, not saver tax.
- Teachers' special funds (sabbatical rules): product rules for `keren-hishtalmut.md`. Only the 8.4% rate is noted (TXH-01).
- 2% and 3% surtax on interest income from non-benefited gains: `tax-general.md`.
- Distributional statistics from the Chief Economist paper (who deposits, benefit per decile): not rules. They may interest the guide page and are listed for `keren-hishtalmut.md`.
- IRA (self-directed) study funds: no IRA-specific tax rule found here; see `tax-gemel-lehashkaa-ira-child.md`.

## Proposed dimensions

- `benefited-vs-non-benefited-split`: whether part of the balance is non-benefited, which changes exit tax. It depends on salary, employer rate and extra deposits. Needed for 3.2 when salary is above 15,712/month or the person deposits above the ceilings.
