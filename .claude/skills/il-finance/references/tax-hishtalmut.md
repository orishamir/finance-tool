## Summary

A study fund is taxed at three points. **Deposit:** for an employee, the employer's deposit up to 7.5% of a "determining salary" capped at 15,712 NIS a month (188,544 a year in 2026) is not taxed when deposited. Anything the employer pays above that is taxed as salary in the month it is paid. The employee's own deposit comes from net salary and gets no deduction. A self-employed person deducts up to 4.5% of income, with income capped at 293,397 NIS (2024 to 2027). **During:** gains are not taxed while they stay in the fund, and changing the asset mix is not a tax event. **Exit:** after 6 years from the first payment (3 years at retirement age or for study), everything withdrawn is exempt, except gains on deposits above the ceilings. Those gains are "interest income" taxed at up to 25%, and the fund withholds 25%. An earlier withdrawal is taxed at marginal rates on the employer's benefited deposits and on the gains, and the fund withholds 47% (2026). Heirs withdraw tax-free. A partial withdrawal takes the taxable money first unless the member asks for another order (TXH-25). The employer's deposits within the ceiling are also free of National Insurance and health contributions, and the excess is charged (TXH-26). As of 2026-10-22 the law is unchanged. The Finance Ministry's chief economist recommended narrowing the benefit (October 2025), and the press reports it is being considered again for 2027. No bill was found, so Q18 stays open.

## Rules

### TXH-01 Employer deposit within the limits is not taxed at deposit (employee)
- Rule: Amounts an employer pays to a study fund for an employee within the limits are treated as the employee's work income only "when he received them" (`בעת שקיבל אותם`), not when paid to the fund. The limit is the collective-agreement rate, capped at 7.5% of the determining salary (8.4% for a teacher, `עובד הוראה`).
- Conditions / exceptions: The limits are those set in a collective agreement, or in an agreement for a similar employee where none applies. The 8.4% applies only to members of three named teachers' funds. The 2025 Chief Economist paper adds that employer deposits within the ceiling are also exempt from National Insurance and health tax (`פטור ממס הכנסה, הפרשות לביטוח לאומי ומס בריאות על הפקדות המעסיק`). *2026-11-06:* National Insurance's own circular says the same, and that the employer's deposits above the ceiling are charged; the National Insurance side is TXH-26.
- Figures: 7.5% (8.4% teachers) of determining salary up to 15,712 NIS/month, which is 188,544 NIS/year (2026). The Chief Economist paper gives the resulting exempt employer deposit as 14,141 NIS/year "as of 2025". For 2026, 7.5% × 188,544 = 14,140.80 NIS/year. That is computed here, not a published 2026 figure. See TXH-02 for how the salary cap is updated.

### TXH-02 Determining salary (`משכורת קובעת`): definition and update
- Rule: The determining salary is work income excluding expense reimbursements, overtime and similar special payments, "but not more than twice the amount that is the ceiling for paying the cost-of-living allowance, as set from time to time in the agreement between the coordination bureau of the economic organisations and the Histadrut" (`אך לא יותר מכפל הסכום המהווה תקרה לענין תשלום תוספת היוקר כפי שהוא נקבע מעת לעת בהסכם בין לשכת התיאום של הארגונים הכלכליים לבין ההסתדרות הכללית של העובדים בארץ ישראל`). The Ordinance names no amount. The amount in force is the Tax Authority's: its 2026 deductions booklet gives 188,544 NIS a year (15,712 × 12).
- Conditions / exceptions: The cap moves only with the cost-of-living-allowance ceiling set in that agreement. It is not CPI-linked: s.3(ה) is not among the amounts that s.120א lists for yearly indexation under s.120ב. Since when the current amount has stood is not stated in the official texts read. *2026-10-25 rule-text pass:* removed "the law text lists 15,400 until 2004; from March 2004, 15,712" (a Wikisource editor note; s.3(ה) in the consolidated Ordinance has no amount). The amount now rests on the 2026 booklet, and the March 2004 start date was dropped for lack of an official source.
- Figures: 15,712 NIS/month, 188,544 NIS/year (deductions booklet 2026, tax year 2026). Updated only if the cost-of-living ceiling in the agreement changes; start date of the current amount not sourced.

### TXH-03 Employer deposit above the limits is taxed as salary when paid
- Rule: Amounts an employer pays above the limits are the employee's work income "when they were paid to the fund" (`בעת ששולמו לקרן`). They are taxed with the month's salary, not at withdrawal.
- Conditions / exceptions: The deposit itself is not a "benefited deposit" (`הפקדה מוטבת`, TXH-08), so gains on it are taxable at exit (TXH-11). The Chief Economist paper says the same: deposits above the ceiling are allowed, but they are taxed. *2026-11-06:* the excess is also charged National Insurance and health contributions (TXH-26).
- Figures: none of its own; the limit is TXH-01.

### TXH-04 Employee's own deposit: no deduction or credit
- Rule: The employee's deposit is computed on gross salary but paid from net salary, after income tax, National Insurance and health tax. It gets no deduction or credit at deposit. It counts as a benefited deposit, so gains on it are exempt at exit, up to TXH-08's limit.
- Conditions / exceptions: s.17(5A) (deduction) covers only the self-employed fund. No employee study-fund deduction or credit appears in the Tax Authority's 2025 table of exemptions, deductions and credits (pp.86-95), which supports the reading. Not binding: the Ministry of Finance Chief Economist's policy paper (October 2025), cited for the deposit being paid from net salary, describes the law; it binds no one.
- Figures: The benefited employee deposit is 2.5% of determining salary (TXH-08). The Chief Economist paper gives about 4,713 NIS/year for 2025. For 2026, 2.5% × 188,544 = 4,713.60, computed here.

### TXH-05 Self-employed deduction (s.17(5A))
- Rule: An individual with business or professional income may deduct amounts paid to a self-employed study fund (`קרן השתלמות לעצמאים`), up to 4.5% of "determining income" (`הכנסה קובעת`). Determining income is taxable business or professional income before this deduction, capped at a ceiling.
- Conditions / exceptions: The Tax Authority guide describes the deduction for 2025 as 4.5% of income, up to a ceiling of 13,203 NIS (4.5% × 293,397). Someone who is both self-employed and an employee has a reduced ceiling (TXH-06). *2026-10-25 rule-text pass:* the yearly ceilings are no longer attributed to "the law" (283,905 and 293,397 were Wikisource editor notes). The Ordinance holds the base amount and its indexation; the 2025 and 2026 amounts rest on the Tax Authority guide and booklet.
- Figures: The Ordinance's text caps determining income at 156,000 NIS a year (s.17(5א)). That amount is one of the "social reliefs" of s.120א, updated each 1 January by the rise in the CPI (s.120ב(א)), with no update on 1 January of 2025 to 2027 (s.120ב(ה), added in 2024). The Tax Authority gives 293,397 for 2026 (deductions booklet 2026, `תקרת הכנסה קובעת בקרן השתלמות לעצמאיים 17(5א)`) and for 2025 (guide), so the maximum deduction is 13,203 NIS in both years (stated by the guide for 2025; computed here for 2026). The amount for 2028 is not known.

### TXH-06 Self-employed who is also an employee: reduced deduction ceiling
- Rule: For someone who is both self-employed and an employee, the deduction applies only to the part of business income within the "ceiling amount" (`סכום התקרה`). That is the lower of (1) business or professional income and (2) 293,397 minus the determining salary on which the employer paid to a study fund.
- Conditions / exceptions: In the guide's worked example, the employer-funded determining salary is capped at 188,544 (`משכורת קובעת מירבית 188,544`).
- Figures: 293,397 and 188,544 (2025 guide; both unchanged in the 2026 booklet).
- Source: [Tax Authority guide 2025](<https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/generalpage/income-tax-guide-knowyourright/he/Guides_IncomeTax_da-2025.pdf>), p.61 (sections 51-52, worked example) and p.92 · checked 2026-10-22 · primary. ### TXH-07 Self-employed early withdrawal is business income; deposits not deducted are excluded
- Rule: Amounts an individual receives from a self-employed study fund, including interest, linkage differences and other gains, are business or professional income when received. Deposits that were not allowed as a deduction under s.17(5A) are excluded. Interest, linkage and other gains received on the dates in s.9(16B), that is, after the holding period, are interest income under s.2(4).
- Conditions / exceptions: Exempt withdrawals under TXH-09 to TXH-11 take precedence. This rule decides what is taxed when the exemption does not apply.
- Figures: none

### TXH-08 What counts as a "benefited deposit" (`הפקדה מוטבת`)
- Rule: Only gains that come from a benefited deposit are exempt at exit. For an employee (s.9(16A)(c)), a benefited deposit is (1) an employer's payment up to the amount or rate that s.3(e) does not treat as income when paid, and (2) the employee's payment up to one of: one third of the employer's payment at the s.3(e) rate; 2.5% of the determining salary; or another rate set for employee payments in a collective agreement approved before 12 June 2002. For the self-employed (s.9(16B)), it is a fixed annual amount.
- Conditions / exceptions: A kibbutz members' fund (s.58A) has its own amount, equal to the self-employed figure. Deposits above these amounts are allowed but not benefited (TXH-03, TXH-11). *2026-10-25 rule-text pass:* removed "the law lists 19,920 (2023), 20,520 (2024) and 20,566" (Wikisource editor notes; s.9(16ב) holds 14,140). The yearly amount now rests on the Tax Authority booklets; the 2024 and 2025 booklets carry a correction dated 30.4.2025 that sets the s.9(16א)-(16ב) amount at 20,566 a year.
- Figures: The Ordinance's text names 14,140 NIS a year for the self-employed benefited deposit (s.9(16ב)). That amount is one of the "social reliefs" of s.120א, updated each 1 January by the CPI (s.120ב(א)), with no update on 1 January of 2025 to 2027 (s.120ב(ה)). The Tax Authority's booklets give 20,566 a year for 2026 (`הפקדה מוטבת 9(16א) ו-(16ב)`), for 2025 and for 2024; the 2024 and 2025 booklets say the amount was updated to 20,566 on 30.4.2025. Under the freeze the 2027 amount is the same (computed from s.120ב(ה)). The Chief Economist paper gives "about 20,538" for 2025, which disagrees with the booklets; the booklets govern. For the self-employed, deposits between the deduction ceiling (13,203) and 20,566 are not deductible, but their gains are exempt. The Chief Economist paper (note 11) says the same.

### TXH-09 Exempt withdrawal after 6 years
- Rule: Amounts withdrawn from the account are exempt if 6 years have passed since the first payment date of that account. This includes deposits, linkage differences, and interest and other gains that come from the benefited deposit. The same applies to employee funds (s.9(16A)) and self-employed funds (s.9(16B)).
- Conditions / exceptions: The first payment date (`מועד התשלום הראשון`) is the earlier of (1) the end of the month in which the first payment was paid and (2) the end of the month for which it was paid, but not before the start of the tax year in which it was paid. The exemption covers all principal, but gains only on benefited deposits; gains on other deposits fall under TXH-11. The exemption is conditional: "if the employee withdrew any amount from his account, the account will be closed to further payments" (`ייסגר החשבון לתשלומים נוספים`), with the exception in TXH-10. The Minister may set rules on account closure and on when several accounts count as one (TXH-19).
- Figures: 6 years, fixed in law.

### TXH-10 Exempt withdrawal after 3 years: retirement age, or used for study
- Rule: The period is 3 years from the first payment date for (a) an employee or self-employed individual who has reached retirement age (`שהגיע לגיל הפרישה`), and (b) amounts that were used for the member's study (`ששימשו ... לצורך השתלמותו`). The Director may set rules on entitlement to withdraw for study only in s.9(16ב)(א), the self-employed fund ("המנהל רשאי לקבוע כללים לענין הזכאות למשוך סכומים לצורך השתלמות בפטור ממס"); s.9(16א), the employee fund, has no such power. The rules made under it, the 1997 rules (read 2026-11-06), say: "יחיד יהא זכאי למשוך סכומים בפטור ממס מקרן השתלמות, לצורך השתלמות, עד לסכום הזכאות ובתנאי שההשתלמות נועדה לשמירת רמתו המקצועית בתחום עיסוקו או משלח ידו" (rule 2). "סכום זכאות" is the money accumulated in the account up to the withdrawal "ואשר אינם עולים על סכום ההוצאות בשל ההשתלמות", and those expenses are the study expenses "המותרות בניכוי על פי הפקודה והתקנות שהותקנו מכוחה" (rule 1). So for the self-employed, a study withdrawal is exempt only up to the study expenses that the Ordinance allows as a deduction, and only for study that keeps the member's professional level.
- Conditions / exceptions: A withdrawal for study in Israel of no more than one third of the balance at the time does not close the account, provided there was no other such withdrawal in the preceding 12 months (s.9(16A)(b)). "Retirement age" (`גיל הפרישה`) is defined in s.1 of the Ordinance as the retirement age under the Retirement Age Law 2004. That law gives the name "גיל הפרישה" only to its s.3 age (67 for a man, 65 for a woman, with a schedule for women born up to 1969); the early age (60) is "גיל הפרישה המוקדמת" (s.5) and the mandatory age "גיל פרישת חובה" (s.4), so the 3-year route opens at the s.3 age (KH-12). The 1997 rules define their fund as "קרן השתלמות – כהגדרתה בסעיף 17(5א) לפקודה"; today s.17(5א) defines "קרן השתלמות לעצמאים" ("קרן השתלמות המיועדת ליחיד שיש לו הכנסה מעסק או ממשלח יד"), so by their words the rules cover the self-employed fund; whether they reach an employee's study withdrawal under s.9(16א), and which study expenses the Ordinance allows as a deduction, were not read (open question 6). The rules set no evidence a fund must collect; what a fund requires is in its bylaws (`keren-hishtalmut.md`).
- Figures: 3 years; one third of the balance; 12 months. All fixed in law.

### TXH-11 Gains on non-benefited deposits: interest income, up to 25% of the real gain
- Rule: Interest and other gains accrued from 1.1.2003 on deposits above the ceiling, withdrawn after the holding period, are taxed at up to 25%. Gains from the benefited deposit, up to the ceiling, are exempt on withdrawal. In law, interest and other gains received from a study fund on the s.9(16A)/(16B) dates are "interest income". The part of the gains equal to linkage differences on the payments is "linkage income" (`הכנסה מהפרשי הצמדה`). The base is the real gain: the Ministry of Finance Chief Economist paper describes the study-fund exemption as an exemption from capital gains tax "בגובה 25% מהרווחים הריאליים", real gains being nominal gains less the cumulative rise in the CPI (its note 7), and its simulation of a lower ceiling would tax at 25% "על כל רווח ריאלי שייווצר על ההפקדות מעל התקרה החדשה" (p.20).
- Conditions / exceptions: The 25% cap is the individual interest rate of s.125C(b), "as the top slice of income". The fund withholds 25% on "taxable interest amounts" (`סכומי ריבית חייבת`, TXH-15). The guide says to report the income in form 1301 section 15, field 157/257. The Tax Authority guide states no base. The real base follows from the law: s.3(ה4)(1) makes the part equal to linkage on the payments "linkage income", and s.9(13) exempts it; the sentence that kept a provident-fund account out of s.9(13) was deleted by Amendment 190 s.4(2) from 1.1.2012 (tax-bases research 2026-10-25; TXK-07). The 2025 guide's s.9(13) row (p.89) still prints the deleted sentence (`הוראות אלה לא יחולו על חשבון בקופת גמל`) and is no longer cited. The 15% of s.125C(c) (unlinked assets) does not reach these gains: the linkage part is carved out as linkage income, and the Chief Economist paper puts the tax at 25% of the real gain (the same reading as TXK-06 for a kupat gemel lehashkaa). *2026-10-25 scope pass:* the figure `real_base` now cites the Chief Economist paper (primary); the two managers' pages (Mor, Clal) that said the same were dropped as sources.
- Figures: up to 25% (s.125C(b), in force 2026); withholding 25%; the base is the real gain (Chief Economist paper, October 2025).

### TXH-13 Early withdrawal by an employee: what is taxed and at what rate
- Rule: Before the period in TXH-09/TXH-10 has passed, the employer's benefited deposits are taxed as work income when received (s.3(e), TXH-01). The final-tax conclusion is inferred from s.3(e) and the withholding order, and the Ministry of Finance's 2024 memorandum describes it in the same terms: "an early withdrawal ... requires paying tax at the marginal rate on the money withdrawn". ### TXH-15 Withholding by the fund
- Rule: A study fund paying a member "amounts from a study fund" (`סכומים מקרן השתלמות`, the non-exempt early amounts defined in the 1997 Order) withholds tax at the top rate of s.121 (47% in 2026): reg. 1 has defined `השיעור המרבי` since 2004 as "שיעור המס הגבוה ביותר הקבוע בסעיף 121 לפקודה" (KT 6326, s.1). A fund paying "taxable interest amounts" (`סכומי ריבית חייבת`: interest and gains paid after the periods, excluding those from a benefited deposit) withholds 25%.

### TXH-16 The fund keeps benefited and taxable money apart
- Rule: A provident fund for savings (`קופת גמל לתגמולים`) or a study fund keeps, for every member, separate records of the parts of the account (rule 49א(ב)): payments up to the cut-off date (31 December 2002; for a study fund for the self-employed, 30 November 2002), and later payments split into benefited payments (`תשלומי ההפקדה המוטבת`) and the other payments (`התשלומים החייבים`). For a salaried member the fund also keeps the employer's, the employee's and (where there is one) the severance component apart (rule 49א(א)). The fund's profits are attributed to the parts, and its expenses charged to them, at least once a month (rules 49ב, 49ג: "פעם בחודש לפחות"). When a member's rights move to another fund, the transferring fund passes on all the annual records of the account (rule 49ד1).
- Conditions / exceptions: Rule 49ב (profits) excludes an old pension fund and a new comprehensive pension fund ("למעט בקרן ותיקה ובקרן חדשה מקיפה"); rule 49ג (expenses) excludes an old pension fund only ("למעט בקרן ותיקה"). Under rule 49א(ב)(1)(ב)(3), the benefited and taxable payments of a salaried member are split between the employer and employee components in proportion to their payments. Since 2005 the fund also records each withdrawal, broken down by the parts it was taken from and the tax withheld (rule 49א(ד)). The chapter ("פרק שביעי 1: ניהול חשבון קופת גמל", rules 49א-49ג) was added by ק"ת 6230 (2003), s.17; ק"ת 6377 (2005) amended rule 49א (sub-rules (ג)(2), (ד), (ה)) and added rule 49ד1. A text search of the 27 amending booklets the Knesset lists after ק"ת 6377 (2005 to 2018) found no mention of rules 49א, 49ב, 49ג or 49ד1, and National Insurance's consolidated copy (file of 18.8.2025, a lead) marks no later amendment of them. During the 2020 temporary order the Tax Authority set benefited money as the default source of a withdrawal, unless the member asked otherwise (TXH-23). In an ordinary partial withdrawal the regulations set the order of the parts and let the member choose another (TXH-25).

### TXH-18 Gains are not taxed while in the fund

### TXH-19 Transfer to another study fund keeps the account's history
- Rule: Money may be moved from one study fund to another (transfer regulations, reg. 3(a)(4)). Where an employee's account at one fund is closed to further payments and payments continue at a second fund, the two accounts are treated as one if both funds were notified and no tax-exempt withdrawal was made from the first account. The first fund must give the second the first payment date of the closed account. When rights are transferred, all component records pass to the receiving fund (TXH-16).
- Conditions / exceptions: The transfer regulations bar a partial transfer from a study fund (reg. 9(c), as replaced in 2014). That is a product rule for `keren-hishtalmut.md` and `switching-and-transfers.md`. A transfer from a study fund to a provident fund for annuity is allowed only if the member was entitled to withdraw the money tax-free (reg. 2(a)(4)). Tax: reg. 3(ב)(1) of the transfer regulations says that a transfer between study funds (reg. 3(א)(4)) is made to an account in the member's name at the receiving fund, "ויראו לעניין דיני המס, את כל מועדי התשלום לקופה המעבירה כמועדי התשלום לקופה המקבלת". The regulation does not use the words "withdrawal" or "tax event"; it keeps every payment date for tax law. The Chief Economist paper (note 6) is consistent: savings policies are exempt "only on a switch between tracks, not between managing companies". *2026-10-25 rule-text pass:* the tax side now rests on reg. 3(ב)(1) (primary) instead of the Chief Economist footnote; open question 11 is closed for payment dates.

### TXH-25 Partial withdrawal: taxable money comes out first unless the member asks otherwise
- Rule: When a member asks to withdraw part of the money in a study-fund account, the managing company pays it "מכל אחד ממרכיבי חשבון קרן ההשתלמות, לפי סדר זה": (1) the part of the taxable payments (`התשלומים החייבים`: payments from the cut-off date that are not benefited deposits); (2) the part of the payments up to the cut-off date (31 December 2002; for a self-employed study fund 30 November 2002); (3) the part of the benefited-deposit payments (`תשלומי ההפקדה המוטבת`, s.9(16א)(ג) or 9(16ב)). The account's components are the employer's payments and the employee's payments. If the member asks to withdraw in a different order, the company pays as asked: "ביקש העמית ... למשוך את הכספים ... באופן שונה מן הסדר ... תשלם לו החברה המנהלת את הכספים לפי בקשתו" (reg. 2(ב)).
- Conditions / exceptions: The regulations speak of a partial withdrawal only; a full withdrawal takes every part. They were published on 14.3.2011 and took effect 90 days later (reg. 3), so from about 12.6.2011 (computed). The same gazette repealed rule 41יג(ב)(2) of the 1964 Income Tax Rules, which had set the order for partial withdrawals until then: in 2003 pro rata in the order pre-cut-off, benefited, taxable; from 2005 pre-cut-off, taxable, benefited. Rule 41יג(ב)(1), still in force, makes a payment to which s.87 of the Ordinance applies come "באופן יחסי מכל אחד מחלקי מרכיבי חשבון קופת הגמל"; s.87(א)(2) covers money a fund paid to members "בניגוד להוראות לפי סעיף 23 לחוק הפיקוח על קופות גמל". Each part keeps its own payments, linkage differences and interest, and the benefited and taxable payments are split between the employer and employee components in the ratio of their payments (rule 49א(ב), TXH-16). The regulations do not say how a withdrawal from one part divides between its payments and its gains, nor how the amount divides between the employer and employee components (open question 15). The tax on what comes out follows the part it comes from: after the period, the gains of the taxable part are taxed (TXH-11), the pre-2003 part's gains are exempt (TXH-12) and the benefited part is exempt (TXH-09); before the period, TXH-13. *Inference, not stated by the regulations:* under the default order a member with taxable money in the account takes the taxable part first, whose gains are taxed and whose deposits were taxed when paid (TXH-03, TXH-04), and has to ask for another order to take exempt money first; how much of a withdrawal from the taxable part is gain and how much is deposit is open question 15. The 2020 temporary order's default (benefited money first, TXH-23) was a Tax Authority instruction for that window only. The regulations are read in the Ministry of Justice's Reshumot booklet of ק"ת 6984 (re-read 2026-11-08; the olaw.org.il copy read first is the same file and is a lead only). The Knesset's secondary-legislation record (Id 2136214) confirms the title, ק"ת 6984, page 748, publication on 14.3.2011 and the status "פורסם ברשומות", and records no amending regulation (no binding in `KNS_SecToSecBinding`).

### TXH-26 National Insurance and health contributions on study-fund deposits and withdrawals
- Rule: For an employee, National Insurance and health contributions are charged on the income from the sources in s.2(2) of the Ordinance (NI Law s.344(א); NII: "הן אותן ההכנסות שחייבות במס הכנסה על פי סעיף 2(2)"). National Insurance's circular on exempt salary income lists the employer's deposits to a study fund (with provident, pension and severance deposits) as exempt "ובלבד שהיא לא מעל לתקרה הקבועה בפקודת מס הכנסה", and "הפרשות המעסיק מעבר לתקרה - חייבות בזקיפת שווי". For a self-employed person, the income charged is the final assessment "לפני כל פטור, ניכויים וזיכויים לפי פקודת מס הכנסה", less the National Insurance deduction of s.47א and the pension deduction of s.47(ב)(1) (NI Law s.345(ב)(1)); s.345(א) takes that income "לאחר שנוכו ההוצאות הקשורות במישרין בהשגת ההכנסה". Whether the self-employed study-fund deduction (s.17(5א), TXH-05) lowers the base is not settled (conditions; open question 4(c)).
- Conditions / exceptions: The ceiling is the s.3(ה) one (TXH-01, TXH-02). *Inference:* the excess is charged in the month it is paid to the fund, because s.3(ה) makes it work income "בעת ששולמו לקרן" (TXH-03) and the circular charges it as imputed value; the circular names no month. Health contributions follow the same income (TXG-34), and the circular covers both ("דמי ביטוח לאומי ודמי ביטוח בריאות"). The employee's own deposit is paid from net salary, after both (TXH-04). **Self-employed deduction.** *Inference, not forced by the text:* the s.17(5א) deduction is not among the amounts s.345(ב)(1) subtracts, which points to its not lowering the base; but s.345(א) deducts the expenses directly connected with producing the income, and s.17(5א) sits inside s.17, whose opening deducts "יציאות והוצאות שיצאו כולן בייצור הכנסתו", so "ניכויים" in s.345(ב)(1) cannot mean every s.17 deduction, and the text does not say on which side the study-fund payment falls. National Insurance's worked example for the self-employed repeats s.345 and subtracts only 52% of the National Insurance contributions and part of a provident-fund deposit ("הסכום שמופחת נקבע על ידי מס הכנסה"); it says nothing about study funds. Kol Zchut (secondary, no source given, page of 5.4.2026) says the opposite: "הפקדה לקרן השתלמות מקטינה את גובה 'ההכנסה החייבת' גם לצורך התשלומים למוסד לביטוח לאומי", with a saving of up to 17.83% of the deposit. Neither reading is used; open question 4(c). **Withdrawals.** The circular reports reg. 2 of the National Insurance (Payment and Exemption from Payment of Contributions) Regulations, 1995: "נקבע, כי לא ישולמו דמי ביטוח מהכנסת עובד הפטורה ממס לפי פקודת מס הכנסה למעט הכנסה לפי סעיף 9(5)". In the law itself, NI Law s.350(א)(7) leaves out of the base "הכנסה שאינה הכנסה מעבודה כעובד או כעובד עצמאי, הפטורה ממס לפי כל דין" (with exceptions for early pension and income the Minister of Finance sets). *Inference:* an exempt withdrawal under s.9(16א) (TXH-09, TXH-10) is therefore not charged: its exempt gains under s.350(א)(7) and reg. 2, the employer's deposits under reg. 2; whether those deposits are work income, which would put them outside s.350(א)(7), is itself a reading. The regulation itself was not read; its file on btl.gov.il was not found. Gains on non-benefited deposits withdrawn after the period are taxed under s.125ג(ב) (TXH-11), and NI Law s.350(א)(6) excludes from the base "הכנסה החייבת במס לפי הוראות סעיף 125ג(ב) או (ג)"; that these gains fall under it is read from the two texts together. Not found: whether the employer's benefited deposits withdrawn before the period, which s.3(ה) makes work income when received (TXH-13), are charged, and on whom; and whether gains withdrawn before the period, taxed at marginal rates (s.125ג(ד)(5)), are charged as income not from work (TXG-26, TXG-34 say they may be). Open question 4.

## Left out and why

- Kibbutz and moshav members' funds (s.58A, s.9(16A)(c)(3)): small population; the benefited amount equals the self-employed figure (TXH-08).
- Controlling shareholders' study-fund payments (s.32(9)(b)(2), 4.5% cap): company tax, not saver tax.
- Teachers' special funds (sabbatical rules): product rules for `keren-hishtalmut.md`. Only the 8.4% rate is noted (TXH-01).
- 2% and 3% surtax on interest income from non-benefited gains: `tax-general.md`.
- Distributional statistics from the Chief Economist paper (who deposits, benefit per decile): not rules. They may interest the guide page and are listed for `keren-hishtalmut.md`.
- IRA (self-directed) study funds: no IRA-specific tax rule found here; see `tax-gemel-lehashkaa-ira-child.md`.

## Proposed dimensions

- `benefited-vs-non-benefited-split`: whether part of the balance is non-benefited, which changes exit tax. It depends on salary, employer rate and extra deposits. Needed for 3.2 when salary is above 15,712/month or the person deposits above the ceilings.
