## Summary

- **FX deposits.** For an asset linked to or denominated in foreign currency, the law's "index" is the exchange rate. An FX deposit is therefore a "linked" deposit: the exchange-rate gain on the principal is exempt linkage, and only the profit above it is taxed, at up to 25% (TXI-04 closed the bucket; TX2-01). Banks withhold 25% in practice (two banks' own documents), although the withholding regulations' definition of the index does not obviously cover deposits (TX2-02). Olim are exempt on FX-deposit interest for 20 years and returning residents for 5 years, under conditions (TX2-03).
- **FX cash.** The exchange-rate gain on a balance at a bank is exempt linkage, on my reading (TX2-04). FX held outside a bank (banknotes, cash at a foreign broker) has no sourced rule.
- **Structured deposits.** Taxed as deposits, not as securities: losses on a securities portfolio cannot be offset against them (Tax Authority circular 10/2025 s.3.7; one bank says the same on its product page). The rate depends on how the payout is linked, and the classification was not found in any official source (TX2-05).
- **Israel Bonds.** Interest the State pays on a dollar loan it received from a foreign resident is exempt for a foreign resident and for anyone who was one when he lent, also after he becomes a resident (the 1991 order as amended in 1998). A resident who bought as a resident has no exemption: a dollar bond, so up to 25% of the interest, the exchange-rate change exempt, and 25% withheld where Israel withholds; the State's memorandum says it pays without withholding, so such interest is reported in a return (TX2-22).
- **Structured bonds and deposit certificates.** Taxed as bonds. Linked only if the whole face value follows the full CPI or a full currency rate: up to 25% of the real gain. Anything else, a share-index note or a shekel deposit certificate, is not linked: 15% of the nominal gain (TX2-23).
- **Corporate bonds and commercial paper.** Same rules as government bonds: unlinked, 15% on nominal interest and gain; CPI-linked, up to 25% on real interest and gain; FX-linked, the exchange rate is the index (TX2-06, TX2-07). A redemption or waiver in a default is a "sale", so a capital loss arises and is offset under the normal rules (TX2-08, derived).
- **REITs.** Pass-through: the distribution keeps the character of the fund's income. Residential rent 20%, "extraordinary income" 70%, depreciation-equivalent amounts as capital gain, other income (for example commercial rent) at the person's ordinary rates, starting at 31% under age 60, and withheld at the top s.121 rate (TX2-09, TX2-10). Selling units is a capital gain; a loss on sale can also absorb same-year REIT distributions (TX2-11).
- **Gold funds.** No gold-specific rule found. An Israeli gold fund is taxed as an Israeli fund under its prospectus tax track (TX2-13). All six tracking gold funds of IBI, Kesem and Meitav state that they are exempt; IBI's and Meitav's unhedged ETFs also state a US-dollar principal currency, so their real gain is measured against the dollar, while the other four are measured against the CPI (TX2-24). A foreign gold ETF or ETC is a foreign security, but whether an ETC counts as a bond, and how a grantor-trust ETF is treated, are open (TX2-14).
- **Managed portfolios.** No separate tax regime found: the person is taxed on the securities in the portfolio, and the custodian bank or TASE member withholds (TX2-15). Management and custody fees for securities transactions in the same tax year reduce the gain. Whether a portfolio-management fee qualifies is not confirmed (TX2-16).

## Rules

### TX2-01 FX deposits: the exchange rate is the index; exchange-rate gain on the principal is exempt, the profit above it is taxed at up to 25%
- Rule: For interest, "index" means the CPI, "ובנכס שערכו צמוד למטבע חוץ או שהוא נקוב במטבע חוץ – שער המטבע". The Tax Authority guide applies this to bank deposits: for a deposit linked to or denominated in foreign currency the index is the exchange rate, and a deposit counts as "not linked" only if it is not fully linked. On a linked deposit, the profit above the rise in the index is taxed at up to 25%. The exchange-rate gain itself is a linkage differential: the Ordinance defines "הפרשי שער" as "סכום שנוסף עקב שינוי בשער החליפין לקרן מילווה, שהיא פקדון במטבע חוץ", and "for exemption purposes" linkage differentials are "כל סכום שנוסף לחוב או לסכום תביעה" "עקב הצמדה לשער המטבע או למדד המחירים לצרכן, לרבות הפרשי שער". s.9(13) exempts linkage differentials an individual receives on an asset if they are not partial, no interest or linkage expense was claimed on the asset, and they are not business income.
- Conditions / exceptions: This rule adds the exemption of the exchange-rate gain to TXI-04 and TXI-05, which already closed Q2 for the interest bucket; it does not repeat them. The TXI-06 exclusions (business income, an employee of the bank where the rate was not set in good faith, and so on) send the interest to marginal rates. A fall in the foreign currency against the shekel: the interest is still taxed in full, as the Supreme Court held for a foreign-currency-linked bank deposit (TXI-25), and the negative exchange difference was not offset against it: the district court found no provision allowing it, and the Supreme Court left open whether a route exists (TXI-25, corrected 2026-10-26); the same reasoning reaches a deposit held in foreign currency, whose exchange difference is a linkage differential under the s.1 definitions above. Age 60 and over: 25% is a maximum rate (TXI-19, TXG-07, TXG-08).
- Figures: up to 25% of interest above the exchange-rate change (tax year 2026; fixed in law, s.125C(b)).

### TX2-02 FX deposits: banks withhold 25% in practice; the regulations' wording does not clearly support it
- Rule: Two banks' own documents say they withhold 25% from an Israeli-resident individual on FX-deposit profits. Bank Hapoalim's fixed-term FX deposit terms: profits of "יחיד תושב ישראל שאין לגביו פטור ספציפי" "חייבים בניכוי מס במקור בשיעור 25%", non-resident individuals are exempt, and "בכל מקרה המס יהיה על פי הדין, שיחול באותה עת". Bank Leumi's FX-deposit page lists "פמ"ח תושב ישראל יחיד" at 25% and says the rates are for withholding only; the final liability is set by the Tax Authority.
- Conditions / exceptions: The withholding regulations of 2005 say that a bank paying interest to an individual withholds 25%, or 15% "על נכס שאינו צמוד למדד", and they define "מדד" "כהגדרתו בסעיף 88 לפקודה". The s.88 definition uses the exchange rate only for "נייר ערך בידי יחיד, הנקוב במטבע חוץ" and for a foreign resident's purchase; a bank deposit is not a security. Read literally, an FX deposit would be "not linked" for withholding and attract 15%. The banks' practice (25%) matches the liability in TX2-01, not this literal reading. Which base the banks apply (FX interest converted at payment, or something else) is not stated in either document (open question 2). Withholding does not change the final tax, which is settled under the law in an annual return if needed (TXG-15 to TXG-17). This answers TXI open question 4 at institution strength.
- Figures: 25% withholding, resident individual; 0% non-resident individual (bank documents; Hapoalim PDF created 24.4.2023; Leumi page undated).

### TX2-03 FX-deposit interest exempt for olim (20 years) and returning residents (5 years)
- Rule: Interest, including exchange differences, on an FX deposit at a banking institution is exempt for a person who became an Israeli resident for the first time (oleh) if 20 years have not passed since then, and for a returning resident ("תושב חוזר") for 5 years from becoming one. Conditions, per the Tax Authority guide: (1) only money the person had abroad before becoming a resident was deposited (for a returning resident, also not from selling assets in Israel); (2) it was deposited within 90 days of transferring it to Israel; (3) the person declared their status to the bank within 14 days of first opening the deposit, on form 2402 (oleh) or 2409 (returning resident); (4) the interest is not business income and not recorded in books; (5) the deposit was not used to give, or secure, a loan to a resident relative or to a resident company the person controls.
- Conditions / exceptions: The bank does not withhold on interest covered by an exemption order (reg. 4). The withholding regulations name the orders: "צו מס הכנסה (פטור ממס על הכנסה מריבית על פיקדון במטבע חוץ), התשס״ג–2002" and "צו מס הכנסה (פטור ממס לתושב חוזר על הכנסה מריבית על פיקדון במטבע חוץ), התשס״ד–2003". The orders themselves were not read (open question 3). Leumi's page adds a minimum deposit term of three months and says a deposit used as loan collateral is taxable; neither point is in the guide. Separate exemptions exist for FX deposits funded by Nazi-persecution compensation and certain WWII disability pensions (guide p.52 section 2(א)); they are narrow and not detailed here. Whether the 2026 changes for olim (TXF-26, TXG-33) touch these orders was not checked (open question 3).
- Figures: 20 years (oleh); 5 years (returning resident); 90 days; 14 days. Fixed in the orders as described by the 2025 guide.

### TX2-04 FX current account and FX held as cash
- Rule: Derived. A "פיקדון" in the Ordinance is "סכום שהופקד בתאגיד בנקאי, או במוסד בנקאי מחוץ לישראל המתנהל על פי הדין". An FX balance in a current account at an Israeli bank is such a sum, so its exchange-rate gain is "הפרשי שער" and exempt under s.9(13) on the same conditions as TX2-01. Any interest a bank pays on it is withheld like deposit interest (TXI-08, reg. 4: any interest a bank pays an individual).
- Conditions / exceptions: (a) That a current-account balance is a "פיקדון" for s.1 is my reading of the definition; no source says it for current accounts. (b) FX held outside a bank has no sourced rule. The s.88 "נכס" covers "כל רכוש, בין מקרקעין ובין מטלטלין" and excludes an individual's movables for personal or family use; the definition neither names nor excludes currency, and no s.97 exemption for currency was found. The s.88 exchange-rate index applies only to securities held by an individual. So for banknotes, and for FX cash held at a foreign broker that is not a bank, it is open whether a conversion gain is a capital gain, how it is measured, and whether a loss is allowable (open question 4; also TXF open question 4). (c) Exchange-rate losses on a bank balance: no source found on whether they can be used.
- Figures: none.

### TX2-05 Structured deposits: taxed as a deposit; the rate depends on how the payout is linked
- Rule: A structured deposit is a bank deposit. Its return is interest, or linkage differentials, under the deposit rules (TXI, TX2-01), not a capital gain on a security. Mizrahi Tefahot's [product page](https://www.mizrahi-tefahot.co.il/media/sfgdrxtd/דף-מוצר-פיקדון-מובנה-sp500-072025-pikadon_sp500_biz_acc.pdf) for its two-year S&P 500 structured deposit (deposit date 21.7.2025) says: "מס: ייגבה על פי דין" and that a structured deposit is classed as a deposit for tax, "ולפיכך לא יותר קיזוז מס בין הפיקדון המובנה לבין תיק ני"ע של הלקוח". A securities loss therefore cannot be offset against it, consistent with TXI-17. Leumi's [campaign page](https://www.leumi.co.il/he/Campiagn/2611) for a one-year shekel deposit paying the higher of USD linkage or 1% says the bank withholds "על פי דין" and "חבות המס ושיעורי המס, ככל שיחלו, עשויים להיות שונים בין המסלולים". The Tax Authority says so generally: securities losses are not offset against interest on "פיקדונות (לרבות פיקדונות מובנים - Structured Products)" (circular 10/2025 s.3.7).
- Conditions / exceptions: No official source classifies the payout of a structured deposit. On the statute alone (derived): a conditional bonus paid on a nominal shekel principal looks like interest on an unlinked asset (15% of nominal, TXI-03); a payout from FX linkage on a fully FX-linked deposit is exempt linkage plus taxable profit above it (TX2-01); a payout that follows the exchange rate only above a floor may be "partial linkage", which is taxable and falls in the 15% bucket (TXI-04, TX2-18). Old bank terms reproduced by a deposit-news site show the withholding rate switching with the outcome: on a 2016 Leumi shekel deposit whose bonus was USD-linked, 25% of real gain if no trigger event occurred and 15% of nominal gain if it did; on a 2015 Leumi USD deposit, 25% of real gain. These are secondary and old; they show that the rate is product-specific, not what it is today (open question 5). A shekel structured deposit guarantees only the nominal principal (Mizrahi page), so the 15% bucket taxes inflation as well.

### TX2-06 Corporate bonds and commercial paper: interest by linkage
- Rule: Interest on corporate bonds and commercial paper follows s.125C like any interest: 15% of the nominal interest on an asset that is not linked, or only partly linked, to the index (TXI-03), and up to 25% of the real interest on a fully linked asset (TXI-02, TXI-05). The guide states this for traded securities: interest on a security taxed at up to 25% is reported in field 15, "אולם אם הריבית משולמת על נכס שאינו צמוד למדד או שהוא צמוד בחלקו ... תחויב במס בשיעור של 15%" (field 13). Because s.125C makes the exchange rate the index for an asset linked to or denominated in foreign currency (TX2-01), a USD-linked or USD-denominated corporate bond falls in the linked, up-to-25% bucket (derived).
- Conditions / exceptions: Withholding: a non-bank payer withholds 25%, or 15% on an unlinked asset (TXI-09); a TASE member that receives interest for its clients withholds (TXI-08). Discount on issue is interest (TXI-01). Accrued interest bought with a bond: TXI-16. Bonds issued before 8.5.2000 and interest accrued before 2012 have legacy rates (TXI-10). A same-year securities loss can absorb corporate-bond interest taxed at no more than 25% (TXI-17, TXS-17). The TXI-06 exclusions apply, including a substantial shareholder in the issuer.

### TX2-07 Corporate bonds and commercial paper: gain on sale or redemption by linkage
- Rule: The gain on selling "איגרת חוב, נייר ערך מסחרי, מילווה או הלוואה, שאינם צמודים למדד" is taxed at up to 15% and the whole gain counts as real (s.91(b)(3)); "not linked" means the face value or amount "אינו צמוד למדד, או שהוא צמוד בחלקו לשיעור עליית המדד". For a bond held by an individual that is denominated in or linked to foreign currency, s.88 makes the exchange rate the index (TXS-04, TXF-03), so the bond is "linked" for s.91(b)(3) and its gain above the exchange-rate change is taxed at up to 25% (derived from the two definitions). Redemption is a sale ("מכירה" includes "פדיון"). How the price of a bond bought below par is split between discount (interest) and capital gain is open (TXI open question 3). Substantial shareholder: up to 20% on unlinked debt.

### TX2-08 Corporate bonds in default: redemption, waiver and debt-for-equity are sales; the loss is a capital loss
- Rule: Derived. "מכירה" includes "חליפין, ויתור, הסבה, העברה, הענקה, מתנה, פדיון" (excluding inheritance). A redemption below cost, a waiver of part of the debt in a debt settlement ("הסדר חוב"), and an exchange of bonds for shares are each a sale. Where the consideration is below cost, the result is a capital loss, offset under s.92: first against capital gains, in the same year also against securities interest and dividends taxed at up to 25%, and carried forward against capital gains only if a return was filed (TXS-17, TXS-18). When a redemption on which discount fees are also paid creates a capital loss, the discount fees are added to the redemption proceeds up to the loss, for a holder who is not a controlling holder and did not hold the bond since issue (TXI-13).
- Conditions / exceptions: No provision on unpaid or written-off bonds was found in ss.88-101A: no "חוב אבוד" or debt-settlement rule for a private bondholder. In a debt settlement approved by a court under s.350 of the Companies Law, Income Tax circular 2/2010 sets the holder's side: consideration given for deferring interest or principal without redemption is interest income (s.4.3.1); in a partial or full redemption the consideration is attributed first to accrued interest and discount fees and then to principal, with a ruling route for a capital-only attribution or for continuity when one debt instrument is swapped for another without a material change (s.4.3.2); a material change of conversion terms is a deemed sale of the old bond for the new one (s.4.3.3). Open: when the loss arises if the bond is not redeemed and simply stops paying (no sale event), and how the bank withholds and nets in a settlement (open question 6). The same default inside an exempt fund: TXS-37. *2026-10-26 open-questions audit:* the circular was added; the treatment of unpaid accrued interest in a settlement is no longer open. A loss on an unlinked bond offsets gains taxed at 25% shekel for shekel, which is worth more than the 15% the gain would have borne; no rule limiting this was found. Circular 2/2010 also says: when the issuer or a related company buys its bonds back, the holder's income is classed and timed as if the bonds were sold to an unrelated third party (s.3); converting a convertible bond is generally not a tax event until the shares are sold (s.4.3.3).

### TX2-09 REIT distributions to an individual: taxed by the source of the fund's income
- Rule: A REIT ("קרן להשקעות במקרקעין") is pass-through. "לצורך חישוב המס, שיעורי המס וקיזוז ההפסדים, תיחשב ההכנסה החייבת של קרן להשקעות במקרקעין" passed to shareholders as their taxable income, taxed when passed to them. The character of the income follows its source in the fund. Rates in the shareholder's hands:
  - "הכנסות חריגות יחויבו במס בשיעור של 70%, בלא זכאות לפטור, לניכוי לזיכוי או לקיזוז". Since Amendment 285 (ספר החוקים 3509, 31.3.2026), "הכנסות חריגות" also include income from selling property that was under construction ("מקרקעין בהקמה") when the REIT bought it, if sold before four years from when it began to be rented (s.64A2, definition, new para. (4)); and income from property under construction joins the kinds that do not count toward the 5% extraordinary-income test (para. (2)(א)).
  - Taxable income from income-producing residential rental property ("מקרקעין מניבים לצורכי דיור להשכרה") that is not short-term-held, and real gain on selling it: "יחויבו במס בשיעור של 20%".
  - Gain on selling short-term-held property: s.121 or s.126 rates.
  - "הכנסה בסכום הוצאות הפחת, יראו אותה כהכנסה מרווח הון או משבח, לפי הענין", and so are amounts passed beyond the fund's taxable income and depreciation.
  - Everything else, for example rent from offices, retail or logistics, is taxed at the person's ordinary s.121 rates, and "ההכנסה לא תיחשב כהכנסה מיגיעה אישית". For a person under 60 that schedule starts at 31%; at 60 or over the lower brackets apply (TXG-03, TXG-04).
- Conditions / exceptions: Income the REIT kept and was taxed on itself, when later distributed, is treated as a dividend (TX2-10). Foreign taxes paid by the REIT are deducted from the related income and give shareholders no credit, and the REIT's losses cannot be offset against shareholders' income (s.64A4, per the fetch tool's summary). The residential definition covers property bought from 7 June 2016 to 31 December 2033. Derived: because REIT income is not personal-effort income, it counts as capital-source income for the 2% surtax (TXG-10). Whether National Insurance and health contributions apply to REIT rent passed through is open: TXG-27 exempts s.125B dividends and s.122 rent, not REIT income (open question 8). The share of each source in a given REIT's distribution is a fund fact (`reits-and-gold-funds.md`).
- Figures: 70%; 20%; ordinary rates from 31% (under 60, 2026; frozen to 2027, TXG-06). Residential window 7.6.2016-31.12.2033. Fixed in law.

### TX2-13 Gold through an Israeli mutual fund or ETF

### TX2-15 Managed and robo portfolios: taxed as the securities held; the custodian withholds

### TX2-17 VAT on portfolio-management fees: a manager that is not a financial institution adds 18%; banks, insurers and TASE members with 75% of turnover from that activity do not
- Rule: VAT is imposed "על עסקה בישראל ועל יבוא טובין" at the rate the Minister sets by order (VAT Law s.2); the order sets "18% ממחיר העסקה או הטובין" (s.1 of the rate order, as amended by ק"ת 11179 from 1.1.2025). A "עוסק" is "מי שמוכר נכס או נותן שירות במהלך עסקיו, ובלבד שאינו מלכ"ר או מוסד כספי" (s.1). A "מוסד כספי" is a body taking current-account deposits or using "בנק" in its name, a financial institution under the Bank of Israel Law subject to liquidity rules, "מבטח", or a type the Minister set by order (s.1); the 1977 order adds "עוסק שהוא חבר הבורסה לניירות ערך בתל־אביב בע"מ וש־75% או יותר ממחזור העסקאות שלו נובעים מעסקאותיו בתור שכזה", a fund manager or trustee under the Joint Investment Trust Law with the same 75% test, and dealers in foreign currency or securities. A financial institution pays "מס שכר וריווח" on its wages and profit instead (s.4(ב)), so it does not charge VAT on its fees.
- Conditions / exceptions: Reading of the texts together: a portfolio-management fee charged by a licensee that is not a TASE member, bank, insurer or other financial institution is a service by a dealer and carries 18% VAT on top of the quoted rate; Fjord quotes "0.5% בתוספת מע"מ" (MGD-20). Trading commissions and custody fees of a bank or TASE member carry no VAT. Whether a given manager is a financial institution (for example a TASE member with 75% of its turnover from that activity, or a group company that is one) is a fact about the manager, to be read from its tariff or invoice (institution). The 18% applies from 1.1.2025: the 2024 amending order replaced "17%" with "18%" (ק"ת 11179). Since the 2026 Economic Plan Law, para. (2) of "מוסד כספי" ends "ולמעט בנק חברתי", and a social bank (a term that law defines) is counted as a non-profit instead; this does not touch portfolio managers. *2026-10-25 rule-text pass:* added the 1.1.2025 start of the 18% and the 2026 social-bank carve-out, both from the official texts already cited.

### TX2-18 Partial linkage differentials are interest income; how a structured deposit's payout is classified (derived from primary texts)
- Rule: s.3(ה6): "הכנסה מהפרשי הצמדה חלקיים, יראו אותה כהכנסה מריבית; בסעיף קטן זה, 'הפרשי הצמדה חלקיים' – כהגדרתם בסעיף 9(13)(1), ואולם לענין זה יראו כמדד את מדד המחירים לצרכן, שער המטבע, לרבות הפרשי שער, או כל מדד אחר". s.9(13)(1) exempts linkage differentials only if "הפרשי ההצמדה אינם הפרשי הצמדה חלקיים" and leaves the definition to the Minister. The 2003 regulations define four cases: "הפרשי הצמדה המחושבים על חלק מסכום או חלק מחוב"; "הפרשי הצמדה המחושבים לפי חלק משיעור עליית המדד"; "אותו חלק מהפרשי ההצמדה העולה על שיעור עליית המדד"; "הפרשי הצמדה המחושבים על חלק מהתקופה שבה נצבר חוב או על חלק מהתקופה מאז שנוצרה תביעה". Interest on an asset "שאינו צמוד למדד, או שהוא צמוד בחלקו לשיעור עליית המדד" is taxed at 15% (s.125C(c)(1), TXI-03).
- Conditions / exceptions: Derived application to the structured deposits in FXD-11 and FXD-12 (my reading, no Tax Authority text on structured deposits): (a) a conditional grant that follows a share index or basket is not "הפרשי הצמדה" at all (s.1 defines them, for the exemption, as sums added for linkage to the exchange rate or the CPI), so it is interest on an unlinked shekel deposit, 15% of the nominal amount, and the principal's loss of purchasing power is not deductible; (b) a payout that follows the exchange rate only above a floor, or a fraction of its move, is partial linkage, interest, 15%; (c) a deposit fully linked to or denominated in a foreign currency pays exempt linkage on the principal and up to 25% on the interest above it (TX2-01); a conditional grant on top of such a deposit (Discount "פיקדון פלוס", FXD-12: USD principal, 4% interest, 4% grant if the rate did not fall) is interest in the deposit currency, 25% under TX2-01, which matches the sheet's "ניכוי מס במקור ליחיד – 25%". The exact wording of each deposit deed decides; the old Leumi terms in TX2-05 (25% real or 15% nominal depending on the trigger) fit this scheme. Not closed: a Tax Authority statement on structured deposits (open question 5). The record's primary strength covers the statutory part (s.3(ה6), s.9(13)(1), the 2003 regulations, s.125C); the application to structured payouts in (a) to (c) is a reading of those texts (noted 2026-10-26, scope settling). Support for reading (a), for bonds only: circular 22/2002 (s.2.2.2) counts as linked only a bond whose whole face value follows the full CPI or a full foreign-currency rate, or that is denominated in foreign currency; linkage above 100% counts as interest (example 4), and "איגרת חוב שערכה צמוד למדד מניות, תחשב כאיגרת חוב שאינה צמודה" (example 5). It was written for the 2003 reform and does not speak of deposits.
- Figures: 15% (s.125C(c)(1)); up to 25% (s.125C(b)). Fixed in law; the 15% by order.

### TX2-19 The olim and returning-resident FX-deposit exemption orders, read in full
- Rule: Olim order (2002): "פיקדון במטבע חוץ" is a deposit "בתאגיד בנקאי, לזמן קבוע שלא יפחת משלושה חודשים". Interest paid to "יחיד שהיה לתושב ישראל לראשונה" on such a deposit is exempt if all hold: only money the person had abroad before becoming a resident was deposited; it was deposited within ninety days of transferring the money to Israel; twenty years have not passed since first becoming a resident; the person declared on form 2402 within 14 days of first opening the deposit; the interest is not business income and is not recorded in books; and the deposit did not serve as a loan or security for a loan the bank gave to the person's relative or a company he controls, if residents. Applies to interest paid from 1.1.2003. Returning-resident order (2003): deposit at a banking corporation (no minimum term); only money the person had abroad, not from selling Israeli assets subject to tax, created while abroad after ceasing to be a resident; deposited within ninety days of transfer; 5 years from becoming a returning resident; declaration on form 2409 within 14 days of depositing; not business income; no loan or security to a relative; and "הריבית משולמת בעבור התקופה שבה זכאי התושב החוזר לפטור".
- Conditions / exceptions: Confirms TX2-03 and adds the three-month minimum term for olim (which Leumi's page stated without a source) and the "created while abroad" and "not from Israeli asset sales" tests for returning residents. The Knesset's secondary-legislation record lists no amendment of either order ([2130567](https://knesset.gov.il/OdataV4/ParliamentInfo/KNS_SecondaryLaw(2130567)), [2131101](https://knesset.gov.il/OdataV4/ParliamentInfo/KNS_SecondaryLaw(2131101)); no amending binding), and the 2026 laws that amended the Ordinance (ספר החוקים 3509, Amendment 285, and 3511, the 2026 Economic Efficiency Law) do not mention a deposit in foreign currency (searched 2026-11-08). The mirror read first is a lead only. The bank does not withhold when an exemption order applies (reg. 4, TX2-03). Closes the "orders not read" half of open question 3.
- Figures: 3 months (olim deposit term); 90 days; 20 years; 5 years; 14 days. Fixed in the orders.

### TX2-22 Israel Bonds (sold through the Bonds organisation): exempt for a holder who lent while a foreign resident; for a resident who bought as a resident, a dollar bond at up to 25%
- Rule: Two cases, by who lent the money to the State.
  - **Lent while a foreign resident: exempt.** Under s.15 the Minister may exempt interest and linkage on a state loan, "בין בכלל ובין רק לגבי הריבית והפרשי ההצמדה המשתלמים לתושבי חוץ". The 1991 order made under it defines "מילווה" as "כל הלוואה או פיקדון במטבע חוץ שקיבלה המדינה מתושב חוץ". Its s.2, as replaced in 1998: "הכנסה מריבית והפרשי הצמדה על מילווה שמשלמת המדינה לתושב חוץ או למי שהיה תושב חוץ כאשר נתן את המילווה, פטורה ממס". So the exemption follows the holder's status on the day he lent, not on the day he is paid: a person who bought Israel Bonds abroad as a foreign resident and later became a resident of Israel stays exempt on their interest (derived from the order's words).
  - **Bought as a resident of Israel: taxed.** Money the State received from a resident is not a "מילווה" under the order, and the 2004 regulations exempt only foreign residents and only state bonds listed on the Tel Aviv exchange ("הרשומים למסחר בבורסה בישראל"). No other exemption was found. The bonds are denominated in US dollars (FXD-19), and s.125C(a) makes the exchange rate the index "בנכס שערכו צמוד למטבע חוץ או שהוא נקוב במטבע חוץ", so they are a linked asset: the interest is taxed at up to 25% (s.125C(b), TXI-02), not at the 15% of s.125C(c)(1), and the exchange-rate change on the principal is a linkage differential exempt under s.9(13) (TX2-01, TXI-05) (derived). An Israel Bond is a "נייר ערך" for capital gains (s.88: "לרבות איגרת חוב או מילווה של מדינת ישראל"), redemption is a sale, and for a foreign-currency security held by an individual the exchange rate is the index (TXF-03). A bond bought and redeemed at 100% of face value (FXD-19, FXD-20) therefore has no real gain; if the dollar fell, the currency loss cannot be offset (TXF-03; for the interest, TXI-25) (derived).
  - **Withholding.** The withholding regulations define "מילווה מדינה" as "איגרת חוב של מדינת ישראל", without a listing condition. A payer withholds 25% from interest paid to an individual, and 15% only on an asset not linked to the s.88 index (reg. 5(א), TXI-09); for a dollar bond the s.88 index is the exchange rate, so 25% (derived). Interest on a bond issued before 8 May 2000 is withheld at 35% (reg. 5(ב)), and interest exempt under the 1991 order or the 2004 regulations is not withheld (reg. 5(ג)). The State's offering memorandum for sales outside the United States says the bonds are paid "without deduction for or on account of any present or future taxes or duties imposed or levied by or within the State of Israel (other than where the Bond holder is subject to such taxes or duties for a reason other than the holding or ownership of the Bond ...)", and that the promoter understands that "payments of interest may be made by State of Israel without deduction of any withholding tax". So a resident paid abroad by the fiscal agent may receive the interest without Israeli tax withheld, and then reports it in an annual return (TXG-15 to TXG-17). Whether the State withholds when it pays a resident through the fiscal agent was not found (open question 19).
- Conditions / exceptions: Interest taxed under s.125C(b) carries no National Insurance or health contributions (TXG-27). At 60 or over the 25% is a maximum (TXI-19). Bonds issued before 8 May 2000: a capital gain on their sale or redemption is exempt (s.97(a)(2)); their interest has legacy rates (TXI-10). Transition of the 1998 amendment: it applies to income produced after it began, "אולם לגבי מילווה שקיבל תושב ישראל לפני תחילתו, יחול הצו העיקרי בנוסחו לפני התיקון", and the original s.2 exempted all interest on such a loan; how that sentence reads is not needed for bonds bought from 1998 on (open question 19). The Savings series compounds its interest and pays it only at maturity (FXD-20); in which year that interest is taxed was not found in a text (open question 19). A lead, not cited: an Israel Bonds instruction sheet on redeeming through the Bank of Israel (library b264487ac409542a) lists the Bank of Israel's withholding from a resident individual as 25% for bonds issued after 8 May 2000 and 35% before, and 0% for a foreign resident who signs a declaration, which matches regs. 5(א) to 5(ג). Who may buy, and how, is FXD-18 and FXD open question 10.
- Figures: up to 25% (s.125C(b), tax year 2026, fixed in law); withholding 25%, or 35% for bonds issued before 8.5.2000 (regs. 5(א), 5(ב), in force since 1.1.2012).

### TX2-23 Structured bonds and deposit certificates on the exchange: taxed as bonds; the rate follows the linkage of the whole face value
- Rule: A structured bond or a deposit certificate ("תעודת פיקדון") is a "תעודת התחייבות" (CORP-27), so it is a "נייר ערך" for capital gains (s.88: "כהגדרה 'ניירות ערך' בחוק ניירות ערך"). Interest on it follows s.125C, and its sale or redemption ("מכירה" includes "פדיון") follows s.91, as for any bond (TX2-06, TX2-07). Which rate applies turns on linkage:
  - **Linked**: only a bond "שכל ערכה הנקוב צמוד למלוא שיעור השינוי במדד המחירים לצרכן בלבד, או שכל ערכה צמוד למלוא שיעור השינוי בשער מטבע חוץ או שהיא נקובה במטבע חוץ" (circular 22/2002 s.2.2.2). Its real interest and real gain are taxed at up to 25%. For a unit denominated in or linked to a foreign currency the exchange rate is the index for an individual (s.88; TXF-03), so the gain in that currency is taxed and the currency's move is not (derived). Linkage above 100% still makes the bond linked, and the part above 100% counts as interest (circular, example 4).
  - **Not linked**: everything else. "איגרת חוב שערכה צמוד למדד מניות, תחשב כאיגרת חוב שאינה צמודה" (circular, example 5), and a bond linked only in part counts as not linked (s.91(b)(3)(c); s.125C(c)(1)). Its interest and its gain are taxed at 15% of the nominal amount: the whole gain counts as real and no inflationary amount is computed (s.91(b)(3)(a); circular s.2.3).
  - A payout that follows an index other than the CPI or a currency, or only part of a rise, is a partial linkage differential, which s.3(ה6) makes interest income, counting as an index "את מדד המחירים לצרכן, שער המטבע, לרבות הפרשי שער, או כל מדד אחר". On a bond that is not linked, such a payout is taxed at 15% whether it is read as interest or as gain, so the label changes the reporting line, not the rate (derived).
  - Applied (derived): a shekel deposit certificate, which passes on the interest of shekel deposits, is taxed at 15% of its nominal gain, so inflation is taxed too; a dollar deposit certificate whose whole value follows the dollar, or that is denominated in dollars, is taxed at up to 25% of the gain above the dollar's move, the move itself is exempt, and a loss caused by a fall of the dollar cannot be offset (TXF-03); a note that pays by a share index is taxed at 15% of its nominal gain.
- Conditions / exceptions: The bank or exchange member withholds at sale or redemption: 25% of the real gain, or 15% of the gain on a security that is not linked (TXS-05). A capital loss on such a bond is offset under s.92 (TXS-17), and interest on it can absorb a securities loss of the same year (TXI-17). In a redemption the discount fees are taken out of the price and taxed as interest (circular s.2.3; TXI-13). Certificates that track a securities index, commodities or futures are funds since s.35א1 (CORP-27), and the fund rules apply to them (TXS-07 to TXS-14), not this rule. A structured deposit at a bank is a deposit (TX2-05, TX2-18). The circular was written for the 2003 reform, when the rates were 15% and 10% and the sections were s.105יא onward (now ss.88 to 91); its test of linkage is the one s.91(b)(3)(c) still uses ("ערכם הנקוב או סכומם אינו צמוד למדד, או שהוא צמוד בחלקו"). Which bucket a given series falls in is in its own terms (CORP open question 17). No Tax Authority text names structured bonds or deposit certificates (open question 20).
- Figures: 15% of the nominal gain or interest (not linked; s.91(b)(3), s.125C(c)(1), changeable by order, TXG-32); up to 25% of the real gain or interest (linked). Tax year 2026.

### TX2-24 Israeli gold funds: the tax track each prospectus states (IBI, Kesem, Meitav)
- Rule: The tax column ("מיסוי", or Kesem's "קרן חייבת / פטורה ומאפייני מס") of three managers' main prospectuses, for the gold funds in RG-16:

  - Meitav's "תכלית סל (4D) LBMA Gold Price AM" (1144666): "פטורה, קרן להשקעות חוץ (מטבע עיקרי דולר ארה"ב)" (Meitav, p.61, row 82). Meitav's currency-hedged "תכלית TTF (4A) LBMA Gold Price AM מנוטרלת מט"ח" (5141627): "פטורה" (p.29, row 158).

Kesem's tax chapter explains the foreign-investment fund in general, but its table names no principal currency for any fund.
- Conditions / exceptions: What it means, by TXS-14 (derived): all six are exempt funds, so the saver pays up to 25% of the real gain on redemption and nothing inside the fund (TXS-09); units in shekels. A fund that breaks the 90% test for more than 10 days in a year stops being a foreign-investment fund for good (TXS-14, circular 7/2003). ## Left out and why

- Physical gold, bullion coins, VAT on gold: out of product scope (D2, Q9 restricted to funds).
- Non-residents' exemptions on FX deposits and Israeli bonds (s.9(15), 9(15D), guide p.52 section 3): the audience is Israeli residents.
- FX deposits from Nazi-persecution compensation and WWII disability pensions: narrow, noted in TX2-03 only.
- REIT fund-level taxation of retained income, short-term-held property and REIT qualification tests: fund facts, summarised only where they reach the saver.
- Convertible bonds, bonds of a company in which the saver is a substantial shareholder, and bonds held as business inventory: not a saver's path.
- Companies as holders.

## Proposed dimensions

- `payout-classification`: whether a product's return is taxed as interest, linkage, dividend or capital gain depends on its legal terms, not its marketing (structured deposits TX2-05, REIT distributions by source TX2-09, gold ETCs TX2-14). It decides the rate, the base (real or nominal) and whether losses can be offset.
