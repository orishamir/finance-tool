## Summary

A brokerage account in Israel is held at one of three provider classes: a bank that is a TASE member, a non-bank TASE member (an investment house's trading arm, plus one mutual-fund-only custodial member), or a foreign broker reached directly or through an Israeli company that holds an ISA permit under section 49A of the Securities Law and says it is not supervised by the ISA. TASE supervises its members through its rules; for banks it supervises only trading and conduct towards clients, for non-bank members also financial stability. Under TASE rules a non-bank member must hold client securities and cash apart from its own, in trust, at the TASE clearing house or at banks and approved custodians, with an annual independent audit. A bank's general terms (Leumi) say client securities are registered through the nominee company, deposited in the bank's name at the clearing house, and kept apart from the bank's own securities in its books but pooled with other clients'. No Israeli investor-compensation scheme was found in any source opened. For banks the Bank of Israel fixes the list of securities fee types (full tariff, part 4), bans custody fees on makam and money-market funds, supervises the price of a transfer to the client's own account elsewhere, and requires two weeks' notice of fee changes. TASE must publish every member's fees in a comparable form, and a member must tell a new client so. TASE says its clearing house pays interest and dividends gross; every bank and TASE member withholds tax on a sale, and a foreign broker withholds no Israeli tax (BRK-27; corrected 2026-10-26, the question was closed on 2026-10-25).

## Rules

### BRK-01 Three provider classes
- Rule: Trading on TASE is done only through TASE members; in TASE's own words, "המסחר בבורסה מתנהל באמצעות חברי הבורסה בלבד" (its periodic report). The TASE rules define the member classes: a member is a company incorporated and registered in Israel, or a first-rank international bank with an Israeli branch that is a foreign bank under the Banking (Licensing) Law ("בנק חוץ"), or a remote member ("חבר רחוק") (rules part 1, s.2); a "חש"ב" is a member that is not a banking corporation, such as a broker (s.1). A person may not offer securities-trading services through a trading system that is not run by an Israeli exchange unless the ISA chair permits it for a system run by an exchange outside Israel (section 49A), so a foreign broker reaches Israeli clients either with such a permit (directly or through an Israeli introducing company) or not at all under that section.
- Conditions / exceptions: The 49A permit is granted on terms the chair sets and only if the chair finds it does not harm the Israeli investing public. The ISA's Q&A answers the own-initiative case: the law does not forbid a person's approach, on his own sole initiative, to a foreign body for trading services, provided the body does not operate in Israel at all, has approached no one in Israel, and the service stays the one asked for (question 5); a body that operates in Israel cannot rely on the client's own initiative (question 6). The ISA publishes the list of permit holders (BRK-06). 2026-10-26 (scope settling): the member classes are now cited from the TASE rules (part 1, ss.1 to 2) instead of TASE's periodic report, which stays only for TASE's own sentence that trading runs through members (no rule sentence saying so was found in the rules part 1 or the part 3 trading guidelines).
- Figures: none.

### BRK-04 Who supervises which provider class
- Rule: TASE supervises its members' compliance with its rules and guidelines ("הפיקוח והבקרה על החברים יתייחסו לקיום תקנון הבורסה והנחיותיה", rules part 1 s.40). TASE's periodic report states how far that goes: for members that are banks, foreign banks and the remote member, the supervision covers fair and orderly trading and fair conduct towards clients in TASE securities only; their financial stability is supervised by other regulators and TASE does not supervise it; for Israeli non-bank members TASE also supervises financial stability and corporate governance. The ISA supervises TASE itself (sound management, stability of the clearing houses) under the Securities Law.
- Conditions / exceptions: Admission conditions for a non-bank member (rules part 1 s.5, pp.9 to 12) include fit-and-proper controlling owners and senior managers, a board with a majority of Israeli residents and an external director, equity under a capital model with a floor, liquid assets, insurance, and named officers. The sentence on the limit of TASE's supervision over bank members is TASE's own statement in its periodic report; no rule sentence saying it was found in the rules part 1 (chapter ו', ss.39 to 49). 2026-10-26 (scope settling): the equity floor is now cited from the guidelines (s.5ב) instead of TASE's English membership guide; the English foreign-bank guide was dropped (its p.4 says only that the ISA supervises TASE).
- Figures: non-bank member equity floor: the higher of the capital-model sum and NIS 17.9 million; until five years from the start of activity as a member, the higher of the capital-model sum and NIS 5.9 million (guidelines to part 1, s.5ב, updated to 1.1.2026). Fixed in TASE guidelines; changes by guideline amendment.

### BRK-06 Foreign brokers and foreign trading through an ISA 49A permit: not ISA-supervised, and the holder must say so
- Rule: Section 49א of the Securities Law bars offering trading services through a system not run by an exchange, unless the ISA chair permits it for a system run by an exchange outside Israel, "בתנאים שיקבע" (Amendment 63). The ISA's general permit conditions (new version, December 2023) list who may hold a permit: a banking corporation; a non-bank TASE member; an entity supervised as a US broker-dealer, an EU MiFID investment firm or credit institution, a UK-authorised firm or a Swiss bank; or a licensed investment adviser or marketer that routes through one of these (alternative B, (1)(a) to (d)). A permit holder that tells anyone it holds the permit must give a disclosure in a fixed form; for alternative B: "The Israel Securities Authorities permit under Section 49A to the Securities Law was issued on the basis of the permit holder's declaration that it is subject to the supervision of ____________. Said entity's activity is not subject to the supervision of the Israel Securities Authority. This permit does not constitute an opinion regarding the quality of the services rendered by the permit holder or the risks that such services entail." Failure to disclose "may lead to the revocation" of the permit. The ISA's Q&A confirms that the permit text fixes the disclosure wording and that it must appear wherever the holder mentions the permit (question 12), and that a foreign broker that executes for a permit holder's clients needs no permit of its own where the contract is between the permit holder and its clients (question 7).
- Conditions / exceptions: The binding Hebrew conditions (new version, 12 December 2023, in force from 11 February 2024) require a **prominent** disclosure ("גילוי בולט"), which the English translation omits; for alternative B: "היתר הרשות לפי סעיף 49א לחוק ניירות ערך ניתן בהסתמך על הצהרת בעל ההיתר כי הוא נתון לפיקוח של __________. פעילות בעל ההיתר אינה מפוקחת על-ידי רשות ניירות ערך. אין בהיתר משום הבעת דעה על טיבו של השירות הניתן בידי בעל ההיתר או על הסיכונים הכרוכים בו", and "אי מתן גילוי כאמור יהווה הפרה של תנאי ההיתר הכללי ועלול להביא בין היתר לשלילתו" (p.7). A holder re-declares every three years and reports at once if a condition stops holding (p.8). The ban reaches anyone who markets trading services to investors in Israel, including for another body that holds a permit (p.3). The Q&A defines an offer as "כל פנייה לאדם (לרבות תאגיד) או שיווק בישראל, במישרין או בעקיפין", including e-mail, agents, the internet and social networks (question 5), and says a group's permitted brokers may not market "האפשרות לפתוח חשבון בגוף הזר" at a group company without a permit (same answer). The ISA publishes the list of permit holders; the file keeps its 2023 name, was last replaced in August 2025 (latest permit date in it 21.01.2025) and prints no "as of" date; the conditions say it covers every holder except those who approach qualified investors only. The English translation stays as a secondary reading. Two permit holders' own statements are BRK-46. Interactive Brokers LLC and Interactive Brokers (U.K.) Limited hold permits (BRK-43); Interactive Israel's company and Bank Yahav are on the list too (BRK-46). Split on 2026-10-25 (scope pass): the companies' part is BRK-46.

### BRK-07 Non-bank member: client securities held apart, in trust, at the clearing house
- Rule: A non-bank member must hold its clients' financial assets separately from its own ("חש"ב יחזיק את הנכסים הפיננסים של לקוחותיו בנפרד מהנכסים הפיננסים שלו", guidelines s.26ג(1), p.96) and keep a separate account for each client ("חבר ינהל חשבון נפרד לכל לקוח", rules s.25ו, p.34). Client assets traded on TASE are held in an account at the TASE clearing house in the member's name, in trust for its clients. Assets traded abroad are held at foreign clearing houses or third-party custodians, in the member's name in trust for clients or in a client-designated account, so that clients' ownership can be identified.
- Conditions / exceptions: Foreign holdings must be in a country with an "appropriate arrangement" ("הסדרה ראויה") for custody. If a client instructs holding in a country without one, the member must report it before acting, warn that the foreign law may not give the same protection of ownership, and get written confirmation; this does not apply to a qualified client ("לקוח כשיר"). The member may not create liens or offset rights over client assets except with written client consent or by law, or for custody fees and the cost of the transaction itself; it may, however, pledge a client's securities portfolio to itself when it guarantees the client or gives the client credit, as the rules provide (guidelines s.26ג(4)). The board sets a client-asset holding policy (custodian selection, rating, legal liability) reviewed at least yearly; an independent auditor checks segregation at least once a year and the member reports to TASE. *2026-10-25 rule-text pass:* the quote now spells "הפיננסים" as both the rules and the guidelines do (it read "הפיננסיים"); the text was re-read in the current consolidated guide and guidelines (updated to 1.1.2026), so the remark that only the 2018 amendment had been read no longer applies; the pledge for credit or a guarantee (s.26ג(4)) was added.

### BRK-08 Non-bank member: client cash
- Rule: A non-bank member may hold client cash only at a banking corporation, at a foreign financial institution supervised for deposit-taking in its country, or at a third-party custodian holding the client's financial assets for activity in them; in accounts in the member's name in trust for clients; separately from its own money; free of any lien, charge or offset of a third party. The member reports to the client the interest earned on the client's cash, and may not lend client cash to a third party.
- Conditions / exceptions: Despite the ban, a member may lend the cash of a client who manages their own portfolio ("לקוח המנהל את תיק ניירות הערך שלו בעצמו") to other clients for buying financial assets, but only with that client's explicit prior written approval given separately from the account-opening agreement. Cash may sit in a pooled trust account at a bank or a foreign financial institution under the same rule (s.26ה(1)(א)-(ב)). 2026-10-25 (scope pass): one member's tariff page, which only restated this, was dropped as a source; the rule is the guidelines'.

### BRK-10 Custody of client securities at a bank and at every TASE member: the custodian's duties
- Rule: The inter-ministerial committee on custody services (ISA, Ministry of Finance, BoI, Tax Authority) published its final recommendations in January 2012: a custodian's basic duties are "הבטחת זכויות הבעלות בנכסים ובמזומנים של הלקוח והבטחת הזכויות הנלוות לבעלות"; "הפרדת נכסים, רישום ותיעוד, דרישות לביצוע התאמות ודרישות דיווח – ... חובות שתכליתן לאפשר זיהוי בנפרד של נכסי הלקוחות"; criteria and a contract for any third-party custodian; liability set by contract; an independent external audit at least once a year. Securities are held in separate accounts "על מנת להבטיח שמירת רישומי הבעלות והפרדת נכסי הלקוח עצמו מנכסי הקסטודיאן". On 16.1.2013 the Supervisor of Banks adopted the recommendations for every bank that gives custody ("תאגידים בנקאיים המעניקים שירותי קסטודי ללקוחות יידרשו לעמוד בסטנדרטים גבוהים"), with a binding Q&A ("עמדתו ופרשנותו המחייבת של המפקח על הבנקים") on client cash at a foreign third-party custodian, selecting and monitoring third-party custodians (a high custody rating or an investment-grade credit rating expected), a presumption of "הסדרה ראויה" in the US, EU MiFID countries and similar OECD countries, disclosure of the bank's custodian-selection policy (recommendation 31) and the external audit (recommendation 35). Directive 461, "פעילות תאגיד בנקאי כברוקר דילר" (07/23), says the broker-dealer framework complements "גילוי נאות, דרישות דיווח והנחיות הפיקוח על הבנקים בעניין שירותי קסטודי" (s.6). Under the TASE rules every member, banks included, keeps "חשבון נפרד לכל לקוח" (s.25ו), and every member's client agreement must include "הוראות לענין החזקה על ידי חבר עבור לקוח של נכסים פיננסים" (s.29ג(6)). Listed securities are registered through the TASE nominee company and held for clearing members (BRK-13).

Split on 2026-10-25 (scope pass): the bank's part is BRK-47.

### BRK-12 The TASE clearing house: central counterparty, risk fund, member failure

### BRK-16 Banks: the full tariff fixes the securities fee types (part 4)
- Rule: A bank's full tariff for clients is as set out in the first schedule of the Banking (Service to Customer) (Fees) Rules 2008. Part 4 ("חלק 4 – ניירות ערך") lists, as common services: buy, sell and redemption of TASE securities (excluding mutual funds, Maof options and futures, and including orders not executed), priced as a percentage with minimum and maximum, separately for shares and bonds and for makam; Maof options; Maof futures; buy, sell and redemption of securities abroad (shares, bonds and mutual funds; warrants); "דמי ניהול פיקדון ניירות ערך (לרבות יחידות השתתפות בקרנות נאמנות)" for securities traded in Israel, traded abroad and not traded, each priced as a percentage with a maximum; transfer of securities; conversion of bonds and options and of dual-listed securities. As special services: handling an order in an offering; distribution fee charged to a buyer of mutual fund units "in cases permitted by" the 2006 distribution-fee regulations; securities lending for short sale; custodian fee ("עמלת קסטודיאן"). Part 5 covers foreign currency, including the conversion fee ("עמלת חליפין", per a breakdown the bank sets, not more than 2 fees).
- Conditions / exceptions: Notes to part 4(א)(1): a split order pays the minimum once per day per order; an order not executed for a reason on the client's side pays the minimum only if placed through a clerk; the fee also applies to non-traded securities; an action done directly online gets a reduced rate. A bank may charge only for services in the full tariff, or services added with the Supervisor's approval (BANK-16; open question 10 closed 2026-10-22).

### BRK-52 Non-bank TASE members: no rule limits their custody fee on makam and money-market units
- Rule: The ban on a custody fee for makam and money-market funds is in the Banking (Customer Service) (Fees) Rules and binds banks only (BRK-17). The distribution-fee regulations, which bar any fee on money-market units held under a distribution agreement (reg. 2(ד), MF-26), bind only a "מפיץ", which they define as "מפיץ שאינו משווק השקעות כהגדרתו בחוק הסדרת העיסוק" (reg. 1); a distributor that is an investment marketer receives a distribution fee under the law instead (s.82(א)(2)). The regulators' joint report of June 2026 (the Supervisor of Banks, the Ministry of Finance and the Securities Authority) says that "מרבית החש"בים מחזיקים ברישיון שיווק" (p.13), that most of them have no distribution agreements with fund managers and are outside the distribution-fee regulations (p.11), and that for non-bank members "לא קיימת הסדרה מקבילה ונדרשת חקיקה" (p.3). No rule read limits a non-bank member's custody fee on makam or money-market units; it follows the member's tariff and client agreement (BRK-24).
- Conditions / exceptions: Not binding: the joint report describes the market and recommends; it binds no one. It also describes practice: at non-bank members a handling fee ("עמלת דמי טיפול בפיקדון ני"ע") is usually a fixed shekel amount, often refunded or not charged, and for custody ("דמי משמרת") "קיים פטור גורף עבור לקוחות החש"בים" (p.11); most non-bank members charge buy and sell commissions on units of active and passive funds instead of a distribution fee (p.11, MF-27). Whether a given member collects its printed custody line on these units is that member's own term (four tariffs: BRK-48). Not known: which non-bank members hold no marketing licence, and whether any of them holds a distribution agreement for a money-market fund (then reg. 2(ד) would bar any fee there). The regulators' press release of 24.6.2026 proposes keeping the banks' distribution-fee model for money-market funds and extending it to the whole market later (a recommendation, not in force). A customer without an online channel receives the periodic information by the means he chose for notices (s.8). The form itself warns that the return is right for money that stayed in the deposit for the whole period, so with large deposits and withdrawals it may not match the gain or loss in shekels (p.5). No equivalent duty was read for a non-bank member (not checked: TASE member rules on this subject).
- Figures: end of the following month; 28 February; two previous years.
- Source: [הוראת ניהול בנקאי תקין 460: הצגת נתוני פעילות בפיקדון ניירות ערך, גרסה 2 (06/22)](https://www.boi.org.il/media/rauj0a4n/460.pdf), p.2, ss.4-8 "שיעור התשואה יוצג בהתאם למספר הימים שבחר הלקוח"; p.3, s.10; p.5, appendix · checked 2026-10-31 · primary
- Scope: general
- Dimensions: `reporting-obligation`, `fee-trade`, `fee-custody`
- Likely to change: possible; first issued December 2021, updated June 2022.

### BRK-55 Banks: no current-account fee on an account kept only to serve your securities account; prepaid fees come back pro rata
- Rule: Bank of Israel proper-conduct directive 415 "נהלים בנושא עמלות": a bank may not charge current-account management fees on a "חשבון תמורה" with no activity other than what directly serves the customer's other accounts; that is a current account kept from the start only for the customer's other accounts, or one that has become so once the customer draws the bank's attention to it (s.2(ג)). A fee for a service charged in advance for a set period, such as quarterly management fees, is refunded pro rata by the day if the service stops before the period ends (s.2(ז)(1)). A fee for an account of a deposit with no activity may not exceed the deposit account's credit balance, though the bank may sell investments it holds for the customer so that no debit arises (s.2(ה)). No fee for giving the information the disclosure rules require (s.2(ד)).
- Conditions / exceptions: Binds banks and credit-card companies (s.1(א)). A bank may set reasonable minimum fees that stay payable when a service stops early, only with the Supervisor's prior approval (s.2(ז)(2)). A recording of a telephone instruction is not basic information (s.2(ד)).
- Figures: none.
- Source: [הוראת ניהול בנקאי תקין 415: נהלים בנושא עמלות, גרסה 6 (1/08)](https://www.boi.org.il/media/n2dlnqyf/415.pdf), p.1, s.2(ג) "חשבון עובר ושב המנוהל מלכתחילה רק לצורך חשבונות אחרים של הלקוח"; p.2, s.2(ה), s.2(ז) · checked 2026-10-31 · primary
- Scope: general
- Dimensions: `fee-custody`, `fee-trade`
- Likely to change: no; last updated 2008.

### BRK-56 Banks: fees and tax on foreign securities can be paid in shekels unless you chose otherwise
- Rule: Bank of Israel proper-conduct directive 437 "חיוב במס הכנסה ובעמלות במטבע חוץ": a bank may not debit a customer's foreign-currency deposits for amounts it could also debit to a shekel account, unless the customer himself chose that, and it must let customers pay both the tax and the fees in shekels (s.3). The fees meant include those on foreign securities held in custody (s.2).
- Conditions / exceptions: Binds banks. It says who pays in which currency, not at what exchange rate. The annex quotes a 1986 Income Tax booklet with a withholding rate that belongs to that year; it is not a current rate.
- Figures: none.
- Source: [הוראת ניהול בנקאי תקין 437: חיוב במס הכנסה ובעמלות במטבע חוץ, גרסה 2 (12/95)](https://www.boi.org.il/media/qlojflzh/437.pdf), p.1, s.3 "יש לאפשר ללקוחות לשלם הן את המס והן את העמלות במטבע ישראלי" · checked 2026-10-31 · primary
- Scope: general
- Dimensions: `fee-fx`, `fee-custody`
- Likely to change: no.

### BRK-57 Joint accounts at a bank: the survivor clause lets the other holder keep operating, but moves no ownership
- Rule: Banking Ordinance s.13א, quoted in Bank of Israel proper-conduct directive 434: where money, securities or other negotiable documents are held in a joint account with a term that on the death of a holder the surviving holder may go on acting in the account, the term stands between the bank and the holders after the death (s.2). It protects the bank's following the survivor's instructions and does not change the heirs' rights (s.3). The directive calls the opposite belief mistaken: the term cannot by itself move assets on the day of death, because the joint-account forms "אינן צוואה" (s.4). The bank must offer the term clearly when a joint account opens, explain what having it or not means and that it does not change ownership between the holders or their heirs (s.7). Where holders sign jointly and severally the bank proposes the term; where they sign only together it obtains their choice; either way the choice is a condition of the contract and is documented (ss.8, 11).
- Conditions / exceptions: Binds banks. A change of signing rights or a new holder reopens the choice (ss.9-10), and the bank was to approach existing holders who had not chosen it (s.14). After a death the bank tells the survivors which payment means of the deceased are linked to the account and how to settle their charges (s.12). The directive says nothing about tax on a joint account or about non-bank members. What companies' terms say on joint accounts stays each company's (BRK open question 17).
- Figures: none.
- Source: [הוראת ניהול בנקאי תקין 434: חשבונות משותפים, תנאי "היוותרות בחיים", גרסה 3 (6/23)](https://www.boi.org.il/media/a4nkg54d/434.pdf), p.1, ss.2-4 "מאחר שההוראות שבטופסי חשבון משותף אינן צוואה"; p.2, ss.7-11 · checked 2026-10-31 · primary
- Scope: general
- Dimensions: `reporting-obligation`
- Likely to change: no.

## Proposed dimensions

The vocabulary allows `fee-<type>`; these types are used above and are proposed for `data/dimensions/`:

- `fee-account`: flat monthly account or handling fee ("דמי טיפול", "דמי שימוש", "דמי ניהול תקופתיים").
- `fee-data`: real-time quotes or advanced trading systems.
- `fee-income-collection`: fee on dividend, coupon or redemption payments.
- `fee-third-party`: pass-through charges by foreign brokers, exchanges or regulators (SEC, TAF, ORF, ECN, ADR custody).
- `fee-interest-on-cash`: interest paid, or not paid, on idle cash in the account (a cost by omission).

## Left out and why

- Tariff figures per provider: data-researcher's job; figures appear above only where they are a regulatory cap (BRK-18), a regulator's count, or an account minimum.
- Derivatives, short selling, margin credit and options lines in the tariffs: derivatives are out of scope (product.md D2).
- Portfolio management, investment advice and robo services offered alongside brokerage: `managed-and-robo-portfolios.md`.
- Order types, trading phases and closing-auction mechanics: no source read showed them changing cost for a buy-and-hold saver; TASE trading guide not opened (Q21).
- Business and large-business tariffs, non-resident tax certificate fees, ESOP and corporate services: not relevant to the audience.
- Foreign banks' Israeli branches (Citi, HSBC) as brokers: retail relevance not established.
- Crypto brokers and leveraged FX/CFD platforms (זירות סוחר, Securities Law s.44יב onward): out of scope (D2).
- Deposit insurance and the general "what is protected" picture: `regulators-and-protection.md`.
