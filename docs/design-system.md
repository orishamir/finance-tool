# Design system

The look of the site and the pieces every page is built from. The tokens live in
`site/app/theme.css`; the shared components are the kit in `site/app/components/kit/`, shown in their states in the gallery at
`/kit` (section 11). The guidelines (`guidelines/`, above all `design.md` and `charts.md`) hold over this document.

- Section 1: the principles the product imposes on the visual design.
- Section 2: the tokens (colour, dark only; type, spacing, shape, motion), the layout patterns, and the conventions for numbers,
  icons and copy.
- Section 3: the site-wide pieces: the shell, provenance, inputs, the guide pages, companies and search, the reference pages and the
  primitives.
- Sections 6 to 9: the pieces of single features: the first page (6); fees and the transfer file (7); banks and investment
  houses (8); the not-covered page (9).
- Section 10: the guide-page visuals. Section 11: the kit. Section 12: the rules every chart follows.

Section numbers are ids that the code and other docs cite; 4 and 5 are not used, nor the ids of pieces that were removed.

No financial figure appears here. Where a component shows a number, it shows a value object through the provenance component (3.2).

## 1. Principles

| # | Principle | From |
|---|---|---|
| V1 | **Provenance is the visual language.** Every number shows whether it is sourced, computed or entered by you, and one tap shows where it came from. Stale, secondary-source and blocked states are visible in place, never only in a footnote. | guidelines/design.md, D3, D7, D10 |
| V2 | **Both screen sizes.** Every screen is checked at 1440 px and 390 px before it goes live. Touch targets at least 44 px. Nothing depends on hover. | guidelines/design.md, product.md §2, K7 |
| V3 | **Right to left throughout.** Layout, reading order, disclosure and navigation flow from the right. Exceptions: every chart axis runs left to right, not only time (gap 8.6, guidelines/charts.md), and so does every slider, like a chart's time axis (guidelines/design.md, "Fields"), while a bar inside a table row may grow from the right with its Hebrew text; numbers, identifiers and Latin names are isolated left-to-right runs. | guidelines/project.md, guidelines/design.md, architecture 3.5 |
| V4 | **Inputs sit next to the result they change.** Every graph and computed number shows the inputs it uses, each editable on the same page. | product.md §2 "interactive by default", K8 |
| V5 | **Calm and neutral.** Warm dark grey, light text, one accent. No bank or manager brand colours, no promotional look, no colour that suggests a recommendation the site has not made. | product.md §2 "neutral by construction" |
| V6 | **Colour never works alone.** Every state also has a pattern, an icon or a word, so it reads in sunlight, in greyscale and to a colour-blind friend. | dataviz method |
| V7 | **Polish is a requirement.** Motion inside a chart mark when values change (a bar grows, a line glides), and the layout moves as little as it can (2.5a); careful type; charts drawn by us. | K12 |
| V8 | **A page answers why a person came to it.** Each page, section, column and visual helps the choice its reader came to make; one that helps none, or a second page for a question another page already answers, is left out. A page about one thing does not bring in another: one company's page does not set its tracks among every company's, and no page carries a goal (copy 2). A need one page leaves unmet is a fault only where the site does not meet it, or the way there is long, since a page is read in the context of the site. A template of sections is a list to choose from, not a form to fill: each page keeps what says something about its own subject. | guidelines/design.md |

## 2. Tokens and layout

### 2.1 Tiers and where they live

`site/app/theme.css`, imported by `site/app/app.css`.

1. **Palette** (`--palette-<hue>-<step>`): raw oklch ramps. Hue ramps share one lightness ladder (step 50 is 0.975 … step 950 is 0.24), so the same step behaves the same in every hue. Components never use these.
2. **Semantic roles** (`--background`, `--entered`, `--chart-fee`, …): what a colour means, one value each, since the site is dark only. Surface and text names follow shadcn/ui (`--background`, `--card`, `--muted-foreground`, `--primary`, `--ring`, …) so components copied from it work unchanged.
3. **Utilities**: `@theme inline` maps each role to a Tailwind colour (`bg-card`, `text-entered`, `border-input`, `fill-chart-fee`). Tailwind's default palette is removed (`--color-*: initial`), so a class can only name a role.

Rules:

- Components use role utilities or `var(--role)`, never a palette step and never a literal colour.
- **Dark only.** There is no light theme and no theme switch. `color-scheme: dark` on the root makes native controls, a phone's scrollbars and the popover backdrop dark too; a computer's scrollbars are drawn by the site (`scrollbar-thumb`). The palette keeps its light steps, unused.
- Layout uses logical properties and utilities only (`ps-`, `pe-`, `ms-`, `me-`, `inset-s-`, `inset-e-`, `border-s`, `rounded-s-`, `text-start`). The `rtl:` variant is for true exceptions such as mirroring an icon. A physical class (`ml-`, `pr-`, `left-`, `text-right`, `rounded-l…`, `border-r…`) stands only in a chart's drawing, whose axes read left to right, and kit guard 26 fails one anywhere else; `left-1/2` with `-translate-x-1/2` centres, which has no direction, and passes.

**A chart field's value is plain (guidelines/charts.md).** A chart's or calculator's field (an amount, years, a return) starts at a default the reader can change. That default, and what the reader types in its place, is a plain number wherever it shows: in the sentence that repeats it, in a calculation sheet's input row, on a chip. No site-parameter tag, no blue, no "הזנתם", and it opens nothing (the one blue is a slider's own number, below, which is the control's ink, the same for the default and a typed value); the field beside it is where it changes, and the field's own reset, at the far end of its label line, brings the default back. ### 2.4 Typography

**Faces.** Inter for Latin letters and digits, Heebo for Hebrew, both variable fonts, self-hosted through `@fontsource-variable` (no font CDN, D1). Stack: `"Inter Variable", "Inter Fallback", "Heebo Variable", "Heebo Fallback", Arial, sans-serif`. Inter comes first because it has no Hebrew, so Hebrew falls through to Heebo glyph by glyph; while the web fonts load (`font-display: swap`) each has a fallback face of its own that holds the layout. The shekel sign comes from Heebo. Only Inter's Latin subset and Heebo's Hebrew subset are loaded (own `@font-face`), about 60 KB for a Hebrew page, both preloaded in the HTML head.

**Fallback faces.** Each web font has a face on the local Arial, matched to its metrics and limited to its own characters (`unicode-range`), so the swap moves the text as little as possible: "Inter Fallback" for Latin and digits, "Heebo Fallback" for Hebrew. Heebo's is `size-adjust: 105.4%` (a Hebrew text as wide in it, at body weight, as in Heebo) with Heebo's ascent and descent (1.048 and 0.421 of the size, written divided by the size-adjust, which scales them: `ascent-override: 99.4%`, `descent-override: 39.9%`, `line-gap-override: 0%`). Measured in Chromium and WebKit on a paragraph of body text 38rem wide: plain Arial laid it out in 5 lines where Heebo takes 6 (27 px shorter at line height 1.7, 49 px at `normal`); the fallback face takes the same 6 lines at the same tops and heights, with each line's width within about 20 px of Heebo's. Safari ignores the three overrides and keeps `size-adjust`: there the lines still match and the text sits under a pixel lower until the swap. Anything outside both ranges falls to plain Arial.

Why this pair: Heebo is clear on small screens and matches Inter's proportions; Inter's digits are made for interfaces, with tabular figures and a slashed zero, which matter on a site made of numbers and fund numbers.

**Scale** (Hebrew reads smaller than Latin at the same size, so the scale starts at 16 px for body and runs taller line heights):

|---|---|---|---|
| `text-xs` | 13 px | 1.5 | captions, axis ticks, source dates; the smallest Hebrew on the site |
| `text-xs-flow` | 13 px | the text's around it | the same size with no line height of its own: a count, a source or a hint inside a line or a box whose spacing its surroundings set |
| `text-sm` | 14 px | 1.6 | secondary text, field labels, table cells on a phone, tags |
| `text-base` | 16 px | 1.7 | body, inputs (16 px also stops iOS from zooming into a field) |

**Weights.** 450 body (a half step heavier reads better on large screens; text with an explicit `font-normal` stays at 400); 500 labels, tags and emphasis; 600 headings and headline numbers; 700 not used. One weight per role.

**Root size.** The scale above holds at the default root of 16 px; from 1600 px wide the root is 17 px and from 2200 px it is 18 px, so every rem-based size grows by those steps. Text size always comes from the scale, never an arbitrary `text-[13px]`, so all of it grows together.

**Hebrew rules.**

- Never letter-space Hebrew; the tracking utilities are removed. Word spacing is 0.05em on body text.
- No italics for emphasis (Hebrew has no true italic); use weight 500.
- Prose is capped at `max-w-measure` (38rem). `ch` measures a Latin zero, so check real Hebrew line lengths in the browser.
- `dir="auto"` on any text the person typed or pasted (catalogue search, uncatalogued names), per element, never on a list wrapper.

**Numbers.** Every number uses `tabular-nums` so values do not jitter while they animate to a new value and columns align. Identifiers (fund numbers, ISINs, security numbers, rule IDs, open-question IDs) also use `slashed-zero`. A headline figure keeps its size: where it does not fit beside its label it moves under the label rather than shrinking.

### 2.5 Spacing and layout

- **Base unit 4 px** (Tailwind's `--spacing: 0.25rem`): `p-1` is 4 px, `p-4` is 16 px. Use steps 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 12, 16.
- **Named spacing:** `px-gutter` (page side padding, `clamp(1rem, 4vw, 2rem)`: 16 px on a phone, up to 32 px), `gap-section` / `my-section` (between page sections, 40 to 64 px), `size-target` / `min-h-target` (44 px touch target), `h-field` (48 px fields and main buttons). **A tap area is what a finger hits, not what the control draws:** a control that must draw shorter than 44 px (an icon button, a chip, a link in a strip or a footer, a chart's legend on a computer) takes the `tap-area` utility, a ::after area 44 px tall centred on it that keeps the look; never on a link inside running text, whose area would cover the lines above and below. A named control narrower than 44 px with room on both sides (a short word such as "ביטול") takes `tap-wide`, whose area is 44 px wide as well; a control beside a field or another control keeps `tap-area`, so no two areas overlap. A block of short lines whose figures often stand alone among their words (a deal's reading) takes `nums-wide`, which makes each figure's area 44 px wide as well as tall, centred on it; each figure's own box still wins over every area. A field's input covers its box's border, so the box's 44 or 48 px is all input, and it lies on the layer of a control that stands alone, over the area of a term or a figure in the hint under it; a field's reset takes its 44 px upward from the box's top edge, so neither covers the box. A figure's or a month's box (`FigureField`, `MonthField`) passes a press anywhere in it to its field, and the hit check measures it by the box. Kit guard 22 fails any fixed height under 44 px on a button, link, summary or field without one of these, and `tools/hit-check.mjs` measures what a finger hits on the built page (not the drawn boxes, which would list rule chips whose areas are already 48 px and miss the short ones a finger meets, such as a field's 42 px input and the links in the fee page's strip).
- **Widths:** `max-w-wide` (96rem) is the page frame, the same on every page and set only by `PageShell` (one width on every page, so the header does not move from page to page); `max-w-measure` (38rem) for prose inside it. What fills the room is the page's own: card grids and lists take more columns, two halves stand side by side (the transfer page), prose keeps its measure. A guard in `kit/guards.test.ts` keeps any page from narrowing its frame.
- **Breakpoints:** Tailwind defaults. Phone is the base style. `md` (48rem): tables become real tables (a list of products is one from `md`, section 11); cards go two across. `lg` (64rem): guide pages get a side section index at the start (right) and the timeline's inputs sit beside the chart. From `lg` the header shows its five sections inline.
- **Two-column pages.** Every page grid that sets columns in `fr` writes them as `minmax(0, …fr)` (`lg:grid-cols-[minmax(0,62fr)_minmax(0,38fr)]`, `[minmax(0,3fr)_minmax(0,2fr)]`, `[minmax(0,1fr)_300px]`, `[360px_minmax(0,1fr)]`): a plain `fr` column grows to its widest content, and a wide table opened inside it pushed the page wider than the screen. A wide table scrolls inside its own `overflow-x-auto` box.
- **Order and room follow importance.** The main thing comes first and takes the room; a side matter goes last, or folded (the choice between tracks is a side matter, so it goes last; the rules page opens with the rules, not the questions). Size reads as importance, so what a person must act on is never the smallest thing on the screen, nor a tiny box for a long list, which would read as less significant. The first screen of a page answers its question and draws the person in, never a row of doors in front of the answer, and never half empty on a wide screen. No loose gaps: fields and blocks keep the rhythm below, with no empty band between two rows of one form, and a card is as tall and as wide as what it holds, never stretched to its neighbour's height or a full row for a narrow column of facts.
- **A control sits above what it changes.** A control that adds or removes fields goes above what it changes, so the control itself never moves when pressed; `tools/jump-check.mjs` presses every chip, switch, tab and disclosure on the main pages and lists the ones that move.
- **Hit areas.** A number, a rule chip, a glossary term and the words a rule states each carry a transparent 44 px `::after` hit area. A number, a term or rule words inside running text keep the line spacing, and its area shares the lines above and below. A control that stands alone (a rule chip, a value pencil, a figure in a readout row) holds a 44 px band of its own: an area drawn over a neighbour is covered by the neighbour's own box (layered at 2, above every area), so a 44 px area in a 27 px line measures 36 to 42 px. The rule chip and the input slot carry vertical margins that make their line 44 px, and a readout row is at least 44 px. Areas are layered: figures and terms at 0, the areas of controls that stand alone at 1, every own box at 2. `tools/hit-check.mjs --checks` measures what a finger can hit on every guide page and on the fee page opened on two kinds at 390 and 1280, and runs with the movement check; the leftovers are listed by name in `hit-leftovers.json`, a list that only shrinks. On a screen shorter than 500 px the sticky bars (chip bars, table heads, the bottom bar) become static. While a sheet is open the page behind does not scroll (`scrollbar-gutter: stable` on `html` so the desktop does not shift).
- **Rhythm inside a section:** 16 px between paragraphs, 24 px between blocks (a chart and its inputs, a table and its note), `section-gap` between sections.

### 2.5a Stable layout

**A preference, not a rule.** Prefer that changing a number moves little on the screen, and use the patterns below where they cost nothing; a small shift is better than a blank gap kept for a case that rarely shows, and no change needs a movement check. The aim it points to: moving a slider, picking an option, typing a number or switching an index changes the figures and nothing else on the screen: no column in view widens, no row or block in view grows or shrinks, no line in view wraps differently, and the part of the page under the person's finger stays where it was. The control the hand is on holds; a list it re-sorts may slide below it. A block out of sight at that moment, such as a note far below the slider, may change its own height; the movement check (`tools/box-check.mjs`) brings each control onto the screen and measures only the boxes in view. One exception (D62): switching index on a phone may move what lies below the switch, with a short, gentle transition instead of a jump; a list keeps one height across the states and plans of the index it shows, not across indices.

**A mark is never alone on a line.** A small mark before or after words (a "?", a caution icon, a lock, a link's arrow) is held to the nearest word: `LeadTail` for a mark after a text, `MarkHead` for one before it (both in `provenance.tsx`); a figure holds its own icons. Punctuation is held the same way: a full stop, a comma or a closing bracket stays on the line of the word, chip or figure before it, and an opening bracket on the line of what it opens. A sentence that ends on a chip (`IdRef`, `RuleRef`) passes its full stop, comma, colon or semicolon to the chip's `end`, which holds it on the chip's line right after its edge, and a chip in text takes no brackets, being its own frame: "פרטים ." and "( פרטים )." read as stray marks (kit guard 20). **Nothing out of line, nothing cut.** Pieces of one row share one top line, a word stays beside its figure, a name in the header does not wrap, and no box clips its text. `tools/alone-check.mjs` finds any mark, and any full stop, comma or bracket, alone at the start or end of a line on every page at four widths, and runs before a change to a head, a link, a figure or a sentence that ends in a chip merges.

**Check.** `tools/pick-check.mjs` taps every row of a page's linked lists and reads where each list's top stands. A builder may run the movement check (`tools/box-check.mjs`), which sets each input to several values (the choices, a slider's ends, a large typed number) and compares the boxes of the parts that must stay (column widths, block heights, the tapped row's top); the result goes in the builder's report. A large shift, or one with no reason, is a finding, fixed in the kit piece, not on the page; a small shift is better than a blank gap kept for a rare case (guidelines/design.md, "Little movement").

### 2.6 Shape, elevation, focus, motion

- **Radius:** `rounded-sm` 4 px (small tags), 6 px (a `Tag`'s `roomy` shape, the label chips, the rule chip), `rounded-md` 8 px (fields, buttons, menu chips, the chips that add a thing), 10 px (the chips that choose one or follow a link: `sec-chip`, `a-chip`), `rounded-lg` 12 px (cards, popovers), `rounded-xl` 20 px (top corners of a bottom sheet), `rounded-full` only for what is round by nature (radio dots, the switch and its knob, slider thumbs, round icon buttons, dots, thin bars and lines, the "?" circle, step numbers).
- **Chips:** a thing you tap has a soft fill and no outline: `secondary`, one step lighter (`secondary-hover`) under a computer's pointer, nothing that depends on the hover. A chosen chip has a `primary-subtle` fill, `primary` words at semibold, a 1 px ring of `primary` at 45% inside its edge, and a check before its words (the guide pages' section strip, which marks where the reader is rather than a choice, has no check). A chip with no border that still needs an edge (a choice that found nothing, the place a lifted chip left) takes a dashed `input` outline inside its edge (`chip-dashed`).
- **Elevation:** surfaces get lighter as they rise (`background`, `card`, `popover`) and a 1 px `border` marks their edges. Shadow (`shadow-lg`) only under sheets and popovers, to lift them off the page.
- **Focus:** a 2 px `ring` outline offset by 2 px on `:focus-visible`, on every control. A figure's box (`FigureField`, `MonthField`) with the focus in it has one frame instead: its own edge turned `ring` with a 1 px outline flush on it, never an edge inside a ring with a gap (a box in error keeps `destructive` in that frame).
- **Motion:** `--duration-fast` 120 ms (press, toggles), `--duration-base` 200 ms (sheets, popovers, a chip's colour), `--duration-fold` 280 ms (every fold opens and closes by height and fades, in the shared stylesheet, and its chevron turns with it), easing `ease-standard`. Only `transform` and `opacity` animate, and height for a fold. A chart and its numbers move in `--duration-chart`, which every chart reads (its CSS transitions and the kit's glide): it is `--duration-chart-change` 400 ms for a one-off change (a new option chosen), and `--duration-chart-follow` 220 ms while the hand is on a slider (held or keyed) or a text field has the focus, so the chart keeps up with the hand; a press on a chip, a select or a switch takes the focus from the field, so its change moves in 400 ms. When the device asks for reduced motion every movement is zero, and a chart's labels, gridlines and lines fade in and out in `--duration-chart-fade`, 150 ms there (otherwise the chart's own duration), instead of no transition at all. `--hover-rest` 650 ms is a wait, not a motion: how long a fine pointer rests on a cell, a row or a head before its hover controls appear (the fee page's grips, knobs, bins and pluses); it stays when the device asks for less motion.
- **Stacking:** sheets and popovers use the browser's top layer (`<dialog>`, `popover`). Layers between pieces come from one scale in `theme.css`: `--z-sticky` 20 (the sticky header and bars), `--z-popover` 30 (the search results under the page field, the menus that open in place), and while something is carried on the fee page (kit/lift.tsx) `--z-reach` 59 (a knob's frame following the pointer), `--z-carry` 60 (the carried thing's picture) and `--z-carry-over` 61 (the bin it is dropped on and the line saying why a place does not take it), over everything else. What a piece draws outside its own box while it is used (a carried thing's picture and its note, a knob's frame, a menu of actions, a dragged chip's copy) is drawn in the open dialog that holds the piece, else on the page's body (`kit/carry-layer.ts`): a sheet is a modal dialog in the top layer, which covers the body and makes it inert, and a menu drawn in it keeps within its box, which cuts off what runs past it. A class names the token (`z-(--z-sticky)`), never its number. What a piece raises inside itself stays inside it (`isolation: isolate`), with values 0 to 2 only, as the tap areas are layered (2.5).
- **Layers of the stylesheet:** every rule sits in a layer: Tailwind's `theme`, `base`, `components` and `utilities`, then `overrides`, declared last in `app.css`, for the few rules that must beat a utility (the "?" under a table's caption row, the short-screen rule that undoes `sticky`, a grid cell's face while something is carried, the grab hand). They are selected by data attributes or this stylesheet's own classes, never by an escaped Tailwind class name. A utility that must not apply in some state takes a variant instead of an override: a hover fill inside a fee table or grid is `resting:hover:…`, which rests while something is carried. `!` is a last resort, written as v4's suffix (`p-5!`) with a comment saying what it beats.

### 2.7 How numbers, dates and identifiers are written

Formatting always goes through `Intl` with `he-IL`; never hand-built strings, and tests compare `Intl` output with `Intl` output (it contains invisible direction marks).

| Kind | Form | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Shekel amount | `1,234.50 ₪` | `Intl.NumberFormat('he-IL', { style: 'currency', currency: 'ILS' })`; comma thousands, point decimals; precision per method §13, display rounding below |
| Dollar amount | `7.00 $`, a price per share `0.0035 $` | `Intl.NumberFormat('he-IL', { style: 'currency', currency: 'USD' })`, two decimals, up to four for a price per share (units `usd`, `usd_per_trade`, `usd_per_share`) |
| Euro and pound amount | `25.00 €`, `5.00 £` | `Intl.NumberFormat('he-IL', { style: 'currency', currency: 'EUR' })` (or `'GBP'`), two decimals (units `eur`, `gbp_per_trade`) |
| Percent | `1.05%` | data stores percent numbers (`1.05` means 1.05%), so divide by 100 for `style: 'percent'` |
| Date | `22.10.2026` | day first, dots, `day: '2-digit', month: '2-digit', year: 'numeric'`; never ISO or a slash form on the page |
| Report period | `ספטמבר 2026` | `month: 'long', year: 'numeric'` |
| Tax year | `2026` | a plain year, isolated |
| Fund number, ISIN, security number | `7978`, `IE00B5BMR087` | strings, never number-formatted (no thousands comma), LTR, tabular, slashed zero |
| Rule and question IDs | `TXS-09`, `TXK OQ3` | LTR, as written in `il-finance` |
| Chart tick | `50 אלף ₪` | compact form; a scale mark, not a figure: ticks carry a `data-scale` attribute, and the provenance crawl (architecture 3.7 item 5) allows digits inside it. Axis text is spelled in visual order inside a left-to-right isolate (`₪ אלף 50`), drawn `direction=ltr`, `text-anchor=start`, because WebKit reorders mixed Hebrew and digits otherwise |

Every number, amount, date and identifier inside Hebrew text is wrapped in an isolating element (`<bdi>` or `dir="ltr"` on its own span), so "25%" or a security number cannot be reordered by the bidi algorithm. The provenance component does this for every figure.

**Display rounding of amounts** (display only; method §13 unchanged). A computed, entered or parameter shekel amount of 1,000 ₪ or more shows whole shekels, and a whole amount of any size shows no agorot (`10,000 ₪`, `0 ₪`); a sourced amount of 1,000 ₪ or more shows no `.00` either (`83,641 ₪`, since "83,641.00 ₪" reads as false precision) and keeps the agorot its source prints (`5,645.29 ₪`); tariffs and fees under 1,000 ₪ (sourced, not parameters) and anything under 1,000 ₪ that has agorot keep two decimals; table cells (`size="cell"`) keep two decimals. On the fee page a sum the site works out in another currency follows the same rule in its own units (`computedSum`: "3,529 $", "3.20 $"), and a sum the person typed shows as typed (`typedSum`: "7 $", "7.50 $"). The right-to-left mark and the no-break space of the `Intl` output are removed so a copied amount is digits, a space and ₪; the element isolates the number (`bdi dir=ltr`).

**One number parser.** Every typed number goes through `parseNumber` (`lib/format.ts`): spaces (also no-break and thin), ₪, % and direction marks are stripped; with both separators the last one is the decimal point ("1.234,5", "1,234.5"); a single point is a decimal point; a comma alone reads by the kind of figure (`NumberKind`). In an amount (the default: a sum of money, a count) a comma is a thousands separator only in correct groups, one to three digits before the first comma with no leading zero and three after each ("12,000", "1,234,567"), and a single comma followed by one or two digits, or by three after a lone zero, is a decimal point ("2,5" is 2.5, "0,125" is 0.125). In a rate (a percentage, a rate, a price per unit: a `FigureField` whose sign is "%", or one given `kind="rate"`) a single comma is always the decimal point an Israeli reader types ("0,125" is 0.125, "1,000" is 1). Anything else is not a number.

**A number and its unit.** One rule wherever a figure stands, in a display, a field, the toolbox's empty boxes and the kit alike: the number, then its unit as the page writes the figure. A sign (₪, $, €, £, %) stands on the number's right in one left-to-right group ("500,000 ₪", "0.3%", a box "[39 ₪ ⌄]" whose sign is a choice), a unit in words after the number in reading order ("10 שנים"), and the two never wrap apart: a figure's element keeps them on one line, and an amount inside plain words (a fee's name, a ladder's step, "עד 10 $") is one left-to-right isolate with a no-break space between them (`amountWords`), so it is neither reordered nor broken. A `NumberField` whose unit is a sign draws it on the number's right (`.field-box-sign`); a `FigureField` draws its unit in the box after the number.

**Date and month inputs.** A month is chosen in the kit's `MonthField` (section 11): a box that shows the month as `03.2027`, with a calendar mark, and opens the site's own picker, the year with a control a year back and a year on and its twelve months by name ("מרץ"); the month can be typed in the box as well. The native month input is not used: two desktop browsers draw `<input type="month">` as plain text. A native date input, where one is used, is `dir="ltr"` with the text at the end, and the chosen day is shown beside it in the `Intl` form (`22.10.2026`), so the page never shows the browser's locale form.

### 2.9 Copy conventions

- Address the reader with the plural imperative ("בחרו", "הזינו", "הקישו") or impersonally ("אפשר", "יש"), never one gender (guidelines/wording.md, "Word choice"; copy 10.2). It is gender-neutral and the common Israeli web form.
- Institution, product and track names appear exactly as the source spells them (data-schema §1); the site's explanation sits beside them, never in their place. A long name wraps and is never cut with an ellipsis, because the exact name is how people recognise it; the same holds for a bank's product name and for the negotiation text (7.4).
- No internal code in a line the reader reads: a rule's (`KGL-02`), a parameter's (`P-05`), a formula's (`F-21`) or a method assumption's (`A-01`). The words carry it: the words of a sentence that state a rule open its card (`Ruled`, D116); a row that is the rule itself has the rule's title as its link; a formula's or a parameter's chip says its name; an open question's says "פרטים". A code stands only inside a rule's own card or sheet (its heading "id · title", D25, marked `data-code-ok`), on the rules page (`/rules`), where a code typed in the field opens its rule, and in addresses and anchors. The site search still finds a rule by its code, though its line does not print it. `site/scripts/rule-codes.mjs` fails the build on a code in what a built page shows outside those places; `tools/code-scan.mjs` counts them per kind of page.
- The wording of every fixed label (the number states, strength labels, dimension marks, "unknown", real and nominal, reset, the table view of a chart, the not-on-the-site state, the near-duplicate resolutions) is in `docs/copy.md` and `site/app/copy.ts`.

### 3.2 Provenance

Called for by the standing rule "every number traces to a source", D3, D7, D10, 3.8 "next to every rule-dependent number, a link that opens the rule", and architecture 3.5 and 3.7. These are the most-used components on the site (`components/provenance.tsx`).

**Num** (the provenance number). The only way to put a number on a page. Takes a value object, never a bare number.

- Renders as a `button` (so a keyboard reaches it) holding the formatted value in an isolating span with `tabular-nums`, then any flags. Its accessible name says the value and its state ("1.05%, ממקור, פרטים"). A transparent pseudo-element extends the hit area without changing the line spacing: 11 px above and below the figure and never less than 44 px tall, for every figure (guidelines/design.md), and a second one, exactly the figure's own box, sits above every such area. So a figure in running text keeps its 44 px, and where its area reaches the line above or below, the figure on that line still takes its own click (so a press on one figure never opens the sheet of a figure on the line above or below; an area that grew only sideways would take 44 px from about 1,200 lone figures). Two figures closer than 44 px share the space between them; neither takes the other's box. A term's area follows the same rule. Kit guard 19.
- State styles: sourced, a solid `--num-underline-thickness` underline in `sourced`; computed, a dotted `--num-underline-thickness` underline in `computed-line`; entered, the `entered` ink alone, with no highlight behind it and no underline (the "entered" tag keeps its own fill, guidelines/design.md); a figure a merged cell's cases hold both from a source and as the person's, both marks, the `sourced` underline and the `entered` ink (`num-mixed`, 7.1). A field's value (`field: true`) is no button and has no style: plain text. Underline offset `--num-underline-offset`.
- Flags, after the value (at the inline end): stale, a 14 px `Clock` in `caution`; under a caution notice that already says the figures' source is old, `StaleSaid` leaves the clock out of every figure inside (the accessible name and the sheet still say it), as on the track browser and a company's track list, where the notice covers every figure of the list. Secondary strength, or institution strength out of its own context (D31: a company's figure about its own product, shown on that product, carries no icon and its sheet names the company's document): a 14 px caution `IconAlert` before the digits (never between a number and its unit word; `select-none`), where the strength word ("משני", "לפי החברה") is in the accessible name and the popover, not in the line (the word stays as a tag only on strength labels, below); a computed figure carries the icon only at figure size, and its sheet names the weak inputs; a sourced figure whose `effective_from` is after the page date carries the same icon, and at figure size "נכנס לתוקף ב־{date}"; inside an `upcoming` callout (3.9), which already says its rules start later, the figure leaves that icon out (the accessible name and the sheet still say the date). A figure its record doubts (`SiteValue.doubt`, a bank's `doubt_he`): the same icon and the digits in `caution-foreground`, with no tint behind them (`num-doubt`); "יש ספק" is in the accessible name, and the sheet opens with a caution callout titled "יש ספק" that says the doubt. The doubt is the value's own, so every place that shows the figure warns, with no chip or note beside it. Site parameter: nothing inline (the popover says it).
- **A caution only where there is a doubt.** The amber icon and the clock say a figure is doubtful or old. An explanation, a kind or a company's figure about its own product never wears one, and the meaning of a label is not worded as a warning, which would read as incomplete data. A doubt, a lock or a badge that every row or card of a list would carry is said once, above the list, as `StaleSaid` does.
- **A note sits on the figure it is about.** A caution that says a figure may be too high or too low goes on that figure, not on another figure that moves the other way because of it: "the current account counted here may be too high" belongs on the account's cost, and on the value left it would say the wrong direction (BANK-35). When one run feeds several figures, each figure takes only the notes about itself.
- `value: null`: shows "לא ידוע" in `muted-foreground`, same underline as its state; the popover gives the reason from `notes`.
- **The sheet says what the figure says.** A value that is not a number (a word, yes or no, or empty) heads its sheet with the `display` word it was shown as; a record's raw value ("none", "unlinked_15") never reaches a reader, and every such value also has a Hebrew word in `numbers.enums` for a figure shown without one. A number's sheet keeps its digits. (Guard 14 in `kit/guards.test.ts`.)
- Sizes: inline (inherits text size), cell (tables), figure (`text-figure`, weight 600, with a state tag below it, because a headline number deserves its label in words).
- When the value changes after an input change, the digits move to the new value over `--duration-chart` (`CountingFigures`, section 11). A figure with its own wording passes `display` as a function of the moving value, so it counts too.

**Provenance popover.** Opens on tap or Enter from any Num. Uses the native `popover` attribute, so it works in the pre-rendered page before any script loads (architecture 3.5).

- Phone: a bottom sheet, full width, `rounded-xl` top corners, `max-h-[70vh]`, scrolls inside. PC: a `popover` card beside the number, 26rem wide and 30rem from 1280 px where the window allows (anchored where the browser supports it, otherwise centred); no backdrop, a maximum height of 60vh with the inner body scrolling, and it flips above or below its anchor by the room there is, never over the site's header. While more of a sheet lies below its edge, a small framed tag (6 px corners) with the words "יש עוד למטה" and a down arrow says so, in a strip of its own under the scrolling body, which is always there at one height and empty at the body's end, so the sign never covers a line and its coming and going moves nothing (`.sheet-more` in the shared `Sheet`; with no sign, a line under the edge gives no hint that more follows; a fade alone reads as the sheet cut off, so the sign says it in words; a sign over the last line hides it and the sheet is misread, so nothing lies over the body). The sign is measured on scroll, on any change of the body's size or of a block inside it (a fold opening in the sheet) and after each render (`watchMoreBelow`). Every sheet opens at its top, whatever the last one was scrolled to, and so does each figure opened inside an open sheet and each step back (`view`, so a figure opened from inside a sheet never keeps the scroll of the one before, its head out of sight).
- Header: state icon and label; strength label; stale flag.
- Body by state. Sourced: the value with its unit, the source as a SourceBlock (3.8a) directly under the figure's name, publisher, effective dates, rule references, notes; for a site parameter also its rationale. The source's title shows when it is Hebrew, else the link's host with the English title behind "שם המקור באנגלית"; English working notes are never shown; a rule without a source link says so; a value valid for exactly one calendar year reads "תקף לשנת {year}". Computed: the formula's name and ID, then each input as a Num (so the chain can be followed), a site parameter with its tag and a figure read from a document with that document's line under it (publisher, full title, place in it), then the rules used, each with its first source's line. A source line wraps at every width and is never cut, so a figure's source, its name and date, stay in view and never end in "…". Entered: "הזנתם את הערך הזה.", the value it replaced as a Num, and the reset button.
- Footer: what a flag means, in one line (for a stale number: its normal cadence and the date of the last value).
- **A source opens where it says it.** A link to a cited source opens at the cited words: a web page by text fragment (`#:~:text=`, at the quoted words as the page prints them), a PDF at its page (`#page=N`; Chrome's PDF viewer does not follow a text fragment), a dataset at this fund's own record rather than the dataset's front page. Every such link is built by `sourceHref` (`site/app/lib/sources.ts`), never from a raw address; a guard in `kit/guards.test.ts` fails an `href` made of a raw `url`, and `tools/quote-fragments.mjs` keeps the words each web page opens at (`data/sources/fragments.json`), only where they stand on the page exactly. A quote that is a translation or a paraphrase opens the page at its top, so a quote shown beside a link is the page's own words wherever it can be.

**State tag.** Icon plus label in `text-xs` weight 500, `rounded-sm`, in the state colour on its subtle fill (sourced: `muted`). Used under figures, in popover headers and on inputs that were changed.

**Strength label.** For rules and sources: primary as a plain `muted` tag; institution (labelled "לפי החברה"; an institution's own document is still the source, D23) and secondary in caution colours with `CircleAlert`; open in blocked colours with `Lock`. A figure or rate that rests on the site's own reading of official texts (the engine's `reading` label, a rate whose reach no official text states) takes `ReadingTag`, "קריאה של האתר", in the same caution colours: a reading is not a secondary source, so it never shows "מקור משני".

**Rule reference** (`Ruled`, D116). The words of a sentence that state what a rule says are the link to it, the way a figure is its own citation: the shortest phrase that states the rule ("מוגבל בצו", "מנכה מס הכנסה"), with the solid grey line of a sourced figure (3.1). A tap opens the rule's card, or with several rules the list of their titles, in the provenance sheet. No chip after a sentence or at the end of a block. A figure from a rule needs no words marked: its sheet names the rule ("חוק או כלל", the rule's title) and opens its card. A guide's rows of facts whose words its content file writes (the money-in-and-out list's switch line and added rows, the note on other countries) take words that carry their rules the same way; a rule the words and figures of a row leave unstated is not shown in it, and a company-terms row under "who offers it", whose name only names a property, carries no rules. A text that is a string (a "?" text, a glossary card) lists its rules under it by title (`RuleTitleList`).

**Blocked notice.** An inline callout in `blocked-subtle` with `Lock`: what cannot be computed, the open-question ID, and why it matters (D10: "the comparison is incomplete for that reason"). Also used for "figure not updated" (method §13), with its own wording.

The ids the site shows (`IdRef`), the words a rule states (`Ruled`) and who a rule applies to are the blocks layer of section 11.

### 3.3 Inputs

Called for by 3.1 of the product ("every value behind it can be changed on the page: amount now, monthly deposit, horizon, return, inflation, age and employment type"; "a changed value is shown as entered by you"; "values follow them to the catalogue and the goal comparison"), the real-terms principle (product.md §2) and architecture 3.9 (sliders with a typed value, steppers, bottom sheets). The fields are the kit's (`kit/fields.tsx`, section 11).

**Field (anatomy shared by every input).** The label line above (`text-sm`, weight 500), optional hint under the control (`text-xs muted-foreground`), the control. What the reader types looks the same as the default: no tag, no blue edge or ink. A slider field's number is the exception in colour only: it is always in the slider's blue, whatever it holds, so it still says nothing about who set it. At the far end of the label line, a small muted reset, "↺ חזרה ל־{default}" (`ResetToStart`: `RotateCcw`, `text-xs`, the default as the field shows it, "חזרה ל־10", "חזרה ל־12,000 ₪"); a slider field's says "↺ איפוס" with no figure, its value shown once in its box. It shows only while the value differs from the default (the field's `start`); it keeps its room while hidden, so the label, the box and the slider never move when it comes, and its tap area reaches 44 px. A field with no default (an optional age) has none. Errors replace the hint in `destructive` with the control's edge in `destructive`. A field is exactly two children, the label line and a body (`.field-body`: the control, then its error or hint, and note), so that fields side by side can share rows.

**Field row.** Fields side by side sit in a `.field-row` grid (the timeline's return and inflation, the pension and study-fund pairs). Every field in it is a subgrid over two of the grid's rows, labels in the first and bodies in the second, so the boxes start at the same height whatever the labels' line count; a one-line label sits at the bottom of the label row, against its box. A slot that holds a field with buttons beside it (a stepper, "לא יודעים") is a `.field-cell`: the same two-row subgrid, the field in its first column and each button in the box row of its own column at the end; the cell and the field share the field's 6 px row gap. Rule for new forms: fields that share a row go in a `.field-row`, never in a bare grid or flex row, and a wrapper between the row and its field is `display: contents`.

**Number field.** `type="text"`, never `type="number"`; `inputmode="numeric"` for an amount of money, which is typed in whole shekels (the phone's whole-number pad), and `inputmode="decimal"` for a rate (interest, inflation, a return) or a fee from a price list, which may have decimals; `autocomplete="off"`, `spellcheck="false"` and `enterkeyhint="done"`; `dir="ltr"` with the text aligned right so it lines up with the label; its unit fixed in the box after the number as the page writes a figure (2.7, "A number and its unit"): a sign on the number's right, a unit in words at the box's end. Height `h-field`, `rounded-md`, edge `input`, fill `card`, 16 px text. Formats with thousands separators on blur, accepts pasted values with commas or ₪ through the one shared parser of 2.7 (comma decimals included), and adopts text typed into the pre-rendered field before the script loaded. An entered value looks the same as the default.

**While the person types.** Every keystroke is read. A right value takes effect at once, with no pause, so the page answers as the person types; a wrong one is not committed, and the last right result stays on screen. What was typed is never cleared: a wrong text stays in the box after it is left, with its error, until the value changes from outside (a reset, a preset). The error shows once the hand rests, on leaving the field or after 600 ms without a keystroke (`kit/error-rest.ts`), never on every keystroke; once shown it stays while the text is still wrong, and the keystroke that puts it right clears it. While the box has the focus its text is the person's: a value changed from outside does not replace it under their fingers. The box's text is derived (what was typed while it is the person's, else the value as the field writes it), never copied into state by an effect. A figure typed in place (`FigureField`) follows the same rules for its error; a chart's range typed as two amounts (`RangeFields`) takes each amount when its box is left or on Enter, so a half-typed bound never redraws the chart.

**Slider field.** For amount, monthly deposit, return and horizon. The label row holds the label at the start, its reset at the end of the label's part (beside the box, or on a second line of the same 44 px row where both do not fit) and a compact number field at the end; the slider sits under it. That number floats: no box, a thin `input` line under it (red on an error), the figure in `slider` blue at weight 600, the unit muted beside it, left out where the label already names it in words ("שנים"; the caller sets `unitInLabel`). A typed value the box refuses shows its message in the reset's place, shown and not announced as an alert, so it is not spoken on every keystroke. The slider runs left to right, like a chart's time axis, its least at the left, and shows its value once, in the box: nothing at its ends. Track 12 px with rounded ends in `slider-track` with an `input` edge, the filled part in `slider` (the entered blue), filling from the left; a solid thumb of 24 px in `slider` with a 44 px hit area. While the slider is held, the filled part and the thumb turn `slider-active`, a step deeper. Its track follows a scale (`slider-scale.ts`): linear for years, a return or a percent (`linearScale`); for money, in parts that each take an equal share of the track, with steps that grow from part to part (`steppedScale`), so a common amount is not pressed against the left end. A drag gives the nearest step; a value typed between the steps keeps its exact place on the track. The arrow keys move one step of the scale, Page Up and Page Down a tenth of the track and at least one step; a key toward an end the value is at or past (a typed value above the range) leaves it alone. The typed field accepts any value the engine accepts, including values outside the slider's range. The ranges are stored with the illustrative scenario in `data/parameters/illustrative-scenario.json` (`sliders`: a linear slider's `step`, a money slider's `segments`; `rangeScale` reads either); they limit only the slider, not what can be typed.

**Segmented control.** For two to four exclusive options: employment type, and real or nominal (real is the default). Full width on a phone, `rounded-md`; the pressed segment is a solid `bg-primary` fill with a check icon (`aria-pressed`), and wherever a segmented control stands above a heading it is `max-w-sm` at the inline start. Radix ToggleGroup with `dir="rtl"`.

**Inputs sheet.** A bottom sheet on a phone (`<dialog>`, `overlay` behind, swipe or X to close, the chart stays visible above it at reduced height where space allows); at `lg`, a panel at the start (right) of the chart instead of a sheet. Fields in the order of 3.1's list. Changes apply as the person types; there is no submit button.

**Button.** Primary (`bg-primary text-primary-foreground`, one per screen), secondary (`bg-secondary`), quiet (text only, `accent` wash on press), add (a quiet action under a list that adds to it, a plus before its words, at its column's start), destructive (a quiet button in `destructive` text, for an inline "מחיקה"; its own variant, because `text-destructive` added to a quiet button loses to its `text-link`), danger (filled `bg-destructive text-destructive-foreground`, 44 px, for the "מחיקה" of `ConfirmAsk`), outline (a bordered face on the card's surface), framed (the same face with its edge and words in the action colour, a card's one action; `LinkButton` has it too), inline (an action inside a sentence, underlined in the link colour; `bold`: the sentence's one action, bold and underlined only under the pointer), icon (44 px square). Height `h-field`; a secondary button and a `LinkButton` are 40 px on a computer (`lg`), with the 44 px `tap-area` (2.5). Labels follow the style guide (copy 10.5): a noun of action ("שמירה") or an infinitive ("לשמור את ההשוואה"), never the imperative.

**Link.** `text-link` with an underline on hover and focus; external links (sources) add a mirrored `ExternalLink` icon and open in a new tab.

**What acts looks like it acts, and does what it looks like.** A link is in the link colour, a button has its edge or fill, and an id or a row that opens something looks openable. What does nothing is never drawn as a button, a chip or a round plus. A control does what its look promises: arrows move the way they point, section names that look like tabs change the view rather than jump down the page, a picker adds rather than replaces, and no action is found only by accident. A table sorts by its column heads. The actions a person expects are there: what they build can be renamed, deleted and dragged into order, and a slider's thumb drags.

**Ask only what the site cannot know.** A field asks for the person's own numbers, never for what the site can look up (an exchange rate, a price), and never "who are you" to sort the person into a group whose answer changes little for most people.

**The reader's own items.** Where a page lets the reader add an item of their own (a fund, an index), the item takes the same settings the site's items have, each set by the reader: the site puts no label, kind or default on it that the reader did not choose.

**A form has room.** A form of more than a few fields opens in a space of its own, its fields full size and grouped, never squeezed into a narrow panel or left a bare stack of boxes.

### 3.4 Timeline chart

Called for by 3.1 of the product ("a timeline of money in, growth, fees and tax out, with the rules it used listed beside it"), method F-67, D9, architecture 3.9 and gap 8.6. `components/chart/TimelineChart.tsx`, inside `components/timeline/Timeline.tsx`.

- **Form.** An area chart over time. Time runs left to right (gap 8.6), from now to the horizon. One value axis in shekels, real by default, nominal when the segmented control says so; a "?" beside the control (`HelpMark` with a text of its own) says what each side's figure is, and the assumption that the monthly deposit rises with prices, which says why the deposits grow past what was typed, stays in sight while a monthly deposit is above 0: a weak line under the switch below `lg`, where "ההנחות של הגרף" is a sheet behind a tap, and from `lg` a weak line under that panel's fields, never both at one width. From the baseline up: net value (teal), tax (rose, during and at exit), fees (orange, by type). The top edge is what the money would have been without fees and tax, so each band shows what it takes. Cumulative contributions are a neutral line over the bands. The exact series are F-67's.
- **Marks.** 2 px lines; bands separated by a 2 px gap in the card colour; the net line in `chart-net` on top. Fee types take the fee steps in a fixed order; tax during and at exit take the tax steps.
- **Labels.** Direct labels at the horizon end of each band that has room for one; a legend above the chart, in reading order (right to left) as chips with swatches, names only the bands too thin to carry their own label, so no band is named twice (section 12, rule 3). Value-axis labels and year ticks as section 12 says (the kit's `AxisLabels` and `yearTicksIn`). A legend item appears only for an area or line that is drawn: the legend drops "מס" when no point has tax.
- **Markers.** A 1 px vertical line in `chart-marker` with a `text-xs` label at the top for lock-up ends, exit points and age milestones that the wrapper's rules define (method §8).
- **Blocked parts.** Where the method cannot compute a part (for example exit tax blocked by an open rule, method §12), that band is replaced by a 45° hatch in `chart-blocked` over `blocked-subtle`, with a `Lock` label naming the open question. The rest of the chart still draws.
- **Entered return.** When the return is entered by the person, the net line and the top edge are drawn dashed in `chart-entered`, and the caption says the projection rests on a return they entered (D9).
- **Readout.** Touch and drag on a phone, hover on PC: a 1 px crosshair in `chart-crosshair` and a readout card pinned above the plot (not under the finger): the year as its heading, then one row per series, label at the start and value at the end, any tag after its value. A click or a tap keeps the year until the same year is clicked or tapped again. `touch-action: pan-y`, so a vertical swipe still scrolls the page. Zoom: section 11, `useYearZoom`.
- **Table view.** A disclosure, "הצגה כטבלה", with one row per year and one column per series, every cell a Num. It is its own full-width card under the chart-and-inputs row (both columns at `lg`), cells do not wrap, and the wrapper carries an edge fade and shadow as a scroll cue (also on the comparison and fee tables); on a phone the card follows the chart card.
- **Rules used.** Under the chart on a phone, beside it at `lg`: the rule references the run used, from the engine's record.
- **States.** Pre-rendered at build on P-05 (the illustrative scenario), so the chart is there before any script. While the worker recomputes for longer than 150 ms, the plot dims to 60% opacity; when the result arrives, shapes and numbers move to the new values over `--duration-chart`.
- **Size.** Full width; 240 px tall on a phone, 320 px at `lg`.

On a guide page with a visual of its own (section 10) the timeline is folded below it as "שחקו עם המספרים שלכם"; a page without one keeps it open in "מה זה".

## 11. Kit (the shared components)

The kit is custom components, so the UI is consistent and programming is easier and faster, like a class but for UI components; it keeps layout faults such as a sentence with a number laid out as a flex column, one piece to a line, or a closed fold inside an open fold that reads as open, from being made page by page. The kit is `site/app/components/kit/`, one file per component or small family, re-exported from `kit/index.ts`. Import from `components/kit`, never from a neighbouring feature. The gallery at `/kit` (pre-rendered, `noindex`, in no menu and no search; every number on it is a labelled example) shows each component in its states, right to left, with its name beside it, and is where a piece is checked at 1440 and 390 px (guidelines/design.md, step 4); who judges how it looks is in guidelines/working.md. The survey of duplicate pieces behind the kit: `8dd1f013:research/specs/2026-10-25-ui-kit/survey.md`.

**The rule.** A new pattern goes into the kit first, with its gallery entry and, where it can be named, a guard in `kit/guards.test.ts` or a ratchet in `kit/ratchets.test.ts`, never into a feature folder; then the feature uses it (guidelines/design.md, steps 1 and 2). A component takes the *choices* as props (`pad`, `size`, `tone`, `scroll`) and never as an overriding class: the site has no class merger, so two classes for one property are decided by the order of the generated stylesheet, not by the order in `class` (the `p-0 md:p-0` on the guide's key/value card never applied for that reason). Each component started as the best existing version; a piece that nothing in the kit fits is added to the kit.

| Component | When to use it |
|---|---|
| `Button`, `LinkButton` | `Button` for an action (`primary` once per screen, `quiet` for a text action, `destructive` for a quiet one that deletes, `icon` for a 44 px icon, `labelled` for the same face with a word beside the icon, the header's "תפריט"), `LinkButton` for a place to go. `outline`: a bordered button on the card's surface, the face `MenuPicker` has, for an action beside such a picker in a bar (the fee page's "קישור לעסקה", "לבחור תבנית"). `ghost`: the face of `RemoveButton`, an icon alone, 36 px and round with a 44 px tap area, muted, no edge, the danger colour only under the pointer or the focus, its words in `aria-label`. `add`: the quiet action under a list or a choice that adds to it or starts a new one (the indices page's "+ להזין תנאים שקיבלתם" under the account list, the popup's "+ עסקה ריקה"): a plus before its words, at the start of its column, its words lined up with the text above it and its side padding pulled out past the edge; never `self-start -ms-2` and a hand-typed plus. `flush` takes the side padding off a `quiet` or `destructive` button, so its words line up with the text above and below them; never `!px-0`. `size="sm"`: a `quiet` or `destructive` button at 13 px with no height of its own and a 44 px tap area, for a text action inside a line of small text (a step's "להחליף" in the fee page's deal builder); A sentence a rule backs never ends on its chip: its words open it (`Ruled`). `titled` gives an open question's chip its title (D49); a formula's chip says the formula's name (`formulaNames`), a parameter's its name (`parametersPage.names`, else "פרמטר"); a formula id (`F-80`, and the ids `formulaNames` names, such as `A-06`, whose sheet and aria-label say "איך זה מחושב" instead of "נוסחה") opens the formula sheet (its Hebrew name, the site parameters it uses, each an `IdRef`, and a link to `docs/method.md` on GitHub, in English); a parameter id (`P-01`) is a link to `/parameters#P-01`, which scrolls to its card below the sticky header; any other `A-nn` is a method assumption, linked only when a parameter card holds it as an input, else nothing; an open-question id (`TX2 OQ6`, also written `TX2-OQ6`) opens the open-question sheet (what the term means on the site, and the title, status and what it blocks once `data/open-questions.json` has the id); a proof id (`M-01`, a statement math proves, `docs/method.md` section 14), which has a rule's shape and is told apart before the rule pattern, is the chip "הוכחה" and opens the proof sheet: its Hebrew title with the code small beside it, what it shows, "מתי זה נכון" and its assumptions, "מה זה לא מכסה" where it says, and a link to the proof in method.md, in English. A proof's words are `site/app/content/proofs.ts`, imported by the sheet's script, so a page's own script does not carry them; `content/proofs.test.ts` keeps them in step with method.md's entries and with the proofs the content files cite. Props: `asLabel` (the id as a label that does nothing: a parameter's own card). An empty id renders nothing, and an id of no kind renders as plain text. `IdText` turns the open-question codes inside a copy string into chips; with `assumptions` it also turns the method's `A-nn` ids into chips and drops their brackets (the parameters page's field names, where "(A-03)" would break at its hyphen on a phone). Never print one of these ids as plain text in a `<bdi>`: `kit/guards.test.ts` scans for it. A formula the site shows has a Hebrew name in `formulaNames`, which the value sheet writes beside its chip; without one the sheet reads "חישוב של האתר F-92", an id with no words, and `kit/ratchets.test.ts` fails a new formula id without a name. The method data (formulas with their parameters, the parameter cards) reaches every page through the root loader, which `PageShell` merges into the provenance data.

**Blocks layer: `Ruled`** (`provenance.tsx`, D116). The words of a sentence that state a rule: `<Ruled id="BANK-17">מוגבל בצו</Ruled>`, or `id={["REG-04", "BANK-23"]}` for several rules on one phrase (`name` heads their list). A tap opens the rule's card, or the list of their titles, in the sheet a figure opens; inside a sheet it pushes, with the back arrow. Mark the shortest phrase that states what the rule says (check it against the rule's `text_he`), one phrase per rule in a block, not every mention; a rule whose only trace in a block is a figure from it gets nothing, since the figure opens it; a rule no words and no figure in a block state is left out of that block, never given a sentence of its own. The words never hold a `Term` or a figure (one control inside another): mark the phrase beside it. A one-letter prefix stays outside (`prefix="ה"`), touching the words with no space, so no line can break between them; there is no no-wrap span, so a long prefixed phrase wraps like any other. The words are an inline element, so the mark that closes a sentence follows them directly (`</Ruled>.`) and never starts the next line; there is no `end`. A phrase of several words wraps with the sentence; a term stays on one line. It shares `Term`'s inner control (`TapWords`), with its own class, `.ruled`, and the look of 3.1's "Words that open where they come from". No ids: the words, plain.

**Blocks layer: `RuleTitleRow`, `RuleTitleList`, `ProofsChip`** (`provenance.tsx`). No chip with a count stands for rules anywhere (D116): the words that state a rule open it (`Ruled`), a figure from a rule opens its sheet, which names it, and a list whose items are rules shows each by its title. `RuleTitleRow` is the row of such a list wherever the row is the rule itself (the list several rules' words open, a company page's terms from its documents, a checklist row's "מאיפה זה", the not-covered page's fold): the title in the link colour, opening the card, inside a sheet with its back arrow, a drill-in arrow and, under a company's term, whose it is; no code; 44 px high. A record the page does not hold reads "חוק או כלל" and still opens its card, which says so. Its look is `TitleRow`, which the list of proofs shares. `RuleTitleList` is the list of them, one row per rule divided by a line, no heading, no count; under the text of a sheet whose text is a string, so no `Ruled` can sit in it: a "?" text (`HelpMark rules`) and a glossary term's card. No ids, nothing. A row of `RuleRef`s is a ratchet in `kit/ratchets.test.ts`. `ProofsChip` is not a rules chip: the statements math proves that a fact rests on, "הוכחה" for one, which opens its sheet, and "הוכחות · N" for several, which opens the list of their titles, each a `TitleRow` opening its sheet; it stands after the sentence it backs first. The build's `site/scripts/rule-codes.mjs` fails on a rule chip ("חוקים וכללים · N") on any built page or search line, and proves itself first on one it plants in /kit's markup.

**Blocks layer: who a rule applies to** (`provenance.tsx`, D30; spec `8dd1f013:research/specs/2026-10-25-rules-scope/spec.md` §4). `RuleRef` shows a company's term (scope `institution`, or `unsettled` seen at named companies) by the company's name instead of its code: on that company's page its own name, elsewhere the first name and "ועוד N"; a general rule's chip is its title. The rule card carries a neutral scope tag ("חל על כולם", "של {company}", "לא הוכח שחל על כולם"), a line that a company's term may differ elsewhere, and a foot that leads to the rules of the page's product or to everything found at the company. `VariesByCompany ids` renders `CompanyLinks` for the companies of the records given. A product context (a guide, an exposure page, "שווה לדעת") cites general rules only: the validator checks the records (`packages/pipeline/src/scope.ts`) and `lib/scope.test.ts` the prose modules, where a company's term may appear only inside `VariesByCompany`.

**Which choice control.** Pick it by what the reader sees happen when they choose, not by the number of options and not by whether the address changes.
1. **It narrows a list:** the rows shown get fewer, and they are the same kind of rows. One of several values: `ChoiceChips` with `counts`. Filters that are each on or off, independent of one another: `FilterChip`. A parent and its children (a kind of product, then a product of it): `ChoicePair`.
2. **It changes how the same content is shown:** grouping, order, units (today's money or nominal), a chart or a table. `SegmentedControl`.
3. **It answers a question the calculation needs:** the reader's situation or an assumption (years, a kind of rate, an amount, "do you hold X"). `SegmentedControl` for two to four answers short enough to sit side by side on a phone; `TileChoice` when each answer needs a line of what it brings; `ChoiceChips` without counts for many preset values, with `other` for the reader's own.
4. **It goes somewhere else:** another page, or a view of its own inside the page that shows another kind of content. `PageTabs` for pages, `TabStrip` for views inside a page, or links (`ChipList` for a row of them).

Where a phone has no room for a row of chips, a `SelectField` stands in for them (a company's products, the sort of a list whose computer heads sort it). Choosing never moves what is below (2.5a): chips keep one width chosen or not, a count has a fixed slot, and `ChoicePair`'s second level is a tray of one height.

**Chips.** A list of things the person can choose among, add to, rename or delete is a `ChipList`; a single choice is `ChoiceChips` (above). A page never writes its own `chip` class, nor the chip's 10 px corners on the `secondary` fill by hand.

**Guards** (`kit/guards.test.ts`, run by `npm test`): no `<details>` outside the kit (`provenance-sheets.tsx`'s two footnote folds are on a visible allow-list until that file is next opened); no `group-open:` anywhere; no `<p>`, and no `<span>` with words of its own, that holds a `Num` is a flex or grid container (a scan of the source by `lib/jsx-scan.ts`); the gallery stays out of the menu and the search and keeps its `noindex`; and the others the file lists (fixed tables, the page frame, ids in `<bdi>`, a grey drill-in arrow outside the kit, the stairs, Hebrew addressed to one gender). Leftovers counted by name are ratchets in `kit/ratchets.test.ts`; their lists only shrink. Among them, the text pieces written by hand that the kit shares: a page title (`PageHead`), a section heading (`Section`), a scroll margin (`anchor-target`), a link within the site (`TextLink`), a link out (`DocLink`), a `!` override of a class (the piece's own prop), a media query for the width (`useMedia`), a fold label painted as a link (`Disclosure look="link"`), a `<button>` painted in the link colour and underlined (`Button` `quiet` or `inline`, read element by element), a label-and-value row in a hand grid (`KeyValueList plain`), a hand muted quotes box (`PrintedQuotes`) and a hand bar in the computed ink (`MeterBar tone="computed"`). `tools/merge-review.mjs` flags the same patterns on a branch's added lines.

**Where the kit stops.** Chart drawings (the SVG plots of sections 3.4 and 10), the provenance component and its sheet, the shell, and rows that are one feature's own anatomy (a fee row, a guide visual) are not in the kit; they are built from it. A feature's own helper that only looks up its copy (the guide visuals' `SourceLine` and `Gap` wrappers) stays with the feature and renders the kit's component.

**Lists of products** (the spec: `8dd1f013:research/specs/2026-10-27-company-fund-card/spec.md`). These hold wherever the site lists several products of one kind (tracks, funds, deposits, accounts):

- **A table, not a stack of cards.** On a desktop the products are rows of one table (`ProductTable`); on a phone each product is one row under the next. A card with a facts column left half of every card empty.
- **The identifier is the link.** Where a product has an official page elsewhere (a fund's page on Maya), its number is that link, with the external icon (`1148808 ↗`), and its accessible name says where it leads. No separate "בדף הקרן במאיה" line. A link inside the site ends with the drill-in chevron (›), a link out with the external icon (↗).
- **The column head carries the words; the cell holds the short value.** "המדד" in the head and "ת"א-125 ›" in the cell, never "עוקבת אחרי מדד …" on every row. On a phone, where there is no head, the row says the one word ("מדד ת"א-125 ›").
- **Columns as wide as their content.** The number, link and figure columns shrink to what they hold; the name takes the rest, so its labels fit on one line.
- **A kind is a label, not a row or a column.** What a product is or does (how a fund follows its index, whether it pays dividends, its currency, a tax that follows from it) is a label chip that opens a sheet with its rule, from one shared set (`fundLabels()` for funds). A yes/no or a kind never gets its own column.
- **Nothing that repeats.** No figure that says again what a label says (a fund's currency ceiling beside "חשיפה לדולר"), no column whose value is the same on every row (every index fund's stock grade is 4), no line under a link that explains what the linked page explains. A label true of every row of a group shows once, in the group's head (D60); a list with no groups gives such a label one head line over the list.
- **Every column helps choose among the rows.** A column, a figure or a line stays only where it tells the rows apart in a way that changes the choice; one that only describes is left out or becomes a label.
- **Every row follows one pattern.** The rows of one list carry the same lines: a line that only some rows would have is left out, and two rows that need telling apart are told apart by what every row already shows, such as the fund number or a label.
- **A line under a row says what the figure uses.** A secondary line under a row (a group's price, a promotion) says in its own words whether the row's figure counts it, and which of its numbers that is.
- **Every option a person has.** A list holds every option a person really has for the holding (D17), foreign funds and every house that sells them included; a row that cannot be priced says what the place does offer and opens it, not only what is missing.
- **Familiar names first.** A list that is not ranked by a figure puts the names people know first, by a sourced measure such as assets or members, and the small institutions in a group of their own lower down.
- **Who offers what.** A list across companies keeps the companies in view, its rows grouped under or named by their company, so the reader sees who offers what and not only a pile of tracks.
- **A column of one number state carries its mark in the head (D60).** Where every figure in a column is sourced, or every one computed, the column head word carries the underline of that state and one line above the list says what it means; the cells stay plain and still open their source or calculation when tapped. A cell whose state differs keeps its own mark; entered numbers keep their blue, flags keep their icon. Every column that holds a figure has a head.
- **A very long list opens folded.** A list of over a hundred products under their companies (the track browser) starts with each company as one row, its count and its money, the first open so the reader sees what a row holds; on a phone each product under an open company is one line (`ProductPhoneFold`). A search or a chosen filter shows its matches open, with nothing to open. Opening a company moves only what is below it, and gently (`useFlip`); nothing above moves (D62, D63).
- **A ceiling is not a holding.** A limit a product declares (a fund's exposure profile, "4A") is never drawn the way a measured share is drawn (the track table's rings, F-87 and F-88). If shown at all, it is words.

## 12. Charts

The rules every chart and visual on the site follows. The charts themselves: the timeline (3.4), the guide-page visuals (10), and the kit's `ChangeChart`, `PileStory`, `StairsChart`, `PairBars`, `YearBars`, `CostStack`, `ShareChart` and `AmountLanes` (11).

**Reading the visuals** (the daily money-market guide shows them: a table with coloured tags and bars, not a filter over cards, a scatter plot, four equal slices or a precise time axis). Every visual follows these:

1. **Draw only true numbers.** A real quantity (a rate, an amount, a time in months) gets a mark on its scale, with its figure printed on the mark itself, or right beside it where it does not fit; a legend never carries the figures in the marks' place, except for a part too small for its amount (guidelines/charts.md, "Stacked bar"). A category or an order (how soon the money is back, a kind of product) gets words, an icon and a colour, never a bar or a meter: equal slices invent a unit the data does not have.
2. **Colour carries meaning, and there is enough of it.** Each colour says one thing the reader can name (teal is the rate, warmer is held longer, stripes are a range); grey on grey says nothing, and a colour that only ranks or decorates is left out. The flags of 3.2 keep their meaning: a scale that borrows their hues stays away from numbers and always has a word and an icon.
3. **Every encoding is said once.** Whatever a colour, a pattern or a shape means is said on the screen, even when every figure is also printed: by a label on the mark, or by a legend for the marks that cannot carry one, never by both, and not again in tiles under the chart. Marks that share a meaning share one legend: a list whose rows each carry the same kind of bar has one legend above the list, never one per row, as a label true of every row shows once in the group's head.
4. **Show the whole comparison; do not make the reader operate it.** A guide page shows its product among its alternatives, all at once, with the page's product marked (a tinted row and a tag). No filter that hides or fades rows, no control the reader must use before the visual says anything. An analysis section may have a control (the indices page's change chart has a marker) when its first state already shows the whole comparison.
5. **The plainest mark that shows the number.** A bar, a line or a dot on a scale; no coins, towers or pictures standing in for an amount. The care goes into what is shown, not into the shape of the mark.
6. **No forced picture, and no what-if no one would plan on.** A visual is drawn only where the truth fits it; where the truth is more complex than a picture, words or a table carry it and the page has no visual. A scenario is one a person would plan on, and a conclusion goes no further than the data: the same fall every year for twelve years, or the past read as the future, is not shown as a likely outcome, and a case few people meet is not put at the centre.

Each mark on the screen can be named in one sentence from what is on the screen alone. The chart check (`site/scripts/chart-check/`, `npm run chart-check`) measures every chart before anything goes live; how a chart looks is judged as guidelines/working.md says ("4. Check before it goes live"), not by a screenshot review.

**Colour inside a chart** (guidelines/charts.md). Every legend identity has its own clearly different hue, checked pair by pair with the palette validator (normal-vision worst pair at least 15 ΔE, colour-blind at least 8, or 6 where a word sits on the mark) and by the chart check before anything goes live. Two hues that pass the validator, such as a rose and a magenta, can still read as "one pink family". Distinctness inside a chart comes before one meaning per colour across the page: within one chart or panel a hue means one thing, a distant section may give it another, and each page's spec carries its colour table. The categorical palette for comparing options on one chart avoids the state hues (blue, violet) and the status hues. Red, amber and green stay status colours, never a series. Blue and violet stay reserved for entered and computed numbers. Every chart's colours are run through the validator together, as drawn. `kit/ratchets.test.ts` checks each chart's roles pair by pair on every run, by the validator's method (`lib/palette-check.ts`): a new chart adds its roles to the list there, and today's pairs below the line are listed by name until they are apart.

**Axes and labels.** Time runs left to right, and so does every other axis (V3). Value-axis labels sit at the plot's left edge, just above their gridline, with a card-colour halo (`text-xs chart-text`); the right of the plot is kept for the direct labels and the horizon, and at the left the bands are still thin, so the labels do not sit on colour. Three to five gridlines, compact form (2.7). Year ticks run along the bottom at whole steps of 1, 2, 5 or 10 years from today (`niceYearTicks(horizon)`, about four ticks; the horizon end is not always a tick, its marker still marks it). Value labels are drawn after the lines, with their card-colour halo, on every chart, so no line runs through them. Names and numbers are written on the chart, with no table needed to decode it.

**Around every chart** the kit's `ChartLegend`, `ChartReadout` and `ChartTable` (a table view of the same numbers); its motion is the kit's glide (`useGlidingSeries` and the rest) and its pointer the kit's zoom and pick (`useYearZoom`, `useChartPick`). A series that is not drawn has no legend item, label, readout row or table column.
