# Design system

The look of the site and the pieces every page is built from. The tokens live in
`site/app/theme.css`; the shared components are the kit in `site/app/components/kit/`, shown in their states in the gallery at
`/kit` (section 11). The guidelines (`guidelines/`, above all `design.md` and `charts.md`) hold over this document.

- Section 1: the principles the product imposes on the visual design.
- Section 2: the tokens (colour, dark only; type, spacing, shape, motion), the layout patterns, and the conventions for numbers,
  icons and copy.
- Section 3: the site-wide pieces: the shell, provenance, inputs, the guide pages, companies and search, the reference pages and the
  primitives.
- Sections 6 to 9: the pieces of single features: the first page (6); fees and the transfer file (7); banks and investment
  houses (8); the not-covered page (9).
- Section 10: the guide-page visuals. Section 11: the kit. Section 12: the rules every chart follows.

Section numbers are ids that the code and other docs cite; 4 and 5 are not used, nor the ids of pieces that were removed.

No financial figure appears here. Where a component shows a number, it shows a value object through the provenance component (3.2).

| # | Principle | From |
|---|---|---|
| V1 | **Provenance is the visual language.** Every number shows whether it is sourced, computed or entered by you, and one tap shows where it came from. Stale, secondary-source and blocked states are visible in place, never only in a footnote. | guidelines/design.md, D3, D7, D10 |
| V2 | **Both screen sizes.** Every screen is checked at 1440 px and 390 px before it goes live. Touch targets at least 44 px. Nothing depends on hover. | guidelines/design.md, product.md §2, K7 |

## 2. Tokens and layout

### 2.1 Tiers and where they live

`site/app/theme.css`, imported by `site/app/app.css`.

1. **Palette** (`--palette-<hue>-<step>`): raw oklch ramps. Hue ramps share one lightness ladder (step 50 is 0.975 … step 950 is 0.24), so the same step behaves the same in every hue. Components never use these.
2. **Semantic roles** (`--background`, `--entered`, `--chart-fee`, …): what a colour means, one value each, since the site is dark only. Surface and text names follow shadcn/ui (`--background`, `--card`, `--muted-foreground`, `--primary`, `--ring`, …) so components copied from it work unchanged.
3. **Utilities**: `@theme inline` maps each role to a Tailwind colour (`bg-card`, `text-entered`, `border-input`, `fill-chart-fee`). Tailwind's default palette is removed (`--color-*: initial`), so a class can only name a role.

Rules:

- Components use role utilities or `var(--role)`, never a palette step and never a literal colour.
- **Dark only** (founder). There is no light theme and no theme switch. `color-scheme: dark` on the root makes native controls, scrollbars and the popover backdrop dark too. The palette keeps its light steps, unused.
- Layout uses logical properties and utilities only (`ps-`, `pe-`, `ms-`, `me-`, `inset-s-`, `inset-e-`, `border-s`, `rounded-s-`, `text-start`). The `rtl:` variant is for true exceptions such as mirroring an icon.

**A chart field's value is plain (founder, 2026-11-08, guidelines/charts.md).** A chart's or calculator's field (an amount, years, a return) starts at a default the reader can change. That default, and what the reader types in its place, is a plain number wherever it shows: in the sentence that repeats it, in a calculation sheet's input row, on a chip. No site-parameter tag, no blue, no "הזנתם", and it opens nothing (the one blue is a slider's own number, below, which is the control's ink, the same for the default and a typed value); the field beside it is where it changes, and the field's own reset, at the far end of its label line, brings the default back. The value carries `field: true` (`asField`, `lib/value.ts`); `Num` draws it as plain text. A line resting on a return the reader typed stays dashed, and the site's real parameters (the tie threshold, the sensitivity bounds) keep their tag.

Why these: most numbers on a page are sourced, so sourced is the quietest. Computed results get their own hue so a calculation is recognisable at a glance. Entered values are in "blue pen", the colour of the person's own handwriting, and are the only state that changes the number's own colour, which makes an entered return impossible to mistake for the site's view (D9). Blue and violet are never used for chart series, so the states keep their meaning inside charts.

### 2.4 Typography

**Faces.** Inter for Latin letters and digits, Heebo for Hebrew, both variable fonts, self-hosted through `@fontsource-variable` (no font CDN, D1). Stack: `"Inter Variable", "Inter Fallback", "Heebo Variable", Arial, sans-serif`. Inter comes first because it has no Hebrew, so Hebrew falls through to Heebo glyph by glyph; Arial has Hebrew and holds the layout while the web fonts load (`font-display: swap`). The shekel sign comes from Heebo. Only Inter's Latin subset and Heebo's Hebrew subset are loaded (own `@font-face`), about 60 KB for a Hebrew page, both preloaded in the HTML head, with an "Inter Fallback" face (Arial with `size-adjust`, ascent and descent set to Inter's metrics) ahead of Arial, so the swap does not move the layout.

Why this pair: Heebo is clear on small screens and matches Inter's proportions; Inter's digits are made for interfaces, with tabular figures and a slashed zero, which matter on a site made of numbers and fund numbers.

**Scale** (Hebrew reads smaller than Latin at the same size, so the scale starts at 16 px for body and runs taller line heights):

| Token | Size | Line height | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| `text-xs` | 13 px | 1.5 | captions, axis ticks, source dates; the smallest Hebrew on the site |
| `text-sm` | 14 px | 1.6 | secondary text, field labels, table cells on a phone, tags |
| `text-base` | 16 px | 1.7 | body, inputs (16 px also stops iOS from zooming into a field) |
| `text-lg` | 18 px | 1.6 | lede, card titles, h3 |
| `text-xl` | 20 px | 1.45 | h2 on a phone |
| `text-2xl` | 24 px | 1.4 | h1 on a phone, h2 on PC |
| `text-3xl` | 30 px | 1.3 | h1 on PC |
| `text-figure` | 32 px | 1.15 | a headline number (digits only, so the tight line height is safe) |
| `text-total` | 54 px | 1.15 | the one answering number on a page, when it is shown large (the indices page's ticket: what is left) |

**Weights.** 450 body (a half step heavier reads better on large screens; text with an explicit `font-normal` stays at 400); 500 labels, tags and emphasis; 600 headings and headline numbers; 700 not used. One weight per role.

**Root size.** The scale above holds at the default root of 16 px; from 1600 px wide the root is 17 px and from 2200 px it is 18 px, so every rem-based size grows by those steps.

**Hebrew rules.**

- Never letter-space Hebrew; the tracking utilities are removed. Word spacing is 0.05em on body text.
- No italics for emphasis (Hebrew has no true italic); use weight 500.
- Prose is capped at `max-w-measure` (38rem). `ch` measures a Latin zero, so check real Hebrew line lengths in the browser.
- `dir="auto"` on any text the person typed or pasted (catalogue search, uncatalogued names), per element, never on a list wrapper.

**Numbers.** Every number uses `tabular-nums` so values do not jitter while they animate to a new value and columns align. Identifiers (fund numbers, ISINs, security numbers, rule IDs, open-question IDs) also use `slashed-zero`. A headline figure keeps its size: where it does not fit beside its label it moves under the label rather than shrinking.

### 2.5 Spacing and layout

- **Base unit 4 px** (Tailwind's `--spacing: 0.25rem`): `p-1` is 4 px, `p-4` is 16 px. Use steps 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 12, 16.
- **Named spacing:** `px-gutter` (page side padding, `clamp(1rem, 4vw, 2rem)`: 16 px on a phone, up to 32 px), `gap-section` / `my-section` (between page sections, 40 to 64 px), `size-target` / `min-h-target` (44 px touch target), `h-field` (48 px fields and main buttons). **A tap area is what a finger hits, not what the control draws:** a control that must draw shorter than 44 px (an icon button, a chip, a link in a strip or a footer, a chart's legend on a computer) takes the `tap-area` utility, a ::after area 44 px tall centred on it that keeps the look; never on a link inside running text, whose area would cover the lines above and below. A field's input covers its box's border, so the box's 44 or 48 px is all input. Kit guard 22 fails any fixed height under 44 px on a button, link, summary or field without one of these, and `tools/hit-check.mjs` measures what a finger hits on the built page (2026-11-07: a check that measured drawn boxes listed rule chips whose areas were already 48 px, while the short ones a finger meets were a field's 42 px input and the links in the fee page's strip).
- **Widths:** `max-w-wide` (96rem) is the page frame, the same on every page and set only by `PageShell` (founder: one width on every page, so the header does not move from page to page); `max-w-measure` (38rem) for prose inside it. What fills the room is the page's own: card grids and lists take more columns, two halves stand side by side (the transfer page), prose keeps its measure. A guard in `kit/guards.test.ts` keeps any page from narrowing its frame again.
- **Breakpoints:** Tailwind defaults. Phone is the base style. `md` (48rem): tables become real tables (a list of products is one from `md`, section 11); cards go two across. `lg` (64rem): guide pages get a side section index at the start (right) and the timeline's inputs sit beside the chart. The header's inline destinations wait for `xl` (80rem), not `lg`.
- **Two-column pages.** Every page grid that sets columns in `fr` writes them as `minmax(0, …fr)` (`lg:grid-cols-[minmax(0,62fr)_minmax(0,38fr)]`, `[minmax(0,3fr)_minmax(0,2fr)]`, `[minmax(0,1fr)_300px]`, `[360px_minmax(0,1fr)]`): a plain `fr` column grows to its widest content, and a wide table opened inside it pushed the page wider than the screen. A wide table scrolls inside its own `overflow-x-auto` box.
- **Order and room follow importance.** The main thing comes first and takes the room; a side matter goes last, or folded (founder, 2026-11-02: "the choice between tracks should be a side note last"; 2026-10-24: the rules page should open with the rules, not the questions). Size reads as importance, so what a person must act on is never the smallest thing on the screen, nor a tiny box for a long list (2026-11-06, on another site: "it took so much less space which in my mind translated to significance"). The first screen of a page answers its question and draws the person in, never a row of doors in front of the answer, and never half empty on a wide screen (2026-10-24: "make sure the part of the site which answers a user's dilemma isnt hidden behind a couple buttons"; 2026-11-06: "the very first screen i see of the site isnt very appealing"). No loose gaps: fields and blocks keep the rhythm below, with no empty band between two rows of one form, and a card is as tall and as wide as what it holds, never stretched to its neighbour's height or a full row for a narrow column of facts (2026-10-30: "compact spaces between sections and compact the input sliders").
- **A control sits above what it changes.** A control that adds or removes fields goes above what it changes, so the control itself never moves when pressed; `tools/jump-check.mjs` presses every chip, switch, tab and disclosure on the main pages and lists the ones that move.
- **Hit areas.** A number, a rule chip and a glossary term each carry a transparent 44 px `::after` hit area. A number or a term inside running text keeps the line spacing, and its area shares the lines above and below. A control that stands alone (a rule chip, a value pencil, a figure in a readout row) holds a 44 px band of its own: an area drawn over a neighbour is covered by the neighbour's own box (layered at 2, above every area), so a 44 px area in a 27 px line measured 36 to 42 px (fix pass 8, 2026-11-07). The rule chip and the input slot carry vertical margins that make their line 44 px, and a readout row is at least 44 px. Areas are layered: figures and terms at 0, the areas of controls that stand alone at 1, every own box at 2. `tools/hit-check.mjs --checks` measures what a finger can hit on every guide page and both fee pages at 390 and 1280, and runs with the movement check; the leftovers are listed by name in `hit-leftovers.json`, a list that only shrinks. On a screen shorter than 500 px the sticky bars (chip bars, table heads, the bottom bar) become static. While a sheet is open the page behind does not scroll (`scrollbar-gutter: stable` on `html` so the desktop does not shift).
- **Rhythm inside a section:** 16 px between paragraphs, 24 px between blocks (a chart and its inputs, a table and its note), `section-gap` between sections.

### 2.5a Stable layout

**A preference, not a rule (founder, 2026-11-08: the strict rule was dropped).** Prefer that changing a number moves little on the screen, and use the patterns below where they cost nothing; a small shift is better than a blank gap kept for a case that rarely shows, and no change needs a movement check. The original aim, kept for reference: Moving a slider, picking an option, typing a number or switching an index changes the figures and nothing else on the screen: no column in view widens, no row or block in view grows or shrinks, no line in view wraps differently, and the part of the page under the person's finger stays where it was. The control the hand is on holds; a list it re-sorts may slide below it. A block out of sight at that moment, such as a note far below the slider, may change its own height; the movement check (`tools/box-check.mjs`) brings each control onto the screen and measures only the boxes in view. One exception (D62): switching index on a phone may move what lies below the switch, with a short, gentle transition instead of a jump; a list keeps one height across the states and plans of the index it shows, not across indices.

| Case | How the site does it | Where |
|---|---|---|
| A table whose figures change | `table-layout: fixed` with a width for every column, on a computer and on a phone; a column left out on a phone stays at width 0, so the next cell keeps its column; headings may wrap, figures may not | the kit's `TableCols` in every table (`ChangeChart`, `ChartTable`, `ProductTable`, `CompareTable`, the rest by hand); `.tbl` is fixed, and a guard in `kit/guards.test.ts` fails a `<table>` without it |
| A table cell with a figure and words beside it ("7,369,509 ₪ יותר") | The column holds the figure and its words at the inputs' far ends with the largest choice; where it cannot on a phone, the words go on a line of their own under the figure on every row, so the row keeps one height (independent check 2, 2026-11-06: the word was cut at 390 px while the figure fit; `box-check.mjs --plans` now measures a cell's whole text) | the indices page's "what changes most" table |
| A figure | Tabular digits (`tabular-nums`, the page body's default), no wrapping inside a figure, isolated in Hebrew text | every `Num` |
| A number the reader sets, inside a sentence | Plain, at its own width (a small shift beats a blank gap) | `InputSlot` (`.input-slot`) |
| A block whose content varies by choice | One shape for every choice: what may be missing is replaced, not removed (on the cheapest pair the fees panel shows the next pair beside it) | the indices page's `FeesPanel` |
| A header that varies by subject | One height, sized for the longest subject | the indices page's `TitleRow`: the line under the title, with every index's line laid out unseen in the same cell |

| A list whose rows differ from one choice to the next | The list holds the tallest height it has had, keyed on what starts it over (the index); it can grow once, the first time a taller case comes up, and never shrinks back; a reason that depends on the choice stands once under the list rather than on each row | `useHeldHeight`: the indices page's stairs, its dollar section and the account list of an index with no fund yet |

| Figures of one row, one of them short | The row's other figures of a hundred million or more read in the same short form, so a smaller figure in full digits never looks the larger beside a short one ("758,833,521 ₪" beside "1.57 מיליארד ₪", fresh readers, round 3); only the figures the screen shows count | `ShortRow` and `shortRow` in `provenance.tsx`: the account list's rows, the fund list's range, the pair tiles, the "what changes most" table |

**A mark is never alone on a line.** A small mark before or after words (a "?", a caution icon, a lock, a link's arrow) is held to the nearest word: `LeadTail` for a mark after a text, `MarkHead` for one before it (both in `provenance.tsx`); a figure holds its own icons. Punctuation is held the same way: a full stop, a comma or a closing bracket stays on the line of the word, chip or figure before it, and an opening bracket on the line of what it opens (founder, 2026-10-25: "there is a random dot there?"). A sentence that ends on a chip (`IdRef`, `RuleRef`) passes its full stop, comma, colon or semicolon to the chip's `end`, which holds it on the chip's line and draws it close to the frame, and a chip in text takes no brackets, being its own frame: "פרטים ." and "( פרטים )." read as stray marks (reader rounds 3 and 5, 2026-11-07; kit guard 20). **Nothing out of line, nothing cut.** Pieces of one row share one top line, a word stays beside its figure, a name in the header does not wrap, and no box clips its text (2026-10-24: "this is misaligned. check for all other misaligned fields and fix them"; 2026-10-25: "the search bar is clipping"). `tools/alone-check.mjs` finds any mark, and any full stop, comma or bracket, alone at the start or end of a line on every page at four widths, and runs before a change to a head, a link, a figure or a sentence that ends in a chip merges.

**Check.** `tools/pick-check.mjs` taps every row of a page's linked lists and reads where each list's top stands. A builder may run the movement check (`tools/box-check.mjs`), which sets each input to several values (the choices, a slider's ends, a large typed number) and compares the boxes of the parts that must stay (column widths, block heights, the tapped row's top); the result goes in the builder's report. A large shift, or one with no reason, is a finding, fixed in the kit piece, not on the page; a small shift is better than a blank gap kept for a rare case (guidelines/design.md, "Little movement").

### 2.6 Shape, elevation, focus, motion

- **Radius:** `rounded-sm` 4 px (tags), `rounded-md` 8 px (fields, buttons, chips), `rounded-lg` 12 px (cards, popovers), `rounded-xl` 20 px (top corners of a bottom sheet), `rounded-full` (state chips, slider thumb).
- **Elevation:** surfaces get lighter as they rise (`background`, `card`, `popover`) and a 1 px `border` marks their edges. Shadow (`shadow-lg`) only under sheets and popovers, to lift them off the page.
- **Focus:** a 2 px `ring` outline offset by 2 px on `:focus-visible`, on every control.
- **Motion:** `--duration-fast` 120 ms (press, toggles), `--duration-base` 200 ms (sheets, popovers, a chip's colour), `--duration-fold` 280 ms (every fold opens and closes by height and fades, in the shared stylesheet, and its chevron turns with it), `--duration-chart` 400 ms (graphs and numbers moving to their new values), easing `ease-standard`. All durations become zero when the device asks for reduced motion.
- **Stacking:** sheets and popovers use the browser's top layer (`<dialog>`, `popover`); only the sticky header needs `--z-sticky`.

### 2.7 How numbers, dates and identifiers are written

Formatting always goes through `Intl` with `he-IL`; never hand-built strings, and tests compare `Intl` output with `Intl` output (it contains invisible direction marks).

| Kind | Form | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Shekel amount | `1,234.50 ₪` | `Intl.NumberFormat('he-IL', { style: 'currency', currency: 'ILS' })`; comma thousands, point decimals; precision per method §13, display rounding below |
| Dollar amount | `7.00 $`, a price per share `0.0035 $` | `Intl.NumberFormat('he-IL', { style: 'currency', currency: 'USD' })`, two decimals, up to four for a price per share (units `usd`, `usd_per_trade`, `usd_per_share`) |
| Euro and pound amount | `25.00 €`, `5.00 £` | `Intl.NumberFormat('he-IL', { style: 'currency', currency: 'EUR' })` (or `'GBP'`), two decimals (units `eur`, `gbp_per_trade`) |
| Percent | `1.05%` | data stores percent numbers (`1.05` means 1.05%), so divide by 100 for `style: 'percent'` |
| Date | `22.10.2026` | day first, dots, `day: '2-digit', month: '2-digit', year: 'numeric'`; never ISO or a slash form on the page |
| Report period | `ספטמבר 2026` | `month: 'long', year: 'numeric'` |
| Tax year | `2026` | a plain year, isolated |
| Fund number, ISIN, security number | `7978`, `IE00B5BMR087` | strings, never number-formatted (no thousands comma), LTR, tabular, slashed zero |
| Rule and question IDs | `TXS-09`, `TXK OQ3` | LTR, as written in `il-finance` |
| Chart tick | `50 אלף ₪` | compact form; a scale mark, not a figure: ticks carry a `data-scale` attribute, and the provenance crawl (architecture 3.7 item 5) allows digits inside it. Axis text is spelled in visual order inside a left-to-right isolate (`₪ אלף 50`), drawn `direction=ltr`, `text-anchor=start`, because WebKit reorders mixed Hebrew and digits otherwise |

Every number, amount, date and identifier inside Hebrew text is wrapped in an isolating element (`<bdi>` or `dir="ltr"` on its own span), so "25%" or a security number cannot be reordered by the bidi algorithm. The provenance component does this for every figure.

**Display rounding of amounts** (display only; method §13 unchanged). A computed, entered or parameter shekel amount of 1,000 ₪ or more shows whole shekels, and a whole amount of any size shows no agorot (`10,000 ₪`, `0 ₪`); a sourced amount of 1,000 ₪ or more shows no `.00` either (`83,641 ₪`; fresh readers, 2026-11-08, read "83,641.00 ₪" as false precision) and keeps the agorot its source prints (`5,645.29 ₪`); tariffs and fees under 1,000 ₪ (sourced, not parameters) and anything under 1,000 ₪ that has agorot keep two decimals; table cells (`size="cell"`) keep two decimals. The right-to-left mark and the no-break space of the `Intl` output are removed so a copied amount is digits, a space and ₪; the element isolates the number (`bdi dir=ltr`).

**One number parser.** Every typed number goes through `parseNumber` (`lib/format.ts`): spaces (also no-break and thin), ₪, % and direction marks are stripped; a single comma followed by one or two digits is a decimal point ("2,5" is 2.5); a comma followed by three digits is thousands ("12,000"); with both separators the last one is the decimal point ("1.234,5", "1,234.5"); anything else is not a number.

**Date and month inputs.** Native date and month inputs are `dir="ltr"` with the text at the end, and the chosen value is shown beside the picker in the `Intl` form (`10.2026`, `22.10.2026`), so the page never shows the browser's locale form.

### 2.9 Copy conventions

- Address the reader with the plural imperative ("בחרו", "הזינו", "הקישו") or impersonally ("אפשר", "יש"), never one gender (guidelines/wording.md, "Word choice"; copy 10.2). It is gender-neutral and the common Israeli web form.
- Institution, product and track names appear exactly as the source spells them (data-schema §1); the site's explanation sits beside them, never in their place. A long name wraps and is never cut with an ellipsis, because the exact name is how people recognise it; the same holds for a bank's product name and for the negotiation text (7.4).
- No internal code in a line the reader reads: a rule's (`KGL-02`), a parameter's (`P-05`), a formula's (`F-21`) or a method assumption's (`A-01`). The words carry it: the rules behind a fact are D48's chip "חוקים וכללים · N" (one rule opens its card at once); a row that is the rule itself has the rule's title as its link; a formula's or a parameter's chip says its name; an open question's says "פרטים". A code stands only inside a rule's own card or sheet (its heading "id · title", D25, marked `data-code-ok`), on the rules page (`/rules`), where a code typed in the field opens its rule, and in addresses and anchors. The site search still finds a rule by its code, though its line does not print it. `site/scripts/rule-codes.mjs` fails the build on a code in what a built page shows outside those places; `tools/code-scan.mjs` counts them per kind of page.
- The wording of every fixed label (the number states, strength labels, dimension marks, "unknown", real and nominal, reset, the table view of a chart, the not-on-the-site state, the near-duplicate resolutions, the legend hint) is in `docs/copy.md` and `site/app/copy.ts`.

### 3.2 Provenance

Called for by the standing rule "every number traces to a source", D3, D7, D10, 3.8 "next to every rule-dependent number, a link that opens the rule", and architecture 3.5 and 3.7. These are the most-used components on the site (`components/provenance.tsx`).

**Num** (the provenance number). The only way to put a number on a page. Takes a value object, never a bare number.

- Renders as a `button` (so a keyboard reaches it) holding the formatted value in an isolating span with `tabular-nums`, then any flags. Its accessible name says the value and its state ("1.05%, ממקור, פרטים"). A transparent pseudo-element extends the hit area without changing the line spacing: 11 px above and below the figure and never less than 44 px tall, for every figure (guidelines/design.md), and a second one, exactly the figure's own box, sits above every such area. So a figure in running text keeps its 44 px, and where its area reaches the line above or below, the figure on that line still takes its own click (independent check, 2026-11-07: "0.7%" opened the "5 שנים" sheet; re-check 2: an area that grew only sideways took 44 px from about 1,200 lone figures). Two figures closer than 44 px share the space between them; neither takes the other's box. A term's area follows the same rule. Kit guard 19.
- State styles: sourced, a solid `--num-underline-thickness` underline in `sourced`; computed, a dotted `--num-underline-thickness` underline in `computed-line`; entered, `text-entered` on `bg-entered-subtle` with `rounded-sm` and 0.15em inline padding, no underline. A field's value (`field: true`) is no button and has no style: plain text. Underline offset `--num-underline-offset`.
- Flags, after the value (at the inline end): stale, a 14 px `Clock` in `caution`; under a caution notice that already says the figures' source is old, `StaleSaid` leaves the clock out of every figure inside (the accessible name and the sheet still say it), as on the track browser and a company's track list, where the notice covers every figure of the list. Secondary strength, or institution strength out of its own context (D31: a company's figure about its own product, shown on that product, carries no icon and its sheet names the company's document): a 14 px caution `IconAlert` before the digits (never between a number and its unit word; `select-none`), where the strength word ("משני", "לפי החברה") is in the accessible name and the popover and the legend, not in the line (the word stays as a tag only on strength labels, below); a computed figure carries the icon only at figure size, and its sheet names the weak inputs; a sourced figure whose `effective_from` is after the page date carries the same icon, and at figure size "נכנס לתוקף ב־{date}"; inside an `upcoming` callout (3.9), which already says its rules start later, the figure leaves that icon out (the accessible name and the sheet still say the date; founder, 2026-11-08). A figure its record doubts (`SiteValue.doubt`, a bank's `doubt_he`): the same icon and the digits in `caution-foreground`, with no tint behind them (`num-doubt`; founder, 2026-11-09); "יש ספק" is in the accessible name, and the sheet opens with a caution callout titled "יש ספק" that says the doubt. The doubt is the value's own, so every place that shows the figure warns, with no chip or note beside it (founder, 2026-11-08: "make it a bit yellow and the warning inside it"). Site parameter: nothing inline (the popover says it).
- **A caution only where there is a doubt.** The amber icon and the clock say a figure is doubtful or old. An explanation, a kind or a company's figure about its own product never wears one, and the meaning of a label is not worded as a warning (founder, 2026-10-25: "a warning seems like we have incomplete data"; 2026-11-05: "מזה ההזהרה הזאת"). A doubt, a lock or a badge that every row or card of a list would carry is said once, above the list, as `StaleSaid` does (2026-11-03: "remove the warnings, keep the one at the top of the page").
- **A note sits on the figure it is about.** A caution that says a figure may be too high or too low goes on that figure, not on another figure that moves the other way because of it: "the current account counted here may be too high" belongs on the account's cost, and on the value left it would say the wrong direction (independent check 2, 2026-11-06, BANK-35). When one run feeds several figures, each figure takes only the notes about itself.
- `value: null`: shows "לא ידוע" in `muted-foreground`, same underline as its state; the popover gives the reason from `notes`.
- **The sheet says what the figure says.** A value that is not a number (a word, yes or no, or empty) heads its sheet with the `display` word it was shown as; a record's raw value ("none", "unlinked_15") never reaches a reader, and every such value also has a Hebrew word in `numbers.enums` for a figure shown without one. A number's sheet keeps its digits. (Independent check 3, 2026-11-07: every deposit card's linkage and tax sheets headed in English; guard 14 in `kit/guards.test.ts`.)
- Sizes: inline (inherits text size), cell (tables), figure (`text-figure`, weight 600, with a state tag below it, because a headline number deserves its label in words).
- When the value changes after an input change, the digits move to the new value over `--duration-chart` (`CountingFigures`, section 11). A figure with its own wording passes `display` as a function of the moving value, so it counts too.

**Provenance popover.** Opens on tap or Enter from any Num. Uses the native `popover` attribute, so it works in the pre-rendered page before any script loads (architecture 3.5).

- Header: state icon and label; strength label; stale flag.
- Body by state. Sourced: the value with its unit, the source as a SourceBlock (3.8a) directly under the figure's name, publisher, effective dates, rule references, notes; for a site parameter also its rationale. The source's title shows when it is Hebrew, else the link's host with the English title behind "שם המקור באנגלית"; English working notes are never shown; a rule without a source link says so; a value valid for exactly one calendar year reads "תקף לשנת {year}". A source line wraps at every width and is never cut (fresh readers, round 3, 2026-11-07: "5.50 ₪" a month had no source in view but the cap's rule under it, and the order's name and date were cut with "…"). Entered: "הזנתם את הערך הזה.", the value it replaced as a Num, and the reset button.
- Footer: what a flag means, in one line (for a stale number: its normal cadence and the date of the last value).
- **A source opens where it says it.** A link to a cited source opens at the cited words: a web page by text fragment (`#:~:text=`, at the quoted words as the page prints them), a PDF at its page (`#page=N`; Chrome's PDF viewer does not follow a text fragment), a dataset at this fund's own record rather than the dataset's front page (founder, 2026-10-30: "we should link to the direct PDF page or a highlighted text"; 2026-11-03: "for each fund link directly to its data?"; 2026-11-07: Psagot's price list opened at its top, not at the quoted fee). Every such link is built by `sourceHref` (`site/app/lib/sources.ts`), never from a raw address; a guard in `kit/guards.test.ts` fails an `href` made of a raw `url`, and `tools/quote-fragments.mjs` keeps the words each web page opens at (`data/sources/fragments.json`), only where they stand on the page exactly. A quote that is a translation or a paraphrase opens the page at its top, so a quote shown beside a link is the page's own words wherever it can be.

**State tag.** Icon plus label in `text-xs` weight 500, `rounded-sm`, in the state colour on its subtle fill (sourced: `muted`). Used under figures, in the legend, in popover headers and on inputs that were changed.

**State legend** (`NumberLegend`). One line per page that shows numbers: a lead, "כך מסומן מספר:", then each state's own word drawn in its real style ("ממקור" with the solid underline, "מחושב" dotted, "שהזנתם" in entered ink; shown as the page header in 3.1 says). ### 3.3 Inputs

Called for by 3.1 of the product ("every value behind it can be changed on the page: amount now, monthly deposit, horizon, return, inflation, age and employment type"; "a changed value is shown as entered by you"; "values follow them to the catalogue and the goal comparison"), the real-terms principle (product.md §2) and architecture 3.9 (sliders with a typed value, steppers, bottom sheets). The fields are the kit's (`kit/fields.tsx`, section 11).

**Field (anatomy shared by every input).** The label line above (`text-sm`, weight 500), optional hint under the control (`text-xs muted-foreground`), the control. What the reader types looks the same as the default: no tag, no blue edge or ink (founder, 2026-11-08). A slider field's number is the exception in colour only: it is always in the slider's blue, whatever it holds, so it still says nothing about who set it. At the far end of the label line, a small muted reset, "↺ חזרה ל־{default}" (`ResetToStart`: `RotateCcw`, `text-xs`, the default as the field shows it, "חזרה ל־10", "חזרה ל־12,000 ₪"), shows only while the value differs from the default (the field's `start`); it keeps its room while hidden, so the label, the box and the slider never move when it comes, and its tap area reaches 44 px. A field with no default (an optional age) has none. Errors replace the hint in `destructive` with the control's edge in `destructive`. A field is exactly two children, the label line and a body (`.field-body`: the control, then its error or hint, and note), so that fields side by side can share rows.

**Field row.** Fields side by side sit in a `.field-row` grid (the timeline's return and inflation, the pension and study-fund pairs). Every field in it is a subgrid over two of the grid's rows, labels in the first and bodies in the second, so the boxes start at the same height whatever the labels' line count; a one-line label sits at the bottom of the label row, against its box. A slot that holds a field with buttons beside it (a stepper, "לא יודעים") is a `.field-cell`: the same two-row subgrid, the field in its first column and each button in the box row of its own column at the end; the cell and the field share the field's 6 px row gap. Rule for new forms: fields that share a row go in a `.field-row`, never in a bare grid or flex row, and a wrapper between the row and its field is `display: contents`.

**Number field.** `type="text"`, `inputmode="decimal"`, `dir="ltr"` with the text aligned right so it lines up with the label; unit (₪, %, שנים) as a fixed suffix at the inline end. Height `h-field`, `rounded-md`, edge `input`, fill `card`, 16 px text. Formats with thousands separators on blur, accepts pasted values with commas or ₪ through the one shared parser of 2.7 (comma decimals included), and adopts text typed into the pre-rendered field before the script loaded. An entered value looks the same as the default.

**Slider field.** For amount, monthly deposit, return, inflation and horizon. The label row holds the label at the start, its reset at the end of the label's part (beside the box, or on a second line of the same 44 px row where both do not fit) and a compact number field at the end; the slider sits under it. That number floats: no box, a thin `input` line under it (red on an error), the figure in `slider` blue at weight 600, the unit muted beside it. A typed value the box refuses shows its message in the reset's place. Track 12 px with rounded ends in `slider-track` with an `input` edge, the filled part in `slider` (the entered blue), filling from the right (inline start) as native RTL range inputs do; a solid thumb of 24 px in `slider` with a 44 px hit area. While the slider is held, the filled part and the thumb turn `slider-active`, a step deeper. The typed field accepts any value the engine accepts, including values outside the slider's range. The ranges are stored with the illustrative scenario in `data/parameters/illustrative-scenario.json` (monthly deposit 0 to 10,000 NIS in steps of 100, years 1 to 40); they limit only the slider, not what can be typed.

**Segmented control.** For two to four exclusive options: employment type, and real or nominal (real is the default). Full width on a phone, `rounded-md`; the pressed segment is a solid `bg-primary` fill with a check icon (`aria-pressed`), and wherever a segmented control stands above a heading it is `max-w-sm` at the inline start. Radix ToggleGroup with `dir="rtl"`.

**Inputs sheet.** A bottom sheet on a phone (`<dialog>`, `overlay` behind, swipe or X to close, the chart stays visible above it at reduced height where space allows); at `lg`, a panel at the start (right) of the chart instead of a sheet. Fields in the order of 3.1's list. Changes apply as the person types; there is no submit button.

**Button.** Primary (`bg-primary text-primary-foreground`, one per screen), secondary (`bg-secondary`), quiet (text only, `accent` wash on press), destructive (a quiet button in `destructive` text, for the "מחיקה" of a delete confirmation; its own variant, because `text-destructive` added to a quiet button loses to its `text-link`), icon (44 px square). Height `h-field`; a secondary button and a `LinkButton` are 40 px on a computer (`lg`), with the 44 px `tap-area` (2.5). Labels follow the style guide (copy 10.5): a noun of action ("שמירה") or an infinitive ("לשמור את ההשוואה"), never the imperative.

**Link.** `text-link` with an underline on hover and focus; external links (sources) add a mirrored `ExternalLink` icon and open in a new tab.

**What acts looks like it acts, and does what it looks like.** A link is in the link colour, a button has its edge or fill, and an id or a row that opens something looks openable (founder, 2026-10-25: "you cant click F-80 for some reason"; 2026-11-03: "make the < next to the track a appear like a link"). What does nothing is never drawn as a button, a chip or a round plus (2026-10-30: "the הסתרת החוקים והכללים is a label button across the site"). A control does what its look promises: arrows move the way they point, section names that look like tabs change the view rather than jump down the page, a picker adds rather than replaces, and no action is found only by accident (2026-10-24: "There is an up and down arrow but it moves left-right"; 2026-11-06, on another site: "i accidentally realized double click adds it"). A table sorts by its column heads (2026-11-03: "instead of the sort buttons make the table clickable"). The actions a person expects are there: what they build can be renamed, deleted and dragged into order, and a slider's thumb drags (2026-10-24: "There is no delete button ... No rename button").

**Ask only what the site cannot know.** A field asks for the person's own numbers, never for what the site can look up (an exchange rate, a price), and never "who are you" to sort the person into a group whose answer changes little for most people (founder, 2026-10-25: "I dont want the user to input the exchange rates. the site should provide them"; 2026-11-04: "never do 'who are you' in this case").

**The reader's own items.** Where a page lets the reader add an item of their own (a fund, an index), the item takes the same settings the site's items have, each set by the reader: the site puts no label, kind or default on it that the reader did not choose (2026-11-05: "who said its צוברת? and who said its חשיפה לדולר?").

**A form has room.** A form of more than a few fields opens in a space of its own, its fields full size and grouped, never squeezed into a narrow panel or left a bare stack of boxes (2026-11-05: "make the interface nice, not like a basic form"; "can you make it more easy to navigate and set? the screen is tiny").

### 3.4 Timeline chart

Called for by 3.1 of the product ("a timeline of money in, growth, fees and tax out, with the rules it used listed beside it"), method F-67, D9, architecture 3.9 and gap 8.6. `components/chart/TimelineChart.tsx`, inside `components/timeline/Timeline.tsx`.

- **Form.** An area chart over time. Time runs left to right (gap 8.6), from now to the horizon. One value axis in shekels, real by default, nominal when the segmented control says so. Cumulative contributions are a neutral line over the bands. The exact series are F-67's.
- **Marks.** 2 px lines; bands separated by a 2 px gap in the card colour; the net line in `chart-net` on top. Fee types take the fee steps in a fixed order; tax during and at exit take the tax steps.
- **Labels.** Direct labels at the horizon end of each band that has room for one; a legend above the chart, in reading order (right to left) as chips with swatches, names only the bands too thin to carry their own label, so no band is named twice (section 12, rule 3). - **Blocked parts.** Where the method cannot compute a part (for example exit tax blocked by an open rule, method §12), that band is replaced by a 45° hatch in `chart-blocked` over `blocked-subtle`, with a `Lock` label naming the open question. The rest of the chart still draws.
- **Entered return.** When the return is entered by the person, the net line and the top edge are drawn dashed in `chart-entered`, and the caption says the projection rests on a return they entered (D9).
- **Readout.** Touch and drag on a phone, hover on PC: a 1 px crosshair in `chart-crosshair` and a readout card pinned above the plot (not under the finger): the year as its heading, then one row per series, label at the start and value at the end, any tag after its value (design review C2). - **Table view.** A disclosure, "הצגה כטבלה", with one row per year and one column per series, every cell a Num. It is its own full-width card under the chart-and-inputs row (both columns at `lg`), cells do not wrap, and the wrapper carries an edge fade and shadow as a scroll cue (also on the comparison and fee tables); on a phone the card follows the chart card.
- **Rules used.** Under the chart on a phone, beside it at `lg`: the rule references the run used, from the engine's record.
- **States.** Pre-rendered at build on P-05 (the illustrative scenario), so the chart is there before any script. While the worker recomputes for longer than 150 ms, the plot dims to 60% opacity; when the result arrives, shapes and numbers move to the new values over `--duration-chart`.
- **Size.** Full width; 240 px tall on a phone, 320 px at `lg`.

On a guide page with a visual of its own (section 10) the timeline is folded below it as "שחקו עם המספרים שלכם"; a page without one keeps it open in "מה זה".

## 11. Kit (the shared components)

The founder asked for "custom components so we have consistent UI and an easier and faster time programming, like the equivalent of a class, but for UI components", after two layout bugs in a day (a sentence with a number laid out as a flex column, one piece to a line; a closed fold inside an open fold that read as open). The kit is `site/app/components/kit/`, one file per component or small family, re-exported from `kit/index.ts`. Import from `components/kit`, never from a neighbouring feature. The gallery at `/kit` (pre-rendered, `noindex`, in no menu and no search; every number on it is a labelled example) shows each component in its states, right to left, with its name beside it, and is where a piece is checked at 1440 and 390 px (guidelines/design.md, step 4); how it looks is the founder's to judge. The survey that found the duplicates is `8dd1f013:research/specs/2026-10-25-ui-kit/survey.md`.

**The rule.** A new pattern goes into the kit first, with its gallery entry and, where it can be named, a guard in `kit/guards.test.ts` or a ratchet in `kit/ratchets.test.ts`, never into a feature folder; then the feature uses it (guidelines/design.md, steps 1 and 2). A component takes the *choices* as props (`pad`, `size`, `tone`, `scroll`) and never as an overriding class: the site has no class merger, so two classes for one property are decided by the order of the generated stylesheet, not by the order in `class` (the `p-0 md:p-0` on the guide's key/value card never applied for that reason). Each component started as the best existing version; a piece that nothing in the kit fits is added to the kit.

| Component | When to use it |
|---|---|
| `Button`, `LinkButton` | `Button` for an action (`primary` once per screen, `quiet` for a text action, `destructive` for a quiet one that deletes, `icon` for a 44 px icon), `LinkButton` for a place to go. `flush` takes the side padding off a `quiet` or `destructive` button, so its words line up with the text above and below them; never `!px-0`. `size="sm"`: a `quiet` or `destructive` button at 13 px with no height of its own and a 44 px tap area, for a text action inside a line of small text (the line of numbers on the indices page's plan card); an `icon` button 36 px and round in the muted ink, its tap area still 44 px, for one that floats over content (a sheet's back and close). A `LinkButton`'s words often carry a name and may wrap on a phone: its height is a minimum, so a second line grows the button instead of spilling out of it. |
| `Tag` | A one-to-three-word label with a tone (state, kind, flag). Never colour alone: give it a word or an icon. `shape="pill"` is fully round with more room at the sides (the `primary` and `outline` tones are pills by default); `size="sm"` sets its words at 14 px and normal weight, for labels among a row's own words (what a deposit lets you do before its end). `wrap` for a label that holds a name, such as a rule card's "של {company}": it breaks onto a second line instead of running past a phone's edge; the card's scope tag names the companies the way the rule chip does (the page's company or the first, and "ועוד N"). |
| `StepTag` | A tag on an ordered scale of four: how soon the money is back, step 1 "at any moment" to step 4 "at the end of the term". A grey pill with the word; only the icon takes the step's colour (`access-*`, teal to red), so the tag stays calm beside the figures and is not read as a flag. Always an icon and a word. For an order, not a measure: never a bar or slices for it (section 12, "Reading the visuals"). |
| `Callout` | A boxed note under a result: `caution`, `blocked`, `info` or `upcoming` (3.9). |
| `Card` (`pad`) | The one raised surface: a card, a list row (`as="li"`), a side panel (`as="aside"`). `pad`: `default` p-4 md:p-6, `snug` p-4 md:p-5, `roomy` p-5 lg:p-6, `aside` p-5, `tight` p-4, `none`. No page writes `bg-card border border-border rounded-lg` itself. |

**Which choice control.** Pick it by what the reader sees happen when they choose, not by the number of options and not by whether the address changes (founder, 2026-11-10; it replaced a rule by the number of options).
1. **It narrows a list:** the rows shown get fewer, and they are the same kind of rows. One of several values: `ChoiceChips` with `counts`. Filters that are each on or off, independent of one another: `FilterChip`. A parent and its children (a kind of product, then a product of it): `ChoicePair`.
2. **It changes how the same content is shown:** grouping, order, units (today's money or nominal), a chart or a table. `SegmentedControl`.
3. **It answers a question the calculation needs:** the reader's situation or an assumption (years, a kind of rate, an amount, "do you hold X"). `SegmentedControl` for two to four answers short enough to sit side by side on a phone; `TileChoice` when each answer needs a line of what it brings; `ChoiceChips` without counts for many preset values, with `other` for the reader's own.
4. **It goes somewhere else:** another page, or a view of its own inside the page that shows another kind of content. `PageTabs` for pages, `TabStrip` for views inside a page, or links (`ChipList` for a row of them).

Where a phone has no room for a row of chips, a `SelectField` stands in for them (a company's products, the sort of a list whose computer heads sort it). Choosing never moves what is below (2.5a): chips keep one width chosen or not, a count has a fixed slot, and `ChoicePair`'s second level is a tray of one height.

**Lists of products** (founder; the design and how it got there: `8dd1f013:research/specs/2026-10-27-company-fund-card/spec.md`). These hold wherever the site lists several products of one kind (tracks, funds, deposits, accounts):

- **A table, not a stack of cards.** On a desktop the products are rows of one table (`ProductTable`); on a phone each product is one row under the next. A card with a facts column left half of every card empty.
- **The identifier is the link.** Where a product has an official page elsewhere (a fund's page on Maya), its number is that link, with the external icon (`1148808 ↗`), and its accessible name says where it leads. No separate "בדף הקרן במאיה" line. A link inside the site ends with the drill-in chevron (›), a link out with the external icon (↗).
- **The column head carries the words; the cell holds the short value.** "המדד" in the head and "ת"א-125 ›" in the cell, never "עוקבת אחרי מדד …" on every row. On a phone, where there is no head, the row says the one word ("מדד ת"א-125 ›").
- **Columns as wide as their content.** The number, link and figure columns shrink to what they hold; the name takes the rest, so its labels fit on one line.
- **A kind is a label, not a row or a column.** What a product is or does (how a fund follows its index, whether it pays dividends, its currency, a tax that follows from it) is a label chip that opens a sheet with its rule, from one shared set (`fundLabels()` for funds). A yes/no or a kind never gets its own column.
- **Nothing that repeats.** No figure that says again what a label says (a fund's currency ceiling beside "חשיפה לדולר"), no column whose value is the same on every row (every index fund's stock grade is 4), no line under a link that explains what the linked page explains. A label true of every row of a group shows once, in the group's head (D60); a list with no groups gives such a label one head line over the list.
- **Every column helps choose among the rows.** A column, a figure or a line stays only where it tells the rows apart in a way that changes the choice; one that only describes is left out or becomes a label (founder, 2026-11-05: "are you sure we show the interesting information in the table? maybe other columns would be better?").
- **Every row follows one pattern.** The rows of one list carry the same lines: a line that only some rows would have is left out, and two rows that need telling apart are told apart by what every row already shows, such as the fund number or a label (2026-11-03: "why does only אלטשולר and אנליסט have a עוקב text underneath? i dont want it remove it.").
- **A line under a row says what the figure uses.** A secondary line under a row (a group's price, a promotion) says in its own words whether the row's figure counts it, and which of its numbers that is (2026-11-04: "its not clear which of these is being used - 0.07 or 0.15").
- **Every option a person has.** A list holds every option a person really has for the holding (D17), foreign funds and every house that sells them included; a row that cannot be priced says what the place does offer and opens it, not only what is missing (2026-10-31: "who said we are buying israeli funds? what about iShares?"; "when i click the broker itself i want to switch to its options that it does provide").
- **Familiar names first.** A list that is not ranked by a figure puts the names people know first, by a sourced measure such as assets or members, and the small institutions in a group of their own lower down (2026-10-25: "honestly i have never heard of the[m] before").
- **Who offers what.** A list across companies keeps the companies in view, its rows grouped under or named by their company, so the reader sees who offers what and not only a pile of tracks (2026-11-03: "i want to have a notion of the different companies not only the different tracks").
- **A column of one number state carries its mark in the head (D60).** Where every figure in a column is sourced, or every one computed, the column head word carries the underline of that state and one line above the list says what it means; the cells stay plain and still open their source or calculation when tapped. A cell whose state differs keeps its own mark; entered numbers keep their blue, flags keep their icon. Every column that holds a figure has a head.
- **A very long list opens folded.** A list of over a hundred products under their companies (the track browser) starts with each company as one row, its count and its money, the first open so the reader sees what a row holds; on a phone each product under an open company is one line (`ProductPhoneFold`). A search or a chosen filter shows its matches open, with nothing to open. Opening a company moves only what is below it, and gently (`useFlip`); nothing above moves (D62, D63).
- **A ceiling is not a holding.** A limit a product declares (a fund's exposure profile, "4A") is never drawn the way a measured share is drawn (the track table's rings, F-87 and F-88). If shown at all, it is words.

## 12. Charts

The rules every chart and visual on the site follows. The charts themselves: the timeline (3.4), the fee-drag chart (7.3), the guide-page visuals (10), and the kit's `ChangeChart`, `PileStory`, `StairsChart`, `PairBars`, `YearBars`, `CostStack` and `PeriodStrip` (11).

**Reading the visuals** (founder, from his choices on the daily money-market guide, where a filter over cards, a scatter plot, four equal slices and a precise time axis were each turned down for a table with coloured tags and bars). Every visual follows these:

1. **Draw only true numbers.** A real quantity (a rate, an amount, a time in months) gets a mark on its scale, with its figure printed on the mark itself, or right beside it where it does not fit; a legend never carries the figures in the marks' place (founder, 2026-11-06: "put the amounts on the bar itself"), except for a part too small for its amount (guidelines/charts.md, "Stacked bar"). A category or an order (how soon the money is back, a kind of product) gets words, an icon and a colour, never a bar or a meter: equal slices invent a unit the data does not have.
2. **Colour carries meaning, and there is enough of it.** Each colour says one thing the reader can name (teal is the rate, warmer is held longer, stripes are a range); grey on grey says nothing, and a colour that only ranks or decorates is left out. The flags of 3.2 keep their meaning: a scale that borrows their hues stays away from numbers and always has a word and an icon.
3. **Every encoding is said once.** Whatever a colour, a pattern or a shape means is said on the screen, even when every figure is also printed: by a label on the mark, or by a legend for the marks that cannot carry one, never by both, and not again in tiles under the chart (founder, 2026-10-26: "remove the legend of the rings because its just a duplicate of the inside"; 2026-10-27: "remove the top legend"). Marks that share a meaning share one legend: a list whose rows each carry the same kind of bar has one legend above the list, never one per row (founder, 2026-11-06: "the legend is repeated on every broker? why? just put it somewhere central"), as a label true of every row shows once in the group's head.
4. **Show the whole comparison; do not make the reader operate it.** A guide page shows its product among its alternatives, all at once, with the page's product marked (a tinted row and a tag). No filter that hides or fades rows, no control the reader must use before the visual says anything. An analysis section may have a control (the indices page's change chart has a marker) when its first state already shows the whole comparison.
5. **The plainest mark that shows the number.** A bar, a line or a dot on a scale; no coins, towers or pictures standing in for an amount. The care goes into what is shown, not into the shape of the mark (founder, 2026-11-02: "אני פשוט לא ממש רואה ערך בכדורים. למה לא BAR?").
6. **No forced picture, and no what-if no one would plan on.** A visual is drawn only where the truth fits it; where the truth is more complex than a picture, words or a table carry it and the page has no visual (2026-10-25: "the truth is just more complex than that. so for now dont"; 2026-11-01: "you are forcing a visualization into something that has nothing to do with it"). A scenario is one a person would plan on, and a conclusion goes no further than the data: the same fall every year for twelve years, or the past read as the future, is not shown as a likely outcome, and a case few people meet is not put at the centre (2026-10-29: "we shouldnt draw conclusions from the past, no?"; 2026-10-25: "if its 1% of people, we should even make it less central").

Each mark on the screen can be named in one sentence from what is on the screen alone. The chart check (`site/scripts/chart-check/`, `npm run chart-check`) measures every chart before anything goes live; how a chart looks is the founder's to judge, not a screenshot review's (guidelines/working.md, "4. Check before it goes live").

**Colour inside a chart** (guidelines/charts.md). Every legend identity has its own clearly different hue, checked pair by pair with the palette validator (normal-vision worst pair at least 15 ΔE, colour-blind at least 8, or 6 where a word sits on the mark) and by the chart check before anything goes live. Fresh readers, who run only when the founder asks, once saw a validated rose and magenta as "one pink family". Distinctness inside a chart comes before one meaning per colour across the page (founder: "i'd rather have two conflicting colors on opposite sites of the pages than a graph with all its lines the same color"): within one chart or panel a hue means one thing, a distant section may give it another, and each page's spec carries its colour table. The categorical palette for comparing options on one chart avoids the state hues (blue, violet) and the status hues. Red, amber and green stay status colours, never a series. Blue and violet stay reserved for entered and computed numbers. Every chart's colours are run through the validator together, as drawn. `kit/ratchets.test.ts` checks each chart's roles pair by pair on every run, by the validator's method (`lib/palette-check.ts`): a new chart adds its roles to the list there, and today's pairs below the line are listed by name until they are apart.

**Axes and labels.** Time runs left to right, and so does every other axis (V3). Value-axis labels sit at the plot's left edge, just above their gridline, with a card-colour halo (`text-xs chart-text`); the right of the plot is kept for the direct labels and the horizon, and at the left the bands are still thin, so the labels do not sit on colour. Three to five gridlines, compact form (2.7). Year ticks run along the bottom at whole steps of 1, 2, 5 or 10 years from today (`niceYearTicks(horizon)`, about four ticks; the horizon end is not always a tick, its marker still marks it). Value labels are drawn after the lines, with their card-colour halo, on every chart, so no line runs through them. Names and numbers are written on the chart, with no table needed to decode it.

**Around every chart** the kit's `ChartLegend`, `ChartReadout` and `ChartTable` (a table view of the same numbers); its motion is the kit's glide (`useGlidingSeries` and the rest) and its pointer the kit's zoom and pick (`useYearZoom`, `useChartPick`). A series that is not drawn has no legend item, label, readout row or table column.
