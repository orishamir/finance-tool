## 1. Principles

| # | Principle | From |
|---|---|---|
| V1 | **Provenance is the visual language.** Every number shows whether it is sourced, computed or entered by you, and one tap shows where it came from. Stale, secondary-source and blocked states are visible in place, never only in a footnote. | CLAUDE.md, D3, D7, D10 |

| V3 | **Right to left throughout.** Layout, reading order, sliders, disclosure and navigation flow from the right. Exceptions: time on a chart runs left to right (founder, gap 8.6), and numbers, identifiers and Latin names are isolated left-to-right runs. | CLAUDE.md, architecture 3.5 |

| V7 | **Polish is a requirement.** Motion when values change, careful type, charts drawn by us. | K12 |

## 2. Tokens

### 2.1 Tiers and where they live

`site/app/theme.css`, imported by `site/app/app.css`.

1. **Palette** (`--palette-<hue>-<step>`): raw oklch ramps. Hue ramps share one lightness ladder (step 50 is 0.975 … step 950 is 0.24), so the same step behaves the same in every hue. Components never use these.
2. **Semantic roles** (`--background`, `--entered`, `--chart-fee`, …): what a colour means, one value each, since the site is dark only. Surface and text names follow shadcn/ui (`--background`, `--card`, `--muted-foreground`, `--primary`, `--ring`, …) so components copied from it work unchanged.
3. **Utilities**: `@theme inline` maps each role to a Tailwind colour (`bg-card`, `text-entered`, `border-input`, `fill-chart-fee`). Tailwind's default palette is removed (`--color-*: initial`), so a class can only name a role.

Rules:

- Components use role utilities or `var(--role)`, never a palette step and never a literal colour.
- **Dark only** (founder, 2026-10-22). There is no light theme and no theme switch. `color-scheme: dark` on the root makes native controls, scrollbars and the popover backdrop dark too. The palette keeps its light steps, unused.
- Layout uses logical properties and utilities only (`ps-`, `pe-`, `ms-`, `me-`, `inset-s-`, `inset-e-`, `border-s`, `rounded-s-`, `text-start`). The `rtl:` variant is for true exceptions such as mirroring an icon.

The tag itself is grey (`muted`); only the icon takes the colour (founder, 2026-10-26, option D of four: a fully coloured tag in teal, green, amber and red read like the flags of 3.2; teal-gold-orange-rose and one strengthening orange were the others) |

Validated with the dataviz validator on the card surface: the three family hues (value, fee, tax) pass every check with all pairs compared (worst colour-blind ΔE 8.5, worst normal-vision ΔE 17.9). The tax steps pass the ordinal checks. Lines and markers clear 3:1 on the card. Every series is also labelled and the chart has a table view, so no series depends on colour alone.

**Fee kinds (2026-10-24, evening; the founder's screenshot of the fee-drag chart: "every legend item has the same colour").** The four fee slots were steps of the orange ramp (700, 600, 500, 400), validated as an ordinal ramp. They are not ordinal: a legend maps a band to a fee kind, which is identity, and as a categorical set the ramp failed the validator (adjacent pairs ΔE 8.1 normal vision, 7.0 colour-blind). Four steps of one hue cannot reach the floor inside the dark lightness band. A first fix kept the slots inside the warm family (orange, gold, terracotta, apricot), which passed on adjacent pairs but not on all pairs, and the founder asked why the colours were still similar: the "one colour per quantity" rule of this section was costing more than it bought on a chart that draws only fees. So the slots are four clearly different hues in a fixed order: orange-500 `oklch(0.64 0.174 48)`, gold-200 `oklch(0.84 0.17 85)`, teal-300 (the ramp's own step), magenta-400 `oklch(0.66 0.17 335)` (two new one-step palette entries, charts only). Validator on the card, all pairs: ΔE 16.1 colour-blind and 20.6 normal (pass), every fill ≥ 3:1; the two light slots sit above the validator's dark-mode lightness band, which guards marks against glare, and are used as fills with a card stroke. Blue and violet stay out: they are the entered and computed number inks beside every chart. Teal-300 is the value family's light step, but no value series is ever drawn on the fee-drag, cost-by-type or cumulative-cost charts; the magenta is 23° from the tax rose, which those charts never draw either. The order is the stack order of 7.3, the type order of 8.3 and the rank order of the cumulative-cost chart.

**Chart audit (2026-10-26).** Every chart's colours were run through the validator together, as drawn. Three pairs failed and were changed: the timeline's contributions line against the net line (above), the fee-drag total line against fee kind 3 (above), and the comparison chart's baseline against the teal steps (6.5: now dotted, since no grey clears all three steps). In the same pass: value labels are drawn after the lines, with their card-colour halo, on every chart; the fee-drag bands are drawn at whole years and the exit month, not month by month (each edge rose and fell between charges and every boundary showed as a saw); the deposit visual's fund lane is the full gold of its bars (it was gold at 55%, a dull olive); ~~the daily money-market guide's ladder puts each row's rate as a dot on one percent axis shared by the four rows~~ (replaced the same day by a table, below: the founder could not tell what the axis's four sections, its two ends and the dot were). Open for the founder: the fee-kind hues follow the size of each fee on the fees page but a fixed kind on the provider page, and the provider page's lines reuse the fee-kind hues for providers next to bars where the same hues mean fee kinds; the pension visual's severance segment wears fee kind 4's magenta.

**Reading the visuals (founder, 2026-10-26).** Drawn from the founder's choices on the daily money-market guide, where a filter over cards, a scatter plot, four equal slices and a precise time axis were each turned down for a table with coloured tags and bars. Every visual follows these:

1. **Draw only true numbers.** A real quantity (a rate, an amount, a time in months) gets a mark on its scale, with its figure printed at the mark. A category or an order (how soon the money is back, a kind of product) gets words, an icon and a colour, never a bar or a meter: equal slices invent a unit the data does not have.
2. **Colour carries meaning, and there is enough of it.** Each colour says one thing the reader can name (teal is the rate, warmer is held longer, stripes are a range); grey on grey says nothing, and a colour that only ranks or decorates is left out. The flags of 3.2 keep their meaning: a scale that borrows their hues stays away from numbers and always has a word and an icon.
3. **Every encoding has a legend.** Whatever a colour, a pattern or a shape means is said on the screen, even when every figure is also printed.
4. **Show the whole comparison; do not make the reader operate it.** A guide page shows its product among its alternatives, all at once, with the page's product marked (a tinted row and a tag). No filter that hides or fades rows, no control the reader must use before the visual says anything.

Before a visual merges, the reading test: for each mark on the screen, one sentence says what it is from what is on the screen alone.

**The daily money-market table (2026-10-26).** The four cash products from the fastest way back to the slowest, in a `CompareTable`: the product (the other three link to their guide pages), when the money is back as a `StepTag` with a line under it where the word leaves out how (the fund's set hour, MF-10 and MF-11; up to 12 months between fixed dates, MF-08), and the annual rate as a `ValueBar` on one scale (the next whole percent above the highest rate), the deposit's range striped from the lowest bank to the highest. A sentence above the table says that waiting does not pay, only while the data says so (`slowerPayNoMore`). Under it a legend (rate, range) and a `NoteList`: before tax, the funds' rate is the site's assumption, the current account's common rate (the same sentence as the current-account page, `CommonRateLine`), and how many of the collected one-year deposits cannot be left before the end (F-86), with a link to each bank's rate.

Not defined yet: a categorical palette for comparing several options on one chart. It arrives with the R2 comparison chart and must avoid the state hues (blue, violet) and the status hues.

### 2.4 Typography

**Faces.** Inter for Latin letters and digits, Heebo for Hebrew, both variable fonts, self-hosted through `@fontsource-variable` (no font CDN, D1). **Approved by the founder on 2026-10-22**, after comparing them on mock R1 screens with Heebo alone, Assistant, Rubik, IBM Plex Sans Hebrew, Noto Sans Hebrew and Open Sans. Stack: `"Inter Variable", "Heebo Variable", Arial, sans-serif`. Inter comes first because it has no Hebrew, so Hebrew falls through to Heebo glyph by glyph; Arial has Hebrew and holds the layout while the web fonts load (`font-display: swap`). The shekel sign comes from Heebo. A Hebrew page downloads about 60 KB of font: Inter's Latin subset and Heebo's Hebrew subset; ~~the other subsets are fetched only if a page uses those characters.~~ Since 2026-10-24 (UX audit) only Inter latin and Heebo hebrew are loaded (own `@font-face`), both preloaded in the HTML head, with an "Inter Fallback" face (Arial with `size-adjust`, ascent and descent set to Inter's metrics) ahead of Arial, so the swap does not move the layout.

Why this pair: Heebo is clear on small screens and matches Inter's proportions; Inter's digits are made for interfaces, with tabular figures and a slashed zero, which matter on a site made of numbers and fund numbers.

**Scale** (Hebrew reads smaller than Latin at the same size, so the scale starts at 16 px for body and runs taller line heights):

| Token | Size | Line height | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| `text-xs` | 13 px | 1.5 | captions, axis ticks, source dates; the smallest Hebrew on the site |
| `text-sm` | 14 px | 1.6 | secondary text, field labels, table cells on a phone, tags |
| `text-base` | 16 px | 1.7 | body, inputs (16 px also stops iOS from zooming into a field) |
| `text-lg` | 18 px | 1.6 | lede, card titles, h3 |
| `text-xl` | 20 px | 1.45 | h2 on a phone |
| `text-2xl` | 24 px | 1.4 | h1 on a phone, h2 on PC |
| `text-3xl` | 30 px | 1.3 | h1 on PC |
| `text-figure` | 32 px | 1.15 | a headline number (digits only, so the tight line height is safe) |

**Weights.** 450 body (400 until 2026-10-24: a half step heavier reads better on large screens; text with an explicit `font-normal` stays at 400); 500 labels, tags and emphasis; 600 headings and headline numbers; 700 not used. One weight per role.

**Root size (2026-10-24, UX audit).** The scale above holds at the default root of 16 px; from 1600 px wide the root is 17 px and from 2200 px it is 18 px, so every rem-based size grows by those steps.

**Hebrew rules.**

- Never letter-space Hebrew; the tracking utilities are removed. Word spacing is 0.05em on body text.
- No italics for emphasis (Hebrew has no true italic); use weight 500.
- Prose is capped at `max-w-measure` (38rem). `ch` measures a Latin zero, so check real Hebrew line lengths in the browser.
- `dir="auto"` on any text the person typed or pasted (catalogue search, uncatalogued names), per element, never on a list wrapper.

**Numbers.** Every number uses `tabular-nums` so values do not jitter while they animate to a new value and columns align. Identifiers (fund numbers, ISINs, security numbers, rule IDs, open-question IDs) also use `slashed-zero`.

### 2.5 Spacing and layout

- **Base unit 4 px** (Tailwind's `--spacing: 0.25rem`): `p-1` is 4 px, `p-4` is 16 px. Use steps 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 12, 16.
- **Named spacing:** `px-gutter` (page side padding, `clamp(1rem, 4vw, 2rem)`: 16 px on a phone, up to 32 px), `gap-section` / `my-section` (between page sections, 40 to 64 px), `size-target` / `min-h-target` (44 px touch target), `h-field` (48 px fields and main buttons).
- **Widths:** `max-w-page` (72rem) for the page frame, `max-w-measure` (38rem) for prose. `max-w-wide` (96rem, 2026-10-27) for a page whose lists sit side by side and gain from the room on a large screen (the index page; founder: "on pc, you have more space to work with"), set by `PageShell wide`; its prose still keeps to `max-w-measure`.
- **Breakpoints:** Tailwind defaults. Phone is the base style. `md` (48rem): tables become real tables; cards go two across. `lg` (64rem): guide pages get a side section index at the start (right) and the timeline's inputs sit beside the chart. The header's inline destinations wait for `xl` (80rem), not `lg`, and the tracks table of the workbench and the company page is a real table from `lg` (phone-style cards below it), not from `md` (2026-10-24, UX audit).
- **Hit areas (2026-10-24, UX audit).** A number, a rule chip and a glossary term each carry a transparent 44 px `::after` hit area without changing the line spacing. On a screen shorter than 500 px the sticky bars (chip bars, table heads, the bottom bar) become static. While a sheet is open the page behind does not scroll (`scrollbar-gutter: stable` on `html` so the desktop does not shift).
- **Rhythm inside a section:** 16 px between paragraphs, 24 px between blocks (a chart and its inputs, a table and its note), `section-gap` between sections.

### 2.6 Shape, elevation, focus, motion

- **Radius:** `rounded-sm` 4 px (tags), `rounded-md` 8 px (fields, buttons, chips), `rounded-lg` 12 px (cards, popovers), `rounded-xl` 20 px (top corners of a bottom sheet), `rounded-full` (state chips, slider thumb).
- **Elevation:** surfaces get lighter as they rise (`background`, `card`, `popover`) and a 1 px `border` marks their edges. Shadow (`shadow-lg`) only under sheets and popovers, to lift them off the page.
- **Focus:** a 2 px `ring` outline offset by 2 px on `:focus-visible`, on every control.
- **Motion:** `--duration-fast` 120 ms (press, toggles), `--duration-base` 200 ms (sheets, popovers, disclosure), `--duration-chart` 400 ms (graphs and numbers moving to their new values), easing `ease-standard`. All durations become zero when the device asks for reduced motion.
- **Stacking:** sheets and popovers use the browser's top layer (`<dialog>`, `popover`); only the sticky header needs `--z-sticky`.

### 2.7 How numbers, dates and identifiers are written

Formatting always goes through `Intl` with `he-IL`; never hand-built strings, and tests compare `Intl` output with `Intl` output (it contains invisible direction marks).

| Kind | Form | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Shekel amount | `1,234.50 ₪` | `Intl.NumberFormat('he-IL', { style: 'currency', currency: 'ILS' })`; comma thousands, point decimals; precision per method §13, display rounding below (2026-10-24, UX audit) |
| Percent | `1.05%` | data stores percent numbers (`1.05` means 1.05%), so divide by 100 for `style: 'percent'` |
| Date | `22.10.2026` | day first, dots, `day: '2-digit', month: '2-digit', year: 'numeric'`; never ISO or a slash form on the page |
| Report period | `ספטמבר 2026` | `month: 'long', year: 'numeric'` |
| Tax year | `2026` | a plain year, isolated |
| Fund number, ISIN, security number | `7978`, `IE00B5BMR087` | strings, never number-formatted (no thousands comma), LTR, tabular, slashed zero |
| Rule and question IDs | `TXS-09`, `TXK OQ2` | LTR, as written in `il-finance` |
| Chart tick | `50 אלף ₪` | compact form; a scale mark, not a figure (section 5, Q4). Axis text is spelled in visual order inside a left-to-right isolate (`₪ אלף 50`), drawn `direction=ltr`, `text-anchor=start`, because WebKit reorders mixed Hebrew and digits otherwise (2026-10-24, UX audit) |

Every number, amount, date and identifier inside Hebrew text is wrapped in an isolating element (`<bdi>` or `dir="ltr"` on its own span), so "25%" or a security number cannot be reordered by the bidi algorithm. The provenance component does this for every figure.

**Display rounding of amounts (2026-10-24, UX audit; display only, method §13 unchanged).** A computed, entered or parameter shekel amount of 1,000 ₪ or more shows whole shekels, and a whole amount of any size shows no agorot (`10,000 ₪`, `0 ₪`); tariffs and fees (sourced, not parameters) and anything under 1,000 ₪ that has agorot keep two decimals; table cells (`size="cell"`) keep two decimals. The right-to-left mark and the no-break space of the `Intl` output are removed so a copied amount is digits, a space and ₪; the element isolates the number (`bdi dir=ltr`). Assumption chips and the saved-goal strip show a whole amount without `.00`.

**One number parser (2026-10-24, UX audit).** Every typed number goes through `parseNumber` (`lib/format.ts`): spaces (also no-break and thin), ₪, % and direction marks are stripped; a single comma followed by one or two digits is a decimal point ("2,5" is 2.5); a comma followed by three digits is thousands ("12,000"); with both separators the last one is the decimal point ("1.234,5", "1,234.5"); anything else is not a number.

**Date and month inputs (2026-10-24, UX audit).** Native date and month inputs are `dir="ltr"` with the text at the end, and the chosen value is shown beside the picker in the `Intl` form (`10.2026`, `22.10.2026`), so the page never shows the browser's locale form.

### 2.9 Copy conventions

### 3.2 Provenance

Called for by the standing rule "every number traces to a source", D3, D7, D10, 3.8 "next to every rule-dependent number, a link that opens the rule", and architecture 3.5 and 3.7. These are the most-used components on the site.

**Num** (the provenance number). The only way to put a number on a page. Takes a value object, never a bare number.

- Renders as a `button` (so a keyboard reaches it) holding the formatted value in an isolating span with `tabular-nums`, then any flags. Its accessible name says the value and its state ("1.05%, ממקור, פרטים"). A transparent pseudo-element extends the hit area to 44 px without changing the line spacing.
- State styles: sourced, a solid `--num-underline-thickness` underline in `sourced`; computed, a dotted `--num-dotted-thickness` underline in `computed`; entered, `text-entered` on `bg-entered-subtle` with `rounded-sm` and 0.15em inline padding, no underline. Underline offset `--num-underline-offset`.
- Flags, after the value (at the inline end): stale, a 14 px `Clock` in `caution`; secondary strength, or institution strength out of its own context (D31: a company's figure about its own product, shown on that product, carries no icon and its sheet names the company's document): a 14 px caution `IconAlert` before the digits (never between a number and its unit word; `select-none`), where the strength word ("משני", "לפי החברה"; "מוסדי" until 2026-10-24) is in the accessible name and the popover and the legend, not in the line (the word survives as a tag only on strength labels, below); a computed figure carries the icon only at figure size, and its sheet names the weak inputs; a sourced figure whose `effective_from` is after the page date carries the same icon, and at figure size "נכנס לתוקף ב־{date}" (the old rule printed the word after nearly every number, which turned the caution colour into wallpaper) (2026-10-24, UX audit); site parameter, nothing inline (the popover says it).
- `value: null`: shows "לא ידוע" in `muted-foreground`, same underline as its state; the popover gives the reason from `notes`.
- Sizes: inline (inherits text size), cell (tables), figure (`text-figure`, weight 600, with a state tag below it, because a headline number deserves its label in words).
- When the value changes after an input change, the digits move to the new value over `--duration-chart`.

**Provenance popover.** Opens on tap or Enter from any Num. Uses the native `popover` attribute, so it works in the pre-rendered page before any script loads (architecture 3.5).

- Phone: a bottom sheet, full width, `rounded-xl` top corners, `max-h-[70vh]`, scrolls inside. PC: a `popover` card up to 22rem wide beside the number (anchored where the browser supports it, otherwise centred); no backdrop, a maximum height of 60vh with the inner body scrolling, and it flips above or below its anchor by the room there is (2026-10-24, UX audit; the phone sheet is unchanged).
- Header: state icon and label; strength label; stale flag.
- Body by state. Sourced: the value with its unit, source title as an external link, publisher, "נבדק ב־{checked}", effective dates, rule references, notes; for a site parameter also its rationale and sign-off date. Source row (2026-10-24, UX audit): the title when it is Hebrew, else the link's host with the English title behind "שם המקור באנגלית"; English working notes are never shown; a rule without a source link says so; a provisional parameter shows "ערך זמני" as a tag in the header and "עוד לא אושר" as its approval date; a value valid for exactly one calendar year reads "תקף לשנת {year}". Computed: the formula's name and ID, then each input as a Num (so the chain can be followed), then the rules used. Entered: "הזנת את הערך הזה", the value it replaced as a Num, and the reset button.
- Footer: what a flag means, in one line (for a stale number: its normal cadence and the date of the last value).

**State tag.** Icon plus label in `text-xs` weight 500, `rounded-sm`, in the state colour on its subtle fill (sourced: `muted`). Used under figures, in the legend, in popover headers and on inputs that were changed.

**State legend.** One line per page that shows numbers: three short samples drawn with the real underline styles and their labels, a fourth for the institution mark (a caution icon, "לפי החברה: המספר מבוסס על מסמך של החברה"; shown only on a page that has a company's figure out of its own context, D31), optionally a fifth for a glossary term, plus "הקישו על מספר כדי לראות מאיפה הוא" (2026-10-24, UX audit: the legend now appears on /rules, /companies, the idle-cash, fees and holdings pages and wherever a page shows numbers). On a phone it collapses to one line under the page header.

**Strength label.** For rules and sources: primary as a plain `muted` tag; primary via a mirror the same with "(עותק)"; institution (labelled "לפי החברה", "מקור מוסדי" until 2026-10-24; an institution's own document is still the source, D23) and secondary in caution colours with `CircleAlert`; open in blocked colours with `Lock`.

**Rule reference.** A chip showing the rule ID (LTR, slashed zero) and, on PC, its short title. Opens a popover with the rule's text, conditions, strength, source and checked date, and a link to its entry on the rules page. It is a link with a 44 px hit area. In running text the chips never sit inside a sentence: they go on one quiet line at the end of the paragraph ("כללים: KGL-06 · TXK-03") (design review C1).

**Blocked notice.** An inline callout in `blocked-subtle` with `Lock`: what cannot be computed, the open-question ID, and why it matters (D10: "the comparison is incomplete for that reason"). Also used for "figure not updated" (method §13), with its own wording.

**Segmented control.** For two to four exclusive options: employment type, and real or nominal (real is the default). Full width on a phone, `rounded-md`, ~~the selected segment in `card` with `shadow-sm` on a `muted` track~~ (until 2026-10-24); the pressed segment is a solid `bg-primary` fill with a check icon (`aria-pressed`), and wherever a segmented control stands above a heading it is `max-w-sm` at the inline start (2026-10-24, UX audit). ### 3.4 Timeline chart

Called for by 3.1 ("a timeline of money in, growth, fees and tax out, with the rules it used listed beside it"), method F-67, D9, architecture 3.9 and gap 8.6.

- **Form.** An area chart over time. Time runs left to right (gap 8.6), from now to the horizon. One value axis in shekels, real by default, nominal when the segmented control says so. From the baseline up: net value (teal), tax (rose, during and at exit), fees (orange, by type). The top edge is what the money would have been without fees and tax, so each band shows what it takes. Cumulative contributions are a neutral line over the bands. The exact series are F-67's; the mockup confirms the stacking.
- **Marks.** 2 px lines; bands separated by a 2 px gap in the card colour; the net line in `chart-net` on top. Fee types take the fee steps in a fixed order; tax during and at exit take the tax steps.
- **Labels.** Direct labels at the horizon end of each band, with a legend above the chart in reading order (right to left) as chips with swatches. ~~Value-axis labels sit in a gutter at the right edge, level with their gridlines and outside the plot (`text-xs chart-text`): the bands fill the right of the plot as the money grows, so labels inside it would sit on colour.~~ Value-axis labels sit at the plot's left edge, just above their gridline, with a card-colour halo (`text-xs chart-text`); the right of the plot is kept for the direct labels and the horizon, and at the left the bands are still thin, so the labels do not sit on colour. The same rule holds for the comparison, fee-drag and cumulative-cost charts (`AxisLabels` in `chart/TimelineChart.tsx`; the right gutter is gone) (2026-10-24, UX audit). Three to five gridlines, compact form. Year ticks along the bottom at whole steps of 1, 2, 5 or 10 years from today (`niceYearTicks(horizon)`, about four ticks; the horizon end is not always a tick, its marker still marks it), on all four charts (2026-10-24, UX audit). A legend item appears only for an area or line that is drawn: the legend drops "מס" when no point has tax.
- **Markers.** A 1 px vertical line in `chart-marker` with a `text-xs` label at the top for lock-up ends, exit points and age milestones that the wrapper's rules define (method §8).
- **Blocked parts.** Where the method cannot compute a part (for example exit tax blocked by an open rule, method §12), that band is replaced by a 45° hatch in `chart-blocked` over `blocked-subtle`, with a `Lock` label naming the open question. The rest of the chart still draws.
- **Entered return.** When the return is entered by the person, the net line and the top edge are drawn dashed in `chart-entered`, and the caption says the projection rests on a return they entered (D9).
- **Readout.** Touch and drag on a phone, hover on PC: a 1 px crosshair in `chart-crosshair` and a readout card pinned above the plot (not under the finger): the year as its heading, then one row per series, label at the start and value at the end, any tag after its value (design review C2). `touch-action: pan-y`, so a vertical swipe still scrolls the page.
- **Table view.** A disclosure, "הצגה כטבלה", with one row per year and one column per series, every cell a Num. Since 2026-10-24 (UX audit) it is its own full-width card under the chart-and-inputs row (both columns at `lg`), cells do not wrap, and the wrapper carries an edge fade and shadow as a scroll cue (also on the comparison and fee tables); on a phone the card follows the chart card.
- **Rules used.** Under the chart on a phone, beside it at `lg`: the rule references the run used, from the engine's record.
- **States.** Pre-rendered at build on P-05 (the illustrative scenario), so the chart is there before any script. While the worker recomputes for longer than 150 ms, the plot dims to 60% opacity; when the result arrives, shapes and numbers move to the new values over `--duration-chart`.
- **Size.** Full width; 240 px tall on a phone, 320 px at `lg`.

- **Deciding line.** Under rank 2 and every lower row: "מכריע מול {ספק מעליו}: {סוג העמלה}" in `text-sm muted-foreground`, the type as a `Term` (F-63 `deciding.line`: the type with the largest absolute gap between the two providers); the gaps by type as computed Nums in the row's detail. Absent inside a tie block (F-80: nothing decides a tie). Why the pair is "this row and the one above" and not "the top two": the product asks which fee type drives the ranking; a person reading row 3 wants to know what separates it from row 2. **Method note:** F-63 names the deciding type between the top two; the page applies the same test pairwise down the table (flow 28).
- **Why one table and not a card per provider:** every row shows the same seven facts in the same states, and the question is comparative; a table reads across. The row detail (a popover on PC, a sheet on a phone) holds what differs in shape: the tariff lines as printed, the entered lines, the four type costs and the remainder.

### 9.3 Exposure chart

Called for by 3.2 Out ("a chart of projected value over time for the top options on one axis"), D7, D9, roadmap R5 ("the 'entered by you' state (D7) must be unmistakable on the chart"), and 6.5.

- **Form.** The comparison chart of 6.5: real value over time, time left to right, one value axis (labels at the left edge, 2026-10-24, UX audit), direct labels at the right (the leader's only), legend above, readout, table view, 240 px on a phone and 320 px at `lg`. One line per drawn exposure.
- **Up to seven series, four at a time.** Exposure mode can rank seven exposures (and more once a tier-2 instrument graduates as an exposure). The chart never draws more than four lines: ranks 1 to 3 in the option ramp (`chart-option-1` teal-200, `-2` teal-400, `-3` teal-600) and the cash-like exposure as a **reference line** in `chart-baseline` (neutral-300, 1.5 px), the role the current account plays in wrapper mode. ### 9.4 Choose and save, the revisit line and banner, the what-changed sheet, diff and value-change rows

Called for by 3.9 ("a saved comparison records inputs, assumptions, the rule, parameter and data versions used, the result and the choice. "לשמור את ההשוואה" under the list saves without a choice. Why an explicit save and not every re-run: a revisit compares today against a frozen moment, and the product names that moment, "the choice they made at the end of a comparison"; a comparison that re-saved itself on every visit would always be "unchanged". Saving again replaces the saved one after the inline confirm line of 6.7 naming the old date, so the person knows which moment they are giving up.
- **Revisit line.** One `text-xs` line on the saved-goal strip (6.7), under the goal line: "נשמרה ב־{תאריך}" (with "הבחירה: {אפשרות}" and `Check` when there is one), then the notice: "לבדיקה מחדש: {מה} השתנה ב־{תאריך}" in `caution-foreground` with `CircleAlert`, or "ללא שינוי מאז {תאריך}" in `muted-foreground`, with the link "מה השתנה". The strip is on every page, so the notice meets the person wherever they land (flow 35). {מה} names the single changed entry when there is one (the product's "rule X changed on date Y") and counts by kind otherwise; the date is the entry's new `checked` date, or the change log's date for a parameter or an assumption (which carry none in the engine's diff). Why caution and not red for "changed": nothing is blocked; the site is asking the person to look again. Why "unchanged" is not green: `positive` means "dimension considered" (2.2) and an unchanged decision is a quiet fact, not an achievement.
- **Revisit banner.** On the result page, under the mode tabs: a `Callout` in `caution` when anything changed or the re-run could not finish, a quiet `card` line when nothing did. Order inside: the **ranking verdict** first when the ranking moved ("הדירוג השתנה: {X} במקום {Y}."), else the notice first and the verdict second ("הדירוג נשמר: {X} עדיין ראשונה."); "קבוצת השקולות השתנתה: {שמות}." when only the tie set moved (`tieSetUnchanged` false); "לא ניתן לבדוק: {סיבה}" with `Lock` when the re-run stopped (a rule past its end date, §13). Two buttons: "מה השתנה" (the sheet) and "לשמור את ההשוואה של היום". Why the verdict is a fixed sentence: like 6.3's deciding line, the site states a computed fact (`topUnchanged`, `tieSetUnchanged`) in fixed words, never a free judgement.
- **What-changed sheet.** A sheet (phone) or a 26rem card (PC): the **diff list** grouped by kind in a fixed order (כללים · פרמטרים · נתונים · הנחות), each an h4 with its count; one **diff row** per entry: the entry's code and words with a link to its page, then "היה {ערך} · עכשיו {ערך}" as `Num`s in their own states (a rule figure sourced with the two `checked` dates in `text-xs muted`; a parameter in the parameter state; an assumption `entered`; a state change written as words, "היה פרמטר · עכשיו הוזן על ידך"; a bracket schedule as a two-column table inside a `Disclosure`); an entry gone from the bundle reads "הוסר מהמאגר" or "הקובץ לא קיים עוד" with `Lock`, a new one "קובץ חדש". Then h4 "מה זז בהשוואה" with one **value-change row** per option whose value moved ("{אפשרות}: {סכום} → {סכום}" as computed `Num`s; `Tag`s "חדשה בהשוואה" / "לא מדורגת עוד"), then h4 "לפני ואחרי" with two compact dated columns (top option, tie set). Footer links: the change-log page since the saved date, and save again. Why only what the saved runs read: F-82 keeps the rules and files the runs used, so this list is the precise answer to "did my decision's basis change"; the change-log page (9.5) is the wide one, and the sheet's last link says so. The empty state is a full sentence ("אף כלל, פרמטר, נתון או הנחה שההשוואה נשענה עליהם לא השתנו."), because the product's "works when" asks that an unchanged decision *shows* as unchanged.
- **Unreadable-record form.** When a saved comparison cannot be read (an older site version the migration does not cover, an unknown mode or id): the strip line and the banner are replaced by one sentence in `caution` ("ההשוואה שנשמרה היא מגרסה ישנה של האתר ואי אפשר לבדוק אותה מחדש."), the readable date and choice as plain text, and two actions ("להשוות מחדש ולשמור", "מחיקה מהמכשיר"); nothing is converted by guesswork, as 6.7's old-version state does for a goal.

## 11. Kit (the shared components, 2026-10-25)

The founder, 2026-10-25, after two layout bugs in a day (a sentence with a number laid out as a flex column, one piece to a line; a closed fold inside an open fold that read as open): "custom components so we have consistent UI and an easier and faster time programming, like the equivalent of a class, but for UI components". The kit is `site/app/components/kit/`, one file per component or small family, re-exported from `kit/index.ts`. Import from `components/kit`, never from a neighbouring feature. The gallery at `/kit` (pre-rendered, `noindex`, in no menu and no search; every number on it is a labelled example) shows each component in its states, right to left, with its name beside it, and is the place to review the look. The survey that found the duplicates is `research/specs/2026-10-25-ui-kit/survey.md`.

**The rule.** A pattern that appears a second time goes into the kit first, then the feature uses it. A component takes the *choices* as props (`pad`, `size`, `tone`, `scroll`) and never as an overriding class: the site has no class merger, so two classes for one property are decided by the order of the generated stylesheet, not by the order in `class` (the `p-0 md:p-0` on the guide's key/value card never applied for that reason). No new look is made in the kit: each component is the best existing version.

| Component | When to use it |
|---|---|
| `Button`, `LinkButton` | `Button` for an action (`primary` once per screen, `quiet` for a text action, `destructive` for a quiet one that deletes, `icon` for a 44 px icon), `LinkButton` for a place to go. |
| `Tag` | A one-to-three-word label with a tone (state, kind, flag). Never colour alone: give it a word or an icon. `wrap` (2026-10-26) for a label that holds a name, such as a rule card's "של {company}": it breaks onto a second line instead of running past a phone's edge; the card's scope tag names the companies the way the rule chip does (the page's company or the first, and "ועוד N"). |
| `StepTag` | A tag on an ordered scale of four (2026-10-26): how soon the money is back, step 1 "at any moment" to step 4 "at the end of the term". A grey pill with the word; only the icon takes the step's colour (`access-*`, teal to red), so the tag stays calm beside the figures and is not read as a flag. Always an icon and a word. For an order, not a measure: never a bar or slices for it (2.3, "Reading the visuals"). |
| `Callout` | A boxed note under a result: `caution`, `blocked` or `info`. |

**Blocks layer: `IdRef`** (`provenance.tsx`, 2026-10-26). One chip for every id the site shows; it reads the kind from the id's shape. A rule id (`TXS-05`) opens the rule card (`RuleRef` is a thin alias); a formula id (`F-80`, and the ids `formulaNames` names, such as `A-06`, whose sheet and aria-label say "איך זה מחושב" instead of "נוסחה") opens the formula sheet (its Hebrew name, the site parameters it uses, each an `IdRef`, and a link to `docs/method.md` on GitHub, in English); a parameter id (`P-01`) is a link to `/parameters#P-01`, which scrolls to its card below the sticky header; any other `A-nn` is a method assumption, linked only when a parameter card holds it as an input, else plain text; an open-question id (`TX2 OQ6`, also written `TX2-OQ6`) opens the open-question sheet (what the term means on the site, and the title, status and what it blocks once `data/open-questions.json` has the id). Props: `closedOn` (a question that was closed: same chip, the sheet says when), `asLabel` (the id as a label that does nothing: a parameter's own card). An empty id renders nothing, and an id of no kind renders as plain text. `IdText` turns the open-question codes inside a copy string into chips; with `assumptions` it also turns the method's `A-nn` ids into chips and drops their brackets (the parameters page's field names, 2026-10-26, where "(A-03)" broke at its hyphen on a phone). Never print one of these ids as plain text in a `<bdi>`: `kit/guards.test.ts` scans for it. The method data (formulas with their parameters, the parameter cards) reaches every page through the root loader, which `PageShell` merges into the provenance data.

**Blocks layer: who a rule applies to** (`provenance.tsx`, 2026-10-26, D30; spec `research/specs/2026-10-25-rules-scope/spec.md` §4). `RuleRef` shows a company's term (scope `institution`, or `unsettled` seen at named companies) by the company's name instead of its code: on that company's page its own name, elsewhere the first name and "ועוד N"; a `RulesLine` where two chips would read the same keeps each one's code beside the name. The rule card carries a neutral scope tag ("חל על כולם", "של {company}", "לא הוכח שחל על כולם"), a line that a company's term may differ elsewhere, and a foot that leads to the rules of the page's product or to everything found at the company. `VariesByCompany ids` renders `CompanyLinks` for the companies of the records given. A product context (a guide, an exposure page, the questions page, "שווה לדעת") cites general rules only: the validator checks the records (`packages/pipeline/src/scope.ts`) and `lib/scope.test.ts` the prose modules, where a company's term may appear only inside `VariesByCompany`.

**Which of the two.** A list of things the person can choose among, add to, rename or delete is a `ChipList`; a row of the numbers a page runs on is `PlanChips`. A page that needs a third pill look uses one of these, never its own `chip` class (the index page had one until 2026-10-25).

**Guards** (`kit/guards.test.ts`, run by `npx vitest run site`): no `<details>` outside the kit (`provenance.tsx`'s two footnote folds are on a visible allow-list until that file is next opened); no `group-open:` anywhere; no `<p>`, and no `<span>` with words of its own, that holds a `Num` is a flex or grid container (a scan of the source by `lib/jsx-scan.ts`); the gallery stays out of the menu and the search and keeps its `noindex`.

**Where the kit stops.** Chart drawings (the SVG plots), the provenance component and its sheet, the shell, and rows that are one feature's own anatomy (a ranked row, a holding card) are not in the kit; they are built from it. A feature's own helper that only looks up its copy (the guide visuals' `SourceLine` and `Gap` wrappers) stays with the feature and renders the kit's component.

**Lists of products** (founder, 2026-10-27, on a company's funds; the design and how it got there: `research/specs/2026-10-27-company-fund-card/spec.md`). These hold wherever the site lists several products of one kind (tracks, funds, deposits, accounts):

- **A table, not a stack of cards.** On a desktop the products are rows of one table, in the shape of the company page's track table (`TrackList`); on a phone each product is one row under the next. A card with a facts column left half of every card empty.
- **The identifier is the link.** Where a product has an official page elsewhere (a fund's page on Maya), its number is that link, with the external icon (`1148808 ↗`), and its accessible name says where it leads. No separate "בדף הקרן במאיה" line. A link inside the site ends with the drill-in chevron (›), a link out with the external icon (↗).
- **The column head carries the words; the cell holds the short value.** "המדד" in the head and "ת"א-125 ›" in the cell, never "עוקבת אחרי מדד …" on every row. On a phone, where there is no head, the row says the one word ("מדד ת"א-125 ›").
- **Columns as wide as their content.** The number, link and figure columns shrink to what they hold; the name takes the rest, so its labels fit on one line.
- **A kind is a label, not a row or a column.** What a product is or does (how a fund follows its index, whether it pays dividends, its currency, a tax that follows from it) is a label chip that opens a sheet with its rule, from one shared set (`fundLabels()` for funds). A yes/no or a kind never gets its own column.
- **Nothing that repeats.** No figure that says again what a label says (a fund's currency ceiling beside "חשיפה לדולר"), no column whose value is the same on every row (every index fund's stock grade is 4), no line under a link that explains what the linked page explains. *Amended 2026-10-27 for labels (founder: "its ok to duplicate the labels even if all the rows are the same. if in the future i find it annoying we'll remove the duplication"):* a label shows on every row it is true of, even when it is true of every row; the rest of this rule stands.
- **A ceiling is not a holding.** A limit a product declares (a fund's exposure profile, "4A") is never drawn the way a measured share is drawn (the track table's rings, F-87 and F-88). If shown at all, it is words.
