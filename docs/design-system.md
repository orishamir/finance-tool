# Design system

The look of the site and the pieces every page is built from. The look was approved by the founder. The tokens live in
`site/app/theme.css`; the shared components are the kit in `site/app/components/kit/`, shown in their states in the gallery at
`/kit` (section 11). CLAUDE.md's ground rules and its design-system summary hold over this document.

- Section 1: the principles the product imposes on the visual design.
- Section 2: the tokens (colour, dark only; type, spacing, shape, motion), the layout patterns, and the conventions for numbers,
  icons and copy.
- Section 3: the site-wide pieces: the shell, provenance, inputs, the guide pages, companies and search, the reference pages and the
  primitives.
- Sections 6 to 9: the pieces of single features: the goal comparison (6); fees, holdings and the transfer file (7); banks and
  investment houses (8); exposure mode, the revisit and the not-covered page (9).
- Section 10: the guide-page visuals. Section 11: the kit. Section 12: the rules every chart follows.

Section numbers are ids that the code and other docs cite; 4 and 5 are not used. A page held for the founder's review (D32: not
linked from the site, still at its address; the list is `site/app/lib/review.ts`) is described here as it is built.

No financial figure appears here. Where a component shows a number, it shows a value object through the provenance component (3.2).

## 1. Principles

| # | Principle | From |
|---|---|---|
| V1 | **Provenance is the visual language.** Every number shows whether it is sourced, computed or entered by you, and one tap shows where it came from. Stale, secondary-source and blocked states are visible in place, never only in a footnote. | CLAUDE.md, D3, D7, D10 |
| V2 | **Both screen sizes, PC judged first.** Mockups are judged on PC; every screen is also checked at 390 px before it goes live. Touch targets at least 44 px. Nothing depends on hover. | CLAUDE.md ground rule 8, product.md §2, K7 |
| V3 | **Right to left throughout.** Layout, reading order, sliders, disclosure and navigation flow from the right. Exceptions: every chart axis runs left to right, not only time (gap 8.6, ground rule 6), while a bar inside a table row may grow from the right with its Hebrew text; numbers, identifiers and Latin names are isolated left-to-right runs. | CLAUDE.md, architecture 3.5 |
| V4 | **Inputs sit next to the result they change.** Every graph and computed number shows the inputs it uses, each editable on the same page. | product.md §2 "interactive by default", K8 |
| V5 | **Calm and neutral.** Warm dark grey, light text, one accent. No bank or manager brand colours, no promotional look, no colour that suggests a recommendation the site has not made. | product.md §2 "neutral by construction" |
| V6 | **Colour never works alone.** Every state also has a pattern, an icon or a word, so it reads in sunlight, in greyscale and to a colour-blind friend. | dataviz method |
| V7 | **Polish is a requirement.** Motion inside a chart mark when values change (a bar grows, a line glides), never a layout that moves (2.5a); careful type; charts drawn by us. | K12 |
| V8 | **A page answers why a person came to it.** Each page, section, column and visual helps the choice its reader came to make; one that helps none, or a second page for a question another page already answers, is left out (founder, 2026-10-30: "The השאלות שכולם שואלים page isnt that useful"; 2026-11-03: "is the /brokerage-account needed? we already have /indices"). A page about one thing does not bring in another: one company's page does not set its tracks among every company's (2026-10-26: "i dont want to compare with other חברות in this case"), and the goal stays on the goal pages (copy 2). A need one page leaves unmet is a fault only where the site does not meet it, or the way there is long (2026-11-06: "we have to think about the context of the page too"). A template of sections is a list to choose from, not a form to fill: each page keeps what says something about its own subject (2026-10-30: "The same template doesn't fit every guide"). | founder |

## 2. Tokens and layout

### 2.1 Tiers and where they live

`site/app/theme.css`, imported by `site/app/app.css`.

1. **Palette** (`--palette-<hue>-<step>`): raw oklch ramps. Hue ramps share one lightness ladder (step 50 is 0.975 … step 950 is 0.24), so the same step behaves the same in every hue. Components never use these.
2. **Semantic roles** (`--background`, `--entered`, `--chart-fee`, …): what a colour means, one value each, since the site is dark only. Surface and text names follow shadcn/ui (`--background`, `--card`, `--muted-foreground`, `--primary`, `--ring`, …) so components copied from it work unchanged.
3. **Utilities**: `@theme inline` maps each role to a Tailwind colour (`bg-card`, `text-entered`, `border-input`, `fill-chart-fee`). Tailwind's default palette is removed (`--color-*: initial`), so a class can only name a role.

Rules:

- Components use role utilities or `var(--role)`, never a palette step and never a literal colour.
- **Dark only** (founder). There is no light theme and no theme switch. `color-scheme: dark` on the root makes native controls, scrollbars and the popover backdrop dark too. The palette keeps its light steps, unused.
- Layout uses logical properties and utilities only (`ps-`, `pe-`, `ms-`, `me-`, `inset-s-`, `inset-e-`, `border-s`, `rounded-s-`, `text-start`). The `rtl:` variant is for true exceptions such as mirroring an icon.

### 2.4 Typography

**Faces.** Inter for Latin letters and digits, Heebo for Hebrew, both variable fonts, self-hosted through `@fontsource-variable` (no font CDN, D1). Approved by the founder after comparing them on mock screens with Heebo alone, Assistant, Rubik, IBM Plex Sans Hebrew, Noto Sans Hebrew and Open Sans. Stack: `"Inter Variable", "Inter Fallback", "Heebo Variable", Arial, sans-serif`. Inter comes first because it has no Hebrew, so Hebrew falls through to Heebo glyph by glyph; Arial has Hebrew and holds the layout while the web fonts load (`font-display: swap`). The shekel sign comes from Heebo. Only Inter's Latin subset and Heebo's Hebrew subset are loaded (own `@font-face`), about 60 KB for a Hebrew page, both preloaded in the HTML head, with an "Inter Fallback" face (Arial with `size-adjust`, ascent and descent set to Inter's metrics) ahead of Arial, so the swap does not move the layout.

Why this pair: Heebo is clear on small screens and matches Inter's proportions; Inter's digits are made for interfaces, with tabular figures and a slashed zero, which matter on a site made of numbers and fund numbers.

**Scale** (Hebrew reads smaller than Latin at the same size, so the scale starts at 16 px for body and runs taller line heights):

| Token | Size | Line height | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| `text-xs` | 13 px | 1.5 | captions, axis ticks, source dates; the smallest Hebrew on the site |
| `text-sm` | 14 px | 1.6 | secondary text, field labels, table cells on a phone, tags |
| `text-base` | 16 px | 1.7 | body, inputs (16 px also stops iOS from zooming into a field) |
| `text-lg` | 18 px | 1.6 | lede, card titles, h3 |
| `text-xl` | 20 px | 1.45 | h2 on a phone |
| `text-2xl` | 24 px | 1.4 | h1 on a phone, h2 on PC |
| `text-3xl` | 30 px | 1.3 | h1 on PC |
| `text-figure` | 32 px | 1.15 | a headline number (digits only, so the tight line height is safe) |

**Weights.** 450 body (a half step heavier reads better on large screens; text with an explicit `font-normal` stays at 400); 500 labels, tags and emphasis; 600 headings and headline numbers; 700 not used. One weight per role.

**Root size.** The scale above holds at the default root of 16 px; from 1600 px wide the root is 17 px and from 2200 px it is 18 px, so every rem-based size grows by those steps.

**Hebrew rules.**

- Never letter-space Hebrew; the tracking utilities are removed. Word spacing is 0.05em on body text.
- No italics for emphasis (Hebrew has no true italic); use weight 500.
- Prose is capped at `max-w-measure` (38rem). `ch` measures a Latin zero, so check real Hebrew line lengths in the browser.
- `dir="auto"` on any text the person typed or pasted (catalogue search, uncatalogued names), per element, never on a list wrapper.

**Numbers.** Every number uses `tabular-nums` so values do not jitter while they animate to a new value and columns align. Identifiers (fund numbers, ISINs, security numbers, rule IDs, open-question IDs) also use `slashed-zero`. A headline figure keeps its size: where it does not fit beside its label it moves under the label rather than shrinking.

### 2.5 Spacing and layout

- **Base unit 4 px** (Tailwind's `--spacing: 0.25rem`): `p-1` is 4 px, `p-4` is 16 px. Use steps 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 12, 16.
- **Named spacing:** `px-gutter` (page side padding, `clamp(1rem, 4vw, 2rem)`: 16 px on a phone, up to 32 px), `gap-section` / `my-section` (between page sections, 40 to 64 px), `size-target` / `min-h-target` (44 px touch target), `h-field` (48 px fields and main buttons). **A tap area is what a finger hits, not what the control draws:** a control that must draw shorter than 44 px (an icon button, a chip, a link in a strip or a footer, a chart's legend on a computer) takes the `tap-area` utility, a ::after area 44 px tall centred on it that keeps the look; never on a link inside running text, whose area would cover the lines above and below. A field's input covers its box's border, so the box's 44 or 48 px is all input. Kit guard 22 fails any fixed height under 44 px on a button, link, summary or field without one of these, and `research/audit/tools/hit-check.mjs` measures what a finger hits on the built page (2026-11-07: a check that measured drawn boxes listed rule chips whose areas were already 48 px, while the short ones a finger meets were a field's 42 px input and the links in the fee page's strip).
- **Widths:** `max-w-wide` (96rem) is the page frame, the same on every page and set only by `PageShell` (founder: one width on every page, so the header does not move from page to page); `max-w-measure` (38rem) for prose inside it. What fills the room is the page's own: card grids and lists take more columns, two halves stand side by side (the transfer page), prose keeps its measure. A guard in `kit/guards.test.ts` keeps any page from narrowing its frame again.
- **Breakpoints:** Tailwind defaults. Phone is the base style. `md` (48rem): tables become real tables (a list of products is one from `md`, section 11); cards go two across. `lg` (64rem): guide pages get a side section index at the start (right) and the timeline's inputs sit beside the chart. The header's inline destinations wait for `xl` (80rem), not `lg`.
- **Two-column pages.** Every page grid that sets columns in `fr` writes them as `minmax(0, …fr)` (`lg:grid-cols-[minmax(0,62fr)_minmax(0,38fr)]`, `[minmax(0,3fr)_minmax(0,2fr)]`, `[minmax(0,1fr)_300px]`, `[360px_minmax(0,1fr)]`): a plain `fr` column grows to its widest content, and a wide table opened inside it pushed the page wider than the screen. A wide table scrolls inside its own `overflow-x-auto` box.
- **Order and room follow importance.** The main thing comes first and takes the room; a side matter goes last, or folded (founder, 2026-11-02: "the choice between tracks should be a side note last"; 2026-10-24: the rules page should open with the rules, not the questions). Size reads as importance, so what a person must act on is never the smallest thing on the screen, nor a tiny box for a long list (2026-11-06, on another site: "it took so much less space which in my mind translated to significance"). The first screen of a page answers its question and draws the person in, never a row of doors in front of the answer, and never half empty on a wide screen (2026-10-24: "make sure the part of the site which answers a user's dilemma isnt hidden behind a couple buttons"; 2026-11-06: "the very first screen i see of the site isnt very appealing"). No loose gaps: fields and blocks keep the rhythm below, with no empty band between two rows of one form, and a card is as tall and as wide as what it holds, never stretched to its neighbour's height or a full row for a narrow column of facts (2026-10-30: "compact spaces between sections and compact the input sliders").
- **A control sits above what it changes.** A control that adds or removes fields goes above what it changes, so the control itself never moves when pressed; `research/audit/tools/jump-check.mjs` presses every chip, switch, tab and disclosure on the main pages and lists the ones that move.
- **Hit areas.** A number, a rule chip and a glossary term each carry a transparent 44 px `::after` hit area. A number or a term inside running text keeps the line spacing, and its area shares the lines above and below. A control that stands alone (a rule chip, a value pencil, a figure in a readout row) holds a 44 px band of its own: an area drawn over a neighbour is covered by the neighbour's own box (layered at 2, above every area), so a 44 px area in a 27 px line measured 36 to 42 px (fix pass 8, 2026-11-07). The rule chip and the input slot carry vertical margins that make their line 44 px, and a readout row is at least 44 px. Areas are layered: figures and terms at 0, the areas of controls that stand alone at 1, every own box at 2. `research/audit/tools/hit-check.mjs --checks` measures what a finger can hit on every guide page and both fee pages at 390 and 1280, and runs with the movement check; the leftovers are listed by name in `hit-leftovers.json`, a list that only shrinks. On a screen shorter than 500 px the sticky bars (chip bars, table heads, the bottom bar) become static. While a sheet is open the page behind does not scroll (`scrollbar-gutter: stable` on `html` so the desktop does not shift).
- **Rhythm inside a section:** 16 px between paragraphs, 24 px between blocks (a chart and its inputs, a table and its note), `section-gap` between sections.

### 2.5a Stable layout

**Nothing on the screen moves when a number changes** (founder; CLAUDE.md standing rule; D63). Moving a slider, picking an option, typing a number or switching an index changes the figures and nothing else on the screen: no column in view widens, no row or block in view grows or shrinks, no line in view wraps differently, and the part of the page under the person's finger stays where it was. The control the hand is on holds; a list it re-sorts may slide below it. A block out of sight at that moment, such as a note far below the slider, may change its own height; the movement check (`research/audit/tools/box-check.mjs`) brings each control onto the screen and measures only the boxes in view. One exception (D62): switching index on a phone may move what lies below the switch, with a short, gentle transition instead of a jump; a list keeps one height across the states and plans of the index it shows, not across indices.

| Case | How the site does it | Where |
|---|---|---|
| A table whose figures change | `table-layout: fixed` with a width for every column, on a computer and on a phone; a column left out on a phone stays at width 0, so the next cell keeps its column; headings may wrap, figures may not | the kit's `TableCols` in every table (`ChangeChart`, `ChartTable`, `ProductTable`, `CompareTable`, the rest by hand); `.tbl` is fixed, and a guard in `kit/guards.test.ts` fails a `<table>` without it |
| A table cell with a figure and words beside it ("7,369,509 ₪ יותר") | The column holds the figure and its words at the inputs' far ends with the largest choice; where it cannot on a phone, the words go on a line of their own under the figure on every row, so the row keeps one height (independent check 2, 2026-11-06: the word was cut at 390 px while the figure fit; `box-check.mjs --plans` now measures a cell's whole text) | the indices page's "what changes most" table |
| A figure | Tabular digits (`tabular-nums`, the page body's default), no wrapping inside a figure, isolated in Hebrew text | every `Num` |
| A blank for a number inside a sentence | A fixed-width blank, whatever the number | `InputSlot` (`.input-slot`) |
| A block whose content varies by choice | One shape for every choice: what may be missing is replaced, not removed (on the cheapest pair the result block shows the next pair beside it) | the indices page's `PairResult` |
| A header that varies by subject | One height, sized for the longest subject | the indices page's `IndexHeader` |
| A block or line that varies, at any width | Every case is laid out unseen in the same cell, so the block has the longest case's height at every width; a sample figure stands in for one that changes | `LongestCase`: the indices page's account and fund headers, the fee page's headline, the study-fund guide |
| A block with too many cases to keep in the page (a list that depends on where the person stands) | The cases are laid out unseen once, measured and dropped; the block holds the tallest until its id (the index) or its width changes, never on a figure, so a slider costs nothing | `HoldTallest`: the three lanes of the indices page's stairs |
| A sentence that is the same under every case, next to words that vary | It stands first, in a block of its own, so the words that vary never push it a line up or down; only the varying words share the held room | the fee card's "ההנחה נגמרת אחרי …" above its verdict's reason (box check, fix pass 8, 2026-11-07: at a 40-year horizon at 390 px it rose a line with its three figures when the reason before it got shorter) |
| Notes under a chart, whose lines come and go | The notes follow one another with no hole between them, and the whole stack sits in one `LongestCase`, so the blank falls at the card's bottom; the card and what is under it keep their place | `LongestCase` around all the notes: the study fund's drawing |
| A list whose rows differ from one choice to the next | The list holds the tallest height it has had, keyed on what starts it over (the index); it can grow once, the first time a taller case comes up, and never shrinks back; a reason that depends on the choice stands once under the list rather than on each row | `useHeldHeight`: the indices page's stairs, its dollar section and the account list of an index with no fund yet |
| A block computed just after the render | The last answer stays until the next is ready; the block never empties and comes back | `useAfterRender` in `lib/settled.ts`: the fee page's and the comparison's "what would change the answer" |
| A list that re-sorts after a tap | The tapped row is held at its place on the screen, and the others slide around it | `PickList` (`holdOnPick`, `useFlip`); `box-check.mjs --rows` taps every row and says "HELD" |
| A control outside a list that re-sorts it (the indices page's own-bank field above the account list) | The control holds: nothing scrolls, and the list's rows slide to their new places below it, the bank's row too (2026-11-07: holding the bank's row moved the field just used by up to 237 px) | `OwnBankField`, `useFlip`; `box-check.mjs` says "held: field HELD" after each select |
| A figure in a fixed column that a typed plan can make very large | A size down as the digits grow, and on a phone a billion or more in its short form, always with two decimals ("2.30 מיליארד ₪"; a thousand billion on a computer too), so no figure is cut at any plan; past what its column holds the short form's words take a line under its digits rather than run over the next cell (lens review S2; fresh readers, round 3, 2026-11-07: "'1.6' has one decimal place while the rest have two") | `figureFit` and `Num` (`shortBillions` in `lib/format.ts`); `box-check.mjs --plans` lists every cut |
| Figures of one row, one of them short | The row's other figures of a hundred million or more read in the same short form, so a smaller figure in full digits never looks the larger beside a short one ("758,833,521 ₪" beside "1.57 מיליארד ₪", fresh readers, round 3); only the figures the screen shows count | `ShortRow` and `shortRow` in `provenance.tsx`: the account list's rows, the fund list's range, the pair tiles, the "what changes most" table |
| A table with more figures in a row than a phone's width holds | A column holds what a field accepts, not what the sliders give: four figures side by side left 63 px on a phone, and a typed "50,000,000 ₪" was printed over its neighbours. Two columns of one kind share a column on a phone, one figure under the other (fresh readers, round 3, 2026-11-07) | `ChangeChart` `stackOnPhone`; `box-check.mjs --plans` lists every cell whose text runs past its cell ("SPILL") and every two figures drawn over each other ("OVERLAP"), not only what a box cuts |
| A list whose rows change with a pick in another list | The list holds the height of its tallest state, measured from each row's other states laid out unseen; a phone's fold holds its tallest rows | `PickList` `states` and `cases`: the indices page's account list; `canShow`: its fund list |
| A list sorted or filtered again (a column head, a filter chip, a search) | The rows that stay slide to their new places and the new ones fade in; the list never fades or redraws as a whole | `useFlip` around a `ProductTable`: the tracks page, a company's page |

**A mark is never alone on a line.** A small mark before or after words (a "?", a caution icon, a lock, a link's arrow) is held to the nearest word: `LeadTail` for a mark after a text, `MarkHead` for one before it (both in `provenance.tsx`); a figure holds its own icons. Punctuation is held the same way: a full stop, a comma or a closing bracket stays on the line of the word, chip or figure before it, and an opening bracket on the line of what it opens (founder, 2026-10-25: "there is a random dot there?"). A sentence that ends on a chip (`IdRef`, `RuleRef`) passes its full stop, comma, colon or semicolon to the chip's `end`, which holds it on the chip's line and draws it close to the frame, and a chip in text takes no brackets, being its own frame: "פרטים ." and "( פרטים )." read as stray marks (reader rounds 3 and 5, 2026-11-07; kit guard 20). **Nothing out of line, nothing cut.** Pieces of one row share one top line, a word stays beside its figure, a name in the header does not wrap, and no box clips its text (2026-10-24: "this is misaligned. check for all other misaligned fields and fix them"; 2026-10-25: "the search bar is clipping"). `research/audit/tools/alone-check.mjs` finds any mark, and any full stop, comma or bracket, alone at the start or end of a line on every page at four widths, and runs before a change to a head, a link, a figure or a sentence that ends in a chip merges.

**Check.** `research/audit/tools/pick-check.mjs` taps every row of a page's linked lists and reads where each list's top stands. The builder runs a script (`research/audit/tools/box-check.mjs`) that sets each input to several values (the choices, a slider's ends, a large typed number) and compares the boxes of the parts that must stay (column widths, block heights, the tapped row's top); the result goes in the builder's report and the structure review. A part that moves is a finding, fixed in the kit piece, not on the page.

### 2.6 Shape, elevation, focus, motion

- **Radius:** `rounded-sm` 4 px (tags), `rounded-md` 8 px (fields, buttons, chips), `rounded-lg` 12 px (cards, popovers), `rounded-xl` 20 px (top corners of a bottom sheet), `rounded-full` (state chips, slider thumb).
- **Elevation:** surfaces get lighter as they rise (`background`, `card`, `popover`) and a 1 px `border` marks their edges. Shadow (`shadow-lg`) only under sheets and popovers, to lift them off the page.
- **Focus:** a 2 px `ring` outline offset by 2 px on `:focus-visible`, on every control.
- **Motion:** `--duration-fast` 120 ms (press, toggles), `--duration-base` 200 ms (sheets, popovers, a chip's colour), `--duration-fold` 280 ms (every fold opens and closes by height and fades, in the shared stylesheet, and its chevron turns with it), `--duration-chart` 400 ms (graphs and numbers moving to their new values), easing `ease-standard`. All durations become zero when the device asks for reduced motion.
- **Stacking:** sheets and popovers use the browser's top layer (`<dialog>`, `popover`); only the sticky header needs `--z-sticky`.

### 2.7 How numbers, dates and identifiers are written

Formatting always goes through `Intl` with `he-IL`; never hand-built strings, and tests compare `Intl` output with `Intl` output (it contains invisible direction marks).

| Kind | Form | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Shekel amount | `1,234.50 ₪` | `Intl.NumberFormat('he-IL', { style: 'currency', currency: 'ILS' })`; comma thousands, point decimals; precision per method §13, display rounding below |
| Dollar amount | `7.00 $`, a price per share `0.0035 $` | `Intl.NumberFormat('he-IL', { style: 'currency', currency: 'USD' })`, two decimals, up to four for a price per share (units `usd`, `usd_per_trade`, `usd_per_share`) |
| Euro and pound amount | `25.00 €`, `5.00 £` | `Intl.NumberFormat('he-IL', { style: 'currency', currency: 'EUR' })` (or `'GBP'`), two decimals (units `eur`, `gbp_per_trade`) |
| Percent | `1.05%` | data stores percent numbers (`1.05` means 1.05%), so divide by 100 for `style: 'percent'` |
| Date | `22.10.2026` | day first, dots, `day: '2-digit', month: '2-digit', year: 'numeric'`; never ISO or a slash form on the page |
| Report period | `ספטמבר 2026` | `month: 'long', year: 'numeric'` |
| Tax year | `2026` | a plain year, isolated |
| Fund number, ISIN, security number | `7978`, `IE00B5BMR087` | strings, never number-formatted (no thousands comma), LTR, tabular, slashed zero |
| Rule and question IDs | `TXS-09`, `TXK OQ2` | LTR, as written in `il-finance` |
| Chart tick | `50 אלף ₪` | compact form; a scale mark, not a figure: ticks carry a `data-scale` attribute, and the provenance crawl (architecture 3.7 item 5) allows digits inside it. Axis text is spelled in visual order inside a left-to-right isolate (`₪ אלף 50`), drawn `direction=ltr`, `text-anchor=start`, because WebKit reorders mixed Hebrew and digits otherwise |

Every number, amount, date and identifier inside Hebrew text is wrapped in an isolating element (`<bdi>` or `dir="ltr"` on its own span), so "25%" or a security number cannot be reordered by the bidi algorithm. The provenance component does this for every figure.

**Display rounding of amounts** (display only; method §13 unchanged). A computed, entered or parameter shekel amount of 1,000 ₪ or more shows whole shekels, and a whole amount of any size shows no agorot (`10,000 ₪`, `0 ₪`); tariffs and fees (sourced, not parameters) and anything under 1,000 ₪ that has agorot keep two decimals; table cells (`size="cell"`) keep two decimals. The right-to-left mark and the no-break space of the `Intl` output are removed so a copied amount is digits, a space and ₪; the element isolates the number (`bdi dir=ltr`). Assumption chips and the saved-goal strip show a whole amount without `.00`.

**One number parser.** Every typed number goes through `parseNumber` (`lib/format.ts`): spaces (also no-break and thin), ₪, % and direction marks are stripped; a single comma followed by one or two digits is a decimal point ("2,5" is 2.5); a comma followed by three digits is thousands ("12,000"); with both separators the last one is the decimal point ("1.234,5", "1,234.5"); anything else is not a number.

**Date and month inputs.** Native date and month inputs are `dir="ltr"` with the text at the end, and the chosen value is shown beside the picker in the `Intl` form (`10.2026`, `22.10.2026`), so the page never shows the browser's locale form.

### 2.9 Copy conventions

- Address the reader with the plural imperative ("בחרו", "הזינו", "הקישו") or impersonally ("אפשר", "יש"), never one gender (copy 1 and 10.2). It is gender-neutral and the common Israeli web form.
- Institution, product and track names appear exactly as the source spells them (data-schema §1); the site's explanation sits beside them, never in their place. A long name wraps and is never cut with an ellipsis, because the exact name is how people recognise it; the same holds for a bank's product name and for the negotiation text (7.4).
- Rules and open questions show their ID beside the words ("שאלה פתוחה TXK OQ10", a rule chip with "KGL-02"), so anyone can look them up (the founder asked to keep this). The words always come first, and a code is never wrapped in brackets at the end of a sentence: "שאלה פתוחה: TXK OQ10" (design review C5).
- The wording of every fixed label (the number states, strength labels, dimension marks, "unknown", real and nominal, reset, the table view of a chart, the not-on-the-site state, the near-duplicate resolutions, the legend hint) is in `docs/copy.md` and `site/app/copy.ts`.

### 3.2 Provenance

Called for by the standing rule "every number traces to a source", D3, D7, D10, 3.8 "next to every rule-dependent number, a link that opens the rule", and architecture 3.5 and 3.7. These are the most-used components on the site (`components/provenance.tsx`).

**Num** (the provenance number). The only way to put a number on a page. Takes a value object, never a bare number.

- Renders as a `button` (so a keyboard reaches it) holding the formatted value in an isolating span with `tabular-nums`, then any flags. Its accessible name says the value and its state ("1.05%, ממקור, פרטים"). A transparent pseudo-element extends the hit area without changing the line spacing: 11 px above and below the figure and never less than 44 px tall, for every figure (ground rule 8), and a second one, exactly the figure's own box, sits above every such area. So a figure in running text keeps its 44 px, and where its area reaches the line above or below, the figure on that line still takes its own click (independent check, 2026-11-07: "0.7%" opened the "5 שנים" sheet; re-check 2: an area that grew only sideways took 44 px from about 1,200 lone figures). Two figures closer than 44 px share the space between them; neither takes the other's box. A term's area follows the same rule. Kit guard 19.
- State styles: sourced, a solid `--num-underline-thickness` underline in `sourced`; computed, a dotted `--num-dotted-thickness` underline in `computed`; entered, `text-entered` on `bg-entered-subtle` with `rounded-sm` and 0.15em inline padding, no underline. Underline offset `--num-underline-offset`.
- Flags, after the value (at the inline end): stale, a 14 px `Clock` in `caution`; under a caution notice that already says the figures' source is old, `StaleSaid` leaves the clock out of every figure inside (the accessible name and the sheet still say it), as on the track browser and a company's track list, where the notice covers every figure of the list. Secondary strength, or institution strength out of its own context (D31: a company's figure about its own product, shown on that product, carries no icon and its sheet names the company's document): a 14 px caution `IconAlert` before the digits (never between a number and its unit word; `select-none`), where the strength word ("משני", "לפי החברה") is in the accessible name and the popover and the legend, not in the line (the word stays as a tag only on strength labels, below); a computed figure carries the icon only at figure size, and its sheet names the weak inputs; a sourced figure whose `effective_from` is after the page date carries the same icon, and at figure size "נכנס לתוקף ב־{date}". Site parameter: nothing inline (the popover says it).
- **A caution only where there is a doubt.** The amber icon and the clock say a figure is doubtful or old. An explanation, a kind or a company's figure about its own product never wears one, and the meaning of a label is not worded as a warning (founder, 2026-10-25: "a warning seems like we have incomplete data"; 2026-11-05: "מזה ההזהרה הזאת"). A doubt, a lock or a badge that every row or card of a list would carry is said once, above the list, as `StaleSaid` does (2026-11-03: "remove the warnings, keep the one at the top of the page").
- **A note sits on the figure it is about.** A caution that says a figure may be too high or too low goes on that figure, not on another figure that moves the other way because of it: "the current account counted here may be too high" belongs on the account's cost, and on the value left it would say the wrong direction (independent check 2, 2026-11-06, BANK-35). When one run feeds several figures, each figure takes only the notes about itself.
- `value: null`: shows "לא ידוע" in `muted-foreground`, same underline as its state; the popover gives the reason from `notes`.
- **The sheet says what the figure says.** A value that is not a number (a word, yes or no, or empty) heads its sheet with the `display` word it was shown as; a record's raw value ("none", "unlinked_15") never reaches a reader, and every such value also has a Hebrew word in `numbers.enums` for a figure shown without one. A number's sheet keeps its digits. (Independent check 3, 2026-11-07: every deposit card's linkage and tax sheets headed in English; guard 14 in `kit/guards.test.ts`.)
- Sizes: inline (inherits text size), cell (tables), figure (`text-figure`, weight 600, with a state tag below it, because a headline number deserves its label in words).
- When the value changes after an input change, the digits move to the new value over `--duration-chart` (`CountingFigures`, section 11). A figure with its own wording passes `display` as a function of the moving value, so it counts too.

**Provenance popover.** Opens on tap or Enter from any Num. Uses the native `popover` attribute, so it works in the pre-rendered page before any script loads (architecture 3.5).

- Phone: a bottom sheet, full width, `rounded-xl` top corners, `max-h-[70vh]`, scrolls inside. PC: a `popover` card up to 22rem wide beside the number (anchored where the browser supports it, otherwise centred); no backdrop, a maximum height of 60vh with the inner body scrolling, and it flips above or below its anchor by the room there is. While more of a sheet lies below its edge, a small pill with the words "יש עוד למטה" and a down arrow says so, in a strip of its own under the scrolling body, which is always there at one height and empty at the body's end, so the sign never covers a line and its coming and going moves nothing (`.sheet-more` in the shared `Sheet`; independent check of pass 5: a third year sat under the edge with no sign that more followed; pass 7: in a still shot the fade alone read as the sheet cut off, so the sign says it in words; independent check of pass 7: the pill over the last line hid it and a reader misread the sheet, so nothing lies over the body). The sign is measured on scroll, on any change of the body's size or of a block inside it (a fold opening in the sheet) and after each render (`watchMoreBelow`). Every sheet opens at its top, whatever the last one was scrolled to (pass 7: a second sheet opened at the first one's end), and so does each figure opened inside an open sheet and each step back (`view`; independent check of pass 7: a figure opened from inside a sheet kept the scroll of the one before, its head out of sight).
- Header: state icon and label; strength label; stale flag.
- Body by state. Sourced: the value with its unit, the source as a SourceBlock (3.8a) directly under the figure's name, publisher, effective dates, rule references, notes; for a site parameter also its rationale and sign-off date. The source's title shows when it is Hebrew, else the link's host with the English title behind "שם המקור באנגלית"; English working notes are never shown; a rule without a source link says so; a provisional parameter shows "ערך זמני" as a tag in the header and "עוד לא אושר" as its approval date; a value valid for exactly one calendar year reads "תקף לשנת {year}". Computed: the formula's name and ID, then each input as a Num (so the chain can be followed), a site parameter with its tag and a figure read from a document with that document's line under it (publisher, full title, place in it), then the rules used, each with its first source's line. A source line wraps at every width and is never cut (fresh readers, round 3, 2026-11-07: "5.50 ₪" a month had no source in view but the cap's rule under it, and the order's name and date were cut with "…"). Entered: "הזנתם את הערך הזה.", the value it replaced as a Num, and the reset button.
- Footer: what a flag means, in one line (for a stale number: its normal cadence and the date of the last value).
- **A source opens where it says it.** A link to a cited source opens at the cited words: a web page by text fragment (`#:~:text=`, at the quoted words as the page prints them), a PDF at its page (`#page=N`; Chrome's PDF viewer does not follow a text fragment), a dataset at this fund's own record rather than the dataset's front page (founder, 2026-10-30: "we should link to the direct PDF page or a highlighted text"; 2026-11-03: "for each fund link directly to its data?"; 2026-11-07: Psagot's price list opened at its top, not at the quoted fee). Every such link is built by `sourceHref` (`site/app/lib/sources.ts`), never from a raw address; a guard in `kit/guards.test.ts` fails an `href` made of a raw `url`, and `research/audit/tools/quote-fragments.mjs` keeps the words each web page opens at (`data/sources/fragments.json`), only where they stand on the page exactly. A quote that is a translation or a paraphrase opens the page at its top, so a quote shown beside a link is the page's own words wherever it can be.

**State tag.** Icon plus label in `text-xs` weight 500, `rounded-sm`, in the state colour on its subtle fill (sourced: `muted`). Used under figures, in the legend, in popover headers and on inputs that were changed.

**State legend** (`NumberLegend`). One line per page that shows numbers: short samples drawn with the real underline styles and their labels (shown as the page header in 3.1 says), one for the institution mark (a caution icon, "לפי החברה: המספר מבוסס על מסמך של החברה"; shown only on a page that has a company's figure out of its own context, D31), optionally one for a glossary term, plus "הקישו על מספר כדי לראות מאיפה הוא". It appears wherever a page shows numbers. On a phone it collapses to one line under the page header.

**Strength label.** For rules and sources: primary as a plain `muted` tag; primary via a mirror the same with "(עותק)"; institution (labelled "לפי החברה"; an institution's own document is still the source, D23) and secondary in caution colours with `CircleAlert`; open in blocked colours with `Lock`. A figure or rate that rests on the site's own reading of official texts (the engine's `reading` label, a rate whose reach no official text states) takes `ReadingTag`, "קריאה של האתר", in the same caution colours: a reading is not a secondary source, which is a press article or a forum post, so it never shows "מקור משני".

**Rule reference.** A chip showing the rule ID (LTR, slashed zero) and, on PC, its short title. Opens a popover with the rule's text, conditions, strength, source and checked date, and a link to its entry on the rules page. It is a link with a 44 px hit area. In running text the chips never sit inside a sentence: they go on one quiet line at the end of the paragraph (design review C1); several rules behind one fact are one `RulesLine` chip (section 11, blocks layer).

**Blocked notice.** An inline callout in `blocked-subtle` with `Lock`: what cannot be computed, the open-question ID, and why it matters (D10: "the comparison is incomplete for that reason"). Also used for "figure not updated" (method §13), with its own wording.

The ids the site shows (`IdRef`), the rules behind a fact (`RulesLine`) and who a rule applies to are the blocks layer of section 11.

### 3.3 Inputs

Called for by 3.1 of the product ("every value behind it can be changed on the page: amount now, monthly deposit, horizon, return, inflation, age and employment type"; "a changed value is shown as entered by you"; "values follow them to the catalogue and the goal comparison"), the real-terms principle (product.md §2) and architecture 3.9 (sliders with a typed value, steppers, bottom sheets). The fields are the kit's (`kit/fields.tsx`, section 11).

**Field (anatomy shared by every input).** Label above (`text-sm`, weight 500), optional hint under the label (`text-xs muted-foreground`), the control, then the value's state. One line above the fields (`ParameterLine`) says that a field left untouched is a starting value and a site parameter; a field shows the "הזנתם" tag and a reset button (`RotateCcw`, 44 px) only once it is entered (`FieldState`, whose line keeps its height either way). Errors replace the hint in `destructive` with the control's edge in `destructive`. A field is exactly two children, the label and a body (`.field-body`: the control, then its error or hint, note and state), so that fields side by side can share rows.

**Field row.** Fields side by side sit in a `.field-row` grid (the question card's three fields, the timeline's return and inflation, the add-holding sheet's two fee fields, the pension and study-fund pairs, the goal form's "זמין מיד" with its month). Every field in it is a subgrid over two of the grid's rows, labels in the first and bodies in the second, so the boxes start at the same height whatever the labels' line count; a one-line label sits at the bottom of the label row, against its box. A slot that holds a field with buttons beside it (a stepper, "לא יודעים") is a `.field-cell`: the same two-row subgrid, the field in its first column and each button in the box row of its own column at the end; the cell and the field share the field's 6 px row gap. On a phone the question card's chip stays beside the years box; from `md` it moves under it, into a third row of the cell. Rule for new forms: fields that share a row go in a `.field-row`, never in a bare grid or flex row, and a wrapper between the row and its field is `display: contents`.

**Number field.** `type="text"`, `inputmode="decimal"`, `dir="ltr"` with the text aligned right so it lines up with the label; unit (₪, %, שנים) as a fixed suffix at the inline end. Height `h-field`, `rounded-md`, edge `input`, fill `card`, 16 px text. Formats with thousands separators on blur, accepts pasted values with commas or ₪ through the one shared parser of 2.7 (comma decimals included), and adopts text typed into the pre-rendered field before the script loaded. An entered value is shown in `text-entered`.

**Slider field.** For amount, monthly deposit, return, inflation and horizon. The label row holds the label at the start and a compact number field at the end; the slider sits under it. Track 4 px in `muted` with the filled part in `primary`, filling from the right (inline start) as native RTL range inputs do; thumb 24 px with a 44 px hit area. The typed field accepts any value the engine accepts, including values outside the slider's range. The ranges are stored with the illustrative scenario in `data/parameters/illustrative-scenario.json` (signed off by the founder: monthly deposit 0 to 10,000 NIS in steps of 100, years 1 to 40); they limit only the slider, not what can be typed.

**Stepper.** For whole numbers with small ranges (age): minus and plus buttons (`IconDash`, `IconPlus`; `h-field`, never up and down arrows; `StepButtons`) around a number field.

**Segmented control.** For two to four exclusive options: employment type, and real or nominal (real is the default). Full width on a phone, `rounded-md`; the pressed segment is a solid `bg-primary` fill with a check icon (`aria-pressed`), and wherever a segmented control stands above a heading it is `max-w-sm` at the inline start. Radix ToggleGroup with `dir="rtl"`.

**Assumptions strip.** The inputs of a computation, shown with its result: a row of chips under the chart, each "label value" with the value's state style (`PlanChips`, section 11). Tapping a chip opens the inputs sheet scrolled to that field; a final chip, "כל ההנחות", opens it at the top. On a phone it scrolls horizontally; at `lg` the full input panel replaces it beside the chart. Entered values stay in blue on the chips, so what the chart rests on is visible without opening anything.

**Inputs sheet.** A bottom sheet on a phone (`<dialog>`, `overlay` behind, swipe or X to close, the chart stays visible above it at reduced height where space allows); at `lg`, a panel at the start (right) of the chart instead of a sheet. Fields in the order of 3.1's list. Changes apply as the person types; there is no submit button.

**Button.** Primary (`bg-primary text-primary-foreground`, one per screen), secondary (`bg-secondary`), quiet (text only, `accent` wash on press), destructive (a quiet button in `destructive` text, for the "מחיקה" of a delete confirmation; its own variant, because `text-destructive` added to a quiet button loses to its `text-link`), icon (44 px square). Height `h-field` for primary and secondary on a phone, 40 px allowed on PC. Labels follow the style guide (copy 10.5): a noun of action ("שמירה") or an infinitive ("לשמור את ההשוואה"), never the imperative.

**Link.** `text-link` with an underline on hover and focus; external links (sources) add a mirrored `ExternalLink` icon and open in a new tab.

**What acts looks like it acts, and does what it looks like.** A link is in the link colour, a button has its edge or fill, and an id or a row that opens something looks openable (founder, 2026-10-25: "you cant click F-80 for some reason"; 2026-11-03: "make the < next to the track a appear like a link"). What does nothing is never drawn as a button, a chip or a round plus (2026-10-30: "the הסתרת החוקים והכללים is a label button across the site"). A control does what its look promises: arrows move the way they point, section names that look like tabs change the view rather than jump down the page, a picker adds rather than replaces, and no action is found only by accident (2026-10-24: "There is an up and down arrow but it moves left-right"; 2026-11-06, on another site: "i accidentally realized double click adds it"). A table sorts by its column heads (2026-11-03: "instead of the sort buttons make the table clickable"). The actions a person expects are there: what they build can be renamed, deleted and dragged into order, and a slider's thumb drags (2026-10-24: "There is no delete button ... No rename button").

**Ask only what the site cannot know.** A field asks for the person's own numbers, never for what the site can look up (an exchange rate, a price), and never "who are you" to sort the person into a group whose answer changes little for most people (founder, 2026-10-25: "I dont want the user to input the exchange rates. the site should provide them"; 2026-11-04: "never do 'who are you' in this case").

**The reader's own items.** Where a page lets the reader add an item of their own (a fund, an index), the item takes the same settings the site's items have, each set by the reader: the site puts no label, kind or default on it that the reader did not choose (2026-11-05: "who said its צוברת? and who said its חשיפה לדולר?").

**A form has room.** A form of more than a few fields opens in a space of its own, its fields full size and grouped, never squeezed into a narrow panel or left a bare stack of boxes (2026-11-05: "make the interface nice, not like a basic form"; "can you make it more easy to navigate and set? the screen is tiny").

| Verdict (F-62) | Word | Icon (Lucide) | Tone |
|---|---|---|---|
| worth negotiating | שווה להתמקח | `Phone` | `caution-foreground` on `caution-subtle` |
| worth avoiding | שווה להימנע | `ArrowLeftRight` (symmetric, not mirrored) | `caution-foreground` on `caution-subtle` |
| not worth the effort | לא שווה את הטרחה | `Minus` | `muted-foreground` on `muted` |
| avoid not evaluated (no comparison) | a line, not a tag: "להימנע: לא נבדק" | `CircleDashed` | `muted-foreground` (the person can fix it: set a goal) |

A `Tag` (3.9) at `text-sm` weight 500, `rounded-sm`, padding 2×8px, icon 14px at the inline start. Under every tag one reason line in `text-sm muted-foreground` from copy 7.1, so the word is never alone: which threshold was reached and whether a reference exists, or what removes the fee.

Why amber for both action verdicts and never red: amber already means "look here, something to act on" (stale, secondary source); red means an open rule the site cannot compute (`blocked`) or a destructive action, and a fee worth avoiding is neither. The two amber tags differ by icon and word; colour is not the only carrier (V6). Why "not worth the effort" is muted and not green: ignoring a fee is an absence of action, not a success; green (`positive`) stays for "fits" and "considered".

### 7.2 Fee row, reference line, cap line, reduction field

Called for by 3.5 Out of the product (cost per fee ranked, the regulator average or "no reference available", what a stated reduction would be worth), D4, D7, D10, D20, method F-61, F-68.

- **Fee row.** A `card` row, `rounded-lg`, padding 16px (24 at `md`), gap 8px. Anatomy, start to end on PC and top to bottom on a phone: the fee's name (h3 `text-lg`, a `Term`) with its rate as a Num in its state and its base in `text-sm muted` ("כל שנה, מהחיסכון"); the cost over the horizon as a computed Num at `text-xl` weight 600 with "ב־{שנים}" in `text-xs muted`; the verdict tag and its reason line; then three quiet lines: reference, cap, and (on an avoid verdict) "במקום גבוה יותר בהשוואה, בלי עמלה כזו: {אפשרות}" (and "עם עמלה כזו נמוכה יותר") linking to each option, holding the room of every alternative whether or not it ranks above; then the reduction field; then the negotiation disclosure; a chevron at the end. Rows are ordered by cost, largest first, and re-order with FLIP after an input change. The rate's state tells the story at a glance: blue for a statement figure, solid underline for a record or a CMA average, dotted for nothing (a rate is never computed).
- **Reference line.** `text-sm`. With a CMA class reference: "ממוצע הרשות לכל המסלולים מהסוג {סיווג}: {אחוז}, לשנת {שנה}, מ־{מספר} מסלולים" with every figure a computed Num (F-68) and the `computed` state tag once at the start. **One measure:** the line shows the mean alone, the figure the verdict, the reduction field and the negotiation text use; a second measure beside it (a median equal to the fee) reads as "the fee is normal" while the verdict compares with the mean (fresh reader, 2026-11-07), and the holdings card's class line follows the same rule; under it "במסלול הזה עצמו שילמו העמיתים בממוצע {אחוז}" with a sourced Num (CAT-37), only where that track is the record the run prices; where the class comes from a track the site chose and the run prices another record (a company's list, a holding's statement), the line says instead that the site chose the track, naming it by its number and linking it, claims no sign-off for it, and says the average is of tracks like it, not of any one fund, so it never reads as the person's fund beside a strip that names another company (fresh reader, 2026-11-07: "which fund is this?"). Without one: "אין ממוצע להשוואה: {סיבה}" in `muted-foreground` and a link out with `ExternalLink` (14px): the Bank of Israel's fee dashboard (D20), the ISA's report, TASE's calculator; no link for direct expenses (G-14). The line never shows a number it does not have, and never leaves the slot empty: the reason is the content.
- **Cap line.** `text-sm muted-foreground`: "התקרה בחוק: {אחוז} בשנה" or "{אחוז} מכל הפקדה" with the rule chips (3.2); "אין תקרה בחוק"; or `Lock` in `blocked` with "התקרה: שאלה פתוחה {קוד}". A secondary-source cap carries the Num's "משני".
- **Reduction field.** A percent `NumberField` (3.3, 48px, 7rem wide) labelled "אם יורידו ל־" inline with "שווה {סכום} ב־{שנים}" (computed Num, `text-base` weight 500). The field starts with the reference in the parameter style of the sensitivity suggestion (6.2: `muted-foreground` text and the tag "הצעה") only where the reference is below the rate charged now; otherwise it starts empty. Typing makes it entered. No negative: at or above the current rate the line reads "זה לא הורדה". A line at 0% now has no field and no negotiation text. **A verdict follows the worth beside it, never the cost** (D90, revised 2026-11-07): "שווה להתמקח" is judged on what bringing the fee to the average is worth, the figure under the field, against the site's threshold; a cost above the threshold beside a small worth reads as advice to call, and fresh readers took it so ("שווה להתקשר" beside 108 ₪ and 187 ₪ over ten years). A card never pairs "שווה להתמקח" with a worth below the threshold, nor with a field that reads "זו לא הורדה"; a fee not worth it now whose discount ends within the period says "לא שווה עכשיו", when the discount ends and what the fee can rise to, which is when to check again; a test on the rendered cards guards it (`components/fees/verdict-worth.test.ts`); where the verdict rests on a rate that comes later (the rate after a discount), its reason says when; where the worth rests on a cut counted for a limited period (a reduction held for the authority's years, D86), the reason or the line beside the worth names the period, on every page that shows such a worth; and an opening sentence never says the opposite of a card below it (founder's review, 2026-11-07; lead, the same day). **"Avoid" only with something better to pick** (D90, revised the same day): a card says "שווה להימנע" only where an option without the fee, or with a lower one, ranks above the product on the same page; otherwise it gives the negotiate verdict alone, so a product the page ranks first never reads "avoid" and needs no softened word. Readers saw "avoid" beside "במקום הראשון" or "במקום 2" and asked which to believe; the same test file guards it. **A verdict never contradicts a place said on the same page**: a general rule for every badge beside a ranking. **What would make it worth it** (fresh readers, 2026-11-07: "the page never says what would push the 108 ₪ above 1,000 ₪"): after a "not worth it" reason with a cut to ask for, one plain line, "שווה להתמקח כשסכום היום {סכום} או יותר, או כשהכסף נשאר {שנים} או יותר.", both computed Nums from F-61's search, each part only where found; it comes with the sensitivity, after the hand rests, into room the reason already holds (the longest sum and horizon it can say). A verdict that says "not now" answers "then when?" on the same card.
- **Interaction-remainder line** under the rows: "השפעה משולבת של העמלות זו על זו: {סכום}" with a `Term` and a signed computed Num, then "הסכום של כל השורות והשורה הזו הוא העלות הכוללת." in `text-xs muted`. Why shown: F-61 reports it as a line, and without it the rows would not add up to the headline.
- **Thresholds line.** `text-sm muted-foreground` with the two P-03 values as parameter Nums and the P-03 chip; the link "לכל הפרמטרים".

## 9. Exposure mode, the revisit and the not-covered page

### 9.1 Mode tabs, the fall fields, the entered-return field, the cash-rate line, the holding-wrapper chips

Called for by 3.2 of the product ("two modes"; "the loss filter per exposure type (D11)"; "equity and bond returns are entered by the person and labelled so"), D7, D9, D11, D20, D21, method A-29, F-71. `components/exposure-mode/`.

- **Mode tabs.** A `SegmentedControl` (3.9): "לפי מוצר | לפי חשיפה", under the saved-goal strip, sticky under the header on a phone. Why a tab and not a second page in the menu: both modes answer the same saved goal with the same money, and the product names them as two modes of one capability; a tab keeps the strip, the banner and the save in one place. Under the control one line in `text-sm muted-foreground` says what the selected mode compares (copy 9.0), so the switch is never a mystery.
- **Accepted-fall field.** A percent `NumberField` (3.3, 48 px), empty, labelled "הירידה הגדולה ביותר שתסכימו לספוג בדרך למטרה", turning `entered` (blue, the pencil) once typed. **No preset fills it and no placeholder suggests a number** (D11: "no questionnaire, no sourced mapping is claimed"; D21: the presets never set it). The hint says it is the person's decision, not a score. On the goal form it sits in the constraints card because it is one (F-83 outcome 2: excluded by the person's constraint); on an exposure row it appears as the **accepted-fall chip** (an `entered` `Tag` "מסכימים לספוג עד {אחוז}") with "לחשיפה הזו בנפרד" opening the same field in place for a per-exposure override (A-29 is per exposure type; the engine's `acceptedFallById` wins over the common one). When the goal has none, the chip is `caution` ("לא הוזן כמה תסכימו לספוג") and links to the field, because a filter the person did not set must be visibly off.
- **Worst-fall field.** The same percent `NumberField`, per exposure row, labelled "הירידה החמורה ביותר שאתם צופים ב{חשיפה}, בדרך למטרה", `entered` once typed. Its hint lives behind an info popover (the row already holds four inputs): the site has no source for such an estimate and offers none; the link goes to the exposure guide's "סיכונים" block, which shows only what the rule base holds (ETF-33 to ETF-42 describe the indices; no drawdown figure is in the rule base, glossary 10.2). Why two fields and not one slider "risk": D11 forbids a score; two plain numbers the person owns, compared by one rule (`worst > accepted` excludes, equal keeps), is the whole filter, and both numbers stay visible on the row so the exclusion is legible.
- **Entered-return field.** The percent `NumberField` of the goal form's assumptions card, repeated per exposure row for an exposure whose `return_source.mode` is `entered`: label "תשואה שנתית ב{מטבע}", empty with the D9 line "לא הוזן: האתר לא מנחש תשואה", `entered` with the pencil once typed. The currency in the label matters: the S&P 500's return is in dollars and the run converts (F-04, A-03), the TA-125's in shekels.
- **Cash-rate line.** For a cash-like exposure (`cash_like: true`) the row shows, instead of an empty field, the rate the run uses and where it came from: **a published bank rate** as a sourced `Num` with the institution mark ("לפי החברה") and "ריבית הבנק, בתוקף מ־{תאריך}" (the deposit record of the holding wrapper's P-05 instrument), the BANK-13 note in its provenance sheet (the bank's published minimum for the type, not an offer), the `Clock` when the record is older than its cadence, and the quiet action "להזין ריבית אחרת" that swaps the line for an entered field (the rate becomes the person's, blue, with "לחזור לריבית שפורסמה"); or, where no record gives a rate (makam: the Bank of Israel's yield is a link, D20), the sentence "אין סדרה ממקור: שערי בנק ישראל מוצגים רק בקישור. הזינו תשואה." with the `ExternalLink` and an empty entered field. Why a line and not a field with a default: the default is a sourced number with a date and a strength, and it must be shown as such (standing rule: every number traces to a source), not hidden as a placeholder.
- **Holding-wrapper chips.** One `text-sm` line "מוחזקת ב{מוצר}" with the wrapper's name as a `Term` and the state of the choice: the parameter chip (P-08, "פרמטר של האתר") until the person changes it, `entered` after; "החליפו מוצר" expands a single-choice chip row (the preset-chip style of 6.1) over the record's `wrapper_ids`, names from the wrapper records. Why the row names a wrapper at all: the engine runs an exposure through one wrapper the caller names (F-71) and never picks it; the honest form shows which one and lets the person change it, and the link "באיזה מוצר להחזיק את זה" hands the full question to wrapper mode.

### 9.2 Exposure row, return label, exclusion tag, the excluded group

Called for by 3.2 Out of the product ("a ranked list of eligible … exposures with, for each: projected real value at the horizon net of fees and tax; the assumptions, each marked sourced or entered"), D7, D9, D11, D17, method F-71, F-80, F-83.

- **Exposure row.** The ranked row of 6.3 with one block added between the name and the figure: the **inputs strip**, a `bg-secondary` `rounded-md` block (padding 12 px) holding the row's four inputs (return or cash-rate line, worst fall, accepted-fall chip, holding-wrapper chips) in one line of four cells at `md` and in two columns on a phone (the names stay in one no-wrap span). Why the inputs live on the row and not in the assumptions sheet alone: there are seven exposures and two numbers each; the empty state of this mode is a list of rows waiting for numbers, and typing where the result appears is the "interactive by default" principle (product §2). The sheet repeats them grouped by exposure. Under the figure the **return label** (below); then the tie tag, the deciding line (6.3, against the row above), the run's assumption chips and the wrapper's caution or lock tags, the links, the choose button (9.4) and the chevron. - **Exclusion tag.** For a row the loss filter removed (F-71, F-83 outcome 2, reason `loss-filter`): a `Tag` in the `entered` style with the `Pencil`, "נפסלה: צופים ירידה של {אחוז}, מסכימים לספוג עד {אחוז}", both numbers as entered `Num`s. Entered styling, not caution or blocked, because the exclusion is the person's own setting, as 6.3 draws the excluded outcome (the `Pencil` icon, `entered` colour). The row keeps its inputs strip live and drops the figure; one line under it says that changing either number brings it back.

- **Loss note.** A ranked row whose filter could not run (one of the two numbers missing) carries "מסנן הירידה לא הופעל: הזינו את הירידה ש…" in `caution-foreground` with `CircleAlert` and the field linked. Caution, because a filter the person asked for silently not running is exactly the kind of thing the amber role flags; the row still ranks (engine: "not-evaluated" verdict leaves the exposure unfiltered).

### 9.3 Exposure chart

Called for by 3.2 Out of the product ("a chart of projected value over time for the top options on one axis"), D7, D9 (the "entered by you" state must be unmistakable on the chart, so an entered return is never read as the site's view), and 6.5.

- **Form.** The comparison chart of 6.5: real value over time, time left to right, one value axis, direct labels at the right (the leader's only), legend above, readout, table view, 240 px on a phone and 320 px at `lg`. One line per drawn exposure.
- **Up to seven series, four at a time.** Exposure mode can rank seven exposures (and more once a tier-2 instrument graduates as an exposure). The chart never draws more than four lines: ranks 1 to 3 in the option roles (`chart-option-1` to `-3`, each its own hue as 6.5 says) and the cash-like exposure as a **reference line** in `chart-baseline` (neutral-300, 1.5 px), the role the current account plays in wrapper mode. A **series chip row** above the chart (multi-select chips, one per ranked exposure, the drawn ones filled) lets the person swap which exposures are drawn; a fifth choice replaces the oldest non-reference one, and the chip row ends with "עוד {מספר} בטבלה". The colour follows the role (position), not the exposure: a swapped-in line takes the freed role's hue. **Why four lines, not seven:** seven clearly different hues cannot be found outside the reserved ones (blue and violet are number states; amber, red and green are flags; orange and rose are fees and tax), and seven labels do not stack in 240 px without overlapping. **Why not small multiples:** the question is comparative on one axis (the product's 3.2 Out: "on one axis"); panels with their own scales would hide the gap the ranking is about.
- **Entered returns on the chart.** As 6.5: a line resting on an entered return is dashed 6/4 in its role colour, and its legend swatch is dashed. The convention of 2.3 (`chart-entered` dashed blue) is for a single series on the timeline; with several entered lines the colour must carry the rank and the dash carries "entered", as 6.5 chose. In this mode most lines are dashed, which is the point: the **caption** "הקווים המקווקווים מבוססים על תשואות שהזנתם, לא על הערכה של האתר." is always rendered under the chart, even when the one drawn line is a sourced cash line, so a screenshot of this chart can never pass as the site's forecast. The reference line is solid only when its rate is a published one; an entered cash rate dashes it too.
- **Labels, markers, readout, table, motion** as 6.5, with the table holding every ranked exposure (not only the four drawn), so nothing ranked is unreachable from the chart card.

### 9.4 Choose and save, the revisit line and banner, the what-changed sheet, diff and value-change rows

Called for by 3.9 of the product ("a saved comparison records inputs, assumptions, the rule, parameter and data versions used, the result and the choice. On a later visit, re-running it … reports which rules, parameters or data points changed since and whether the ranking still holds"; "works when": "re-check: rule X changed on date Y" and "unchanged otherwise"), D1, D7, method F-82, and the need to tell a change that affects the saved ranking from one that does not, so a routine data refresh is not read as a reason to decide again. `components/revisit/`.

- **Choose button and save button.** "בחרתי בזה" is a quiet 44 px button on every ranked row; tapping it marks the row as the choice (the label becomes "הבחירה שלכם, מ־{תאריך}" with `Check`) and saves the comparison. "לשמור את ההשוואה" under the list saves without a choice. Why an explicit save and not every re-run: a revisit compares today against a frozen moment, and the product names that moment, "the choice they made at the end of a comparison"; a comparison that re-saved itself on every visit would always be "unchanged". Saving again replaces the saved one after the inline confirm line of 6.7 naming the old date, so the person knows which moment they are giving up.
- **Revisit line.** One `text-xs` line on the saved-goal strip (6.7), under the goal line: "נשמרה ב־{תאריך}" (with "הבחירה: {אפשרות}" and `Check` when there is one), then the notice: "לבדיקה מחדש: {מה} השתנה ב־{תאריך}" in `caution-foreground` with `CircleAlert`, or "ללא שינוי מאז {תאריך}" in `muted-foreground`, with the link "מה השתנה". {מה} names the single changed entry when there is one (the product's "rule X changed on date Y") and counts by kind otherwise; the date is the entry's new `checked` date, and an entry that carries none (a parameter or an assumption) reads "השתנה מאז {תאריך}" with the saved date. Why caution and not red for "changed": nothing is blocked; the site is asking the person to look again. Why "unchanged" is not green: `positive` means "dimension considered" (2.2) and an unchanged decision is a quiet fact, not an achievement.
- **Revisit banner.** On the result page, under the mode tabs: a `Callout` in `caution` when anything changed or the re-run could not finish, a quiet `card` line when nothing did. Order inside: the **ranking verdict** first when the ranking moved ("הדירוג השתנה: {X} במקום {Y}."), else the notice first and the verdict second ("הדירוג נשמר: {X} עדיין ראשונה."); "קבוצת השקולות השתנתה: {שמות}." when only the tie set moved (`tieSetUnchanged` false); "לא ניתן לבדוק: {סיבה}" with `Lock` when the re-run stopped (a rule past its end date, method §13). The **choice line** follows when a choice exists: "בחרתם ב{אפשרות}; היא עדיין במקום {מספר}" or, in `caution-foreground`, "בחרתם ב{אפשרות}; היום היא {תוצאה}: {סיבה}" (the choice is now listed, excluded or not evaluated). Two buttons: "מה השתנה" (the sheet) and "לשמור את ההשוואה של היום". Why the verdict is a fixed sentence: like 6.3's deciding line, the site states a computed fact (`topUnchanged`, `tieSetUnchanged`) in fixed words, never a free judgement.
- **What-changed sheet.** A sheet (phone) or a 26rem card (PC): the **diff list** grouped by kind in a fixed order (כללים · פרמטרים · נתונים · הנחות), each an h4 with its count; one **diff row** per entry: the entry's code and words with a link to its page, then "היה {ערך} · עכשיו {ערך}" as `Num`s in their own states (a rule figure sourced with the two `checked` dates in `text-xs muted`; a parameter in the parameter state; an assumption `entered`; a state change written as words, "היה פרמטר · עכשיו הזנתם"; a bracket schedule as a two-column table inside a `Disclosure`); an entry gone from the bundle reads "הוסר מהמאגר" or "הקובץ לא קיים עוד" with `Lock`, a new one "קובץ חדש". Then h4 "מה זז בהשוואה" with one **value-change row** per option whose value moved ("{אפשרות}: {סכום} → {סכום}" as computed `Num`s; `Tag`s "חדשה בהשוואה" / "לא מדורגת עוד"), then h4 "לפני ואחרי" with two compact dated columns (top option, tie set). Footer: save again. Why only what the saved runs read: F-82 keeps the rules and files the runs used, so this list is the precise answer to "did my decision's basis change". The empty state is a full sentence ("אף כלל, פרמטר, נתון או הנחה שההשוואה נשענה עליהם לא השתנו."), because the product's "works when" asks that an unchanged decision *shows* as unchanged.
- **Unreadable-record form.** When a saved comparison cannot be read (an older site version the migration does not cover, an unknown mode or id): the strip line and the banner are replaced by one sentence in `caution` ("ההשוואה שנשמרה היא מגרסה ישנה של האתר ואי אפשר לבדוק אותה מחדש."), the readable date and choice as plain text, and two actions ("להשוות מחדש ולשמור", "מחיקה מהמכשיר"); nothing is converted by guesswork, as the strip's old-version state does for a goal.

### 9.6 Graduation status, blockers, closures, graduated list

Called for by D2 ("a tier-2 instrument moves to tier 1 when it has a sourced record and its tax questions are closed"), 3.1 of the product ("one further page lists tier-2 instruments with what is known and which open question blocks each"), method G-24. `routes/not-covered.tsx`: one card per tier-2 instrument, with what is known and the "מצב הכיסוי" block. The cards are the D2 list (`notCoveredCards` in `lib/graduation.server.ts`, from `tier2Table`), never every row of the glossary's section 3: a fee, label, role or event of a tier-2 holding stays a term, and a term a covered page explains never shows here (2026-11-07; the rule is `docs/copy.md` section 7, the guard `lib/not-covered.test.ts`).

- **Graduation status.** One line in weight 500 at the head of the card's "מצב הכיסוי" block, in one of three forms with its icon: **blocked**, `Lock` in `blocked`, "חסום: {מספר} שאלות פתוחות" (the lock is the site's sign for an open rule, 2.2); **remaining**, `CircleDashed` in `caution`, "השאלות נסגרו; נותר: {רשומה עם מקור · שיטת חישוב}" (the dashed circle is "not evaluated yet" everywhere else, and that is what an instrument with closed questions and no record is); **graduated**, `Check` in `positive`, "עבר לכיסוי מלא ב־{תאריך}", which appears only in the page-end list, since the card itself leaves the page. The status is computed from data (the instrument's questions and their state, whether a record exists under `data/instruments/`, whether the method has a formula), never typed: a hand-written "almost there" would be the site guessing.
- **Blockers list.** The card's blocked `Callout`s, one per open question, each with the question in words (its `title_he` in `data/open-questions.json`; the code stays in its sheet), plus a line under a question that was narrowed ("השאלה צומצמה ב־{תאריך}, לפי" and the rules chip, never a bare rule code, 2026-11-07) or reopened ("נפתח מחדש ב־{תאריך}" in `caution`). Narrowed stays a blocker: a smaller open question is still open. Where the only answer is an institution's (a savings policy's tax base in two insurers' words, TXA-28; a REIT's withholding practice, TX2-20) the callout adds, in `caution-foreground`, that the site computes on it only if decision F1 extends D10 to institution-strength rules: the honest blocker is then a decision of the site, and the card says so rather than hiding it behind "open question".
- **Closures list.** Under h5 "מה נסגר": the questions that closed, in `muted-foreground` and not struck (a struck date beside a bare count read as a fragment, 2026-11-07), each "נסגר ב־{תאריך}: {השאלה במילים}" with the rules as a chip, or "שאלה נסגרה ב־{תאריך}, לפי" and the chip when the rule base records no question number; the empty state is "עוד לא נרשמו סגירות." and never "nothing closed", because the absence of a record is not a fact about the world. Why show closures on a page about what is *not* covered: progress is the honest answer to "is it covered yet?", and a friend holding a savings policy should see that the fee cap closed this week even though the tax question did not.
- **No path line.** A fixed "כשייסגר: יופיע ב…" sentence closed every card until 2026-11-07; the same promise on each card said more than the site would do for each product, and it was removed.
- **Graduated list.** An h2 "עברו לכיסוי מלא" at the page's end with one line per instrument that graduated in the last release (name, date, the link to its new guide page), present only when it has items. Why one release: a friend who saw the card last month looks for it here first; after that the guide list is where it lives.
- **Nearest line.** "הקרוב ביותר לכיסוי מלא" in `text-sm muted` on one card only, and only when a ranking with its reason is in the data file. A ranking that lives in a research note is not shown.

## 11. Kit (the shared components)

The founder asked for "custom components so we have consistent UI and an easier and faster time programming, like the equivalent of a class, but for UI components", after two layout bugs in a day (a sentence with a number laid out as a flex column, one piece to a line; a closed fold inside an open fold that read as open). The kit is `site/app/components/kit/`, one file per component or small family, re-exported from `kit/index.ts`. Import from `components/kit`, never from a neighbouring feature. The gallery at `/kit` (pre-rendered, `noindex`, in no menu and no search; every number on it is a labelled example) shows each component in its states, right to left, with its name beside it, and is the place to review the look. The survey that found the duplicates is `research/specs/2026-10-25-ui-kit/survey.md`.

**The rule.** A new pattern goes into the kit first, with its gallery entry and, where it can be named, a guard in `kit/guards.test.ts` or a ratchet in `kit/ratchets.test.ts`, never into a feature folder; then the feature uses it (CLAUDE.md, "Shared components first"). A component takes the *choices* as props (`pad`, `size`, `tone`, `scroll`) and never as an overriding class: the site has no class merger, so two classes for one property are decided by the order of the generated stylesheet, not by the order in `class` (the `p-0 md:p-0` on the guide's key/value card never applied for that reason). No new look is made in the kit: each component is the best existing version.

| Component | When to use it |
|---|---|
| `Button`, `LinkButton` | `Button` for an action (`primary` once per screen, `quiet` for a text action, `destructive` for a quiet one that deletes, `icon` for a 44 px icon), `LinkButton` for a place to go. A `LinkButton`'s words often carry a name and may wrap on a phone: its height is a minimum, so a second line grows the button instead of spilling out of it. |
| `Tag` | A one-to-three-word label with a tone (state, kind, flag). Never colour alone: give it a word or an icon. `wrap` for a label that holds a name, such as a rule card's "של {company}": it breaks onto a second line instead of running past a phone's edge; the card's scope tag names the companies the way the rule chip does (the page's company or the first, and "ועוד N"). |

**Lists of products** (founder; the design and how it got there: `research/specs/2026-10-27-company-fund-card/spec.md`). These hold wherever the site lists several products of one kind (tracks, funds, deposits, accounts):

- **A table, not a stack of cards.** On a desktop the products are rows of one table (`ProductTable`); on a phone each product is one row under the next. A card with a facts column left half of every card empty.
- **The identifier is the link.** Where a product has an official page elsewhere (a fund's page on Maya), its number is that link, with the external icon (`1148808 ↗`), and its accessible name says where it leads. No separate "בדף הקרן במאיה" line. A link inside the site ends with the drill-in chevron (›), a link out with the external icon (↗).
- **The column head carries the words; the cell holds the short value.** "המדד" in the head and "ת"א-125 ›" in the cell, never "עוקבת אחרי מדד …" on every row. On a phone, where there is no head, the row says the one word ("מדד ת"א-125 ›").
- **Columns as wide as their content.** The number, link and figure columns shrink to what they hold; the name takes the rest, so its labels fit on one line.
- **A kind is a label, not a row or a column.** What a product is or does (how a fund follows its index, whether it pays dividends, its currency, a tax that follows from it) is a label chip that opens a sheet with its rule, from one shared set (`fundLabels()` for funds). A yes/no or a kind never gets its own column.
- **Nothing that repeats.** No figure that says again what a label says (a fund's currency ceiling beside "חשיפה לדולר"), no column whose value is the same on every row (every index fund's stock grade is 4), no line under a link that explains what the linked page explains. A label true of every row of a group shows once, in the group's head (D60); a label that is exactly one group leaves the label filter too; a list with no groups gives such a label one head line over the list.
- **Every column helps choose among the rows.** A column, a figure or a line stays only where it tells the rows apart in a way that changes the choice; one that only describes is left out or becomes a label (founder, 2026-11-05: "are you sure we show the interesting information in the table? maybe other columns would be better?").
- **Every row follows one pattern.** The rows of one list carry the same lines: a line that only some rows would have is left out, and two rows that need telling apart are told apart by what every row already shows, such as the fund number or a label (2026-11-03: "why does only אלטשולר and אנליסט have a עוקב text underneath? i dont want it remove it.").
- **A line under a row says what the figure uses.** A secondary line under a row (a group's price, a promotion) says in its own words whether the row's figure counts it, and which of its numbers that is (2026-11-04: "its not clear which of these is being used - 0.07 or 0.15").
- **Every option a person has.** A list holds every option a person really has for the holding (D17), foreign funds and every house that sells them included; a row that cannot be priced says what the place does offer and opens it, not only what is missing (2026-10-31: "who said we are buying israeli funds? what about iShares?"; "when i click the broker itself i want to switch to its options that it does provide").
- **Familiar names first.** A list that is not ranked by a figure puts the names people know first, by a sourced measure such as assets or members, and the small institutions in a group of their own lower down (2026-10-25: "honestly i have never heard of the[m] before").
- **Who offers what.** A list across companies keeps the companies in view, its rows grouped under or named by their company, so the reader sees who offers what and not only a pile of tracks (2026-11-03: "i want to have a notion of the different companies not only the different tracks").
- **A column of one number state carries its mark in the head (D60).** Where every figure in a column is sourced, or every one computed, the column head word carries the underline of that state and one line above the list says what it means; the cells stay plain and still open their source or calculation when tapped. A cell whose state differs keeps its own mark; entered numbers keep their blue, flags keep their icon. Every column that holds a figure has a head.
- **A ceiling is not a holding.** A limit a product declares (a fund's exposure profile, "4A") is never drawn the way a measured share is drawn (the track table's rings, F-87 and F-88). If shown at all, it is words.

## 12. Charts

The rules every chart and visual on the site follows. The charts themselves: the timeline (3.4), the comparison chart (6.5), the fee-drag chart (7.3), the exposure chart (9.3), the guide-page visuals (10), and the kit's `ChangeChart`, `PileStory`, `StairsChart`, `PairBars`, `YearBars`, `CostStack` and `PeriodStrip` (11). Drawing a major chart before it is built, and the choice its drawing opens with, are CLAUDE.md's ground rule 1.

**Reading the visuals** (founder, from his choices on the daily money-market guide, where a filter over cards, a scatter plot, four equal slices and a precise time axis were each turned down for a table with coloured tags and bars). Every visual follows these:

1. **Draw only true numbers.** A real quantity (a rate, an amount, a time in months) gets a mark on its scale, with its figure printed on the mark itself, or right beside it where it does not fit; a legend never carries the figures in the marks' place (founder, 2026-11-06: "put the amounts on the bar itself"). A category or an order (how soon the money is back, a kind of product) gets words, an icon and a colour, never a bar or a meter: equal slices invent a unit the data does not have.
2. **Colour carries meaning, and there is enough of it.** Each colour says one thing the reader can name (teal is the rate, warmer is held longer, stripes are a range); grey on grey says nothing, and a colour that only ranks or decorates is left out. The flags of 3.2 keep their meaning: a scale that borrows their hues stays away from numbers and always has a word and an icon.
3. **Every encoding is said once.** Whatever a colour, a pattern or a shape means is said on the screen, even when every figure is also printed: by a label on the mark, or by a legend for the marks that cannot carry one, never by both, and not again in tiles under the chart (founder, 2026-10-26: "remove the legend of the rings because its just a duplicate of the inside"; 2026-10-27: "remove the top legend"). Marks that share a meaning share one legend: a list whose rows each carry the same kind of bar has one legend above the list, never one per row (founder, 2026-11-06: "the legend is repeated on every broker? why? just put it somewhere central"), as a label true of every row shows once in the group's head.
4. **Show the whole comparison; do not make the reader operate it.** A guide page shows its product among its alternatives, all at once, with the page's product marked (a tinted row and a tag). No filter that hides or fades rows, no control the reader must use before the visual says anything. An analysis section may have a control (ground rule 3: the indices page's change chart has a marker) when its first state already shows the whole comparison.
5. **The plainest mark that shows the number.** A bar, a line or a dot on a scale; no coins, towers or pictures standing in for an amount. The care goes into what is shown, not into the shape of the mark (founder, 2026-11-02: "אני פשוט לא ממש רואה ערך בכדורים. למה לא BAR?").
6. **No forced picture, and no what-if no one would plan on.** A visual is drawn only where the truth fits it; where the truth is more complex than a picture, words or a table carry it and the page has no visual (2026-10-25: "the truth is just more complex than that. so for now dont"; 2026-11-01: "you are forcing a visualization into something that has nothing to do with it"). A scenario is one a person would plan on, and a conclusion goes no further than the data: the same fall every year for twelve years, or the past read as the future, is not shown as a likely outcome, and a case few people meet is not put at the centre (2026-10-29: "we shouldnt draw conclusions from the past, no?"; 2026-10-25: "if its 1% of people, we should even make it less central").

Before a visual merges, the reading test: for each mark on the screen, one sentence says what it is from what is on the screen alone.

**Colour inside a chart** (ground rule 6). Every legend identity has its own clearly different hue, checked pair by pair with the palette validator (normal-vision worst pair at least 15 ΔE, colour-blind at least 8, or 6 where a word sits on the mark) and then by fresh readers (who saw a validated rose and magenta as "one pink family"). Distinctness inside a chart comes before one meaning per colour across the page (founder: "i'd rather have two conflicting colors on opposite sites of the pages than a graph with all its lines the same color"): within one chart or panel a hue means one thing, a distant section may give it another, and each page's spec carries its colour table. The categorical palette for comparing options on one chart avoids the state hues (blue, violet) and the status hues. Red, amber and green stay status colours, never a series. Blue and violet stay reserved for entered and computed numbers. Every chart's colours are run through the validator together, as drawn. `kit/ratchets.test.ts` checks each chart's roles pair by pair on every run, by the validator's method (`lib/palette-check.ts`): a new chart adds its roles to the list there, and today's pairs below the line are listed by name until they are apart.

**Axes and labels.** Time runs left to right, and so does every other axis (V3). Value-axis labels sit at the plot's left edge, just above their gridline, with a card-colour halo (`text-xs chart-text`); the right of the plot is kept for the direct labels and the horizon, and at the left the bands are still thin, so the labels do not sit on colour. Three to five gridlines, compact form (2.7). Year ticks run along the bottom at whole steps of 1, 2, 5 or 10 years from today (`niceYearTicks(horizon)`, about four ticks; the horizon end is not always a tick, its marker still marks it). Value labels are drawn after the lines, with their card-colour halo, on every chart, so no line runs through them. Names and numbers are written on the chart, with no table needed to decode it.

**Around every chart** the kit's `ChartLegend`, `ChartReadout` and `ChartTable` (a table view of the same numbers); its motion is the kit's glide (`useGlidingSeries` and the rest) and its pointer the kit's zoom and pick (`useYearZoom`, `useChartPick`). A series that is not drawn has no legend item, label, readout row or table column.
