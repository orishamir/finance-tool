# Product definition

What the site is: who it is for, its decisions, its capabilities and what it will not do. Written from `docs/discovery.md`, the reports in `research/discovery/` and the founder interview, and confirmed by the founder on 2026-10-22. The decisions in section 1 may change as he learns more: a change is recorded in its row with its date and reason, and the capabilities that depend on it are re-checked.

Marking convention for each capability:

- **[R]** supported by research: the problem and the shape of the solution come from the reports or the interview.
- **[P]** a proposal of the product's author: not in the reports or interview; included because the site would be incomplete for the stated decisions without it.
- Mixed cases say which parts are which.

No figure, rate, ceiling or fee appears in this document. Where one is needed, the capability names the data file, rule or parameter it will read from.

A wrapper record holds tax treatment, liquidity, fee bases, ceilings and eligibility with dates (securities account, kupat gemel lehashkaa, hishtalmut, deposit, and so on). ## 2. Who it is for and what it must be

**Users.** The founder (22, salaried, Israeli tax only, no self-directed investment yet) and friends and family with similar questions but different ages, employment and holdings. All in Israel. Hebrew speakers. Using a phone at least as often as a PC. Each on their own device. A friend often arrives on a page from a link the founder sends, so every page stands on its own and leads back to its list.

**The job.** A person has a goal, or money sitting idle, or an instrument already chosen, or holdings they never chose, or a product or track name in a bank or manager app they do not understand. They want to know, in Hebrew, with graphs: what their options are, how each works, what the thing an institution is advertising actually is, which is best for their case net of fees and tax over their horizon, which fund variant and which provider and track to pick for it, which fees are worth fighting, and what they might be failing to consider. Then they want to act and be able to come back later and see whether anything changed.

**Done when** (site-level success, from the founder's interview; reviewed with the founder one quarter after first use):

1. The founder has moved the idle cash into a chosen wrapper and exposure at a chosen provider, using the site.
2. The founder can list what the site considered for that decision and says the list contained at least one dimension he had not thought of.
3. The founder has negotiated or deliberately ignored at least one fee based on the site's shekel figure.
4. One friend has gone from a goal to a concrete choice using the site alone, on a phone, in Hebrew.
5. Every product and track offered by the banks and managers the founder uses is in the site's catalogue, explained in plain Hebrew, and is considered by every comparison it is eligible for, or shown there as not evaluated with the reason (D17). The test case is the Meitav app (not Meitav Trade): its "open a new saving" opens a kupat gemel lehashkaa (fund 7857) whose regulator record lists 16 tracks, one of them "כללי"; all 16 are in the catalogue, so the eight the app shows are among them, and whatever the app's second "כללי" turns out to be, it is one of the Meitav products and names the catalogue holds (CAT-28).

**Fixed constraints** (from `guidelines/project.md` and `guidelines/research.md`): Hebrew UI, right-to-left; phone and PC; non-commercial; no accessibility, legal or commercial work; every number traces to a file under `data/` with its source and date, to a computation in `docs/method.md` over such figures, or to the person's own entry; no financial or tax rule from memory, only from the `il-finance` rule base; lists are starting points.

**Principles the research adds:**

- **Neutral by construction.** No referrals, no agents, no affiliate links. The site can show "the best option that pays nobody" (UX report 4.3, 6).
- **Fees before past returns.** Ranking by past return predicts weakly; fees predict better (UX report 6, Morningstar evidence). Returns are shown as history, not as a ranking key.
- **Collapse equivalent options.** Much of the choice set is noise (UX report 4.4). The site says "these are the same for your purpose" rather than ranking indistinguishable funds.
- **Show the dimension, not just the answer.** The founder's blocker is unknown unknowns. Every comparison shows the list of what it considered.
- **Process is part of the price.** Getting the real fee and switching provider are where people get hurt (UX report 4.5).
- **Real terms by default.** Every projected value is shown in real terms, with nominal available on request; the deflator is defined in `docs/method.md`.

**Principles the founder adds:**

- **Interactive by default.** Every graph and computed number responds to the person's inputs. The inputs a computation uses (the assumptions in `docs/method.md` §2.2) start from a sourced value or a site parameter, can be changed on the page where the result is shown, and once changed are shown as "entered by you" (D7). Rules from `il-finance` are not inputs and cannot be changed by the person; site parameters that are judgements (the tie threshold, verdict thresholds, sensitivity bounds) are shown, not edited, so a ranking cannot be tuned into a different answer.
- **No blind spots** (D17). A recommendation is only as good as the options it knows. Every comparison chooses from the complete set of options at its level, accounts for each one with its outcome and reason, and says how many it considered. An option the site knows exists but cannot evaluate yet is shown with the reason, never silently left out. In the same way a gap is never read as an answer: a reader cannot mistake old data for current data, a missing or old input for a considered one, a missing reference for a fair price, a missing rule for permission, an unmatched track for one that fits, a missing tariff for an absent provider, an uncollected fund for one that does not exist, an institution's statement for a rule, an entered return for the site's view, an unfiltered row for a filtered one, a bank's published rate for an offer, a change to a rule a saved comparison never read for a change to its decision, or an instrument whose questions have closed for a covered one.

**The first page.** The site's name, one line and a search field for any product, track or term name; then three groups by what the reader came to do (D95): to understand (the guide's kinds as pills, P-10, and the whole guide), to compare (the deposits, the tracks, the indices and the companies, each with one line saying what the page answers) and to check what they were told (two of the rules page's own example claims, each opening that page with the claim typed, and the whole rules page); then a link to the tier-2 "not covered yet" page and a line leading to the rules page.

### 3.5 The fees in a deal

[R]

When it is rebuilt it gives no steps for carrying out a move (D40). What stays meanwhile: the guides' one-line fact on switching, the engine's switching cost F-66, the switching and transfer rules in the rule base and their regulator sources (dependency 9), and the checklist ticks saved on devices.

### 3.8 Rule base, parameters and method

[R as constraint, P as a user-facing capability]

**Problem.** Ten rules are likely to change or be misremembered in the next two years (tax report 10). The founder's own tax claim did not match the report. The site must never state a rule it cannot cite, and must not pass off product judgements as rules.

**Three stores, kept apart:**

- **Rules** (`il-finance`): legal and product facts with text, conditions, exceptions, figures by tax year, source link, date checked, strength (primary, secondary, institution, open; D10, D23), scope (D30) and the dimensions they affect. Seeded from the tax report (sections 1 to 9) and the domain report (ceilings, liquidity, caps). Their open questions are tracked in `data/open-questions.json`.
- **Parameters** (`data/parameters/`): site judgements such as the tie threshold, fee-verdict thresholds and sensitivity bounds, each with value, rationale and founder sign-off date (N1), shown on the page as "site parameter". Now: no sign-off date; parameters are Claude's to decide (guidelines/working-with-the-founder.md; founder, 2026-11-08).
- **Method** (`docs/method.md`): every calculation defined (real vs nominal, the deflator, compounding, fee bases, tax at exit, equal-gross-return comparison), so the finance-reviewer can check the engine against it. `tests/golden/` holds hand-worked expected outputs for every "Works when" above.

**Out, for the reader.** Next to every rule-dependent number, a link that opens the rule, its source, its strength and its date; the rules behind a fact are one chip with a count (D48). The rules page, with the claim field, the filters and the "הידעת?" facts (D25, D47); every rule and every sourced number shows its source block first (publisher, document, locator, quote, date, strength only when weak) and a copy-citation button. A parameters page. A documents page, the plain answer to "where does all this come from": every document the rules and figures were read from, and the data records the site read, each with what rests on it and the page that uses it; it claims no size (no counts, no boasts) and lists no TASE funds (dependency 7). Rules, parameters and documents in the search. These pages are for the founder, a careful friend and a reviewer who want the rule or judgement behind a number. A "שווה לדעת" block on each wrapper guide. No change log (D24).

**Works when.** A reviewer can take any number on any page back to a rule, a parameter, a data file or "entered by you", each with a date. No calculation uses a rule marked open. Each of the ten "likely to change" rules has a re-check date.

### 3.9 Saved state and revisit

[P]

**Problem.** Friends and family use the site on their own devices without accounts (D1), and a decision made on today's rules should be revisitable without redoing the work. Nothing in the research covers this.

**In.** The person's inputs across 3.2, 3.4, 3.5 and 3.6, and the choice they made at the end of a comparison.

**Out.** One local state per browser, saved automatically, exportable and importable as a file so the founder can help a friend without any data on a server; the export and import panel is also a page of its own (`/transfer`), the link the founder sends a friend. A saved comparison records inputs, assumptions, the rule, parameter and data versions used, the result and the choice. On a later visit, re-running it against the versions it stored (D24) reports which rules, parameters or data points changed since and whether the ranking still holds.

**The export format** (N3) is versioned from the first write, so an old file still imports: the file is `mekor-state` version 3, one state document under the key `mekor.v2.state` (the key did not change with the version); an older version migrates on load and on import, and a newer one is refused. Beside the state document the file carries four side stores kept on the device (`site/app/lib/side-stores.ts`): the entered offers, the variant assumptions, the playbook ticks and the entered rates. No page reads the offers or the variant assumptions (D52, D53), nor the ticks (D38); they stay in the file.

**Data and rules.** The versioned stores in 3.8.

**Works when.** A decision recorded before a rule change shows "re-check: rule X changed on date Y" after it, and shows as unchanged otherwise. An exported file from one phone imports on another and reproduces the same results.

### 3.10 Catalogue of every option: what each institution offers

[R: founder interview; data report 1.2]

**Problem.** A recommendation can only choose among the options it knows about. Institutions offer many more products and tracks than a generic list of wrappers and exposures names, each under its own names, and the names do not match from one institution to the next. The founder's example: the Meitav app's "open a new saving" leads to a kupat gemel lehashkaa and offers eight tracks, two of them named "כללי". The regulator's data lists 16 tracks for that fund, one of them "כללי" (CAT-25, CAT-28), so an app can show fewer options than exist. A site that knows only what one app shows cannot say which track fits a goal. Most of these names are also unfamiliar to the founder and his friends, so every option needs a plain-Hebrew explanation. The same holds for every bank's deposit and savings-plan names. The catalogue is not an app decoder: it does not mirror an app's screens (D2, D17).

**In.** The comparisons (3.2, 3.4) read the catalogue directly: the person does not have to find an option for it to be considered. To browse it: a company chosen from the companies list, or a product or track name typed or pasted as it appears in an app, ad or statement. A photo is out of scope (no server-side processing, D1).

**Out.** Per company (a group of legal companies is one, D33): every product and track it offers, grouped by wrapper, each under the name the institution uses. The page shows one product at a time beside a list of the company's products, after an overview of them all; a bank's deposits and savings plans are one table each. For a track: the CMA fund record it corresponds to (fund number, classification, fee, date), the exposure it holds in plain Hebrew, with the share of its assets in stocks and in foreign currency (D34), one line on why a person might pick it, and the dimension checklist for it (3.3); no goal and no comparison with other companies (D34, D35). Tracks that share a name are flagged as distinct funds or as one fund listed twice, according to the CMA data, or as unresolved. The "default" or "most common" track is named for what it is. For a product name (for example a bank's deposit name): the wrapper it is, with a link to its guide page (3.1). For a name the site cannot match: "not catalogued yet", because an unknown name is a gap in coverage, with a copy button so the person can send the name to the founder (for example on WhatsApp); copying also records it in the person's local state. It sends nothing anywhere (D1).

**Data and rules.** The complete list of funds and tracks per managing company from the CMA datasets, with classification and fees (data report 1.2; the master catalogue, complete for provident, hishtalmut, pension, gemel lehashkaa and child savings). A hand-maintained `data/catalog/` for what the CMA data does not hold: bank deposit and savings-plan products, and an institution's own name for a product or track where it differs from the CMA name. Each entry records the institution, where it was seen and the date seen, and maps to a wrapper record and, where one exists, CMA fund numbers. Bank products from tariff snapshots and product pages (data report 2.1). A company's funds are the records the site holds in `data/funds/`, never the full fund list, which TASE's terms keep off the site (D33, CAT-43). The rule base for what each wrapper is.

**Works when.** All 16 tracks of the Meitav kupat gemel lehashkaa (fund 7857) are in the catalogue, each tied to its CMA record and explained in plain Hebrew, so the eight the app shows are among them. Each of them is a candidate in the track comparison (3.4), or is shown there as not evaluated with the reason. A name the app shows that is not the CMA name (for example an old track name) finds its track, or is recorded as "not catalogued yet". For every bank and manager the founder or a friend uses, every product and track has an entry or a "not catalogued yet" record. A track name typed from a statement finds its fund.

## 4. What the site will not do

- Hold accounts or any personal data on a server. No login, no aggregation by credentials, no clearing-house connection.
- Execute anything: no trades, transfers or applications.
- Rank by past return.
- Carry referral, affiliate or agent links of any kind.
- Give steps for how to do something, such as opening an account, buying a fund or setting a standing order (D40). The site names the best option for the stated goal and stops there (interview stance).
- Show any number it cannot trace to a source, a defined computation, or the person's own entry. In particular it shows no expected equity return of its own, no "app reliability" or service-quality judgement (no sourced data exists; UX 10.1 item 7), and no fund-level fee it has not collected by hand.
- Allocate money across goals automatically (D12).
- Track offers for the person: there is no offer tracker (review item 26).
- Keep several people's profiles on one device: there is no profile switching; export and import (3.9) cover the founder helping a friend (review item 27).
- Cover mortgages, consumer credit, insurance other than savings policies and executive insurance (both tier 2, D2), stock picking, trading, derivatives, physical real estate, or private-equity products.
- Serve the general public. It is for a known group; no accessibility, legal or commercial work.

## 5. Dependencies

What a capability needs before it can ship. Each still holds for anything new built on it.

1. **The rule base** (3.8), with open questions marked and the strength policy (D10) applied. Nothing that computes ships before the rules it needs.
2. **`docs/method.md` and `tests/golden/`** (3.8), so every "Works when" has a reference answer.
3. **Wrapper, security and exposure records** under `data/instruments/` with sources and dates (D6).
4. **CMA field definitions confirmed** (discovery O11) before any fund fee or yield is displayed. They are confirmed enough to display.
5. **Tariff snapshots** in `data/fees/` for the providers in 3.4, each dated with its source (discovery O12). A provider whose tariff could not be obtained is a missing record, listed as "not comparable, data missing".
6. **The fund set and the fund records** (N4). The Maya fund list (CAT-33, CAT-34) and TASE's list of foreign funds traded on TASE (ETF-32) are the set the fund choice draws from (D17), but TASE's terms forbid publishing the information or a database built from it without written consent (CAT-43), so the site applies dependency 7's fallback in its stricter form: the counts with a link (CAT-42, ETF-43) and the by-hand download path, and no fund list. `data/funds/` holds each fund's details, hand-collected, first for the exposures the founder and friends ask about; every other fund shows as not evaluated. Foreign funds traded abroad have no complete list.
6a. **`data/catalog/`** (3.10) holds the names the CMA data does not hold for the institutions the founder and friends use (an institution's own name for a track where it differs from the CMA name, starting with Meitav's), and each bank's deposit and savings-plan product names (`data/terms/deposits/`). Bank-side product names have no regulator catalogue like the CMA fund data (N6), so how complete the bank side is depends on tariff snapshots, product pages and what people paste in; bank records are collected by hand. No fund manager's funds are catalogued from the Maya list (dependency 6).
7. **Data terms** confirmed before a source's figures ship (the terms gate, `docs/architecture.md` 3.3; discovery O13, O14; CAT-34). Fallback where republication is not permitted: an open copy of the same series, or a link out with no number. As read: the Bank of Israel does not permit it without written consent, and the data.gov.il copies do not exist, so its series are links (D20), except the policy rate quoted as one figure (D22). CBS: confirmed (open licence v2.1, with its attribution line). data.gov.il (the Ministry of Finance child-savings file, the CMA data): confirmed, source named. The ECB: allowed with citation (D26). TASE: not permitted; the Data Hub is a licensed, mostly paid store (BRK-39) and TASE's site terms govern Maya too (CAT-43), so the site shows counts with links and ships no fund list (D22). The ISA's site forbids copying, so its fund-fee averages (MF-55) are a link (D22). Asking TASE, the ISA or the Bank for written consent is the founder's call.
8. **Registers fetched by hand** where they block scripts (discovery O10): the bank list (data.gov.il dataset 375); the TASE member list and the ISA registers (BRK-32). The providers compared are the providers with a snapshot in `data/fees/`.
9. **Regulator sources for the switching process** (3.7), read by hand because gov.il blocks scripts: the CMA transfer circular 2017-9-17 (SW-41), the clearing-house letters and the uniform-structure circular (SW-42), directive 448 version 4 (SW-43) and the clearing-house payment circular 2026-10-6 (SW-44). The records stay for switching's rebuild (D38).
10. **`CLAUDE.md`** states the audience of D1.

## 6. Data refresh and snapshots

[P]

Because D3 and 3.9 depend on history that no public source keeps:

| Source | Cadence | What is kept | On fetch failure |
|---|---|---|---|
| CMA fund datasets (data report 1.2) | Monthly, after the new period appears | Each monthly file, dated | Previous period shown with "older than one month" flag |
| Bank of Israel series (data report 1.3) | Not fetched while they are links (D20); once consent is granted, daily for rates and FX, monthly for per-bank averages, quarterly for fee averages | Each series value with its date | Last value shown with its date and a stale flag |
| ECB euro reference rates (D26) | Daily, after the ECB publishes | Each rate with its date | Last rate shown with its date and a stale flag |
| CBS CPI (data report 1.5) | Monthly | Each index value | Same |
| Ministry of Finance child-savings rates (data report 1.4) | Daily | Each day's file | Same |
| Bank and broker tariffs (data report 2) | Manual snapshot, at least quarterly, and whenever a change notice is seen | Every snapshot with date and source URL | Shown with its snapshot date; flagged if older than the stated cadence |
| Maya fund list (CAT-33, CAT-34) and TASE's foreign-fund list (ETF-32) | Not collected while TASE's terms forbid republishing them (dependency 6); once they consent, monthly, by hand (the pages block scripts) | Each export, dated | Previous export shown with "older than one month" flag |
| `data/funds/` | Manual, on prospectus change | Every version dated | Same |
| `data/catalog/` (3.10) | Manual, whenever an app, ad or statement shows a name the site does not know; the CMA side refreshes monthly with the fund datasets | Every entry with institution, where seen, date seen | A track in the CMA data with no catalogue entry is still in the catalogue from the data; an institution name with no CMA match shows "not catalogued yet" |

A number whose source is older than its cadence is shown with a stale flag; it is never hidden and never silently replaced.
