# Product definition

What the site is: who it is for, its decisions, its capabilities and what it will not do. Written from `docs/discovery.md`, the reports in `research/discovery/` and the founder interview, and confirmed by the founder on 2026-10-22. The decisions in section 1 may change as he learns more: a change is recorded in its row with its date and reason, and the capabilities that depend on it are re-checked.

Marking convention for each capability:

- **[R]** supported by research: the problem and the shape of the solution come from the reports or the interview.
- **[P]** a proposal of the product's author: not in the reports or interview; included because the site would be incomplete for the stated decisions without it.
- Mixed cases say which parts are which.

No figure, rate, ceiling or fee appears in this document. Where one is needed, the capability names the data file, rule or parameter it will read from.

| D6 | **Instruments are modelled as wrappers holding securities, and the money in a security is an exposure.** A *wrapper* is a container whose rules apply to whatever it holds and override the security's own rules (a study fund is exempt whatever its track; an IRA's tax follows the wrapper it was approved as, F-51). A wrapper record holds tax treatment, liquidity, fee bases, ceilings and eligibility with dates (securities account, kupat gemel lehashkaa, hishtalmut, deposit, and so on). Inside a wrapper sits a *security* (נייר ערך), what the person actually buys: units of a fund, or a security held directly. A rule that travels with a security into any account, and gives way inside a wrapper that sets its own tax, belongs to the security. An *exposure* is what the money is invested in (a tracked index, a bond type, cash); the same exposure can be compared across wrappers. If the arbitrage reform merges wrappers, a new wrapper record is added and the old ones are dated as closed.<br>By that test makam, government bonds and the two kinds of money-market fund are securities, not wrappers: an individual buys and holds them in a securities account at a bank or TASE member (GOV-21, GOV-22, MF-11), and their own rules (the tax on the discount, TXI-11; the state as debtor, GOV-02; a fund's fixed redemption dates, MF-35; a bank's custody ban on makam and money-market units, BRK-17) are rules of the security or fund. A guide page and a comparison option stand for a *product*: a wrapper, or a security held in one. ## 2. Who it is for and what it must be

**Users.** The founder (22, salaried, Israeli tax only, no self-directed investment yet) and friends and family with similar questions but different ages, employment and holdings. All in Israel. Hebrew speakers. Using a phone at least as often as a PC. Each on their own device. A friend often arrives on a page from a link the founder sends, so every page stands on its own and leads back to its list.

**The job.** A person has a goal, or money sitting idle, or an instrument already chosen, or holdings they never chose, or a product or track name in a bank or manager app they do not understand. They want to know, in Hebrew, with graphs: what their options are, how each works, what the thing an institution is advertising actually is, which is best for their case net of fees and tax over their horizon, which fund variant and which provider and track to pick for it, which fees are worth fighting, and what they might be failing to consider. Then they want to act and be able to come back later and see whether anything changed.

**Done when** (site-level success, from the founder's interview; reviewed with the founder one quarter after first use; these are not the roadmap's goals, D39):

1. The founder has moved the idle cash into a chosen wrapper and exposure at a chosen provider, using the site.
2. The founder can list what the site considered for that decision and says the list contained at least one dimension he had not thought of.
3. The founder has negotiated or deliberately ignored at least one fee based on the site's shekel figure.
4. One friend has gone from a goal to a concrete choice using the site alone, on a phone, in Hebrew.
5. Every product and track offered by the banks and managers the founder uses is in the site's catalogue, explained in plain Hebrew, and is considered by every comparison it is eligible for, or shown there as not evaluated with the reason (D17). The test case is the Meitav app (not Meitav Trade): its "open a new saving" opens a kupat gemel lehashkaa (fund 7857) whose regulator record lists 16 tracks, one of them "כללי"; all 16 are in the catalogue, so the eight the app shows are among them, and whatever the app's second "כללי" turns out to be, it is one of the Meitav products and names the catalogue holds (CAT-28).

**Fixed constraints** (from `CLAUDE.md`): Hebrew UI, right-to-left; phone and PC; non-commercial; no accessibility, legal or commercial work; every number traces to a file under `data/` with its source and date, to a computation in `docs/method.md` over such figures, or to the person's own entry; no financial or tax rule from memory, only from the `il-finance` rule base; lists are starting points.

**Principles the research adds:**

- **Neutral by construction.** No referrals, no agents, no affiliate links. The site can show "the best option that pays nobody" (UX report 4.3, 6).
- **Fees before past returns.** Ranking by past return predicts weakly; fees predict better (UX report 6, Morningstar evidence). Returns are shown as history, not as a ranking key.
- **Collapse equivalent options.** Much of the choice set is noise (UX report 4.4). The site says "these are the same for your purpose" rather than ranking indistinguishable funds.
- **Show the dimension, not just the answer.** The founder's blocker is unknown unknowns. Every comparison shows the list of what it considered.
- **Process is part of the price.** Getting the real fee and switching provider are where people get hurt (UX report 4.5).
- **Real terms by default.** Every projected value is shown in real terms, with nominal available on request; the deflator is defined in `docs/method.md`.

**Principles the founder adds:**

- **Interactive by default.** Every graph and computed number responds to the person's inputs. The inputs a computation uses (the assumptions in `docs/method.md` §2.2) start from a sourced value or a site parameter, can be changed on the page where the result is shown, and once changed are shown as "entered by you" (D7). Rules from `il-finance` are not inputs and cannot be changed by the person; site parameters that are judgements (the tie threshold, verdict thresholds, sensitivity bounds) are shown, not edited, so a ranking cannot be tuned into a different answer.
- **No blind spots** (D17). A recommendation is only as good as the options it knows. Every comparison chooses from the complete set of options at its level, accounts for each one with its outcome and reason, and says how many it considered. An option the site knows exists but cannot evaluate yet is shown with the reason, never silently left out. In the same way a gap is never read as an answer: a reader cannot mistake old data for current data, a missing or old input for a considered one, a missing reference for a fair price, a missing rule for permission, an unmatched track for one that fits, a missing tariff for an absent provider, an uncollected fund for one that does not exist, an institution's statement for a rule, an entered return for the site's view, an unfiltered row for a filtered one, a bank's published rate for an offer, a change to a rule a saved comparison never read for a change to its decision, or an instrument whose questions have closed for a covered one.

**The first page.** The site's name and one line, a search field for any product, track or term name, doors to the guide and the main pages, a link to the tier-2 "not covered yet" page and a line leading to the rules page. Nothing on it needs data of its own. The question card of the goal comparison waits with that comparison (D58).

### 3.1 Instrument guide

[R]

**In.** Nothing required. Optionally the person's age, employment type and horizon, which highlight the parts that apply to them and also feed the timeline; and the amount now, monthly deposit, return and inflation behind the timeline (see Out).

**Out.** One page per tier-1 product (a wrapper, or a security held in one, D6) and per exposure type that has one (D18). ### 3.2 Goal to wrapper and exposure comparison

[R]

The goal comparison ("השוואה למטרה", `/goal` and `/goal/compare`) and the idle-cash page are held for review (D32, D58).

**Problem.** Given a goal and a horizon, which wrapper and exposure fit, net of fees and tax over the whole horizon, including rules such as exemptions and tax-free track switches. The UX report found no Israeli tool doing this, as an inference from absence (UX 1, point 6; 10.1 item 2 leaves open whether the ISA runs a live fund-fee tool). Founder's rank 1.

**Two modes** (D9):

- **Wrapper mode:** the same exposure inside each eligible wrapper, at an equal gross return. Fully supported by sourced fee and tax data. This is the founder's own ETF-vs-provident question.
- **Exposure mode:** which exposure type suits the horizon and the stated loss filter. Cash-like exposures use sourced series. Equity and bond returns are entered by the person and labelled so.

**Presets:** an **idle cash** entry point (the founder's current state and the Bank of Israel's description of households, UX 4.6): two inputs, the current-account balance and the part that must stay available at once, run through the same engine with the "liquid reserve" preset filling the other attributes. Meant to be the first thing a friend sees.

**In.** A goal as attributes (D13): horizon; amount now and monthly contribution; how much must stay available, and from when; optionally a preset (liquid reserve, purchase at a known date, long-term wealth, a lump sum, a child) that fills these as a starting point and can be edited freely (D21); the split of the available balance across the person's goals, in their order (D12); age; employment type; gross monthly salary and current employer and employee contribution rates, with "where to find this on the payslip", because wrapper ceilings and deposit-side benefits depend on them (tax report 3.1, 3.2; domain report 4.3); whether the person already has a pension and hishtalmut through work; the loss filter per exposure type (D11); constraints such as "must be withdrawable at any time" or "must not need an annual tax return". For the idle-cash preset: also the deposit offer the person sees in their own bank app, entered by them, since no public feed of current offers exists (data report 6.3 item 5; banks must display offers since April 2025, domain 4.1).

**Out.** A ranked list of eligible wrappers (or exposures) with, for each: projected real value at the horizon net of fees and tax; the assumptions, each marked sourced or entered; what is locked and when; which rule, fee or parameter decides the ranking; whether the difference to the next option is a tie (D5). The securities account is priced on the collected tariff that leaves the goal the most (D21, F-80), and its card links to the indices page with the plan carried over (D28). A chart of projected value over time for the top options on one axis. A line in plain Hebrew: "X is best for this goal because Y." The dimension checklist (3.3) with every item marked considered, not applicable, or blocked by an open rule (D10). A **sensitivity panel**: which assumption changes would flip the top result and by how much, within bounds set as site parameters; this panel is a shared engine feature also used by 3.4, 3.5 and 3.6. For the idle-cash preset: the computed monthly cost of leaving the money where it is, against the liquid options, on the rates the person enters, labelled "entered by you"; the Bank of Israel's per-bank averages are a link, not a number (D20). A **coverage line** (D17, method F-83): how many options were considered, and every tier-1 wrapper that is not ranked, with why (listed but not ranked, excluded by the person's constraint, not eligible, or not evaluated); every tier-2 instrument named as not evaluated, with its open question; the tracks and fund variants inside each wrapper are compared in 3.4, and the line links there.

**Data, rules and parameters.** Wrapper, security and exposure records; fee data per wrapper type: CMA average fees for provident, hishtalmut and pension tracks (data report 1.2), the ISA's annual average fee by mutual-fund category including money-market funds (domain report 4.2, from the ISA report; one category average per year, not per fund; shown as a link, D22), bank and broker tariffs from 3.4's snapshots; the tax rule base for each wrapper's treatment, ceilings and exemptions (tax report sections 1 to 3, 7); for cash-like projections, the Bank of Israel policy rate as a quoted figure and CBS CPI as a series, with per-bank deposit averages and makam yields as links and the rates the person enters (D20; data report 1.3, 1.5); the tie threshold and sensitivity bounds as site parameters.

**Works when.** `tests/golden/` holds hand-worked cases, checked by the finance-reviewer against `docs/method.md`, including: the founder's apartment goal (every tier-1 wrapper and tier-2 instrument is accounted for in the coverage line, every one that fits is ranked, and the ranking matches the case when horizon or employment type changes); the founder's ETF-vs-provident example in wrapper mode, with the exit-tax and track-switch dimensions marked as considered; a friend's idle-cash case completed on a phone in under a minute. Two people with the same inputs get the same answer and can see why. A reviewer with the tax report in hand finds no rule applied that is not in the rule base. The founder reports that the output includes at least one dimension he had not thought of.

### 3.3 Dimension checklist, or "what you are not considering"

[R problem, P form]

**Problem.** The founder's stated reason for not investing: a decision looks complete until a dimension appears that was not on the list. The most-viewed beginner guide on the forum is exactly a sequence of dimensions (UX 3.1, Q8). The research supports the problem; the form here is a proposal.

**In.** A wrapper, an exposure, a fund variant, or a comparison in progress.

**Out.** The full list of dimensions that matter for it, each with a one-line explanation, its source, and a mark showing whether the current comparison has taken it into account, found it not applicable, or is blocked by an open rule. Dimensions include at least: tax at exit, real vs nominal taxation, exemptions by age or use, tax events on switching, loss offsetting, foreign withholding and domicile, distribution policy, every fee type, fee changes over time, liquidity and lock-ups, deposit ceilings, protection and what is not insured, currency exposure, minimums, who the regulator is, and reporting obligations. The list is shown inside 3.2, 3.4 and 3.6, not on the guide pages (D49), and not as a separate article.

**Source of truth.** The list is hand-maintained in `data/dimensions/` with a source per dimension, drawn from the domain report (4.x tables), the tax report (sections 1 to 8, 10) and the UX report (Q8). The rule base tags each rule with the dimensions it affects, so a new rule can mark a dimension as covered or open without the list being rewritten.

**Works when.** The founder's ETF-vs-provident case shows the exit-tax and track-switch dimensions marked as considered. A rule entering the rule base as open changes a dimension's mark to blocked without any page edit.

### 3.4 Provider, track and fund-variant workbench

[R]

**Problem.** Once the wrapper is chosen, three choices remain that the interview and the forum name together: which provider gives the best deal on the whole cost structure, not one headline fee (UX 4.2); which track inside the provider (passive vs active, index concentration, UX Q4); and, for a brokerage wrapper, which fund variant to buy for a given exposure: Israeli tracking fund, Israeli ETF, foreign-registered fund on TASE, or foreign-domiciled fund, which differ in fees, domicile, distribution policy and the security number the bank asks for (interview; UX Q1, Q13). Founder's rank 2. The data is the weakest in the landscape (data report 2, 6.3), so this capability degrades honestly.

**Where it lives.** For the securities account, the providers and the fund variants are one page, the indices page ("השקעה במדד", D28, D52, D53), which prices every house with every fund of the chosen index. For the provident wrappers, the tracks are the track browser (`/tracks/<product>`, D54) and the company and track pages (D33 to D36), and the managers' list fees are a listing per wrapper, not ranked (`/workbench/providers/<wrapper>`).

**In.** The wrapper and exposure (on the indices page, the index). The plan: amount today, monthly deposit, years and return, with inflation, employment and age behind a button (D37); a monthly saver buys with one order a month. Optionally a broker or fund the person was offered, typed by hand (D44).

**Out.**

- **Providers:** every provider that offers the wrapper, with total expected cost per year and over the holding period, broken into fee types; list price, regulator average where one exists for that provider class and fee type, and "entered by you" (D4); the date and source of each tariff; a ties marker (D5); minimums; regulator and protection status (for example, a foreign broker not supervised by the ISA, domain report 4.4). A house is priced on its binding documents (D56) for the ordinary customer, ranks on a kind of holding only where its own document names it (D42), and states its group prices without ranking them (D43). A clear "not comparable, data missing" row for providers whose tariff could not be obtained, with the reason. A provident wrapper's listing shows every manager's list fees from the snapshots, not ranked.
- **Tracks:** for provident, hishtalmut, pension and gemel lehashkaa wrappers, **every** track each provider offers for that wrapper, as listed in the CMA data and as the provider names it (3.10), with the CMA fees, classification, stock and currency exposure, past yields and assets per track (D54); the yields are a past record, never a sort key.
- **Fund variants:** for brokerage wrappers, every fund available for the chosen exposure (D17): every Israeli fund in the Maya fund list (CAT-33, CAT-34) that matches the exposure by classification and reference asset, every foreign fund traded on TASE in TASE's list (ETF-32) that matches it, and the foreign-domiciled funds traded abroad held in `data/funds/`, compared on fee, tax treatment by domicile and distribution policy from the rule base, and the dimension checklist. A fund whose details are not collected yet in `data/funds/` (fund name, security number, manager, domicile, distribution policy, prospectus fee, date) is shown as "not evaluated: details not collected". Foreign-domiciled funds traded abroad have no complete list (ETF OQ4), so the table names them as a group and says how many it holds. Since TASE's terms do not allow republishing the list (dependency 7), the Israeli funds without a record are one line with their count and a link to Maya. Where a variant's tax treatment is an open question (for example, accumulating foreign funds, tax report Q1), the variant is shown with that dimension blocked.

**Data, rules and parameters.** Provider list per wrapper from the regulator datasets and reports (domain report 5.1 to 5.5). Tariffs: bank and broker price lists snapshotted per institution with date (data report 2.1, 2.2; Leumi, Mizrahi and Discount publish structured files, others PDF or blocked). Regulator averages: CMA datasets for fund tracks (data report 1.2); Bank of Israel per-bank fee averages for the banks only (data report 1.3); no average exists for non-bank brokers, and the page says so. Fee rules: which fee types apply to which wrapper, and fee caps where regulated (domain report 4.3; caps are used only once sourced). Protection and supervision status per provider (domain report 4.4, 5.5). The Maya fund-list export as the set of Israeli funds (CAT-34) and TASE's list of foreign funds traded on TASE (ETF-32), and `data/funds/` for each variant's details, hand-collected from prospectuses (data report 6.3 items 2, 3; discovery O13).

**Works when.** For "ETF in a brokerage account with a given monthly purchase", the indices page ranks the brokers and banks for which tariffs were snapshotted, states the total cost per provider, and shows which fee type drives the ranking. Every provider without a tariff is listed as missing, not omitted. A person who typed their own fee sees the comparison shift accordingly. For a hishtalmut, every provider's tracks appear with fee and classification, and none of the tracks the catalogue holds for that wrapper is missing. For a chosen index, every Israeli fund in the Maya list that tracks it appears, compared or marked "not evaluated" with the reason, and the security number is shown.

### 3.5 Fee materiality

[R]

**Problem.** "Which battles to fight": some fees are negligible and some are a large lifetime difference, and people cannot tell which (interview; UX 4.2). Founder's rank 3.

**In.** A wrapper or holding; amount and contributions; horizon; each fee type with its current rate, either entered from a statement or taken from the workbench (3.4). The fee page (`/fees`) runs on its own plan numbers, not a saved goal (D37).

**Out.** For each fee type, its real shekel cost over the horizon, computed and labelled as computed (D7), ranked largest to smallest; a verdict per fee: worth negotiating, worth avoiding by choosing a different provider or wrapper, or not worth the effort, with the verdict thresholds shown as site parameters; the "avoid" verdict and the "first among every option" line come from a comparison of every option (F-83, D37); the regulator average for that fee type as a reference where one exists (D4), "no reference available" otherwise; what a stated reduction would be worth. A chart of fee drag over time per fee type. Plain-Hebrew text for the negotiation: which fee, what reference to cite, where the reference comes from. The sensitivity panel from 3.2.

**Data, rules and parameters.** Fee types per wrapper and their bases (on deposits, on balance, per trade, per conversion) from the domain report; regulator averages where they exist (data report 1.2, 1.3); caps where sourced; verdict thresholds as site parameters; the compounding and deflation rules in `docs/method.md`.

**Works when.** The founder enters his hishtalmut and a hypothetical brokerage plan and gets a ranked list that says which fee to negotiate first and which to ignore, matching the test case. The thresholds are visible on the page and live in `data/parameters/`, not in code.

### 3.6 Holdings review

[R]

The holdings page is held for review (D32).

**Problem.** Many people hold funds and tracks they never chose: parental kupot gemel, a child-savings plan, a default pension fund. Fees may be at list price and tracks may not fit the horizon (UX 3.1 Q10, 4.6). Founder's rank 4, "important for older people".

**In.** Each holding: wrapper type, managing company, track name, balance, fee on deposits and on balance, as read from the annual statement or the clearing-house report. The site shows where on the statement to find each, using `data/terms/`. The years, asked once for the page, not read from a saved goal (D37).

**Out.** Per holding: the track's CMA classification against the person's stated horizon and loss filter (not the asset mix, since CMA holdings data stopped in 2023, data report 6.3 item 9); whether several holdings of the same wrapper type at different managers could be consolidated and what the transfer rules allow; whether the child-savings plan is in a chosen or default track. Everything fee-related is handed to 3.5, which returns the ranked actions by shekel impact.

**Data and rules.** CMA fund-level data: fees, yields, assets and track classification per fund, monthly (data report 1.2), displayed once its field definitions are confirmed (dependency 4). Wrapper liquidity and transfer rules (domain report 4.3). Child-savings rules from the National Insurance pages (domain report 4.3).

**Works when.** The founder enters his parental kupot gemel and child-savings plan and gets, for each, a classification-fit statement and a consolidation answer, plus 3.5's fee figures, matching the test case. Nothing is shown for a fund that is not in the CMA data; the page says so.

### 3.8 Rule base, parameters and method

[R as constraint, P as a user-facing capability]

**Problem.** Ten rules are likely to change or be misremembered in the next two years (tax report 10). The founder's own tax claim did not match the report. The site must never state a rule it cannot cite, and must not pass off product judgements as rules.

**Three stores, kept apart:**

- **Rules** (`il-finance`): legal and product facts with text, conditions, exceptions, figures by tax year, source link, date checked, strength (primary, secondary, institution, open; D10, D23), scope (D30) and the dimensions they affect. Seeded from the tax report (sections 1 to 9) and the domain report (ceilings, liquidity, caps). Their open questions are tracked in `data/open-questions.json`.
- **Parameters** (`data/parameters/`): site judgements such as the tie threshold, fee-verdict thresholds and sensitivity bounds, each with value, rationale and founder sign-off date (N1), shown on the page as "site parameter".
- **Method** (`docs/method.md`): every calculation defined (real vs nominal, the deflator, compounding, fee bases, tax at exit, equal-gross-return comparison), so the finance-reviewer can check the engine against it. `tests/golden/` holds hand-worked expected outputs for every "Works when" above.

**Out, for the reader.** Next to every rule-dependent number, a link that opens the rule, its source, its strength and its date; the rules behind a fact are one chip with a count (D48). The rules page, with the claim field, the filters and the "הידעת?" facts (D25, D47); every rule and every sourced number shows its source block first (publisher, document, locator, quote, date, strength only when weak) and a copy-citation button. A parameters page. A documents page, the plain answer to "where does all this come from": every document the rules and figures were read from, and the data records the site read, each with what rests on it and the page that uses it; it claims no size (no counts, no boasts) and lists no TASE funds (dependency 7). Rules, parameters and documents in the search. These pages are for the founder, a careful friend and a reviewer who want the rule or judgement behind a number. A "שווה לדעת" block on each wrapper guide. No change log (D24).

**Works when.** A reviewer can take any number on any page back to a rule, a parameter, a data file or "entered by you", each with a date. No calculation uses a rule marked open. Each of the ten "likely to change" rules has a re-check date.

### 3.9 Saved state and revisit

[P]

**Problem.** Friends and family use the site on their own devices without accounts (D1), and a decision made on today's rules should be revisitable without redoing the work. Nothing in the research covers this.

**In.** The person's inputs across 3.2, 3.4, 3.5 and 3.6, and the choice they made at the end of a comparison.

**Out.** One local state per browser, saved automatically, exportable and importable as a file so the founder can help a friend without any data on a server; the export and import panel is also a page of its own (`/transfer`), the link the founder sends a friend. A saved comparison records inputs, assumptions, the rule, parameter and data versions used, the result and the choice. On a later visit, re-running it against the versions it stored (D24) reports which rules, parameters or data points changed since and whether the ranking still holds.

**The export format** (N3) is versioned from the first write, so an old file still imports: the file is `mekor-state` version 3, one state document under the key `mekor.v2.state` (the key did not change with the version); an older version migrates on load and on import, and a newer one is refused. Beside the state document the file carries four side stores kept on the device (`site/app/lib/side-stores.ts`): the entered offers, the variant assumptions, the playbook ticks and the entered rates. No page reads the offers or the variant assumptions (D52, D53), nor the ticks (D38); they stay in the file.

**Data and rules.** The versioned stores in 3.8.

**Works when.** A decision recorded before a rule change shows "re-check: rule X changed on date Y" after it, and shows as unchanged otherwise. An exported file from one phone imports on another and reproduces the same results.

### 3.10 Catalogue of every option: what each institution offers

[R: founder interview; data report 1.2]

**Problem.** A recommendation can only choose among the options it knows about. Institutions offer many more products and tracks than a generic list of wrappers and exposures names, each under its own names, and the names do not match from one institution to the next. The founder's example: the Meitav app's "open a new saving" leads to a kupat gemel lehashkaa and offers eight tracks, two of them named "כללי". The regulator's data lists 16 tracks for that fund, one of them "כללי" (CAT-25, CAT-28), so an app can show fewer options than exist. A site that knows only what one app shows cannot say which track fits a goal. Most of these names are also unfamiliar to the founder and his friends, so every option needs a plain-Hebrew explanation. The same holds for every bank's deposit and savings-plan names. The catalogue is not an app decoder: it does not mirror an app's screens (D2, D17).

**In.** The comparisons (3.2, 3.4) read the catalogue directly: the person does not have to find an option for it to be considered. To browse it: a company chosen from the companies list, or a product or track name typed or pasted as it appears in an app, ad or statement. A photo is out of scope (no server-side processing, D1).

**Out.** Per company (a group of legal companies is one, D33): every product and track it offers, grouped by wrapper, each under the name the institution uses. For a track: the CMA fund record it corresponds to (fund number, classification, fee, date), the exposure it holds in plain Hebrew, with the share of its assets in stocks and in foreign currency (D34), one line on why a person might pick it, and the dimension checklist for it (3.3); no goal and no comparison with other companies (D34, D35). Tracks that share a name are flagged as distinct funds or as one fund listed twice, according to the CMA data, or as unresolved. The "default" or "most common" track is named for what it is. For a product name (for example a bank's deposit name): the wrapper it is, with a link to its guide page (3.1). For a name the site cannot match: "not catalogued yet", because an unknown name is a gap in coverage, with a copy button so the person can send the name to the founder (for example on WhatsApp); copying also records it in the person's local state. It sends nothing anywhere (D1).

**Data and rules.** The complete list of funds and tracks per managing company from the CMA datasets, with classification and fees (data report 1.2; the master catalogue, complete for provident, hishtalmut, pension, gemel lehashkaa and child savings). A hand-maintained `data/catalog/` for what the CMA data does not hold: bank deposit and savings-plan products, and an institution's own name for a product or track where it differs from the CMA name. Each entry records the institution, where it was seen and the date seen, and maps to a wrapper record and, where one exists, CMA fund numbers. Bank products from tariff snapshots and product pages (data report 2.1). A company's funds are the records the site holds in `data/funds/`, never the full fund list, which TASE's terms keep off the site (D33, CAT-43). The rule base for what each wrapper is.

**Works when.** All 16 tracks of the Meitav kupat gemel lehashkaa (fund 7857) are in the catalogue, each tied to its CMA record and explained in plain Hebrew, so the eight the app shows are among them. Each of them is a candidate in the track comparison (3.4), or is shown there as not evaluated with the reason. A name the app shows that is not the CMA name (for example an old track name) finds its track, or is recorded as "not catalogued yet". For every bank and manager the founder or a friend uses, every product and track has an entry or a "not catalogued yet" record. A track name typed from a statement finds its fund.

## 4. What the site will not do

- Hold accounts or any personal data on a server. No login, no aggregation by credentials, no clearing-house connection.
- Execute anything: no trades, transfers or applications.
- Rank by past return.
- Carry referral, affiliate or agent links of any kind.
- Give steps for how to do something, such as opening an account, buying a fund or setting a standing order (D40). The site names the best option for the stated goal and stops there (interview stance).
- Show any number it cannot trace to a source, a defined computation, or the person's own entry. In particular it shows no expected equity return of its own, no "app reliability" or service-quality judgement (no sourced data exists; UX 10.1 item 7), and no fund-level fee it has not collected by hand.
- Allocate money across goals automatically (D12).
- Track offers for the person: there is no offer tracker (review item 26).
- Keep several people's profiles on one device: there is no profile switching; export and import (3.9) cover the founder helping a friend (review item 27).
- Cover mortgages, consumer credit, insurance other than savings policies, stock picking, trading, derivatives, physical real estate, or private-equity products.
- Serve the general public. It is for a known group; no accessibility, legal or commercial work.
