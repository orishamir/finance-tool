# Product definition

What the site is: who it is for, its decisions with their reasons, what each capability answers and what the site will not do. Written from `docs/discovery.md`, the reports in `research/discovery/` and the founder interview. It does not describe pages: the site does (`guidelines/project.md`). When a decision changes, its row is rewritten as it now stands; git history holds what it was.

Marking convention for each capability:

- **[R]** supported by research: the problem and the shape of the solution come from the reports or the interview.
- **[P]** a proposal of the product's author: not in the reports or interview; included because the site would be incomplete for the stated decisions without it.
- Mixed cases say which parts are which.

No figure, rate, ceiling or fee appears in this document. Where one is needed, the capability names the data file, rule or parameter it will read from.

## 2. Who it is for and what it must be

**Users.** The founder (22, salaried, Israeli tax only, no self-directed investment yet) and friends and family with similar questions but different ages, employment and holdings. All in Israel. Hebrew speakers. Using a phone at least as often as a PC. Each on their own device. A friend often arrives on a page from a link the founder sends, so every page stands on its own and leads back to its list.

**The job.** A person has a goal, or money sitting idle, or an instrument already chosen, or holdings they never chose, or a product or track name in a bank or manager app they do not understand. They want to know, in Hebrew, with graphs: what their options are, how each works, what the thing an institution is advertising actually is, which is best for their case net of fees and tax over their horizon, which fund variant and which provider and track to pick for it, which fees are worth fighting, and what they might be failing to consider. Then they want to act and be able to come back later and see whether anything changed.

**Done when** (site-level success, from the founder's interview; reviewed with the founder one quarter after first use):

1. The founder has moved the idle cash into a chosen wrapper and exposure at a chosen provider, using the site.
2. The founder can list what the site considered for that decision and says the list contained at least one dimension he had not thought of.
3. The founder has negotiated or deliberately ignored at least one fee based on the site's shekel figure.
4. One friend has gone from a goal to a concrete choice using the site alone, on a phone, in Hebrew.
5. Every product and track offered by the banks and managers the founder uses is in the site's catalogue, explained in plain Hebrew, and is considered by every comparison it is eligible for, or shown there as not evaluated with the reason (D17). The test case is the Meitav app (not Meitav Trade): its "open a new saving" opens a kupat gemel lehashkaa (fund 7857) whose regulator record lists 16 tracks, one of them "כללי"; all of them but its Halacha track (D113) are in the catalogue, so the eight the app shows are among them, and whatever the app's second "כללי" turns out to be, it is one of the Meitav products and names the catalogue holds (CAT-28).

**Fixed constraints** (from `guidelines/project.md` and `guidelines/research.md`): Hebrew UI, right-to-left; phone and PC; non-commercial; no accessibility, legal or commercial work; every number traces to a file under `data/` with its source and date, to a computation in `docs/method.md` over such figures, or to the person's own entry; no financial or tax rule from memory, only from the `il-finance` rule base; lists are starting points.

**Principles the research adds:**

- **Neutral by construction.** No referrals, no agents, no affiliate links. The site can show "the best option that pays nobody" (UX report 4.3, 6).
- **Fees before past returns.** Ranking by past return predicts weakly; fees predict better (UX report 6, Morningstar evidence). Returns are shown as history, not as a ranking key.
- **Collapse equivalent options.** Much of the choice set is noise (UX report 4.4). The site says "these are the same for your purpose" rather than ranking indistinguishable funds.
- **Show the dimension, not just the answer.** The founder's blocker is unknown unknowns. Every comparison shows the list of what it considered.
- **Process is part of the price.** Getting the real fee and switching provider are where people get hurt (UX report 4.5).
- **Real terms by default.** Every projected value is shown in real terms, with nominal available on request; the deflator is defined in `docs/method.md`.

**Principles the founder adds:**

- **Interactive by default.** Every graph and computed number responds to the person's inputs. The inputs a computation uses (the assumptions in `docs/method.md` §2.2) start from a sourced value or a site parameter, can be changed on the page where the result is shown, and once changed are shown as "entered by you" (D7). Rules from `il-finance` are not inputs and cannot be changed by the person; site parameters that are judgements (the tie threshold, sensitivity bounds) are shown, not edited, so a ranking cannot be tuned into a different answer.
- **No blind spots** (D17). A recommendation is only as good as the options it knows. Every comparison chooses from the complete set of options at its level, accounts for each one with its outcome and reason, and says how many it considered. An option the site knows exists but cannot evaluate yet is shown with the reason, never silently left out. In the same way a gap is never read as an answer: a reader cannot mistake old data for current data, a missing or old input for a considered one, a missing reference for a fair price, a missing rule for permission, an unmatched track for one that fits, a missing tariff for an absent provider, an uncollected fund for one that does not exist, an institution's statement for a rule, an entered return for the site's view, an unfiltered row for a filtered one, a bank's published rate for an offer, a change to a rule a saved comparison never read for a change to its decision, or an instrument whose questions have closed for a covered one.

### 3.1 Instrument guide

[R]

**Problem.** The founder and his friends do not yet know what each instrument is for, how it works, and when it beats another. The founder named this as the first thing the site must do. In a 2019 survey for the regulator, about half of respondents said they understood their pension report little or not at all (UX 4.1).

**What it answers.** One page per tier-1 product (a wrapper, or a security held in one, D6) and per exposure that has one (D18), plus funds of every kind (D55) and the limited-period bond fund, which stand for no product. A page keeps the sections that say something about its product (D49), from: what it is; who offers it and under which regulator; how money goes in and out (ceilings, lock-ups, exit points); what it costs; how it is taxed at deposit, during and at exit, including exemptions and what changes without a tax event; risks and what is not protected; typical use and horizon; the terms institutions use for it, matched to what appears on a statement. Only general rules appear (D30). A visual sets the product against its nearest alternative on the same holding (D29, D50). An example run on the illustrative scenario (P-05) can take the person's own values, shown as "entered by you" (D7); a return the person enters is never the site's view (D9); values entered are shared with the pages that take them (D37). One page lists the tier-2 instruments with what is known and which open question blocks each (D2), and what the site deliberately does not do (section 4).

**Data and rules.** Wrapper, security and exposure records in `data/instruments/`; tax treatment from the rule base; institution lists and regulators per wrapper; terms in `data/terms/`.

**Works when.** A friend who has never heard of a money-market fund can say, after reading the page, what it is, what it costs, how it is taxed and when a deposit beats it. Every claim links to a dated source. The founder's age-60 example from the interview is answered on the kupat gemel lehashkaa page in a way that matches the tax report. A term copied from a pension statement finds the right page.

### 3.5 The fees in a deal

[R]

**Problem.** A person is given a deal by a bank, an investment house or a fund as a list of fees with small print: a minimum and a maximum, a price that starts after a free period, a cap in law. **What it answers.** For one deal as the person was given it (each fee's price parts and its prices over time, with cases where a price changes by up to three things, one case per set of values, never a condition inside a condition), read with no amount, deposit, horizon or return: what is charged on each thing, with the rates that add; what changes and when; where a minimum or a maximum binds; what an action or a year of holding costs as a share of its amount, at amounts the reader points at, never one the site picks; and how each price stands against the legal cap, with its rules. **Data, rules and parameters.** Each product's fee bases and caps (`fee_bases_allowed`, `fee_caps` with `binds` and `caveat`); the price lists and tracks, as templates only; the exchange rate (F-84), only for a bound typed in another currency; `docs/method.md` §10.6. No site parameter.

**Works when.** The founder types the deal he was given for a securities account (a free period, a commission with a minimum, a conversion fee) and reads when each fee starts, below what order the minimum decides the price, and that no cap in law applies; and types a provident fund's two fees and sees each against its cap, today and after the discount ends.

Institutions offer many more products and tracks than a generic list names, each under its own names, which do not match from one institution to the next. The founder's example: the Meitav app's "open a new saving" leads to a kupat gemel lehashkaa and offers eight tracks, two of them named "כללי", while the regulator's data lists more tracks for that fund, one of them "כללי" (CAT-25, CAT-28), so an app can show fewer options than exist. Most of these names are unfamiliar, so every option needs a plain-Hebrew explanation; the same holds for every bank's deposit and savings-plan names. The catalogue is not an app decoder (D2, D17).

**What it answers.** The comparisons read the catalogue directly: the person does not have to find an option for it to be considered. Per company (a group is one, D33): every product and track it offers, by wrapper, under the institution's own name. For a track: its CMA record, the exposure it holds in plain Hebrew with its stock and currency shares (D34), and why a person might pick it; no goal and no comparison with other companies (D34, D35). Tracks that share a name are flagged as distinct funds, one fund listed twice, or unresolved, by the CMA data. The default track is named for what it is. A product name leads to its wrapper's guide. A name the site cannot match is "not catalogued yet", a gap in coverage, which the person can copy and send to the founder; nothing is sent anywhere (D1). A photo is out of scope (no server-side processing).

**Data and rules.** The CMA datasets, complete for provident, study fund, pension, gemel lehashkaa and child savings. A hand-maintained `data/catalog/` for what the CMA data does not hold (bank products, and an institution's own name where it differs from the CMA name), each entry with the institution, where it was seen and the date, mapped to a wrapper record and CMA fund numbers. A company's funds are the records in `data/funds/` (D33), never the full fund list (dependency 6).

**Works when.** Every track of the Meitav kupat gemel lehashkaa (fund 7857) the site keeps (D113) is in the catalogue, tied to its CMA record and explained, so the eight the app shows are among them, each a candidate in the track comparison or shown as not evaluated with the reason. A name the app shows that is not the CMA name finds its track, or is recorded as "not catalogued yet". A track name typed from a statement finds its fund.

## 4. What the site will not do

- Hold accounts or any personal data on a server. No login, no aggregation by credentials, no clearing-house connection.
- Execute anything: no trades, transfers or applications.
- Rank by past return.
- Carry referral, affiliate or agent links of any kind.
- Give steps for how to do something, such as opening an account, buying a fund or setting a standing order (D40). The site names the best option for the stated goal and stops there (interview stance).
- Show any number it cannot trace to a source, a defined computation, or the person's own entry. In particular it shows no expected equity return of its own, no "app reliability" or service-quality judgement (no sourced data exists; UX 10.1 item 7), and no fund-level fee it has not collected by hand.
- Allocate money across goals automatically (D12).
- Track offers for the person: there is no offer tracker.
- Keep several people's profiles on one device: there is no profile switching; export and import (3.9) cover the founder helping a friend.
- Cover mortgages, consumer credit, insurance other than savings policies and executive insurance (both tier 2, D2), stock picking, trading, derivatives, physical real estate, or private-equity products.
- Serve the general public. It is for a known group; no accessibility, legal or commercial work.

## 5. Dependencies

What a capability needs before it can ship. Each still holds for anything new built on it.

1. **The rule base** (3.8), with open questions marked and the strength policy (D10) applied. Nothing that computes ships before the rules it needs.
2. **`docs/method.md` and `tests/golden/`** (3.8), so every "Works when" has a reference answer.
3. **Wrapper, security and exposure records** under `data/instruments/` with sources and dates (D6).
4. **CMA field definitions confirmed** (discovery O11) before any fund fee or yield is displayed. They are confirmed enough to display.
5. **Tariff snapshots** in `data/fees/` for the providers in 3.4, each dated with its source (discovery O12). A provider whose tariff could not be obtained is a missing record, listed as "not comparable, data missing".
6. **The fund set and the fund records** (N4). The Maya fund list (CAT-33, CAT-34) and TASE's list of foreign funds traded on TASE (ETF-32) are the set the fund choice draws from (D17). Neither list is collected: the site shows the funds that have records and links out to Maya, and gives no count and no fund list. `data/funds/` holds each fund's details, hand-collected, first for the exposures the founder and friends ask about; every other fund shows as not evaluated. Foreign funds traded abroad have no complete list.
6a. **`data/catalog/`** (3.10) holds the names the CMA data does not hold for the institutions the founder and friends use (an institution's own name for a track where it differs from the CMA name, starting with Meitav's), and each bank's deposit and savings-plan product names (`data/terms/deposits/`). Bank-side product names have no regulator catalogue like the CMA fund data (N6), so how complete the bank side is depends on tariff snapshots, product pages and what people paste in; bank records are collected by hand. No fund manager's funds are catalogued from the Maya list (dependency 6).
7. **Attribution** for each source whose figures ship (discovery O13, O14). CBS: its open licence v2.1, with its attribution line. data.gov.il (the Ministry of Finance child-savings file, the bond-coupon series `mof-gov-bond-auction-coupons`, the CMA data): the source named. The ECB: cited (D26).
8. **Registers fetched by hand** where they block scripts (discovery O10): the bank list (data.gov.il dataset 375); the TASE member list and the ISA registers (BRK-32). The providers compared are the providers with a snapshot in `data/fees/`.
9. **Regulator sources for the switching process** (3.7), read by hand because gov.il blocks scripts: the CMA transfer circular 2017-9-17 (SW-41), the clearing-house letters and the uniform-structure circular (SW-42), directive 448 version 4 (SW-43) and the clearing-house payment circular 2026-10-6 (SW-44). The records stay for switching's rebuild (D38).
10. **`CLAUDE.md`** states the audience of D1.

## 6. Data refresh and snapshots

[P]

Because D3 and 3.9 depend on history that no public source keeps:

| Source | Cadence | What is kept | On fetch failure |
|---|---|---|---|
| CMA fund datasets (data report 1.2) | Monthly, after the new period appears | Each monthly file, dated | Previous period shown with "older than one month" flag |
| Bank of Israel series (data report 1.3) | Not fetched (D20) | — | — |
| ECB euro reference rates (D26) | Daily, after the ECB publishes | Each rate with its date | Last rate shown with its date and a stale flag |
| CBS CPI (data report 1.5) | Monthly | Each index value | Same |
| Ministry of Finance child-savings rates (data report 1.4) | Daily | Each day's file | Same |
| Bank and broker tariffs (data report 2) | Manual snapshot, at least quarterly, and whenever a change notice is seen | Every snapshot with date and source URL | Shown with its snapshot date; flagged if older than the stated cadence |
| Maya fund list (CAT-33, CAT-34) and TASE's foreign-fund list (ETF-32) | Not collected (dependency 6) | — | — |
