# Method: how every number on the site is computed

Built from `docs/product.md` and the `il-finance` rule base (`.claude/skills/il-finance/references/`). Every rule is cited by its ID; no rule is stated from memory. Where the rule base is open, the formula is marked **blocked** and the engine must not compute it (D10).

Status: **in force.** The basis decisions B1 to B3 are the founder's (section 1); the rest Claude settled at the founder's request, and the founder has not reviewed this document line by line. The golden scenarios in `tests/golden/scenarios.md` exercise every formula below; the expected results are computed by `tests/golden/expected_calc.py` and worked in `tests/golden/workings.md`.

How to read it:

- `A-nn` is an assumption: a visible input with a default and a source. Every assumption is listed in section 2. No formula uses a constant that is not an `A-nn`, a rule figure read from `data/rules/`, or a site parameter read from `data/parameters/` (`P-nn`).
- `F-nn` is a formula. The engine implements formulas by number; the finance-reviewer checks the engine against them.
- A formula written as math (GitHub's $`…`$) is what the formula's sheet on the site shows, each in the entry's order, in place of a link to this document; the Hebrew key to its symbols is `formulaKeys` in `site/app/copy.ts`, and `site/app/lib/formula-tex.test.ts` keeps the two in step (founder, 2026-11-08). The rest of the formulas keep the link until they are written so.
- `G-nn` is a gap: something the method cannot compute yet, with the open question that blocks it (section 12).
- Rule IDs (`TXS-09`, `PF-02`, ...) point into `il-finance`. The strength shown is the rule's strength as recorded there; `institution` strength is flagged, not treated as primary (coverage.md F1).

## 1. The comparison basis

Chosen by the founder from the alternatives below.

**B1. Liquidation value at the horizon, at equal net outlay and equal gross return, in real terms.** Every eligible wrapper receives the same shekels out of the person's pocket on the same dates (the amount now and the monthly contribution, section 3). The exposure earns the same gross return in every wrapper (D9 wrapper mode). The engine applies each wrapper's deposit-side tax effect, its fees, the drag inside the instrument, tax during the holding period and tax at exit, as if everything were withdrawn at the end of the horizon. The result is the real value of that withdrawal (F-40). Ranking and ties use that one number.

Alternatives considered and rejected: ranking on the annualised net real return (IRR), which handles irregular contributions but hides shekel differences and makes the tie threshold hard to state; and a two-figure basis (value if held, value if withdrawn), more honest for lock-ups but with two ranking keys. The chosen basis understates wrappers whose benefit is deferral past the horizon; the output says so wherever a wrapper's lawful exit is later than the horizon (B3).

**B2. Only the person's own money ranks.** Employer-funded deposits (hishtalmut, pension, provident fund for annuity) are computed by the same engine and shown as a separate line, "only available in this wrapper", with their value at the horizon (F-52). They do not enter the ranking, because no other wrapper can receive them.

**B3. Wrappers still locked at the horizon are listed, not ranked.** A wrapper whose lawful exit (section 8) is after the horizon appears with "locked until <date>" and its value at its own first exit date, computed by the same engine with the horizon moved to that date. Contributions stop at the goal's horizon; the balance grows until that date (product D16). It enters the ranking only if the person opts into early withdrawal, in which case the early-withdrawal tax rule applies (F-47, F-48). The same treatment applies to a wrapper whose exit tax is blocked by an open rule (G-list): it is listed with its pre-tax value and the reason, and it is not ranked.

### 2.4 From a wrapper record and P-05 to a run's inputs

`runInputs` in the engine (`packages/engine/src/inputs.ts`) builds the inputs of one run from three things: a wrapper record (`docs/data-schema.md` §3.3), the parameters in `data/parameters/`, and the instrument terms that neither of them holds. It is the step behind F-67 and behind every comparison that starts from wrapper records (F-83). Values pass through unchanged. The only conversions are percent to a fraction (`5` becomes `0.05`) and years to months. Anything missing or unexpected, or that the engine cannot compute yet, stops the build with the field named. It never falls back to a default or drops a field. A run the method cannot evaluate yet stops with its gap, as F-83 "not evaluated": monthly deposits into a renewable money-market fund stop with G-30.

**Wrapper or security record.** It is checked against its schema (`packages/schema`: `wrapperRecord`, or `securityRecord` when it carries `held_in`, D6); a field the schema does not name is an error at every level. A security runs its own ledger (makam F-24, a government bond F-25, a money-market fund F-08 with F-23) inside the account it is held in: the account's tariff lines for its class are its instrument terms, and the member's kind (`memberKind`) goes with them (F-14).

**Where the illustrative run takes its instrument terms.** In production the guide page's illustrative run (F-67) takes them from the records P-05's `instruments` map names (`docs/data-schema.md` §3.2), chosen as the first collected record of each kind. The person's entered values still replace P-05's six numbers only (F-67); the instrument terms are not editable on the page. Conversions, each recorded on the value the sheet shows, but for a deposit's exit points, which change no amount and stand on no value (they decide only which note a number carries, G-16 or G-34): percent to a fraction; a tariff line's per-period custody rate stays per period with `everyMonths` of its `period`; a per-period balance rate (IRA manager) is multiplied by the periods in a year, simple, and carries `computed_from` F-51; a per-period account fee in NIS is divided by the months in the period (F-17); the fund's median spread is halved for A-08's half-spread. Deposit `linkage` `none` is `unlinked` (`prime` when the record's anchor is prime), `cpi` is `cpi`, and `fx` has no ledger. A deposit's `exitFirstMonth` is the record's `exit_points.first_after_months` and its `exitEveryMonths` the record's `exit_points.every_months`, each when `exit_points.exists` and the figure is a positive whole number of months; otherwise, and always for a current account, the run gets none of that figure. The run knows no exit point without `exitFirstMonth`, so a record that prints only how often exit points come reaches it with none (F-41). A fund kind comes from the wrapper and the record: the renewable wrapper takes `money-market-renewable` and no drag (MF-37), the daily one `money-market-taxable` when the record's tax track is taxable, otherwise `money-market-daily`; an Israeli fund with an exempt track is `israeli-fund`; a foreign fund with a TASE trading currency is `tase-foreign-fund`, otherwise `foreign-fund`. A bond reads `linkage` and `coupon_every_months`, and a makam optionally `term_months`, as figures of the GOV rule `series_rule` names; `exitPriceChange` is 0 (A-27); the IRA `clockStartMonth` is 0. **Money-market funds are modelled as bought through a bank that has a distribution agreement for the fund, the common case:** the trade lines are 0 (MF-26: a distributor with a distribution agreement may not charge the holder for buying, holding or redeeming a money-market fund) and custody is 0 (BANK-18); the tariff's `mutual_fund_units` lines are not read, because at Leumi the matching line is a tracking-fund standing-order line, not a money-market line. Each zero is a sourced value object carrying its rule, with the assumption in its note. **When no list fee is published** for a provident-family wrapper (a study fund: no managing company publishes one), and P-05's entry names a `track`, the illustrative run uses the regulator's average actually charged on that named track, labelled as such: `avg_annual_management_fee` is the balance fee and `avg_deposit_fee` the deposit fee (§3.13 of the data schema, each with its calendar year), direct expenses are 0 (G-14). Where the regulator reports no deposit average for a study-fund track the run takes 0 from PF-06 (a study fund has no deposit-fee item), shown as that rule's figure. A current account has no term: its run is the month-to-month balance (`termMonths` 1) and the record's null `term_months` is not an error. A distributing fund, a fund quoted in a foreign currency (A-03 and the start rate are not in P-05) and a record that lacks a needed field leave the page without a timeline and the build logs the missing input; the build never fails for missing data.

The fund's kind must suit the wrapper: `money-market-daily` or `money-market-taxable` for `money-market-fund-daily`, `money-market-renewable` for `money-market-fund-renewable`, and `israeli-fund`, `tase-foreign-fund` or `foreign-fund` for `brokerage-account` and `ira-hishtalmut`. The taxable kind sits under the daily wrapper because the engine runs it with a daily fund's liquidity (F-23, S13). That is a modelling choice, not a rule. The rule base counts the two taxable funds (TXS-24) and names them (TXS-26), but not whether each is daily or renewable, so each fund's own record must say. The profile (A-12 to A-14) passes through unchanged.

## 3. Time, money and the deflator

**F-01 Time grid.** Months `m = 0, 1, …, T`, `T = 12 × horizon in years`. Dates are calendar months; the tax year is the calendar year (TXG-01). Age-based rules use the person's age on the first day of the month.

**F-02 Monthly inflation.** `π_m = (1 + π)^(1/12) − 1` (A-02).

**F-03 CPI index path.** `I_0 = 1`; `I_m = I_{m−1} × (1 + π_m)`. Where a sourced CPI history exists (`data/series/cbs-cpi`, the CBS general index), past months use actual index values rescaled so that `I_0 = 1`. Tax rules use "the index last published before the day in question" (TXG-12, TXG-14); with a monthly grid the engine uses `I_m` for an event in month `m` and records this one-to-two-month simplification as a convention of the method, not a reading of the rule.

**F-04 Exchange-rate path.** `X_0 = 1`; `X_m = X_{m−1} × (1 + f)^(1/12)` (A-03). For an instrument denominated in or linked to a foreign currency held by an individual, `X` replaces `I` as the tax index (TXS-04, TXF-03; for an Israeli foreign-investment fund, its principal currency, TXS-14). A TASE foreign fund takes the index TXF-04 states, and TXF-04 is open (its only source, a copy of the Tax Authority's 2019 letter, is not cited): neither the exchange rate nor the CPI is known for it, with or without its 90% test. So every run of a TASE foreign fund stops on TXF-04 (D10; section 13: an open rule never enters a formula) and computes no value after tax: the tax at exit would rest on a guessed index, and the value after that tax is what ranks it (on the 2026-11-14 data the CPI would put iShares MSCI ACWI, 1159235, at the top of its index). The engine's `securitiesBlockedBy` says so before a run. Product D84 and B3 list such a fund with its value before the exit tax and the account's cost from the same run (F-118), a plain note that its tax is not confirmed, and no rank, never the headline; the goal comparison gives it "not evaluated" with the reason `rule-open` TXF-04 (F-83). The gap is G-35. **A hedged fund.** A shekel fund on a foreign exposure whose record says it neutralises the exposure's currency (`hedged: true`) is not moved by A-03: its return is A-01 alone. It hedges that one currency against the shekel; an index priced in several currencies (`other_currencies` on the exposure, ETF-48) keeps the others, against the exposure's currency, inside A-01.

**F-118 The value before the exit tax of a run an open rule blocks (product D84, B3; `runSecuritiesBeforeTax`, `providerWorkbenchBeforeTax`, `beforeTaxPathOf`).** For a securities run whose exit tax an open rule blocks before the run (`securitiesBlockedBy`; on the 2026-11-14 data every TASE foreign fund, on TXF-04, F-04), the engine runs the same ledger and stops before the exit tax. Every month is F-08's, with the fund's own costs (F-10, F-107), the account's commission, spread, conversion, custody, fixed fee and current account (F-12 to F-14, F-17, F-102), a price for a time (F-101), the whole units of F-109, and F-04's exchange-rate path `X` as a market path: it moves the fund's value where A-01 is in the fund's currency and converts every price set in a foreign currency (F-84), and is not read as a tax index. At `T` the holding is sold as F-09 sells it, at the bid, the commission and the conversion paid out of it: `V_T^pre = R_T + w_T`, nominal, with `w_T` the cash still waiting for a whole unit (F-109), and `V_T^pre,real = V_T^pre / I_T` (F-06: the CPI here deflates; it is not the fund's tax index). **Nothing in it rests on the open rule.** A lot's index `J` is read only by F-09's exit tax (`K`, `infl`, `real`); the run reads no `J`, cites no tax index rule (TXF-03, TXF-04) and no exit-tax rule (TXF-02, TXS-02, TXS-06, F-42, F-43), and raises none of the exit tax's notes (G-07, G-18, G-20). **What it rests on instead.** That nothing is taxed before the sale, so the run's tax during is 0, is TXF-11's: cited where the fund's own annex reports the ruling, else labelled a reading, as the after-tax run does, and the figures carry that label. The fund's own costs inside its price stay, the US tax inside an Irish fund among them (F-107, TXF-16): they rest on the fund's records and TXF-16, not on TXF-04. **A fund that pays out keeps no figure, on one question.** Its distributions would be taxed inside the run (F-20). For a fund organised as a company the 25% is settled (TXF-05, TXF-30); for one organised as a trust or a contractual arrangement it is open (TXF open question 19). A fund record says its legal form only in words (`legal_form`), with no field the engine reads, so the form is unknown to the run: `beforeTaxPathOf` reads only a fund with no distribution (A-10 none), and a distributing TASE foreign fund is listed with the reason and no figure (`runSecuritiesBeforeTax` stops on "TXF-OQ19"). A record field for the form would let a company fund's distributions be taxed as the after-tax path taxes them. On the 2026-11-14 data no such fund distributes (the iShares lines 1159235, 1159243 and 1159250 and the Invesco lines 1183441, 1185164 and 1186063 accumulate). **Which figures are shown.** `V_T^pre,real`, labelled before tax ("נשאר לפני מס רווחי הון"), and what the account charges over the horizon, F-91's account part `B(∅) − B(F_p)` from the same runs, where `B = V_T^pre,real` since nothing is taxed inside them (TXF-11). It is the same figure F-91 gives that account once the rule is settled, whichever index it settles on, when both runs buy the same way: of a house's ways of buying (F-109) F-118 takes the one that leaves more before tax and F-63 the one that leaves more after tax, and the two can differ, since a commission stays out of the gain (G-20) while the growth more units bring is taxed. A Tel Aviv line is always bought in whole units (F-109: a house's `by_amount` names only listings abroad), so for a TASE foreign fund each house has one way and the two figures are the same. **Which are not.** Anything after tax: what is left after tax, the state's part of F-91, a gap to another way, F-89 to F-95. And no rank: the value enters no F-80 ranking, no tie, no headline, no stairs, no worth line, no order of funds, and no account is ranked on it. Of one house's ways of buying (by amount or in whole units, F-109) the one that leaves more before tax is shown, as F-63 takes the one that leaves more after tax: it picks which of the house's prices is shown, not a rank. The run returns no after-tax value (`BeforeTaxRun` has no `nominal`, `real` or `taxAtExit`), its outcome is "listed, not ranked" with the open rule as the reason, and its workbench's rows are not compared rows and carry no rank. **On the indices page.** Under the funds, each such fund the chosen account prices stands in a list of its own, in the records' order, under a heading in the blocked colours and a plain note that its tax is not known, whose words open the rule's card (TXF-04): its name, the account's cost and its value before tax. It is never chosen as the pair and never set against it, so no receipt, stairs or tile reads it; at an account that does not price it, it stands with the funds that have no price, under the rule's reason. A fund the person types of this kind is priced the same way once added, when it accumulates and its fees are typed (a fee or the payout left unsaid is named instead, D56); while it is typed, its preview says only that its tax rests on the open rule and it is not ranked. A kind of fund with no fund record (an index the person typed) is not priced before tax: that page ranks the accounts by the cheapest kind's cost, and a cost of this kind would enter that ranking, so the kind keeps its reason there. State "computed". `packages/engine/test/before-tax.test.ts` holds one value worked by hand (whole units at a share price, a currency path in the return, F-107's drag), and the value, the fees and the months to the same run with TXF-04 settled either way (the golden harness's copy of the letter, exchange rate or CPI), so the value depends on neither; `site/app/lib/invest.test.ts` and `site/app/components/invest/render-sections.test.ts` hold the page.

**F-84 Exchange rate (cross rate) (D26).** NIS per one unit of currency `c`: $`S_c = \frac{E_{\text{ILS}}}{E_c}`$, where `E_x` is the ECB's euro reference rate for `x` (units of `x` per one euro) on the same reference day; `S_EUR = E_ILS`, `S_ILS = 1`. Source: `data/series/ecb-fx.json` (ECB eurofxref-daily, terms read 2026-10-25: free use with the ECB cited; the site states that it computes a cross rate from them), state "computed" with sourced inputs. It is a reference rate for the ECB's reference day, not a rate anyone trades at, and it is stale after the series' `stale_after_days` (D3). It is the start rate `S_0` of every foreign-currency run (F-63, F-65 and the indices page, D28); the person never types one. The rate's future path stays F-04 with A-03 (default 0). A currency the ECB does not quote cannot be priced, and the run says so. Example: 2026-10-23, `S_USD = 3.4939 / 1.1193 = 3.1215`, `S_JPY = 3.4939 / 175.51 = 0.01991` (shown as 1.99 per 100 yen).

**F-05 Contributions.** `C_0` = amount now; for `m ≥ 1`, $`C_m = C \times I_m`$ if A-04 is "constant real", else $`C_m = C`$. A wrapper's deposit ceiling (section 8) caps `C_m`; the excess is reported and routed to the next goal in the person's order (D12), never silently dropped.

**F-06 Real value.** Any nominal amount `V` at month `m` is shown as $`\frac{V}{I_m}`$. Projected values are real by default and nominal on request. The result page may also show an amount that does not grow as $`\frac{V}{I_T}`$ with `V` the amount now: what it is worth in shekels of today at the horizon; it is the same conversion, applied to an unchanged amount. **What was paid in, in today's money** is the sum of each deposit at its own month, $`P = \frac{C_0}{I_0} + \sum_{m=1}^{T} \frac{C_m}{I_m}`$, never the nominal sum divided by `I_T`, which shrinks the amount now and every deposit by the whole horizon's inflation: on the current account (P-05, 1% on up to 10,000 ₪) that would show 62,681 ₪ paid in against 63,375 ₪ left, a gain, where 70,000 ₪ in today's money was paid in and 6,625 ₪ of it lost. Site helper `paidInToday`. A running total, the fees or the tax paid along the way, is converted the same way: each month's part at its own month, and what the exit adds at `T` (site helper `totalToday`).

**F-106 What was paid in, in today's money, with deposits constant in today's money.** When A-04 is "constant real", deposits are monthly and the run takes every deposit as entered, F-06's `P` is the amount now and the monthly deposit, twelve a year over `n` years: $`P = C_0 + 12 \times n \times C`$, since each deposit `C × I_m` (F-05) at its own month's index (F-06) is `C`, whatever path the index takes. `C` is the monthly deposit as entered, the person's net outlay (F-08 step 1); `n` is the years of deposits, `T / 12`, or the years up to the year shown. It rests on the amount now, the monthly deposit and the years, not on the return or the inflation. Where a ceiling or a rule takes part of a deposit, the figure is the run's own `P` and stays F-06. Shown as the indices page's "הפקדתם", the guide timeline's paid-in figure in today's money in each year it holds, and the study fund drawing's own deposits and an employee's employer deposits (F-96, `C` = the rate × the salary). State "computed".

**F-07 Annual to monthly rates.** `g = (1 + r)^(1/12) − 1` for returns and yields quoted as effective annual rates (bank deposits are quoted that way, BANK-04). Fees quoted as "% a year of balance" are charged as `φ / 12` of the end-of-month balance (PF-09 says balance fees are collected monthly and prorated by days; the monthly twelfth is the method's convention and the golden tests use it).

## 4. The ledger (one run per wrapper, provider and instrument)

The ledger is the single engine behind 3.1, 3.2, 3.4, 3.5 and 3.6. State at the end of month `m`:

- `B_m` market value of the holding (nominal shekels);
- `P_m` nominal tax basis, the sum of the amounts invested in units still held;
- `K_m` indexed-basis accumulator, `Σ basis_i / J_i` over lots `i` still held, where `J` is the tax index of the instrument (`I` or `X`, F-03, F-04). The adjusted basis at any later month `t` is `K_m × J_t` (TXG-12: adjusted cost = cost × index at sale ÷ index at purchase). Lots leave in first-in-first-out order (TXS-06);
- `Φ_m` cumulative fees paid, by fee type; `Θ_m` cumulative tax paid during the holding period;
- `E_m` cumulative employer money (B2) tracked as its own sub-ledger.

**F-08 Month step.** For `m = 1 … T`:

1. Deposit-side tax effect: gross deposit `D_m` from the person's net outlay `C_m` (F-30 to F-32; `D_m = C_m` where no benefit applies).
2. Entry costs: the cash left after the deposit fee and the FX fee, `A_m = D_m × (1 − φ_dep) − fee_fx(D_m)`, pays for the trade and its commission: `A_m = V_m + fee_trade(V_m)`, where the commission is a percentage of the trade value `V_m` (F-12, BRK-16). An exchange purchase is at the ask, so the NAV value bought is `N_m = V_m / (1 + A-08)` (F-13). Lot `m` opens with `basis = V_m`, the price paid including the half-spread (TXG-12), and `J = J_m`. Commissions and FX fees are not added to the basis (G-20). Where there is no trade (provident family), `N_m = V_m = A_m`.
3. Growth: `B'_m = (B_{m−1} + N_m) × (1 + g_m − δ_m)`, where `g_m` is from A-01 and `δ_m = (d + A-07 + A-09 + A-11) / 12` (F-10).
4. Distributions and tax during: F-20 to F-26 for the instrument type; net amounts reinvested open a new lot.
5. Holding costs: `B_m = B'_m − φ_bal/12 × B'_m − fee_fixed_m − fee_custody_m` (F-14 to F-16). On a securities holding, a custody or account fee is paid by removing units at NAV, oldest lot first. The units removed take their share of `P` and `K` with them, so earlier years' fees do not reduce the taxable gain (TXS-06). The removal is not treated as a taxable sale; this is a simplification of the method, kept in the fund and bond ledgers. The makam ledger counts it (F-24): a slice sold for custody is a sale before redemption, its gain a capital gain of its tax year.
6. Fee schedule changes: F-16 updates `φ_bal`, `φ_dep` when a discount period ends.

**F-09 Exit at T (liquidation, B1).** The holding is sold at the bid: `G_T = B_T × (1 − A-08)`. The commission and FX fee are paid out of it: `R_T = G_T − fee_exit(G_T) − fee_fx(G_T − fee_exit(G_T))`. For tax the consideration is `G_T`; sale costs are not deducted (G-20). `gain = G_T − P_T − E_y`, where `E_y` is the management and custody fees paid in the exit tax year, deducted once (TXS-06). For makam the exit year's capital gains also hold the gain on the slices sold that year to pay custody (F-24): `gain = G_T − P_T + S_y − E_y`. Then `infl = K_T × J_T − P_T`, `real = gain − infl`, tax at exit `τ_exit` from section 7, and `V_T = R_T − τ_exit`, shown as `V_T / I_T` (F-06). For securities, `real` is the sum over lots of `units_i × price_T × (1 − A-08) − basis_i × J_T / J_i`, less `E_y`. **A falling index.** That holds for a lot whose index rose (`J_T ≥ J_i`). For a lot whose index fell (CPI deflation, or the currency of a foreign-currency security weakening against the shekel), the inflationary amount is zero, not negative, so the lot's real gain is its nominal gain, `units_i × price_T × (1 − A-08) − basis_i`; and when that is a loss, the part of it equal to the fall, `basis_i × (1 − J_T / J_i)`, is not allowable, so the lot contributes `min(0, units_i × price_T × (1 − A-08) − basis_i × J_T / J_i)` (TXS-02 and TXF-03: circular 10/2025, appendix, examples 3 and 4, and the Supreme Court in ע"א 3555/15 מוזס, para 26: a negative inflationary amount, "בין אם מקורו בשינוי ערך מדד או בשינוי שער חליפין", is not part of the tax base). So the taxable gain never exceeds the nominal gain. The same per-lot rule applies to a CPI-linked bond's sale (F-25, F-40). The engine: `indexedCost` in `ledgers/lots.ts`, used by the securities and bond ledgers; `detail.fallDisallowed` is the part of a loss the fall made not allowable. Rising-index runs are unchanged.

**F-10 Gross growth less drag.** `δ` collects what the instrument itself costs regardless of wrapper: the fund's fixed management fee, trustee fee and variable fee (MF-16, MF-20, ETF-13, ETF-14), tracking difference beyond fees (A-07), and fund-level foreign withholding (A-11). A foreign fund has no variable or trustee fee line, because Israeli fund law does not apply to it (ETF-71); its published yearly charge is the fixed management fee. For a renewable money-market fund the expected yield is already net of fund fees (MF-37), so `δ = 0` and `g` is the expected yield. For a daily money-market fund `g` is the BoI policy rate (A-20, the ISA's description of what these funds track, MF-34, labelled "proxy") and `δ` the fund's fees.

**F-107 The US tax inside an Irish fund that holds US shares (A-11).** For a fund domiciled in Ireland that holds its index's shares (`replication.method: physical`), on an index whose exposure record carries `us_dividends` from the index provider's own factsheet, and whose record has neither a figure of its own (`fees.fund_level_withholding_drag`, which replaces it) nor a published 12-month gap (A-07 from ETF-08: what that gap was measured against is not recorded, so it may hold the tax already; no Irish record has one): $`w = \rho \times y_{US} \times s_{US}`$, a fraction of the fund a year, where `ρ` is TXF-16's US treaty rate on dividends paid to an Irish resident, `y_US` the dividend yield of the index's US shares (the S&P 500: S&P's indicated dividend yield; MSCI World and MSCI ACWI: MSCI USA's) and `s_US` their share of the index (MSCI's country weight; 1 for the S&P 500, an index of US companies). Today's yield stands for every year of the run, as A-10's does. It enters F-10's `δ` as A-11. A fund the person types takes it when they say it is Irish and holds the shares: it follows from what they said (D44). A swap fund holds no US shares and takes none. Against A-07: an A-07 from the fund's yearly returns stands against a net index (F-92 (d)), whose returns have tax taken off the dividends at the index's own rate (MSCI: 30% on US dividends, ETF-49; S&P's rate is not in the rule base, ETF open question 24). A fund that suffers TXF-16's lower rate gets the difference between the two rates as a lead over the net index, which F-92 (b) nets against its shortfall. Where A-07 is at its floor of zero, F-107 adds the fund's own tax and nothing is counted twice; every Irish fund on the records that holds its index's shares and has an A-07 from its years is there, which a test holds (on the S&P 500 every such fund leads the net index in every year reported, the iShares fund by 0.24 points a year on average over 2016 to 2025). Above the floor the run would miss the difference between the index's rate and the fund's (G-33). Not counted: tax other countries withhold from an Irish fund's dividends, US tax inside an Israeli fund that holds US shares (the rule base gives neither rate), and whether every dividend in the index's yield falls under TXF-16's rate, which is for dividends from US corporations. Whether a given Irish fund obtains TXF-16's rate, which needs it to pass the treaty's limitation-on-benefits article, is TXF open question 7: the run and the figure carry the flag `TXF-OQ7` (G-33). No figure where the index provider prints no dividend yield (the NASDAQ-100). Each fund's figure sheet on the indices page lists it with its inputs. State "computed".

### 4.1 Fee types and their bases

Every fee is a record in `data/` (see `docs/data-schema.md`) with a base, a rate, a minimum, a maximum, a period and the security types it applies to. The engine evaluates fees only by these formulas.

**F-11 Deposit fee** (provident family): `φ_dep × D_m`, capped by the regulated maximum for the wrapper: 4% for provident funds and kupat gemel lehashkaa (PF-02, PF-03, KGL-14), 6% for a new comprehensive pension fund (PF-04, PEN-21), 4% for a new general pension fund (PF-05), none for hishtalmut and IRA (PF-06, KH-22).

**F-12 Trade commission** (brokerage): per order, `max(min, min(pct × amount, max))`, where `amount` is the trade value (on a buy, cash = trade value + commission), from the tariff line for the security type (TASE securities, makam, foreign securities; BRK-16), one order per contribution and one at exit (A-28). Money-market fund units at a bank under a distribution agreement: 0 (MF-26). Makam held to redemption: one purchase order, no sale order. **Per share, dollar bounds and a cap (indices page).** A foreign broker prints its commission per share (`usd_per_share`: Interactive Israel 0.01 a share, Interactive Brokers 0.005 a share) with a minimum and a cap: `fee = min(cap × amount, max(min, min(pct × amount + c × n, max)))`, where `n = amount / S_c / P` is the share count of the order, `P` the unit price in the fund's currency (the fund record's `share_price`, moved with the NAV and the exchange rate), `S_c` the shekels per unit of that currency at the trade, and `c` the per-share fee in shekels (the printed dollars times the rate of the dollar). The cap is a share of the trade value (`cap`, unit `pct_of_trade`) and wins over the minimum. Interactive Brokers prints this (its note: the cap applies when it is below the minimum, BRK-42), and so do the six banks that print a cap, 25% or 30% of the trade (BRK-67: Mizrahi-Tefahot's and Leumi's words cap the minimum, Hapoalim's, Yahav's, First International's and Massad's the whole fee; F-112 says how both are read). Excellence's 2% (BRK-48) and Interactive Israel's 2% (BRK-40) are printed with nothing on which wins: that their cap wins over the minimum too is the site's reading, not their price lists' words. A minimum or maximum printed in dollars (`usd_per_trade`; Altshuler Trade's `usd`), euros (`eur`: Psagot's European line, 15 euros, its tariff part 4(א) section 5, which says the minimum is converted to the currency of the trade) or pounds (`gbp_per_trade`) is converted to shekels at the run's rate for that currency (the run input `fx`, F-84); the converted bound is fixed for the run. Example: Interactive Israel, an order of 400 NIS with the dollar at 3.70: 0.108 USD of units at 500 USD a unit is 0.216 of a unit, the per-share fee 0.008 NIS, the minimum 2.5 USD = 9.25 NIS, the cap 2% × 400 = 8 NIS, so the order pays 8 NIS (the cap winning over the minimum, the site's reading of Interactive Israel's tariff, above). Interactive's tariff rounds fractional US shares up to a whole share for the fee; the engine does not (a difference only below the minimum). `test/foreign-broker.test.ts`. **Standing orders (D41).** A monthly purchase is priced on the tariff's standing-order line for that security type and channel (band `הוראת קבע`) where the tariff prints one and the house's documents show the ordinary customer can use it; a lump sum, a reinvested distribution and a sale pay the regular line, and so does every order where no such line applies. A line the documents do not show the customer can use carries `not_ranked` (D56): the ranked price never reads it, every order pays the regular line, and the run names the line as set aside (`standingAside`) so the indices page shows it beside the row with its reason. Leumi's, Massad's and First International's standing-order lines (for a tracking fund and an Israeli ETF, MF-58, and at Massad and First International for a fund bought abroad) are all set aside, so no standing-order line prices a ranked figure today. A foreign fund listed on TASE is named by no such line. In the materiality run the standing-order rate moves in proportion to the regular one. A line may print a flat price for an order up to a sum (`flat_up_to`, PEPPER, BRK-54; six banks price a small conversion the same way, BRK-68, F-13): such an order pays only that, and a larger order pays the line as usual; on a buy the sum is measured on the trade value after the flat price. **Whole units (F-109).** Where the fund is bought in whole units, the commission is the one on the trade the order actually makes (its whole units at the ask), and a month whose cash buys no unit places no order and pays none.

**F-14 Custody fee** (brokerage): per period from the tariff, `max(min, min(pct × B, max))` on the holdings it applies to; a minimum or maximum printed per month is multiplied by the months in the charging period; never on makam or money-market funds at a bank (BRK-17, BANK-18); at non-bank members per their tariff: no rule found limits a non-bank member's custody fee on makam or money-market units, since the bank ban binds banks only and the distribution-fee regulations leave out an investment marketer, which most non-bank members are (BRK-17, BRK-52, MF-26), so the engine uses the tariff as published. A member that is not an investment marketer and holds a distribution agreement for the fund falls under reg. 2(ד) of those regulations, which bars any fee on its money-market units (MF-26); which members hold one is not known (BRK-52). **Makam (D6).** Makam is a security held in the securities account, so the account's member decides its custody: the makam run takes the tariff's custody line for the `makam` class and `memberKind`, charges nothing at a bank (BRK-17, BANK-18) and the line as published at a non-bank member (BRK-52), paid, in the method's model (F-24), by selling an equal slice of each series held at the month's price, each slice a sale before redemption whose own gain is a capital gain of its tax year (TXI-12, F-24), with each year's custody taken off that year's capital gains once (TXS-06). **Bank or not.** The securities run takes `memberKind` (`bank`, the default, or `non-bank`): a money-market unit pays no custody at a bank whatever the tariff says, and pays a non-bank member's custody line as published. The provider workbench (F-63) sets it from the snapshot's provider class, and so do the fund-variant runs (F-65) and the switching-cost runs (F-66) that price a provider's tariff: only `bank` is a bank, a `broker` or `foreign_broker` is `non-bank` (`memberKindOf`). The page shows that line with a note: no rule limiting it was found; a member with a distribution agreement for that fund may charge nothing on it (reg. 2(ד), MF-26); and the regulators' joint report of June 2026 (p.11) reports a blanket waiver at non-bank members of custody fees proper ("דמי משמרת") only, while a handling fee on the securities deposit ("דמי טיפול בפיקדון") is usually a fixed sum, often refunded or not charged. **F-101 A price for a time (an account on your own terms, started from a house; `runSecurities` with `tariff.until`, `EnteredTariff.until`).** A group's or an offer's price usually ends (a student price while a student, an opening offer of no custody for a year), and after it the account pays the house's list price; two prices of one group may end at different times. An entered tariff therefore carries a **sequence** of tariffs: `F¹` applies to the run's months `1 … u₁`, `F²` to the months `u₁ + 1 … u₂`, and so on, the last to every month after the last end. The ends are whole months counted from the start of the run, in order (`u₁ ≤ u₂ ≤ …`, each `≥ 0`); an end that repeats or goes backwards leaves its tariff empty. **A month's price.** A purchase made in month `m` (the amount now and the first deposit in month 1, each month's deposit, a reinvested distribution) pays the commission and the standing-order price of `F^{k(m)}`, `k(m)` the first `k` with `m ≤ u_k` (the last tariff if none); the custody fee charged in month `m` and the fixed fee of month `m` are those of `F^{k(m)}`, charged on F-14's phase as always (a quarterly line charges at months 3, 6, 9, … of the run's grid, the period's own `everyMonths` deciding the months); the sale at the exit month `T` pays the commission and the conversion fee of `F^{k(T)}`. A minimum or a maximum is the one of the tariff in force in that month and nothing is carried over: `F¹`'s custody minimum does not apply to a charge of month `u₁ + 1`. `u = 0` puts every month, the first purchase and the sale on the next tariff; `u ≥ T` keeps the first all along; a tariff without a period is the tariff of F-08 to F-14. The conversion fee of a foreign-currency fund follows the same month (`F^{k(m)}.fx`, the plan's price on a monthly purchase). Months are counted in the run (`gridOffset` shifts only the charging phase). **An end falls on a charge.** An end `u` must be a multiple of the custody line's period `n` in both tariffs it separates (`(u + gridOffset) mod n = 0` where `n > 1`); the engine refuses any other end instead of prorating (section 12, G-31). **The standing-order price stays under a regular one.** Where the group or the person sets the regular price of a side, the house's list keeps its standing-order price for a monthly purchase (F-12) and its conversion price for a recurring plan (F-13), and the person can place the order either way. Two flags say which side was set, one for each: `cheaperOfStandingTrade` (the regular trade price was set; the list has a standing-order price) and `cheaperOfStandingFx` (the regular conversion price was set; the list has a plan conversion price). Neither is set from the other side's price. A monthly purchase of cash `c` in month `m` is then priced as **a whole order**, the conversion and the commission together: the *standing way* costs `fx^s(c) + f^s(c − fx^s(c))`, with the standing commission `f^s` (the regular one where the tariff prints none) and the plan's conversion `fx^s` (the regular one where none); the *regular way*, considered only if at least one flag is set, costs `fx^r(c) + f^r(c − fx^r(c))`, with the regular conversion `fx^r` and the regular commission `f^r` where `cheaperOfStandingTrade` is set, the standing commission `f^s` otherwise (F-12: a person who set only the conversion price does not make the commission the lower of the two). The order pays the cheaper of the two, order by order, and never the commission of one way with the conversion of the other, because no single order offers that mix. A tariff with neither flag pays the standing way alone, as F-12 and F-13 say; a flag whose side prints no standing line changes nothing. A lump sum, a reinvested distribution and the sale pay the regular prices, as always. **Where it is built.** The provider workbench (F-63) builds each later tariff as it builds any entered tariff, from the lines `then` names over the provider's own list lines, so a line the person left alone is the house's list line in every period; `layered` hands the account's builder the provider's own list tariff for the fund and channel (F-63's `buildTariff` at level `list`), which builds the sequence in layers (`site/app/lib/own-account.ts`): the list tariff, then the group's prices on the lines they name, then the person's own. **The layering rules.** (1) A value replaces only what it states: a stated rate replaces the commission's whole rate shape (a flat price, a price per share) and keeps its minimum and maximum unless the value states its own; a typed or a group's custody rate keeps the list's minimum and maximum, per month, in every period. (2) Each group price ends at its own `for_months` where it has one (a price that prints none does not end), and no later than the person's stay in the group, which is empty for the whole plan; the person's own values hold for the whole plan. (3) A price whose words name a purchase alone or a sale alone (`side`) leaves the other side the list's price. (4) A group price whose words do not name fund units is not applied to a fund (no price from analogy, D42): it is shown beside the list price, and the person may adopt its numbers as their own values. The fee split (F-61) removes or changes a line in every tariff of the sequence, and a line counts as charged if any of them charges it. **Example (Leumi, `data/fees/leumi@2026-11-04.json`).** 100,000 ₪ held for twelve months at no return, custody only, charged every quarter on the balance: the student price 0.075% a quarter for the first six months (the record's student custody line), then the list price 0.15% a quarter (`leumi-custody-israel`). **F-15 Balance fee** (provident family and IRA): `φ_bal / 12 × B'_m`, capped: 1.05% provident and kupat gemel lehashkaa (PF-02), 0.5% comprehensive pension (PF-04), 2% hishtalmut and IRA (PF-06, KH-22), 0.3% lost-contact accounts (PF-08). **F-16 Fee change over time.** A discounted fee reverts to the list fee at the end of the discount period (A-19), no earlier than 5 years after it started unless a PF-14 trigger occurs. This holds for the balance fee and the deposit fee alike, each with its own period and list rate: a deposit made after the period pays the list rate. That PF-13 covers a deposit-fee discount is a reading: the circular covers any fee below the legal maximum, which for a kupat gemel lehashkaa has both bases (PF-02, KGL-14), KGL-16 covers the deposit fee, and PF-16's notice names both rates. The pension run reads a discount on either fee as the provident run does (no collected pension list prints one; a cut stated on the fee page or a statement's rate below the line's own makes one, D86). For a pension fund the deposit-fee reading is the same: each pension cap has a deposit item (PF-04, PF-05, PEN-22), so a deposit fee below it is a fee below the legal maximum, PEN-25 applies the circular to pension funds, and PF-16's notice names both rates. PF-14's cases (six months after contributions stop, a withdrawal, a transfer out) are the circular's general exceptions that end a discount before its period; the engine applies none of them, because the plan assumes deposits continue and none of those events comes (A-19, D83), except on a plan with no deposit after month 0. Whether one deposit and no more counts as contributions stopping is open (PF OQ18), and the run takes the cautious reading (D87, 2026-11-08): with `n` the printed period in months, the discount's last month is `e = max(n, 12 × min_discount_years)` on a plan that keeps depositing, and `e = min(max(n, 12 × min_discount_years), months_after_deposits_stop)` on a plan longer than `months_after_deposits_stop` (PF-14, six months) with no deposit after month 0; month `m` is charged `φ_disc` for `m ≤ e` and `φ_list` (A-19's `reverts_to`, else the cap) for `m > e`, on the balance fee and the deposit fee alike. So the one-deposit plan's deposit at month 0 pays the discounted deposit rate, its balance fee is discounted in months 1 to 6, and from month 7 both lines are at the rate without the discount; PF-13 is not flagged as lengthening a period the cut ends first. The six months are PF-14's figure `months_after_deposits_stop` in `data/rules/pf-14.json` (circular 2017-9-15 p.2, s.3(ב)(1), "שישה חודשים אחרי הפסקת תשלומים לחיסכון המועברים לקופת הגמל עבור העמית"), which the run reads from the rule book only on a discounted plan with no deposit after month 0. Mutual-fund fee increases take effect on 1 January only (MF-17, ETF-16): a known increase is applied at the next 1 January.

**F-17 Fixed fees.** Monthly account or system fees at non-bank members (BRK-24) are subtracted as `fee_fixed_m`. **Owed whether or not a unit is held.** The price lists print it as a fee for the account, a sum a month or a period ("דמי ניהול תקופתיים", "דמי טיפול" "אחת לחודש", "דמי שימוש"), not as a share of what is held, so it is charged on the account and not on the units: each month, after custody and the IRA manager's fee (shares of the units, charged on the units alone), `fee_fixed_m = min(f, U_m + w_m)`, with `U_m` the units' value left after those fees and `w_m` the cash waiting for a whole unit (F-109; zero by amount). The units pay first, sold oldest first as before; the waiting cash pays only what they cannot (`max(0, fee_fixed_m + account_m − U_m)`), its shekels first and then the fund's currency a distribution left, at the month's rate, its conversion not priced (a few shekels, as F-13 leaves out the spot conversion of a sale). **The order of payment** is the site's choice: no rule read sets it. Directive 415 s.2(ה) (BRK-61) says only that a bank's fee on a deposit account with no activity may not exceed the account's credit balance and that the bank may sell the customer's investments so that no debit arises, and BRK-28 says only that Leumi debits and credits a securities deposit through the client's account for trades and income. The choice errs toward a higher cost, and only a little: a unit sold for a fee stops growing, while the shekels that could have paid it wait earning nothing (F-109). **What neither can pay is not charged** (a decision of 2026-11-12, new, for the reviewer): the fee is charged up to what the account holds, units and cash, and no debt is carried to a later month. At a bank this follows directive 415 s.2(ה) (BRK-61): a fee on a deposit account with no activity may not exceed its credit balance. At a non-bank member no rule read says so, and it is the site's own choice, from the fee's own words and the rule the engine already applied to the units ("never more than is held"); it understates the cost only when the monthly deposit is below the monthly fees, so that the account cannot pay them. Before 2026-11-12 the fee was taken out of the units alone, so a plan whose monthly deposit waits for a unit paid no account fee in the months it held none (at one year, often nothing). `test/whole-units.test.ts`. **Securities in the account (D6).** An account fee is the account's, on whatever it holds: the makam and bond runs charge it too (`tariff.monthlyFee`), paid like their custody, by selling a slice of what is held (makam) or units oldest first (a bond); the exit year's fixed fees come off the sale gain once, as custody does (TXS-06).

**F-102 The current account at a bank (D64; `currentAccountFee`, `countsCurrentAccount`).** On the indices page a bank's row counts the monthly minimum `a_p` of a current account at that bank (`current_account.monthly_minimum` of the bank's record, NIS a month, the price list's minimum for an ordinary customer, read on its ceiling under D56): `account_m = min(a_p, held_m + w_m)`, paid each month out of what is held after the account's own fees (F-14, F-17), by selling units oldest first, and where the units cannot pay it out of the cash waiting for a whole unit `w_m` (F-109), as F-17 is: it is a price for the account, owed whether or not a unit is held; except at the bank the person marks as their own, which they pay anyway, and at an investment house, whose record has none. That a bank's securities account needs a current account at the same bank is D64's premise (the founder's decision); the rule base holds it only for Bank Leumi (BRK-28, the deposit is debited and credited through the client's account) and has no general rule that a securities account needs one, so the run rests on the decision, not on a rule. A general rule bears on its price: directive 415 (BRK-61) bars "דמי ניהול חשבון עובר ושב" on a "חשבון תמורה", a current account kept only for the customer's other accounts with no activity other than what directly serves them (s.2(ג); an existing account counts only once the customer draws the bank's attention to it). The cap `held_m + w_m` above is the site's own choice for a current account: the directive states such a cap only for a fee on a deposit account with no activity (s.2(ה), which F-17 leans on at a bank) and does not say it of a current account. **Open (BRK-61 s.2(ג)), read in full on 2026-11-12 and not settled by the text:** the fee counted here is the monthly minimum ("עמלת מינימום"), which the fee rules print as a note to the clerk-action price and not as a management fee; the directive, last updated in 1/2008, does not mention it, does not define "other accounts" and does not name a securities deposit (`research/2026-11-12-directive-415-current-account.md`; three banks' own words are BRK-66). It waits for the founder; until then the run counts the minimum as D64 decided. It is not a fee for securities transactions, so it never comes off the sale gain (TXS-06). It is its own fee line (`account`) in F-61 and F-63, and in the account's part of F-91. Every other page leaves it out. **A group's own price.** An account built on a bank (F-101) whose group's annex prints its own current-account price pays that price for the group's printed end, then `a_p` (`groups.entries[].current_account`, a stretch of F-101's sequence): Discount's students of a first degree for 48 months and of a second degree for 24 ("עמלת מינימום פעולות" "פטור", annex A pp.35 to 36), and its released soldiers for 12 (annex A p.34), which is recorded but not read today: no price of that group that names fund units has a number (its custody price is words only), so the page offers no pick for the group (D43) and nothing reads its current-account price until one does; Mercantile's students for 48 months and released soldiers for 24 (the teller and the direct action "פטור", annex A p.31; with no minimum row for these groups, and the ordinary minimum capped at one teller action's price, the minimum is read as nothing). A group whose end is not a number of months (young people to an age, soldiers for their service) keeps `a_p`, since a stretch needs its months, though Mercantile's annex prints their actions "פטור" too; and a group whose annex was not read for its current account pays `a_p`, the most the price list lets the bank charge (D56): its benefit is not applied until it is read. The row's sources keep both prices: the annex's for the exempt stretch and the price list's minimum after it (`lineSources`, line `current-account`). **A bank that is part of another.** A bank's record that is part of another bank (`part_of`: Pepper, part of Leumi) carries no current account; it is not a row of its own on the indices page, which lists the bank it belongs to (the data check holds both, `checkPricePremises`). **The caps of 2027 (open).** Until 30 June 2027 the track rules apply (BANK-17). From 1 July 2027 the Governor's order caps managing a payment account, a current account included, by the month's actions: `account_fee_up_to_2_actions` for 0 to 2 and `account_fee_3_to_100_actions` for 3 to 100 (BANK-35). Eight of the ten recorded minimums are above the first cap, so for an account with at most two actions a month the cap would lower them from then on; but how the amended rules count an action is not settled by the rule base: BANK-35 now carries the amendment's annex ו, the list of services inside the capped fee, and the list does not say whether a monthly purchase of securities or the transfer that pays for it is one of them. The rule base gives no reading, so the run does not choose one: it keeps the nominal `a_p` for the whole plan, as a fixed fee is, and flags BANK-35 on the first month, from the run's month that holds the cap's `effective_from` on, whose stretch charges an `a_p` above the first cap: an exempt stretch charges nothing, so a group exempt past the cap's start is flagged where its exemption ends. BANK-35 is listed among the run's rules only on a run it flags: a run whose minimum stays at or under the cap reads the cap without citing it. The flag sits on the account's cost (F-63, F-91), the figure that may be too high from then on, and not on the value left, which it would make too low. Applying the cap waits on the rule: how an action is counted. Worked example (`tests/golden/expected_d64_d67.py`): 10,000 NIS now and 500 a month for 24 months at no return, a commission of 0.1% with a 5 NIS minimum and custody of 0.15% a quarter; at 5.50 NIS a month the account takes 132 NIS and 21,518.13 NIS is left; a group exempt for 12 months pays 66 NIS and keeps 21,583.46 NIS.

**F-103 A charge for each corporate event abroad (D67; `corporateEventFee`).** A price list's charge for each corporate event of a security held abroad (a `corporate_event` line in `usd_per_event`: One Zero's custodian cost "עד 50 דולר ארה"ב ללקוח לכל אירוע חברה", priced at its ceiling under D56) is read as a charge on each dividend of a fund bought abroad that pays dividends: on each distribution month of F-20, `e_m = min(c × S_m, held_m)`, `c` the amount in the fund's currency (dollars as printed; another currency through the shekel at the start), `S_m` the month's rate, paid by selling units oldest first after the distribution is reinvested. That a dividend is a corporate event is the site's reading of a term the price list does not define (D67). An accumulating fund has no dividend and pays nothing; a fund bought in Tel Aviv is not in the line's class. In F-61 it is part of the account's custody line: removed with it, and a custody line at another rate moves it in proportion (a custody line at nothing gives no proportion, and it stays as printed). It follows the custody line at an average level too: where an average replaces the custody line it is left out, since an average is what customers paid, a custodian's charge passed on included, as the spread follows the conversion line (F-13); where custody stays at the list, so does it. It is not deducted from the sale gain: whether a custodian's charge per event counts as a custody fee under TXS-06 is not settled by the rule base (G-32), and leaving it out keeps the price at the most the documents allow. A charge that falls in the exit tax year is flagged G-32 on the value left, whose exit tax may then be a little too high. Worked example (`tests/golden/expected_d64_d67.py`): 100,000 NIS in a fund paying 4% a year in four dividends, at no return, 3.70 NIS a dollar and a conversion of 0.2%, for 24 months: eight charges of 185 NIS, 1,480 NIS; 96,161.55 NIS left after the sale.

**F-109 Whole units (D94; `runSecurities` with `wholeUnits`, `wholeUnitsAt`, `unitWaysAt`).** An exchange-traded fund is bought in whole units unless the house's own documents say it sells that listing by amount. **Which run.** A fee snapshot's `by_amount` (data-schema 3.6) names, per listing (`us`, `europe`), the orders the house buys by amount, with the document's words: `all` orders, or only the monthly purchases of its `recurring_plan` (where the snapshot prices that plan's conversion, F-13). An entry whose house limits fractions to a list it does not publish (`list_only`) names none of the site's funds and changes nothing (D42). An entry whose words name shares and no fund (`shares_only`: Altshuler Shaham Trade's "כמות שברית של מניות", where its price list writes "מניות ו-ETF's" when it means funds) is silence for a fund and changes nothing. With no entry for the listing, the house buys in whole units (silence is not a yes, D56). **The cheaper way.** A house that sells by amount, for every order or on its recurring plan, can also buy whole units: both runs are made, its own and whole units, and the house is priced on the one whose real value at the horizon is higher (its own on a tie), as a monthly purchase takes the standing line or the regular one (F-12, F-13). By amount, a monthly order below the commission's minimum pays the minimum every month; in whole units it waits and pays it once in several months, so with a high minimum (USD 9 on 500 ₪ a month) whole units can leave more. Both years of the row (horizon and year one) and F-61's split take the way chosen at the horizon. On a way in whole units that split is by what each line paid, carried to the horizon (F-91). A Tel Aviv listing is always whole units (ETF-67: the trading unit is one unit). An Israeli tracking mutual fund is bought by amount, as its units are (MF-11: the investor gives a sum and receives units; the ISA's investor guide, 2017, pp.8, 27), and so is a fund whose record has no `share_price`: its row says the unit price was not collected. **Unit price.** `P` is the fund record's `share_price`, the issuer's net asset value per share on a date, in the fund's currency; its price in shekels at the start is `U_0 = P × S_0`, with `S_0` the run's rate for that currency (1 for shekels), and in month `m` `U_m = U_0 × nav_m`, moved with the fund's value and the exchange rate as the fund is (F-08). A Tel Aviv line of a foreign fund takes the issuer's NAV of the same share where its Israeli prospectus annex names that share (its ISIN, and the line as the units it offers): the issuer's dollar NAV at the run's rate of the dollar. No TASE or Maya price is used (their terms forbid republishing). **An order.** The cash an order has is `C = d_m + w`: the month's deposit (or the amount now) and what waits from earlier orders. Its trade is `n × a_m`, with `a_m = U_m × (1 + h)` the ask (`h` the half-spread, F-13) and `n` the largest whole number for which the trade, its commission (F-12, on that trade) and, abroad, the conversion of the shekels it needs (F-13: `c = owed + fx(c)`, only the shekels spent are converted; money already in the fund's currency, a dividend not yet spent, is used first) fit in `C`. A flat price for a small trade or conversion (F-12, F-13) can make a smaller order cost less than one that spends the whole cash, so the search for `n` starts from the units the cash would buy at the lower of the two prices on each side (the flat price, or the price on the whole cash) and goes down, each count checked exactly. What is left, `w' = C − cost of the order`, waits in the account in its own currency, **earning no interest** (an assumption the page says: no house's interest on idle cash is modelled), and joins the next order. A fee a month for the account (F-17, F-102) that the units cannot pay comes out of it. If `n = 0` the order is not placed and pays nothing. The amount now is one order, every deposit after it one more. A monthly purchase that may go on a standing line or the regular one (F-12, F-13) takes the one that buys more units, then the cheaper. A recurring plan's monthly purchases are by amount (F-08) and the amount now is whole units; what it leaves joins the first purchase. **At the horizon** the waiting cash is part of what is left: shekels as they are, the fund's currency converted with the sale's proceeds; it is no gain and pays no tax. **Not modelled:** TASE's minimum value for an order (ETF-67: NIS 500 in an equity-market fund). It binds in continuous trading only: in the pre-opening, the pre-close and TAL orders the quantity need only be one trading unit (TASE trading guide, part three, ch. ה' s.2ב(6), file p.42: "בשלב טרום הפתיחה ... בשלב טרום הנעילה ובפקודות TAL תהיה הכמות גבוהה או שווה ליחידת מסחר אחת"), so an order of one unit below NIS 500 can be placed; whether each house lets a customer place orders in those phases was not checked. Nor is an order placed less often than monthly by the person's choice. **On the indices page** the run with no account fees (F-63's `none`) stays by amount, so what whole units do lands in the account's cost, in its remainder (F-61, F-91), which says so where the house buys in whole units; a row where most monthly deposits bought no unit says so in a line.

**F-18 Fee for interest and dividend handling** (some non-bank tariffs, BRK-24): per distribution event, from the tariff.

## 5. Tax during the holding period, by instrument

All rates are read from `data/rules/` by rule ID; the figures below are quoted for readability and are the 2026 figures recorded in the rule base.

**F-20 Distributions from a fund or share.**
- Israeli exempt fund (including money-market funds, tracking funds and ETFs): 25% of the distribution, withheld (TXS-10). Net reinvested as a new lot.
- Foreign-domiciled fund or TASE foreign fund held through an Israeli bank or member: Israel 25% (TXF-08, withheld by the intermediary, TXF-10, TXF-05). Foreign tax withheld on the distribution is credited up to the Israeli tax on it (TXF-17, TXF-18): `τ_IL = max(0, 0.25 × dist − credit)`, `credit = min(w_foreign × dist, 0.25 × dist)`. For a US fund held with a W-8BEN, `w_foreign = 25%` (TXF-12, TXF-13), so `τ_IL = 0` and the total is 25%. Without a W-8BEN, `w_foreign = 30%`; the excess 5% is not credited (TXF-18) and is lost. For an Irish fund's own distributions, Ireland deducts nothing from an Israeli resident: no exit tax on units held in a recognised clearing system, such as an ETF's, or paid to a holder who gave a non-resident declaration (TXF-28); the engine uses 0 and cites TXF-28.
- At a foreign broker nothing is withheld in Israel; the same tax is paid by advance by 30 April of the next year (TXF-21). The engine charges it in the month of the distribution (timing difference ignored, stated in the output).
- Distributions from a TASE foreign fund are stated by the Tax Authority not to be offsettable against securities losses (TXF-07); losses are not modelled (G-07), so this has no effect.
- Taxable fund (TXS-11): the holder pays nothing on distributions or sale; the fund pays at individual rates on its income. The engine models only a shekel money-market taxable fund, as 15% of nominal interest at fund level (TXS-12 condition (b), derived; circular 7/2003 s.2.1.1 states the principle that a taxable fund bears an individual's rates, TXS-11), as an extra `δ`; any other taxable fund is blocked (G-08).

**F-21 Bank deposit, laddered.** Each contribution opens its own deposit of term `n` (A-15) at the rate in force (A-21 or A-26; a 447 rate is nominal annual before tax, BANK-13). At maturity: `interest = principal × ((1 + r)^(n/12) − 1)`. Tax withheld by the bank (TXI-07, TXI-08):
- unlinked or partly linked: `0.15 × interest` (TXI-03, TXI-04). Prime-linked deposits fall here: the statutory index is the CPI or an exchange rate, and the Tax Authority's guide counts a deposit as unlinked unless fully index-linked (TXI-04);
- fully CPI-linked: linkage exempt, `0.25 × real interest` where the quoted rate is the real rate and linkage is added at `I` (TXI-05, TXI-02, BANK-26; the quoting as the banks read show it, BANK-36).
The net amount, principal plus after-tax interest, rolls into a new deposit at the then rate; the tax basis resets. The low-income deduction (TXI-20) and the 1948-cohort reliefs (TXI-21, TXI-22) apply only on inputs that meet their conditions; the engine applies TXI-20 as `interest_taxable = max(0, interest − min(deduction, interest))` when the couple's income is under the favoured ceiling, from `data/rules/`. Breaking a deposit before maturity is not modelled (G-16).

**F-22 Savings plan.** A savings plan is the F-21 ladder with the plan's own rate, term and exit points; no rule distinguishes it for tax (TXI-07, BANK-28).

**F-23 Money-market fund.** Exempt fund (TXS-12, TXS-24): no tax during; distributions per F-20; exit per F-40. Daily fund: `g = A-20`, `δ = fund fees` (F-10). Renewable fund: `g = A-24`, `δ = 0`, liquidity only on fixed dates (MF-35); if the horizon ends between fixed dates the exit is at the last fixed date before it. On the renewable fund's guide, the next fixed date is the series date its record holds, when that is today or later; the wait is the days from the day the money is needed to that date, when the day is on or before it; and the latest the date after it can fall is that date plus MF-08's longest interval between fixed dates, in months (a day the month does not have falls back to its last day). Two taxable money-market funds exist (TXS-24); for them F-20's taxable-fund case applies.

**F-24 Makam, rolled yearly.** Price per 100 face value `p = 100 / (1 + y)^(N/365)` with `y = A-22` and `N` days to redemption (GOV-06). At redemption the discount `100 − p` is interest taxed at 15% and withheld at 15% (TXI-01, TXI-11, TXI-09). The net proceeds buy the next series (12 series, one issued a month, GOV-22, GOV-23). A sale before redemption is a capital gain at up to 15% with the whole gain treated as real (TXI-12); the engine sells at the price implied by the unchanged yield when the horizon falls inside a series. **The 15% is a ceiling, charged in full.** TXI-12 words the rate "שלא יעלה על 15%". Under 60 no bracket for capital income is below 31%, so the ceiling is the rate (TXG-08 case (a)); for 60 and over, whether the reduced brackets lower it on an unlinked bond's or makam's gain is open (TXG OQ10, TXI-19 covers linked interest only), so the engine charges 15% at every age and makes no bracket reduction (`exitTax(…, true)`, as F-42 says for fixed-rate items). The guide's calculator has no age or income field (guidelines/design.md); its page says in words that the rate is a ceiling and names the over-60 question as open. The surtax (F-43) still applies above its threshold. **Custody slices (2026-11-07).** Custody and the account's monthly fee are paid by selling an equal slice of each series at the month's price (F-14, F-17). Paying them by selling paper is the method's model, a choice like the equal share below, not a term read: a house may debit them from cash in the account instead, and the run charges no sell commission on the slices. Each slice is a sale before redemption, so its gain, `slice × (value − cost)` of the series held, is a capital gain of the month's tax year at TXI-12's rate, and that year's custody and account fees come off that year's capital gains (TXS-06). In the exit year a sale's gain and the year's slice gains are one base, less the year's fees (`detail.taxedGain`). A year with no sale never owes on its slices: a slice's gain is less than its value, which is the fee it pays, so the year's fees always exceed its slices' gain. Whether the rest of those fees lowers the tax on a redemption's discount in the same year is open (TX2 OQ11): TXS-06's conditions, read with s.92(א)(5) and TXI-17, may make it a capital loss of that year set against interest on securities of the same year. That would be an offset within the year, not a loss carried to a later one, so G-07 does not settle it; the engine makes no such offset and the guide's page says the question is open. `detail.deductedFees` is what the fees took off capital gains, each year at most its gains, so the taxed base plus the deducted fees plus the taxed discounts is every gain of the run. Which series a slice comes from is the method's choice (an equal share of each): different series are not identical securities (TXS-06's definition, "מאותו סוג ושל אותו מנפיק" read as one series, inferred), and within one series every lot was bought at issue at one price, so first in, first out gives the same cost. **What stays open.** The split of a slice's price, like a sale's, between accrued discount and capital gain is open (TXI OQ3). Both are taxed at 15%, so the rate does not depend on it, but TXS-06 deducts the fees only "בחישוב רווח ההון": if part of a slice's gain were discount, the fees would not come off that part directly, and would reach it only as a capital loss of the year (s.92(א)(5)) set against interest on securities of the same year (TXI-17, an inference from the two rules). The engine treats a slice's whole gain as capital gain, as it does a sale's. Whether the member deducts the fees at withholding is open too (TXS OQ12); the engine takes the year's tax as settled, through a return where the member did not. That the account's monthly fee (F-17) is a fee "בעד פעולות בניירות ערך" under TXS-06 is the method's reading, not a sentence read; the house the guide uses charges none. The 2025 guide's "20% at realisation" line conflicts with the statute's 15% (TXI OQ3); the engine uses 15% (primary, s.125C(c)) and flags the conflict (G-09). **A redemption's commission.** Where a house's own tariff names redemption of makam, the run charges it: the line with `band` `פדיון` under `data/fees/` (data-schema 3.6), read by the engine's tariff step and the guide path alike. Paper of one maturity is one series, so a month has one redemption, charged on the face value redeemed that month with the line's minimum, maximum and flat price (F-12). It comes out of the proceeds and does not reduce the taxed discount (G-20). The price is the line that names redemption, not the reduced price of an order placed online: those lines name buying and selling or an action done online, and a redemption places no order (the wording is read; that it therefore stays at the section's price is inferred). Banks' tariffs follow the First Schedule, part 4, item 4(a)(1), which names buying, selling and redemption, and BANK-18 keeps those commissions on makam. All 14 collected houses with a makam line name redemption: 13 on makam's own row or a section that holds it, and Excellence on the heading of its one line for securities on TASE. PEPPER's annex prints 4 NIS up to 30,000 NIS an order and nothing above; above it the line stays at Leumi's section 4(a)(1) (inferred). A house whose document is silent keeps no charge. A government bond's run sells at the horizon (F-25) and never redeems, so no such line is read there. On P-05's example plan (10,000 NIS now, 500 NIS a month, 10 years, a 3.25% yield, 2% inflation) there are 109 redemptions; at Psagot the trade costs move from 573 to 858 NIS with them, and its custody adds 560 NIS. Psagot then leaves 71,898 NIS in today's money, alone on top under P-01 (F-80) since PEPPER became part of Leumi (2026-11-04, D43), and the next house, Yahav, 68,636.

**F-25 Government bond.** Coupon `c` on face value, once a year for CPI-linked series (GOV-11); for fixed-rate series the frequency comes from the series data; GOV-13 sources twelve-month interest periods for the type auctioned today, "אג"ח ממשלתית" (ק"ת 6457 regs. 1 and 5(ג)); unlinked (fixed or floating): `0.15 × coupon` withheld (TXI-15, TXI-09); CPI-linked: principal and coupon scale with `I` (GOV-11, GOV-12, no floor), linkage exempt, `0.25 × real coupon` (TXI-14). If the CPI falls, the reduced coupon is still taxed in full (TXI-25: interest and linkage are separate incomes, the Supreme Court); the negative linkage is not offset, as the district court held, and the Supreme Court left open whether any route allows the offset (TXI-25, para 23). The bond's sale at the horizon follows F-09's falling-index rule (TXS-02). Net coupons reinvested in the same bond as a new lot. At the horizon the bond is sold at par plus A-27: unlinked gain taxed at up to 15% on the whole gain (TXI-12); linked gain at up to 25% real (TXI-14). Accrued interest bought between coupon dates is not modelled (G-10). **Price after a change in rates.** For the guide page's "if rates move" drawing, `bondPrice(c, y, years, perYear)` gives the price per 100 face of a bond that pays `c` a year in `perYear` equal coupons and redeems at 100 after `years`: `P = Σ_{k=1..n} (100 c / f) / (1 + i)^k + 100 / (1 + i)^n`, `f = perYear`, `i = (1 + y)^(1/f) − 1`, `n = years × f`; at `y = c` the price is 100. The coupon is the site's assumption (`cash_rate`, D20) and `perYear` comes from the series rule (GOV-11: one coupon a year). For a CPI-linked series the index multiplies price and face alike, so the percentage change is the same. Before fees and tax; the visual sets it against a makam of the ledger's term priced by F-24 at the same yield.

**F-26 Current-account credit balance.** Monthly interest `B × g` at A-25, taxed 15% at crediting (TXI-08 reaches current-account interest). The bank pays the rate on the balance up to a ceiling (`balanceCeiling`, the deposit-terms record's `amount_range.max`, BANK-09; Leumi 10,000 NIS): interest is `min(B, ceiling) × g`, taxed 15% as before. The ceiling belongs to the published rate: a rate the person enters applies to the whole balance. Only the current account reads it; a deposit's amount range is a condition of the offer, not a cap on the ladder. **The current-account page.** The page's table gives, per bank, the most it pays in a year on the person's balance `B`: `r × min(B, ceiling) × (1 − t)`, where `t` is the withholding on the interest (TXI-08, TXI-03: 15%). The balance is flat and the interest is not compounded; the figure is the ceiling of what the arrangement can pay, and only to a customer who meets the bank's conditions. `currentAccountYearlyInterest`, `packages/engine/test/account-interest.test.ts`.

**F-27 Provident family (kupat gemel lehashkaa, hishtalmut, pension, provident fund for annuity, child savings, IRA).** No tax during (TXK-03, TXH-18, TXP-13, TXK-17). Foreign tax withheld inside the fund is a cost inside the fund (TXK-18), carried in A-18 or A-11. Track switches and transfers between funds of the same kind are not tax events (TXK-04, TXH-17, TXH-19, PF-23).

**F-28 National Insurance and health contributions.** National Insurance is zero on everything the engine models: interest and dividends at special rates are exempt (TXG-27), and capital gains are outside the base (TXG-28). The deemed interest on a kupat gemel lehashkaa lump sum is income taxable under s.125C(b) or (c), which the National Insurance Law leaves out of the base (s.350(a)(6)), so neither contribution applies (TXK-27). A recognized annuity from kupat gemel lehashkaa money before retirement age is still open (TXK OQ9), and the engine does not model it. Health contributions (מס בריאות) are charged on the same income as National Insurance (TXG-34), so they are zero on the same items. Under product D14 the site does not show them. Interest taxed at marginal rates under s.125C(d) is outside both exemptions; an example is study-fund gains withdrawn early (F-47). The engine does not model NI or health on it (G-27).

## 6. Deposit-side tax effects (equal net outlay, B1)

The person's outlay `C_m` is net money. Where a wrapper returns part of a deposit as tax relief, the engine finds the gross deposit `D_m` whose net cost is `C_m`:

**F-30 Equal net outlay.** `D_m = C_m + benefit(D_m)`, solved exactly (benefit is piecewise linear in `D`, so the solution is found by testing each piece). The year's relief is spread evenly over the months of that year that have a deposit; whether payroll grants the credit monthly is open (TXP OQ6), and a self-employed deduction comes through the return, a timing difference stated.

**F-31 Pension and provident fund for annuity, employee who is not a beneficiary member.** Credit `= 0.35 × credited`, `credited = min(paid, max(floor, 0.07 × E))`, `E = min(eligible income, 116,400 NIS a year)` (TXP-01, TXP-02; floor 2,268). The floor applies only to money paid (TXP-02's guide quote). Existing pension deposits through work (A-12) use the cap first: the credit for new money is `credited(existing + new) − credited(existing)`. Marginal rate `τ_m` for deductions comes from the 2026 personal-effort brackets on the person's salary (TXG-02). Figures are frozen for 2025 to 2027 (TXP-27, TXG-06) and read from `data/rules/` by tax year.

**F-32 Beneficiary member and self-employed.** If deposits for the year, employer money included, reach 16% of the average wage (26,436 NIS in 2026, TXP-03), the credit follows TXP-04 and the deduction TXP-06 (11% first tier, 7% + 4% second tier, each at `τ_m`). A person who is not a beneficiary member and deposits without employer deposits gets the TXP-05 deduction: for business income (a self-employed person), limb (1), `7% × E` with `E = min(income, 164,400)` plus `4% × E` on the part of the deposit above `12% × E`, and the credit on the rest at 5% of `E` (TXP-02's no-work-income rate); for work income, limb (2), 5% of the work income that is not insured, up to the cap. For a beneficiary member, one year's deposits split as in the guide's worked example (TXP-31). First tier: `11% × income for a self-employed member`. Second tier: on the base `deposits − beneficiary threshold`, deduct `min(base, 7% × AI) + min(4% × AI, max(0, base − 12% × AI))`, where AI is the additional income. What is left of the deposits goes to the s.45A(e) credit (TXP-04). The extra 0.5% credit (TXP-07) and the age-50 enlargement (TXP-08, 2026 amounts not found) are blocked (G-13).

**F-33 Everything else.** A self-employed person's study-fund deposit earns the TXH-05 deduction, 4.5% of income up to its ceiling, at the person's brackets (TXG-02) through F-30; the employee's study-fund deposit is not deducted. A self-employed person who is also an employee (TXH-06) is not modelled. Otherwise `D_m = C_m`: kupat gemel lehashkaa (TXK-01), hishtalmut employee deposit (TXH-04), brokerage, deposits, funds, bonds, child savings (TXK-19).

## 7. Tax at exit (liquidation at T)

**F-40 Securities and fund units (brokerage wrapper, all instruments held as securities).**
- Real-basis instruments: shares, exempt-fund units including money-market funds, tracking funds and ETFs, TASE foreign funds, foreign-domiciled funds, CPI-linked bonds: `τ_exit = ρ × max(0, real)`, `ρ = 25%` (TXS-01, TXS-09, TXS-13, TXF-02, TXI-14), withheld by the bank or member (TXS-05, TXF-05) or paid by advance at a foreign broker (TXF-22). The index `J` is CPI, or the exchange rate for a foreign-currency security (F-04).
- Nominal-basis instruments: unlinked bonds, makam sold before redemption, commercial paper: `τ_exit = 0.15 × max(0, gain)` (TXS-03, TXI-12). The 15% is the law's ceiling, charged in full at every age (F-24: below 60 it is the rate, and for 60 and over a reduction is open, TXG OQ10); for makam `gain` holds the exit year's custody slices (F-24).
- `real < 0`: no tax. The loss is not carried anywhere (G-07).
- A falling index: `real` comes from F-09's per-lot rule, so a lot bought at a higher CPI or exchange rate than at the sale is taxed on its nominal gain, never more, and the part of its nominal loss equal to the fall is not allowable (TXS-02, TXF-03).
- The inflationary amount accrued after 1993 is exempt (TXG-13, TXS-02); nothing in scope predates 1994.

**F-41 Deposits, savings plans and current accounts.** Tax was charged at each maturity or crediting (F-21, F-26). At `T` the last open deposit is treated as matured on the exit day: interest to date taxed under F-21. **Known gap (G-16), found 2026-11-12, flagged by the run since then.** A deposit still inside its term at `T` is priced as if it had matured that day: the interest to date in full, and on a CPI-linked deposit the full linkage to date, exempt, with 25% on the real interest. The run raises the flag G-16 on such a result, and the number's sheet says so in words. What a break would give is not modelled. The deposit records hold whether a break is allowed (`break.penalty`: none, with a penalty, or not allowed) and the bank's words on it, but not what a break pays (BANK OQ12). **Exit points.** The run's input carries a deposit's exit points when the record says when the first one falls: `exitFirstMonth`, a piece's age in months at its first exit point (`exit_points.first_after_months`), and, where the record gives it, `exitEveryMonths`, the interval between exit points after the first (`exit_points.every_months`; BANK-14, BANK-27). The engine takes either figure as absent unless it is a positive whole number of months. Without a first the run knows no exit point, whatever the interval: the interval says how often exit points come, not when the first falls, and the first is often later (Bank Hapoalim's חושבים קדימה, record `hapoalim-hoshvim-kadima`, after 120 months and every 12 months after that; Leumi's prime plan, record `leumi-tashlumim-prime-3y`, after 6 months and every 3 after that), so it is never taken to fall one interval after the start. A piece's age on month `m` is `m − start + 1`, the months it has earned with its first month included, so that it matures at age `n`. On `T` a piece inside its term is at an exit point when its age equals the first, or exceeds it by a whole multiple of the interval; with no interval the first is the only exit point the run knows. Such a piece can be withdrawn that day without a break (BANK-27), and for an unlinked deposit the run raises G-34 in place of G-16: the number's sheet says that the sum received may differ from the number, because there is money in a deposit not yet ended and the run assumes it is withdrawn that day at an exit point with all the interest to date; and that an exit point is a day on which money can be taken out without a break, while what it pays, and whether it needs notice, each bank sets in the deposit's terms. The sentence is general and names no bank's term as the product's (some banks' plans ask for notice in advance, BANK-38). Which of the two notes a piece raises decides nothing else in the run. Its amount is unchanged: interest to date, as for any piece at `T`. That an exit point pays the interest to date at the deposit's rate is not sourced as a rule: BANK-27 says only that the money can be taken out there without a break, and the products' own terms differ (BANK OQ20). Some pay less: four of Bank Hapoalim's five standing-order plans set the rate by the withdrawal date (BPN-10), and the product page of one of Mercantile's three stepped standing-order plans, whose records hold the rate at the end of the term, prints 0.40% "בתחנות - מתום חודש וכל חודש לאחר מכן" beside 1.40% "מתום 5 שנים" (record `mercantile-hok-nazil-hodshi-0626`, read 2026-11-07; the three records carry a doubt: the page shows a rate that rises at each exit point, and the bank's table prints several rows for a plan without naming the exit point). An older form printed a share: Bank of Jerusalem's plan form of August 2018 for שבתאי צמוד מדד gave at an exit point a share of the interest that grows with the years, 80% after two years (BANK OQ12); no record holds that plan, and the two plans in the bank's table in force from 20.10.2026 are both ירח (BANK-38). A term can change: Bank Hapoalim's ספרינט דיגיטלי paid 70% of the interest accrued by its 2023 brochure (`bank-product-names.md`, Hapoalim table, row 61), and the bank's table read on 2026-11-06 prints all of it (record `hapoalim-sprint`). Some pay more than the run's rate gives: Discount's stepped deposits add to the rate at each exit point, on the whole term, and the bank's table does not print the rate after the addition (record `discount-hok-nazil-shnati-0628`, BANK OQ20). The records do not hold what an exit point pays, so the number may be higher or lower than what the product pays that day; G-34 says so on the number (gap G-34). A CPI-linked piece raises G-16 at an exit point too, never G-34. The rule base holds no rule for the tax on a linked deposit withdrawn at an exit point that does not pay its linkage in full: TXI-07 speaks of a deposit or plan broken before its term, and whether the same holds at an exit point is not sourced (TXI OQ14). So the run reads no exit point for a linked deposit: it prices the piece as matured that day, the linkage exempt and 25% on the real interest, as it does any piece inside its term, and shows G-16's note. That note says the piece is counted as if its deposit ended that day and that the sum may be higher than what can be withdrawn then; it does not say that the day is an exit point of the piece, and it says nothing of the tax. A deposit's exit points count from the deposit's own start. A savings plan's may count from the plan's start (BPN-10) or from each instalment's, and the record does not say which, so the rule is the cautious one: a plan's piece is taken as at an exit point only when both counts let it out. The run opens a plan on its first month and a new one every `n` months, so the plan a piece was paid into began on month `start − ((start − 1) mod n)`, and by that plan's count the piece is in the plan's month `age + ((start − 1) mod n)`. Before the plan's last month (that month below `n`) the plan's count lets the piece out only on an exit point of the plan, so that month must be an exit point too. From the plan's last month on (`n` or more) the plan's count holds the piece no longer: by it the plan has ended and paid out, so it lets the piece out on any day and the piece's own count decides alone. The plan's count names no exit point past its term: an instalment paid in month 8 of a twelve-month plan is, on month 17, ten months old with its plan ended on month 12; it is counted neither as in month 17 of that plan nor as in the fifth month of the plan opened on month 13 (`pieceAtExitPoint`). "Has paid out" is true of a plan that does not renew, and the run does not read whether a plan renews: the site hands a savings plan's exit points to the run only where its record says the plan does not renew (`auto_renew.value` false, as in every one of the 48 records that give a first exit point on 2026-11-12), so a renewing plan reaches the run with no exit point and raises G-16 (`buildInstrumentTerms`). Before the plan's last month a piece that only one count would let out raises G-16, so a plan that counts from its start and would let every instalment out on that day is still flagged. The schema holds `first_after_months` from 2026-11-12, and every record with exit points carries it: a number, or null where the bank prints only how often they come (47 of the 129 records with exit points give an interval alone, and 34 neither figure). A record whose first is null reaches the run with no exit point, and its pieces inside their term raise G-16. The records hold an interval in months, so a deposit that can be left on any day after its first exit point (Bank Hapoalim's three-year deposits: "מ 25 חודש ובכל יום בהמשך", record `hapoalim-pikadon-3-shanim-kavua`) is held with a first and no interval: the run knows its first exit point alone and raises G-16 on the months after it, though the money can be withdrawn then. On the records as read on 2026-11-12, all 37 CPI-linked records (35 products, two of them filed twice by date) bar a break, 13 of them with exit points. The deposits the site runs all bar a break and have no exit points: the two P-05 names (Leumi's one-year deposit and its three-year savings plan) and the one-year deposit F-86 picks for the bank-deposit page, whose selection leaves out a deposit that can be broken or has exit points. For these the money still inside its term at `T` cannot be taken out that day at all, so the result is better than what the product gives. Where a break is allowed, TXI-07 reads the linked case otherwise than the run (a reading of today's s.125ג(ג) beside a 2003 circular, not a Tax Authority statement of today's rate): where a linked deposit or savings plan is broken before its term and the break does not pay its linkage in full, all its income is taxed at the unlinked rate. No site run uses a CPI-linked deposit today. B3 (a wrapper locked at the horizon is listed, not ranked) is not applied to these pieces: a deposit stays ranked, and the flag only adds a note to its number. Whether to apply B3 as written, or to change the run instead (hold the money that cannot finish a term outside the deposit, or open the last deposits for the months that remain), was put to the founder on 2026-11-12 and is his to decide.

**F-42 Age 60 and over at exit.** For "maximum rate" items (real capital gains, exempt-fund gains, linked interest) the person's brackets apply first (TXG-04, TXG-07, TXG-08, TXI-19, TXS-23): `τ_exit` = the sum, over the slice from `Y` to `Y + real`, of `min(bracket rate, ρ)` for each part of the slice, with the bracket rates from the 2026 reduced schedule 10/14/20/31/35/47% (TXG-04, TXG-02) and `Y` is A-13. Withholding stays at the flat rate (TXS-23); the difference is recovered through a return or form 0135 (TXG-25) when A-14 is on, and is otherwise lost. Fixed-rate items are not reduced: 15% unlinked interest (TXG-08 case (c); TXI OQ9 noted), and the gain on an unlinked bond or makam, which the law words as a ceiling ("שלא יעלה על 15%", TXI-12) but whose reduction at 60 is open (TXG OQ10), so the engine charges the ceiling (F-24). Under 60: no change (TXG-08 case (a)). The same brackets apply at the kupat gemel lehashkaa exit (F-44); its withholding is the manager's, 25% of the gains (TXK-26), so its settlement in the return is F-44's.

**F-43 Surtax.** With `Z = Y + real` (inflationary amounts excluded, TXG-09): `surtax = 0.03 × max(0, Z − 721,560) − 0.03 × max(0, Y − 721,560)` plus, on capital-source income `Cap = real + other capital income`, `0.02 × max(0, Cap − 721,560)` (TXG-10). Settled through the return (TXG-11). Applied in every wrapper's exit tax and to taxable distributions in the year they fall.

**F-44 Kupat gemel lehashkaa, lump sum at any age.** Gains withdrawn are interest income at up to 25% (TXK-05, TXK-06, primary). The base is the real gain by law: the part equal to linkage on the money is linkage income, exempt under s.9(13), whose provident-account exclusion Amendment 190 deleted from 1.1.2012, and the 2016 bill's notes say "25% of the real gain" (TXK-07, primary). The engine computes `τ_exit = 0.25 × max(0, real)`; the base carries no label. No 15%-of-nominal alternative is shown beside it: the law fixes the base (D73). A partial withdrawal is pro rata (TXK-13). The 60+ reduction (F-42) applies to this deemed interest too: s.125C(b) is a maximum rate, so the reduced brackets s.121(b)(1) gives to all taxable income of a person aged 60 or over apply first, and since the gains are the top slice it helps only where other income leaves those brackets unused (TXK-06). The engine computes the slice from `Y` to `Y + real` at `min(bracket rate, 25%)` with the 2026 reduced schedule (TXG-04, TXG-07), as F-42 does, and `detail.fullRateTax` keeps the flat 25% beside it. **Withholding and settlement.** The manager withholds 25% of the gains whatever the person's age, and nothing from the principal with its linkage (the Tax Authority's withholder file 856, types 87 and 88, TXK-26; reg. 3(א) of the 2005 withholding regulations reads 20%, and which provision sets 25% is open, TXK OQ3; Harel's own 25% is TXK-28, which agrees). Withholding is an advance: the final tax, at the reduced brackets from 60, is settled against the 25% in the annual return, so money comes back on a part of the slice whose bracket is below 25% (TXK-26); under 60 the withholding equals the income tax at the flat 25%. The run cites TXK-26 and flags it (`TXK-26`). G-21 is not cited at this exit: its evidence is a bank's or TASE member's withholding. With the refund not claimed (A-14 off) the engine keeps the flat 25% and flags the relief it leaves out (flag `TXK-06`); that is the only case the flag marks. With a 25% withholding that figure is the tax withheld, which is what a person who claims no refund pays (until 2026-11-06 the record read reg. 3(א)'s 20% and this line called the figure an upper bound); the site never turns A-14 off. TXS-23 is not cited here: it is the securities withholding regulations'. **A falling CPI.** s.3(ה4)(3) deems the gains withdrawn to be interest and carves out only "רווחים ... בסכום הפרשי הצמדה" as linkage income (TXK-05, TXK-07); when the CPI fell over a deposit's life there is no such gain, so the engine counts that deposit at its amount and the taxable base never exceeds the gains withdrawn. No text says this for a fall in so many words, so a run where it bites carries the label "reading" on TXK-07. The study fund's excess (F-46, TXH-11) follows the same rule and label.

**F-45 Kupat gemel lehashkaa, annuity route from 60.** Transfer to a provident fund for annuity from age 60, annuity fully exempt (TXK-08 to TXK-10, TXK-12). The engine shows this route on the guide page as text and as "value transferred at 60" (the gross balance); the annuity itself is blocked (conversion factors, PEN OQ7; commutation TXK OQ4; G-03).

**F-46 Keren hishtalmut, lawful exit.** After 6 years from the first payment date (3 at retirement age or for study), everything from benefited deposits is exempt (TXH-09, TXH-10). Benefited own deposit: up to 2.5% of determining salary, determining salary capped at 15,712 NIS a month (TXH-08, TXH-02); benefited self-employed deposit 20,566 NIS a year (TXH-08). Gains on deposits above the benefited amount are taxed at withdrawal at 25% of the real gain (TXH-11; the real base is the Chief Economist paper's, primary). When the CPI fell over an excess deposit's life, that deposit counts at its amount, as in F-44, labelled "reading" on TXH-11. `τ_exit = 0` on the benefited part. The 6-year clock runs from the first payment date (KH-11); joining a fund that already exists keeps the clock (A-12).

**F-47 Keren hishtalmut, early withdrawal (only if the person opts in, B3).** Employer benefited deposits are taxed as work income at `τ_m` when received (TXH-13, TXH-01); gains, the whole nominal gain with its linkage part (a reading for an employee, TXH-13), at the s.121 rates for income not from personal effort, which start at 31% under 60 (TXH-13, s.125C(d)(5), TXG-03), stacked on the salary plus the employer deposits withdrawn (TXG-07) and at the TXG-04 rates at 60 or over; the fund withholds 47% (TXH-15) and the difference is recovered through a return (A-14). Own deposits are not taxed again (they came from net salary, TXH-04).

**F-48 Pension and provident fund for annuity, early withdrawal (only if the person opts in).** Non-compliant withdrawal: `max(0.35, τ_m) × amount` (TXP-18), where `amount` is the whole sum paid: reg. 3(1) of the 1962 regulations taxes the member "על אותם כספים", and s.87(ד) applies the ordinary tax where it is higher. The engine does not compute it yet, so it is still **blocked** and the wrapper stays "listed, not ranked" even when the person opts in, until F-48 is built. The run's reason is "F-48" (not built), and no gap is flagged. Lawful lump sums (severance component, small balances) follow PEN-29, PEN-30 and are listed, not modelled.

**F-49 Pension and provident fund for annuity at or after retirement.** The lawful exit is an annuity (PEN-27), partly exempt (TXP-14, 57.5% of 9,430 NIS a month in 2026, rising to 67% from 2028), with the offset formula (TXP-15) and commutation rules (TXP-17, PEN-28). The annuity value needs conversion factors that are not collected (PEN OQ7). **Blocked** (G-03): the wrapper is listed with its gross balance at the horizon and the text of the exemption rule; it is not ranked.

**F-50 Child savings.** Deposits, the parental addition and grants are not income (TXK-19). Gains are taxed on withdrawal (TXK-20). No Tax Authority text names child savings. National Insurance's circular 1356 says 25% of the gains for both routes, without naming the base (TXK-20). The provident route's fund is a kupat gemel lehashkaa for every purpose, so the Ordinance's rule for a lump sum from one applies: up to 25% of the real gain, from the law (TXK-23, TXK-07), with no label. On the bank route the Ministry of Finance says the existing tax rules apply, and by them 15% on unlinked tracks (s.125ג(ג)(1), TXI-03) and up to 25% of the real interest on the linked one (s.125ג(ב), the linkage exempt by s.9(13), TXI-02, TXI-05) (TXK-29); the rates are cited from the Ordinance in the Tax Authority's text, and applying them to child savings is the site's reading, since no official text names child savings: labelled "reading" on TXK-29. Provident route: 25% of the real gain. Bank route, CPI-linked deposit: 25% of the real gain. Bank route, unlinked deposit: 15% of the nominal gain. Withheld at withdrawal. **25% is the ceiling.** On the provident route and the CPI-linked bank deposit the law's rate is "not exceeding 25%" as the top slice of income (TXK-06, TXI-02): 25% is the most the gain can pay and what is withheld, and the engine computes it; where the young adult's own brackets give less, the difference comes back only through a return or a refund request (TXK-06), which the engine does not compute. The unlinked bank deposit's 15% is a fixed rate. **Open on both routes:** whether the Ordinance's trust chapter (s.75ג) reaches the child's statutory trust account (the law keeps the money "in a trust account for the child", managed by the parent), which would change whose income the gains are (TXK-23, TXK-29; TXK OQ1). Deposits, the parental addition and grants are principal. **A falling CPI.** The provident route is a kupat gemel lehashkaa (TXK-22), so F-44's rule applies: a deposit or grant made at a higher CPI than at the withdrawal counts at its amount, labelled "reading" on TXK-07. The CPI-linked bank deposit keeps the plain indexation, its interest taxed in full even when the linkage is negative. That is a reading, not a holding: the Supreme Court held it for a deposit linked to a foreign currency (it dismissed the appeal and left open whether the linkage loss can be offset), and applying it to a CPI-linked deposit is an inference from the same reasoning (TXI-25). So a run where a deposit's CPI fell carries the label "reading" on TXI-25, as the other inferred steps do.

**F-51 IRA.** Tax follows the wrapper it was approved as (TXK-16, TXK-17): an IRA hishtalmut uses F-46 and F-47; an IRA provident fund for annuity uses F-48 and F-49 (blocked). Fees: the manager's balance fee within the 2% cap (PF-06, IRA-17) plus the brokerage tariff inside the account (IRA-21, IRA-22), both through F-12 to F-15.

## 9. Employer money (B2) and what only a salary wrapper gets

**F-52 Employer line.** For a hishtalmut: the employer deposit is the payslip rate (A-12; 7.5% in KH-04) on the whole salary; own deposit 2.5% (KH-04, TXH-08). The employer part above the TXH-01 and TXH-02 ceiling (`7.5% × min(salary, 15,712)` a month) is work income taxed at `τ_m` when paid (TXH-03), subtracted from the line's value; it also carries employee National Insurance and health at the salary rates (TXG-35; National Insurance's circular 1473 puts the excess in that base, TXH-26). The circular charges the excess as imputed value on the salary ("חייבות בזקיפת שווי"), so it is added on top of the salary and the contributions are those of the sum, at the bands of TXG-35. Only the month is the rule base's inference (TXH-26: the excess is salary "when paid", TXH-03; the circular names no month); with the same salary every month it changes nothing, and it is not labelled. Its gains are taxed at up to 25% of the real gain at withdrawal (TXH-11, primary). **Employer part above the limit inside a payslip run:** when a study-fund run splits `goal.monthly` into own and employer money (`employerMonthly`, F-61, S42) and the employer part is above TXH-01's limit, the engine does not stop: the part up to `7.5% × min(salary, 15,712)` is benefited, the part above is salary when paid and enters the excess pot (taxed at `τ_m` at deposit, with National Insurance and health, and its gains taxed at exit at 25% real, TXH-11, as in the employer line), and the own part keeps TXH-08's 2.5% benefit; the salary tax on the part above is subtracted from the run's value at the amounts paid, as in the employer line. For a pension: the employer deposit is the payslip rate (6.5% + 6% severance, PEN-03) on the whole salary, since PEN-04 caps only the mandatory minimum at the average wage, unless the run names a lower salary the employer deposits on (`employer.salary`; F-67's pension visual names `min(salary, average wage)`). That salary is TXP-10's "salary", "the salary on which the employer paid the fund", and the rate deposited on it is the payslip rate: the employer component is not taxed at deposit up to `min(7.5%, rate) × min(salary, 34,423)` a month (TXP-10), nor the severance up to its ceiling (TXP-11). TXP-10's excess is the person's work income when paid, taxed at `τ_m` on the whole gross salary plus the excess; that tax is withheld from the salary, so the fund's balance holds the whole deposit (`detail.grossBalance`) and the line's value is that balance less the tax at the amounts paid, as in the study fund's line. The line runs through F-08 with the wrapper's fees and exit rule and is reported as "value at the horizon of money only available here", never ranked.

**F-53 Pension insurance cost.** Premiums for death and disability cover are deducted monthly from the account (PEN-15) at A-17; tables are not collected (G-12), so the figure is "entered by you". Premiums are charged on employer deposits too (PEN-15).

**F-54 Child savings accumulation.** Monthly `58 × I` from NII, where `I` is the index at the last 1 January, because the amount is updated once a year (CS-03), doubled if the parent adds (CS-04), from birth to 18; grants per cohort (CS-13); fees 0 until 21 (NII pays, CS-18), then in the provident route the yearly fee on the balance comes out of the savings, within the general provident-fund cap of 1.05% of the balance and 4% of deposits (CS-18, fee regulations reg. 2(א)); growth at `r_g` for the chosen track. Exit tax per F-50. **The fee after 21.** No law sets a rate after 21; each manager fills in its own (CS-18: the standard bylaw leaves it blank). So the run uses the cap, an upper bound shown as "עד" wherever the figure appears, and flags it (flag `CS-18`); a rate the person enters from their own manager's bylaw (`feeAfter21`) replaces the cap and is bounded by it, as F-15 caps every provident fee. There are no deposits after 18, so the deposit cap does not bite. Harel's and Altshuler Shaham's 0.23% (CS-30) is those companies' own rate and appears only as a named example on the guide.

## 10. Outputs built on the ledger

### 10.1 Comparisons, fees, providers and guides

**F-60 Idle-cash preset (3.2).** Monthly cost of leaving `B` where it is, $`M = B \times \left[(1 + r_{\text{alt}})^{1/12} - (1 + r_{\text{cc}})^{1/12}\right]`$, after tax, where `r_cc` is A-25 taxed per F-26 and `r_alt` is each liquid option's net rate (daily money-market fund F-23 with F-40; one-month deposit F-21 at A-21 labelled "average paid at this bank last month", and at A-26 labelled "entered by you"; makam F-24). The part that must stay available (D13) is excluded from `B`. The candidate set is wrapper mode's (F-83): the current account is the baseline; the options above are compared against it; every other tier-1 wrapper and tier-2 instrument is accounted for by F-83, those the preset does not run as "not evaluated" with G-28. Until the Bank of Israel consents (D20) the "bank average" row is the bank's own published rate (A-21 note), the one-month row at the person's offer (A-26) is run on a one-month term, and the money-market fund and makam rows use P-05's `cash_rate` as a site parameter. **Yearly line:** the page also shows twelve times the headline's monthly cost, labelled approximate; it is a reading of the monthly figure, without compounding and without re-pricing the one-month deposit, not a yearly run. **Yearly verdict on an offer:** when the person gives the offer and its term (one month, three months, a year, or 1 to 60 months), the page runs the deposit at A-26 on that term (rolled at the same rate within the year; a longer term is read as one year of it) and the daily money-market fund over twelve months, both after tax from F-21 and F-23 with F-40, shows each yearly gain and their gap, and reads the gap against P-03's negotiate threshold ("below the threshold" or "worth checking"); with no offer the same reading is applied to the headline's yearly line, and a sum whose yearly line is under the threshold is a small sum (MF-15, MF-26, BANK-18 line shown).

**F-61 Fee materiality.** No page reads this formula; the engine keeps it with its tests. For a wrapper or holding with fee set `F`: total drag `= V_T^{real}(∅) − V_T^{real}(F)`; cost of fee `f` `= V_T^{real}(F \ {f}) − V_T^{real}(F)`; interaction remainder `= total − Σ_f cost_f`, reported as a line. Value of a stated reduction `f → f'` `= V_T^{real}(F[f → f']) − V_T^{real}(F)`. All runs hold A-01 to A-29 fixed. **A discounted line (A-19, F-16)** is one line over both of its periods: it counts as a fee when its rate or the rate it reverts to is above zero (a 0% deposit fee that becomes 4% after five years is a line, not part of the interaction remainder), and removing it removes both periods. In a stated reduction on such a line, `f'` replaces the discounted rate and the line still reverts to its list rate after the period, because a lower rate a company agrees to is a discount too (PF-13, D83); `f'` is a reduction when it is below the discounted rate. On a provident or pension line with no discount, `f'` holds for PF-13's period and then the line returns to its own rate (A-19, D86): counting a lower rate for every year would promise what no binding document promises. The fee page names both periods of the line and how long the discount lasts, and under the reduction field how long a lower rate holds. F-62 judges a discounted line whose discount ends within the horizon by the rate after it (D88), and its negotiate test reads this section's value of a stated reduction to the reference (D90 revised). **What would make a cut worth it (2026-11-07).** Readers asked what would push a cut worth 108 ₪ past the threshold. For a line whose verdict is "not worth the effort" or "not worth it now" (never "worth avoiding", whose card names what to pick instead) and whose cut to the reference is worth more than zero, the fee page searches this section's value of that reduction on moved plans, every other input as it is: the amount today, estimated from two runs (a first guess only: the value bends at the provident fund's yearly deposit ceiling and the study fund's benefited share), rounded up to a round sum (500 ₪ under 10,000, 1,000 under 100,000, 10,000 under a million, then 100,000) and checked by a run, raised a tenth at a time at most twice, so three sums in all, up to 10,000,000 ₪; and the fewest whole years from the horizon entered up to 40, by halving, where the value at 40 years reaches the threshold. A holding's balance is its own and is not moved. The card says either after its reason; it says nothing where neither is found. The search runs with F-81, after the hand rests. **A holding under review (3.6).** The existing balance enters the run as it is: no deposit fee, no deposit-side relief, not counted as paid in. Its deposits are the flat shekel amounts the statement prints, so A-04 (contributions constant in real terms) does not apply to them. A study-fund balance is benefited money once TXH-09's lock has passed at the start (scenario S42 states "exempt at exit, past 6 years"); while the lock has not passed, the tax layers of the balance are needed from the detailed annual statement (PF-36) and the run stops with a missing input. A kupat gemel lehashkaa balance runs with the balance as its opening cost basis for fees and growth; with `embeddedGain` (the gain already inside it, from the detailed statement, PF-36) the balance less that gain is the basis at month 0 and the exit tax is TXK-07's on the real gain; without it the exit tax is not computed (`taxAtExit` 0, flag PF-36) and F-61's fee costs, which are before exit tax, are still produced. A payslip's own and employer deposits run together, the employer's part staying under TXH-01's limit (above it the part is salary when paid, F-52, and runs separately). **The regulator reference.** The reference for `f` is the average for that provider class and fee type (D4), as D20 and D22 let the site show it. For provident, study-fund and pension tracks the reference is the F-68 class mean, computed over the CMA class (CAT, KH-23, PEN-24), with the track count beside it; the mean as the page shows it (rounded as shown) is the one measure the verdict, the reduction field, the opening sentence and the negotiation text use, and the page shows no median beside it (2026-11-07: a study fund's median equal to its fee read as "the fee is normal" while the verdict compared with the mean). The mean, because F-62 and P-03 named it already and it moves with every track's fee, where the median of a class whose tracks cluster on a few rates sits on one of them; beside it the page shows the Authority's official class average from its annual report (PF-39: provident funds taken together, new comprehensive pension funds), quoted with source and date (D22) as a second, sourced line that does not enter the verdict, because it has no study-fund or kupat gemel lehashkaa class. The Bank of Israel's bank averages (BRK-26, FEES_DASH) are a link, not a number (D20); the ISA's category averages for funds (MF-18, MF-19, ETF-17, MF-55) are a link, not a number, because its terms forbid copying and allow linking only (MF-55, D22); TASE's per-member averages and its fee calculator for brokers (BRK-33, BRK-37) are a link (D22). For funds, banks and brokers the line reads "no average to compare" with the reason and the link. Where a regulator figure is in the data as a snapshot `averages` row (F-63) it is shown as that row. "No reference available" otherwise. **Caps** are shown beside the fee, each with the rules the method names: study fund KH-22 with PF-06; kupat gemel lehashkaa PF-03 and PF-02; pensions PEN-21 with PF-04 (comprehensive) or PF-05 (general); provident fund for annuity PEN-22; and PF-06's 2% a year on the IRA hishtalmut manager line; the figures are the ones of PF-02, PF-04, PF-05, PF-06 and PEN-22. **Per month.** The fee page draws `cost_f(t) = V_t^{real}(F \ {f}) − V_t^{real}(F)` and the total drag `V_t^{real}(∅) − V_t^{real}(F)` at every month `t` from the series of the same re-runs (`feeMaterialitySeries`), with `V_t^{real}` the balance at the end of month `t` over the CPI index `I_t`, before exit costs and exit tax; the figure at the horizon in F-61 above is after them, so the two differ by what the exit takes, and the page says so under the chart. **Opening sentence of the fee page:** the fee page's one plain sentence above the chart is this section's value of a stated reduction: the largest fee brought to the regulator-average reference of its class (F-68), over the horizon, the same figure as the "if they lower it" field; it is shown only when a fee has such an average, and it is not a per-year figure. On a provident or pension line the reduction holds only for the discount period (D86), and the sentence says so in the field's own words (D90). **A securities tariff (F-101 to F-103).** A line is changed or removed in every stretch of a price for a time; the current account (`account`) is set to the new rate in every stretch that charges one, and a stretch that charges nothing (a group exempt for a time) stays at nothing, as a recurring plan's free conversion does. The custodian's charge per dividend (F-103) is part of custody: removed with it, and moved with it in proportion when custody takes another rate. The conversion spread (F-13) is part of FX: removed with it, and a rate set for FX is the whole conversion's, so it replaces the spread too.

**F-62 Fee verdict.** No page reads this formula; the engine keeps it with its tests. From P-03: `worth_f ≥ threshold_negotiate` and a reference exists below the current rate → "worth negotiating", where `worth_f` is F-61's value of a stated reduction `f → ref`, the reference as the page shows it, offered only where it is below the rate charged now (else `worth_f = 0`: nothing to bring down); `cost_f ≥ threshold_avoid` and an option in the same comparison that charges no such fee, or a lower rate of it than the rate judged, ranks above the subject → "worth avoiding" (there is a better option to pick: one that saves the whole cost, or part of it at a lower rate); else "not worth the effort". **Why the worth (D90 revised, 2026-11-07).** A call is worth what it can win: judged on the cost, the verdict said "worth negotiating" beside a cut worth 108 NIS or 187 NIS over ten years, and fresh readers read it as advice to call; one decided not to. With the negotiate half failing on the worth, a line whose cost passes the avoid test showed that half, also on a fund ranked first. **Only with something better to pick (D90, revised the same day).** Readers saw "avoid" on the option ranked first and second and asked how the two fit, so the avoid half counts only the options that rank above the subject on the page's own comparison for the same plan: a better place, never a shared one; a subject the comparison lists without a place, or does not list, is below every ranked option, and an option listed without a place never ranks above. A track's place is its own, the comparison running it at the track's fees; a holding's is still that of its product as the site runs it (open). An alternative's rate is compared on the subject's footing: the rate after a discount that ends within the horizon. Where none does, the card gives the negotiate half alone ("not worth the effort", or "not worth it now" with when the discount ends), and the softened relative wording for the first place is gone. In the site's example the provident fund for investing's balance fee stays "worth avoiding" (the brokerage account ranks first and charges no such fee) and the study fund's, ranked first, reads "not worth the effort". The card names the options above it in two groups, without the fee and with a lower rate of it, and keeps the room of every alternative, ranked above or not (D63). F-81 holds the base's answer to "is there an option above" fixed while it moves one input. Thresholds are shown on the page as site parameters. When both tests pass the verdict is "worth negotiating" (the order above) and the result says the avoid test passed too. The share of total drag `cost_f / total` is reported beside the verdict and compared with `negotiate_share` (P-03); it does not change the verdict. **The rate judged (D88, 2026-11-07).** "The current rate" is the rate charged now, except on a discounted provident or pension line whose discount ends within the horizon (at its period, never before PF-13's minimum, or after month 6 on a plan with no deposit after month 0, F-16 and D87): that line is judged by the rate after the discount (`rateAfterDiscount`), since the rate during it (0% on a deposit fee) says nothing about what the fee costs; a 1,293 ₪ deposit fee that is 0% for five years and 4% after is judged at 4%. **What the verdict says (D90, 2026-11-07, revised the same day).** A reduction agreed now holds only through the discount (D86) and none past it is priced, so the worth of a reduction now is F-61's as computed, and a line whose discount ends within the horizon says when it ends and the rate the fee can rise to, whatever its verdict; where the fee can rise above the reference then, that is when to check again. A line not worth negotiating now for that reason reads "not worth it now" (`check-after`), never "worth negotiating" beside a worth below the threshold; a line at 0% now with a reference of 0% has nothing to ask for, so its verdict is never "worth negotiating". Extended the same day: on a line with no discount of its own, a cut holds for PF-13's period and then the line's own rate returns (F-61, D86), so the fee page's card says so once, in the reduction field's line beside the worth, naming the rate that returns, and not again in the verdict's reason (D90); the holdings page, which has no such field, says it beside its worth at the class average (3.6). Where no line offers a reduction now but one can rise above the reference when its discount ends, the opening sentence says the fees are not above the average now but can rise above it when the discount ends. The rate after the discount is judged at no more than the line's legal cap, the most the run charges, also where F-81 scales it past the cap. The page offers the F-68 mean as the reduction only where it is below the rate charged now, and a line at 0% now offers none (no field, no script). **Reference and comparison on the page.** The reference F-62 takes for a provident, study-fund or pension track is the F-68 mean of its class, as the page shows it, and the page shows no other measure beside it (F-61). A wrapper with no track of its own takes the class of the track P-05 names for it (D21); the provident fund for investing names Meitav's general track 7978 (D88, chosen by Claude), the same manager's general track the study fund and the provident fund for annuity name, and its run still prices the collected binding documents (F-80). Where the class comes from a track the run does not price (a company's list, a holding's statement), the page says the site chose that track, naming its number, and that the average is of tracks like it and not of any one fund, and shows no average of it as the product's own. The authority's yearly average for all provident funds together (PF-39) is shown beside it and does not enter the verdict. The fee page reads no saved goal (D37): "the same comparison" is the F-83 candidate set run on the page's own plan (amount today, monthly deposit, years, return, and the assumptions behind its button), and a candidate that is ranked and charges no fee of that type, or one at zero, or a lower rate of it, is an alternative when it ranks above the subject (D90 revised the same day); so the avoid half is always evaluated there.

**F-63 Provider workbench (3.4).** For each provider `p` with tariff `F_p` at the chosen fee level (list, regulator average, entered; D4): `cost_p(horizon) = V_T^{real}(∅) − V_T^{real}(F_p)`; `cost_p(year 1) = V_12(∅) − V_12(F_p)` nominal; broken into fee types by F-61 (a way in whole units by what each line paid, carried to the horizon, F-91). Behaviour from A-28 drives the trade, FX and custody counts. Ties by F-80. On the indices page a bank's row also counts its current account (F-102) unless the person marks it as their own, and a fund bought abroad that pays dividends pays a price list's charge per corporate event (F-103). A provider without a tariff is a row "not comparable, data missing" with the reason from `data/fees/`. How a snapshot becomes a tariff: a line applies when its `applies_to` names the fund's instrument class, the wrapper or `all`, and the person's channel matches (a line with no channel fits every channel); at an average level the snapshot's `averages` row replaces the rate of its line as a percentage with no minimum or maximum, and a line with no average stays at the list price (a line whose own `level` is `regulator_average` or `tase_average` is read the same way, its minimum and maximum dropped; the computed value's strength and the source of each line read are the average's own, the regulator's or TASE's, not the list line's, `lineSources`; a provider with no average row or average-level line for any line that applies is "not comparable", because an average is a percentage with no bounds and the list price is not an average); `∅` is the same run with no provider fees (the fund's own costs and the spread stay); the fee types are the provider's trade, custody, FX and fixed-fee lines and, where the row counts it, the current account (`account`, F-102), each removed in turn as in F-61; a quarterly or yearly fixed fee is spread evenly over its months; a snapshot line that no formula here charges (F-18's distribution handling among them) is listed as not modelled; the person's trades a month are split into equal orders, each with its own commission (A-28); and an entered tariff replaces the lines the person gives, the rest staying at the list. **Chart series and the page's readings.** The cumulative-cost chart draws, for each provider, the provider's own five lines (trade, custody, FX, fixed fee, and a bank's current account where F-102 counts it) paid up to month `t`, in nominal shekels and in shekels of today (divided by `I_t`, F-06), read from the run's month series (`cumulativeProviderFees`); the last month adds the exit commission and FX fee, so the series ends at the run's five fee totals. It is not `V_t(∅) − V_t(F_p)`: the fund's own drag and the spread are not provider fees. The deciding fee type is the top-two test of this formula applied pairwise down the table: for each row, the type with the largest absolute gap to the row above; a row inside the tie block of the cheapest provider (F-80) has none, and neither has a pair where either row is in whole units, whose lines carry no after-tax figure (F-91, F-109). Providers within θ of the cheapest share rank 1. A snapshot with two lines for one base that both apply to the fund's class and channel (two sub-class bands of one asset type, for example Psagot's US and European foreign-securities lines) is not given a price the engine would have to guess; the page lists it as not comparable with the lines named, and the person's own offer can still bring it into the ranking. **Choosing between lines:** when several lines of one base apply, the line with no `band` (the standard price) is used and a banded line is passed over; a plan line (a `band` that names a plan, with a fixed monthly fee of its own, as One Zero's ONE) is used, with its fixed fee, only when the person names the plan (`band`); a banded fixed fee alone is never charged unasked; a bond is `tase_bonds` and an ETF `etf_units`, so a bonds-only line (Psagot's) does not price an ETF; only lines still tied after this (for example two sub-class bands) are the ambiguous case above. **Foreign brokers and one provider's failure (indices page).** The run takes `fx`, shekels per unit of a currency by ISO code, for the lines set in dollars or pounds (F-12). A provider is "not comparable" with its own reason instead of failing the run when: a per-share line applies and the fund has no price (`needs-share-price`); a line needs a currency `fx` does not hold (`needs-fx-rate`); or the engine cannot read its snapshot for this fund (`record-error`, the engine's message as detail: two lines for one base that the run did not tell apart, a unit the tariff does not read, a channel not named, an entered level with no entered tariff); one such provider never stops the run for the others. A snapshot that splits a base by band (Interactive Israel's US and European trade lines; Interactive Brokers' US, London and Tel Aviv lines by plan, and its automatic currency conversion) is told apart by `band`, which may be several texts, one for each base: a line is asked for when its band contains any of them. The abroad option's generic fund (indices page) gives each foreign broker the line of the market the fund trades on. **No line, no price.** A tariff with no buy/sell line for the fund's instrument class gives that provider no price: its row is "not comparable" (`no-applicable-line`), never a free trade, because a distributor may charge for buying and selling tracking funds (MF-26) and a missing line only means the tariff does not say. Money-market funds at a bank are the exception (bought, held and redeemed free under a distribution agreement, MF-26; no custody, BRK-17, BANK-18), so a missing line there stays a zero. **At a non-bank member** it is no exception. A non-bank member is mostly an investment marketer, which the distribution-fee regulations leave out (MF-26), and the bank custody ban binds banks only (BRK-17; BRK-52), so a missing buy/sell line for money-market units gives no price ("not comparable", `no-applicable-line`) and the tariff's custody line is charged (F-14), with F-14's note (no rule limiting it was found; the regulators report a blanket waiver of custody fees proper only, and a handling fee usually fixed and often refunded). A tracking fund is `mutual_fund_units`, an Israeli ETF `etf_units`. **The indices page (D28).** Per option (Israeli tracking fund `mutual_fund_units`, Israeli ETF `etf_units`, foreign fund on TASE `etf_units`, abroad `foreign_securities`) each provider's cost is this formula on a generic fund with no fund costs, accumulating, in shekels (in dollars abroad), so it exists for an index with no fund record. A fund's value is its own run at the option's cheapest provider. Abroad, a provider whose tariff splits by exchange is read on each exchange's line (its `band`) and the cheapest that computes is shown with the exchange named; a fund abroad uses its own exchange's line. A tariff that prints more than one pricing plan for an exchange (Interactive Brokers' Fixed and Tiered) is read on each plan, and a house that prints a way to convert besides its standard line (a banded conversion line with no currency of its own and no recurring plan: Interactive Brokers' automatic conversion, F-13) is read with each way; the cheapest reading that computes is the house's price, and an account built on the house (F-101) starts from that reading. The exchange and clearing fees Tiered adds are left out, as every house's are (D66). A sourced `availability: false` is "not available"; a provider an open rule blocks for a fund (G-18) is shown, not ranked. **Every house, the funds at a house, and the worth lines (indices page).** A fund is priced at every house that sells it (one F-65 run per fund, over all the houses at once), so the page holds `V(h, f)`, the real value left at the horizon, for each house `h` and fund `f` on the index; a house's tariff for the fund's class is read as above, and a house that does not sell the class (`availability: false`) or cannot be priced has no `V(h, f)`. An Israeli house is read on the three Israeli types (Israeli ETF, tracking fund, foreign fund on TASE), a foreign broker on the funds abroad. A house's cheapest way is its fund with the highest `V(h, f)` among those not resting on an open rule (G-18); the rows are sorted by it, and a row's gap is `V(h*, f*) − V(h, f_h)`, shown only when positive, where `(h*, f*)` is the headline's cell. The **worth lines** are two slices through that cell, changing one thing at a time. **House line, same fund and another house:** `V(h*, f*) − min V(h, f*)` over the other houses of the headline's group where `f*` computes and does not rest on an open rule; with no other house the page says the fund is priced at one house only. **Fund line, same house and another fund:** `V(h*, f*) − min V(h*, f)` over the other funds on the index of the headline's group that compute at `h*` and do not rest on an open rule; with no other fund it says so. Both are computed values in today's shekels over the plan's horizon (F-06), each the difference of two F-65 values, so each is at most the headline's value and zero only when the headline is the lowest in its slice. The page's view computes both (`worth`, the larger to be written first) and the bars of broker, fund, ETF against tracking fund and Israel against abroad (`bars`); the indices page shows neither. **The indices page's cost split (D28):** for the account and the fund the person has chosen (and the cheapest pair beside it), the page shows what is put in, `C_0 + C × months` in today's shekels (F-05 with A-04's constant real deposits, F-06), the value left (F-65), and the cost split by who takes it, by F-91: the account's lines and the fund's costs are taken before the tax they save, the fund with no account and with all its own costs (withholding inside it included), and the state's part is what remains, so the parts add up to `X − V_T^{real}(u, p)`. The parts are drawn side by side in one bar. The accounts list's cost and the broker cells show the account's part before tax; the cells are ordered by what is left (F-80). A broker the person types on the indices page (D44) runs at the entered level: its yearly custody is charged a twelfth a month with the monthly minimum (F-14), a field left empty is not known, so the account has no price (the conversion alone left empty leaves only `foreign_securities` without a price there), and it has no list lines. A typed fund with a fee left empty has no price, reason `fee-unknown`. A fund the person types is a record of its kind with the typed fees and the payout the person states (F-65), so a foreign accumulating one stays listed and not ranked until G-18 closes. **D66 on the indices page.** A price for a fund bought abroad leaves out the charges a third party abroad passes on, with a printed ceiling or without (F-13), and One Zero's custodian costs of up to 5,000 US dollars a month, a ceiling not stated per client (F-14); it counts One Zero's charge per corporate event (F-103).

**F-64 Track workbench.** Every track of every provider for the wrapper from the latest CMA period (F-83), with `AVG_ANNUAL_MANAGEMENT_FEE` and `AVG_DEPOSIT_FEE` (units per CAT-35: percent values, the balance fee a yearly share of assets and the deposit fee a share of deposits; shown) and the fit mark from F-70. Each track also carries the class reference of F-68. Tracks of one company with an identical name are flagged as a near-duplicate group, never dropped. The coverage line (F-83) reads: considered N; shown N; grouped K; fee not reported M. An empty deposit fee that the data marks with PF-06 (data-schema 3.13) is not "not reported": it reads "אין", and wherever a calculation takes the track's fees (F-61, the tariff fees of method 2.4) it is 0 by that rule. The study-fund and provident-fund-for-investing guides state the lowest and the highest fee reported on the tracks open to the general public, without self-directed (IRA) tracks, beside the legal cap, because the cap alone reads as the price; on the provident fund for investing it follows the managers' document rates (F-105), and its page says that the example run prices those documents, the discounted rate for its period and then the cap (F-67, A-19), while what members paid on average is this range. The brokerage guide points to the ISA's published averages by fund type (ETF-17) and to this range, an average beside an average, never an average against the cap.

**F-65 Fund variants.** For a chosen exposure, the candidates are every Israeli fund in the latest Maya export that matches the exposure by classification and reference asset (CAT-34), every foreign fund traded on TASE in TASE's latest list that matches it (ETF-32), and every foreign-domiciled fund traded abroad in `data/funds/` for the exposure (F-83). Each candidate with a `data/funds/` record runs F-08 with its own `δ` (A-06 to A-11), currency (F-04), distribution policy (F-20) and domicile rules (F-40). Accumulating foreign-domiciled funds are taxed only at sale under TXF-11, a reading the law supports by implication (no deemed-distribution rule reaches a non-controlling holder; no Tax Authority sentence says it, and no secondary source is cited since 2026-11-07). Their value is computed and labelled a reading (not confirmed by the Tax Authority), and the variant is listed, not ranked. A fund whose record carries `tax_ruling`, the Tax Authority ruling its own Israeli prospectus annex reports, naming a sale and a distribution as the only taxed events, is taxed at sale on that ruling, carries no label, and is ranked; so are the records of the same ISIN bought abroad (the records that carry it: iShares Core S&P 500, iShares NASDAQ 100 and iShares MSCI ACWI (Acc)). Other accumulating foreign funds keep the label and stay listed. A candidate without a `data/funds/` record is "not evaluated: details not collected"; foreign-domiciled funds traded abroad that are not in `data/funds/` are one group line (G-29). The group line counts as held only the foreign-domiciled records traded abroad (domicile not Israel and no trading currency on TASE); a foreign fund traded on TASE is a candidate row of its own. Matching: a list row with a `data/funds/` record is a candidate when the record's `exposure_id` is the exposure; a row with no record is a candidate when its exposure code is one the exposure's records carry (or the caller names) or its reference asset equals one of the exposure's names (or the caller names), ignoring case and spacing; a foreign-domiciled record for the exposure that no list row stands for is a candidate traded abroad. A fee the record leaves empty is taken as 0 where A-07 to A-11 give 0, and named as not collected; an empty fixed management fee makes the candidate "not evaluated: details not collected". A distributing fund runs on the yield its record holds (A-10): IUSA and VUSA, Irish, are ranked with F-20's rules, and take A-11 by F-107 as every Irish fund that holds its index's shares does. A US-domiciled fund on the indices page has no price and says why: no official statement was found on a US fund in trust form (TXF-37, TXF rule base open question 23). **A fund in another currency than its exposure (indices page).** Such a fund (a USD fund on a EUR exposure, say) has a run. It runs with A-01 as the exposure's return (in the exposure's currency) and A-03 as the change of the fund's currency against the shekel (default 0); the FX line (F-13) is charged when the fund is bought abroad, on the fund's currency and its start rate (F-84, `fxStartRates`), and the tax index of TXF-03 is the exchange rate of that currency (F-04). **F-66 Switching cost.** Brokerage: moving from fund A to fund B at month `s` is a sale (TXS-21): tax per F-40 on A's lots at `s`, the net proceeds open B's lot. Provident: a track switch or a transfer to the same kind of fund is costed 0, its tax neutrality a reading (below) (TXK-04, TXH-17, TXH-19, TXP-32, PF-19, PF-23); a hishtalmut moves to another fund whole, never in part (PF-18, KH-17), while a track switch may move part (KH-18, PF-23). Cost of switching `= V_T(no switch) − V_T(switch)`. The brokerage switch runs A to month `s` and sells there (F-09, F-40), then B from month `s` with the net proceeds, on the same calendar (tax years, custody quarters) and with later contributions raised by the index of month `s`; a hishtalmut moved in part to another fund is "not allowed", and a transfer between two kinds of fund is "not evaluated": no rule says it is free, except between annuity kinds, where TXP-32 says it is not taxed but its conditions are not modelled (which money may move between the kinds; the severance exception, TXP-32; reg. 2(ד)(1)'s cap on what a comprehensive fund may receive, SW-40; reg. 4(8), no move into a comprehensive fund while still active in the other, SW-22), and the refusal cites those rules. **A loss at the switch:** when A's sale at month `s` is a real loss (G-07), the loss is a capital loss carried into leg B and offsets B's real gain at exit before tax, never beyond that gain: in the same tax year under TXS-17 (the institution nets inside itself, TXS-19), in a later year as a carried loss under TXS-18, which needs a return filed for the year of the loss and is flagged. It is carried only between two funds of the same domain, since a loss abroad is offset first against foreign gains (TXS-17), and not at all when a fund is a taxable fund (TXS-11); the loss is used in nominal NIS as it stands, no linkage is stated by the rules, and the 3.5-to-1 offset against taxable inflationary amounts and the offset against interest and dividends are not modelled. **Provident transfers:** a transfer of the member's own money between two funds of the same kind costs 0 and cites the rules of its pair. Two study funds: SW-39 and TXH-19 (reg. 3(ב)(1) keeps every payment date for tax law). Not modelled: TXP-32's exception, severance money moved to a "new account" in a provident fund for annuity that is not a pension fund or central fund, which has income tax withheld under s.164 on its part not exempt under s.9(7א) before it moves (Provident Funds Law s.23(א)(2א)); F-66 costs every annuity-to-annuity transfer at 0, and names the exception, in its note and with the flag `TXP-32`, only on a transfer between provident funds for annuity, since the new account is never in a pension fund. ### 10.4 The indices page

**F-89 A plan number changed by a share (the indices page's section on what moves the result).** For one of the plan's four numbers `k` (amount now, monthly deposit, years, A-01) and a share `p`, the changed value is `k' = k × (1 + p)`, years rounded to whole years and at least 1 (a run takes whole years), and never below 0; the other three numbers stay as the plan has them. Its effect is `Δ_k(p) = L(plan with k') − L(plan)`, where `L` is F-63's value left at the horizon in today's shekels for the pair the indices page prices (the person's account and fund, else the cheapest pair; F-65). The same share for every number makes them comparable without the site choosing what a "step" is. Computed, not a forecast and not a ranking key; no value when the page prices no pair.

**F-90 Every plan number's line (the indices page's spider chart).** For each of the four numbers, `L(plan with k')` (F-89) at `p` from −50% to +50% in steps of 5% (P-05 `sliders.change_share`), drawn as one line per number on one chart, left to right from less to more, all crossing at the plan (`p = 0`). A steep line is a number that moves the result a lot; a line that bends up is a number whose effect grows as it grows (years and return, by compounding). The person picks a share with a marker (P-05's start value, +20%, until they move it); the table under the chart lists each number in the plan, after the change, `L` after the change and `Δ_k(p)`, sorted by `|Δ_k(p)|`, and the headline names the first. The account's and fund's costs are not a line. Values in today's shekels after fees and tax, as F-65.

**F-91 A way's cost by who takes it (the indices page's cost split, which adds up to the gap in what is left).** Every figure is a value at the horizon in today's shekels (F-06 at `m = T`). For a run `r`, `tax_real(r) = (tax_during + tax_at_exit) / I_T` and the value before tax `B(r) = V_T^real + tax_real(r)`; a difference of two `B` is a cost before the tax it saves (F-61's costs are after it). For one securities account `p` and one fund `u` on the plan:
- `X = B(u with no cost of its own, no distribution, no account)`: the deposits grown at the plan's return alone. It is the same for every account and fund on the plan.
- The account: `B(u, no account fees) − B(u, p)`, by line `B(u, p \ {f}) − B(u, p)`, and the remainder no single line explains (F-63 before tax). **In whole units (F-109)** a line is not that difference: a run without one line places its orders in other months (the cash the line no longer takes buys a unit sooner, and each order then pays its own minimum), so removing a line can leave less, and the difference read below zero on 164 cells of the 2026-11-12 scan. There a line is what was paid on it, each payment carried to the horizon at what a shekel held in the account grows to from the month it was paid, and divided by `I_T`: `line_f = Σ_m paid_{f,m} × R_T / R_m / I_T` (`feesCarried`), never below zero. `R` grows each month by the fund's total return (its growth less its own costs, a distribution kept in) and shrinks by the share of the holdings that month charges at the margin: custody's rate in a month it is charged and its minimum and maximum do not bind (where one binds, another shekel pays no more), and the IRA manager's fee; a fixed sum takes no share. So a line is what the money it took would have become in this account; a fee paid at the start of a month (an order's commission and conversion) is carried from before that month's growth, a fee paid at its end (custody, the fixed fee, the current account) from after it, and the sale's fees not at all. The account's whole cost stays `B(u, no account fees) − B(u, p)`; the remainder is that less the lines, and holds what whole units do (cash that waits earns nothing), the sale's costs on the carried money, and what the carried fees would have paid in tax; it can fall below zero, by up to about 2% of the account's cost on the largest plans of the scan (2,000,000 NIS for 30 years), and the receipt writes it with a plus, as it writes any remainder below zero. F-63's split gives such a line no after-tax figure and leaves its after-tax remainder empty, since an after-tax line taken from a carry before tax would mix the two; so where either of the top two is in whole units, no fee type is named as deciding between them (F-63). A run by amount keeps the difference.
- The fund: `B(u with no cost of its own, no account) − B(u, no account)`. Its own costs are the fixed and variable management fee, the trustee fee, the tracking difference, the spread and the tax withheld inside the fund (a fund-level cost, TXF-16, A-11). Measured with no account, it is the same at every account.
- The state: what remains, `X − V_T^real(u, p) − account − fund`. For a fund that pays nothing out it is `tax_real(u, p)`. For one that pays out (A-10), it adds the growth the tax paid along the way would have earned by the horizon.
- The receipt (the indices page's side rail) writes them in whole shekels that a reader adds up (`receiptAmounts`, site/app/lib/receipt.ts): with `D` the deposits (F-106), what the index added before any cost is `X − D`, and `D + (X − D) − state − account − fund = V_T^real(u, p)`. Each of `D`, `X` and `V` is rounded on its own; the account is the account list's whole figure, shared among its lines and the remainder by largest remainder; the fund and the state share what is left of `X − V − account` the same way. A cost line is written with a minus; a line under zero (the remainder, or a part the rounding took under zero) is written with a plus, since it gave money back (`takenWords`). The gain is `V − D`, and the bar of what went on the way gives each of the state, the account and the fund its whole share in percent of `X − V`, adding up to 100 (largest remainder).

The three add up to `X − V_T^real(u, p)` by construction, so the gap in what is left between two ways on the same plan is the gap in their costs. The engine's `costByWho` takes `X`, the value left, the account's part and the fund's part and returns the three. The stairs on the indices page show a step as the difference between two ways on one plan, taken between the whole-shekel figures shown so that the page adds up: the change in what is left is `round(V_T^real(u', p')) − round(V_T^real(u, p))`, and the changes in the account's, the fund's and the state's parts are whole shekels (largest remainder) that add up to minus it, by the identity above. Of all the priced ways that are not on an open rule and not currency-hedged, the highest is the ceiling the stairs climb toward; each step of the climb is the single change (another account, another kind of fund, another fund of the kind) that leaves the most.

Not covered: the tax a taxable money-market fund pays inside its price (TXS-12(b)), which no way on the indices page holds.

**F-92 A fund's gap to its index, from its reports.** A fund record's `tracking_record` holds, for each full calendar year its report prints, the fund's return `u_y` and its tracking asset's `t_y` in percent (a part year is left out). Over different years (a rate changed, or a year without its fees) it does not cancel, and the unprinted fee can move the cost either way. (c) The band: A-07 by (b) in the fund's cheapest and dearest year, `min_y` and `max_y` of `max(0, −(u_y − t_y)/100 − c_y)`, with `c_y` as in (b). (d) Every record says what its benchmark is (`tracking_record.benchmark`), Israeli and foreign alike, and only a record whose own documents say its index counts dividends after the tax withheld at source (`dividends: net`: a prospectus or Israeli annex on "the net total return", an Israeli annual report's "מדד השקעה נטו ממס (NTR)", TASE's own "... - NTR", or the issuer's own factsheet naming the net index as the fund's benchmark, "MSCI All Country World Index (Net)", "S&P 500 Net Total Return") has a gap and an A-07 from its years whatever its list. ### 10.5 The study fund's drawing

**F-96 A study fund's money, step by step (`moneyStory`).** For each year `y` of the drawing, a withdrawal at the end of `y`. An employee's own and employer's money are two runs of F-27 with `goal.monthly` = the rate × the salary, the employer's with `money: "employer"` (F-52): `own = paidIn(own run)`, `extra = paidIn(employer run)`, each deposit in today's money (F-06), which is F-106's sum with `C_0 = 0`; the drawing labels them F-106. `tax = Σ (taxAtExit + taxDuring) / I_T + Σ paid_m / I_m`, where `paid_m` is the income tax, National Insurance and health on what the employer paid above the limit (F-52; each tax year's tax at the index of the deposits it is on, `employerPaidToday`). `taxFromSalary = Σ paid_m / I_m` is the part paid out of the salary month by month (TXH-03), and the rest of `tax` is paid at the withdrawal; the drawing shows the two apart. `net = Σ gross / I_T − tax`, which differs from F-52's real value only where something is above the limit, since F-52 subtracts those amounts at face over `I_T`. `gain = net + tax − own − extra = gross / I_T − own − extra`, the return above prices after fees, below zero when fees and rising prices take more than the return gives. `fees = Σ_runs Σ_m (Φ_m − Φ_{m−1}) / I_m`, each month's charges (`Φ_m` the run's cumulative fees, §4) at that month's index, as the deposits (F-06); in a study fund they are the balance fee, since the deposit fee is nil (PF-06) and the guide's terms carry no direct expenses. The drawing shows the return before fees, `gain + fees`, and the fees as their own cut. It is what the charges cost when they were taken, so it leaves out what they would have earned: the zero-fee gain less `gain + fees` is `Σ f_m [(1+g)^{T−m} / I_T − 1 / I_m]`, positive when the return is above inflation and negative below it, and small next to the fees themselves. The page rounds each part to whole shekels so that the parts it shows add up to the `net` it shows. *Waiting for the lock's end.* For a year `y` before the lock year `L` (TXH-09), what waiting adds is `(net_L − own_L − extra_L) − (net_y − own_y − extra_y)`, what is left above what was paid in at `L` less the same at `y`: the deposits of the years between are paid either way (after an early withdrawal they go on into the fund), so they are not counted as gained, while the return on them is; the income tax waiting saves is `tax_y − tax_L`. The multiple of what was paid in at `y` is `net_y / (own_y + extra_y)` for an employee, so the employer's deposits are not read as growth, and `net_y / own_y` for a self-employed person. Before the lock ends both runs opt into F-47, and the own run's gains are stacked on the salary plus the employer's benefited deposits and gains withdrawn; that order is a modelling choice the rule base does not settle, as in F-47 (TXG-07 does not state it, and the part above the limit is left out because it was income when paid, TXH-03). The figure assumes the refund is claimed (A-14), and the page says so. **The limit follows the salary:** deposits are constant in real terms (A-04), so the salary is taken to be too (a modelling choice, stated on the page), and each month's benefited limit is the rule's share of `min(salary × I_m, 15,712)` (TXH-08, TXH-01); the ceiling is held at today's amount, since it is not linked to the index and moves only with its agreement (TXH-02). This is `salaryFollowsPrices`; only this drawing sets it, and without it a deposit exactly at the limit crosses it as soon as prices rise. The brackets and the income they are read at stay in today's terms and the amounts read against them are nominal, as everywhere in the engine. A self-employed person has one run with `goal.monthly` = the net outlay (F-30): `own = paidIn`, `extra` = the tax the deduction returned, each deposit's part at its own month's index (`reliefToday`), and `net`, `tax` and `gain` as above; the income, the deduction ceiling and the benefited amount stay at today's figures while the deposits rise, though TXH-05 and TXH-08 are indexed (the engine's existing treatment), which understates `extra` by about 9% at year 10 at the example's income; before the lock ends there is no figure (TXH-07 is not in the engine). The range at the year picked is the same run at the highest and the lowest balance fee reported on the index, over the tracks (rule 3), and for a self-employed person the same outlay in a securities account (F-08, F-40) at the collected tariffs that leave the most and the least. All at A-01 as entered, A-02 of P-05, and the track's reported balance fee or the one the person types (up to PF-06's cap).

**F-97 Where a study fund's deposits stand against the limit (`limitStanding`).** Read from the runs of the drawing's last year, which hold every deposit. An employee: `over` per run = the run's monthly deposit less the rule's share of `min(salary, 15,712)` at today's amounts, not below zero (TXH-08, TXH-01, TXH-02); `room` per run = what its share leaves above its deposit; one run's room is not the other's, since each has its own share. A self-employed person: `over` = what the first tax year's deposits, before the tax back, put above the yearly benefited amount (TXH-08), and `room` = what that amount leaves. `firstOverYear` = `⌈m / 12⌉`, for the first month `m` any deposit goes to the excess pot of F-46; deposits rise with prices (A-04) and the ceiling does not (TXH-02, F-96), so a salary under the ceiling can pass it within the drawing. For a self-employed person this year rests on the engine's treatment in F-96, which holds an indexed amount at today's figure, and the page says the calculation holds it. `grows` = `salary × I_T > 15,712` at the last index: only then does what is above the limit keep growing in today's money. Being above the limit is not yet tax: an own deposit above it is taxed only on a real gain (F-46), so the page ties the two together only where some year from the lock's end has tax. An employee's figures are at today's amounts; a self-employed person's are the first tax year's as deposited.

**F-98 An early withdrawal's withholding, and what a return gives back (`earlySettlement`).** Over the runs of one withdrawal before the lock ends, in today's money at `I_T`: `withheld = Σ detail.earlyWithheld / I_T`, F-47's rate of TXH-15 on the gains and the employer's benefited deposits (TXH-13: the own deposits and what was income when paid are outside its base); `back = max(0, withheld − Σ taxAtExit / I_T)`, the return's refund (A-14). `withheld − back` is the part of F-96's `tax` paid at the withdrawal whenever `back` is above zero, so the sentence and the column agree. Surtax is in `taxAtExit`, so it reduces `back`. `owed = max(0, Σ taxAtExit / I_T − withheld)` is what the return still asks for, and `first = F-96's net + Σ taxAtExit / I_T − withheld` is what reaches the account at the withdrawal: the column's `net` is `first + back − owed`.

### 10.6 The fee page: a deal read on its own

The fee page reads one deal: the fees of one product as the person typed them, each with its own prices over time. It runs no plan (no amount, deposit, horizon or return), so it states no total cost and no worth of a change. Every figure on it is a typed value, a value of the kind's record, or one of the readings below. A fee's price may change by up to three things (what is bought or held, the channel, the track the person chose, the currency, the size of the action or of what is held, the trading in a month, the number of actions in a month, or a condition in the person's own words); F-114 and F-115 say which of its prices F-110 to F-113 read. The site's price lists and tracks only fill a deal's fields, as a start the person edits. A fee with a price not given yet (a rate or a sum nobody typed and no template filled) that runs in or after the reading's first month is not read: F-110 to F-113 leave it out until a figure is typed or the price is made free, which, like a typed zero, is a price. A price not given that ended before that month is never read, so it holds nothing back. A link carries such a price as it is.

**F-110 A deal's periods (`dealPeriods`).** Each fee `f` of a deal carries a sequence of prices `p¹_f, p²_f, …`: the `k`th ends at the start of calendar month `e^k_f` (`e¹_f < e²_f < …`) and the last has no end. A price may be "free": nothing is charged while it holds. The fee's price in month `m` is the first whose end is after `m`, and the last where none is. The reading is made for a month `t`, the device's month. An end at or before `t` has passed and its price is not read. The deal's change points are the distinct ends after `t` over all its fees, in order, `c_1 < … < c_n`; they cut time from `t` into `n + 1` periods, `[t, c_1), [c_1, c_2), …, [c_n, ∞)`, and within a period every fee has one price. Two prices are equal where they charge the same: a free price and a price of zero are equal (a share or a sum per unit of zero charges something where a minimum or a flat price above nothing is typed; a cap of 0 holds every action to nothing, so a price with one charges nothing unless a flat price above nothing is typed, which is charged alone beside the cap, F-12), a share's currency counts only where a bound or a flat price is typed, and a sum's always. An end whose two prices are equal is not a change point. A fee with no change point after `t` **holds throughout**; every other fee **changes** at each of its change points. A change is named by the two prices around it: it **starts** where the earlier charges nothing (free, or a price of zero), **ends** where the later charges nothing, and otherwise **rises** or **falls** by the price's main figure, or just **changes** where the main figures are equal and a bound or the months between charges differ, or where the two prices are of different forms (a share and a sum). The main figure is the rate for a share of an action, the yearly rate of F-111 for a share of what is held, the yearly sum for a sum per period, and the sum for a sum per action or per unit, each in its currency (a sum in dollars and one in shekels just change). The first period begins at `t`. Ends are calendar months, and no price is read from one. The time to a change is the count of calendar months from the device's month to its change point, because the page says it as a time from now ("in 14 months").

**F-111 What a fee is charged on, and what adds (`chargedOn`, `groupRate`).** Each fee base is charged on one of three things: an **action**, **what is held** (a share of its value) or **the account whatever it holds** (a sum per period). *By base.* A purchase or sale: `trade`, `spread`, `creation_redemption`, and on a purchase alone `load`, on a sale alone `redemption_deduction`. A conversion: `fx`. A deposit: `deposit`. A distribution: `distribution_handling`, and `corporate_event` as F-103 reads it. A transfer out: `transfer_out`. A withdrawal: `exit`, which adds to nothing. What is held: `balance`, `custody`, `trustee`, `direct_expenses`, `variable_management`, and the management fee inside a fund that is held. The account: `fixed_period`. `insurance_premium` buys cover and is not a fee: it is listed on its own, adds to nothing and marks no sum (F-112). A fee the person names, and a price typed in a form its base does not usually take, is placed by the price's form: a share every `k` months is on what is held, a sum every `k` months is on the account, and a price per action adds to nothing. *Per year.* A share of what is held, `ρ` charged every `k` months, is stated as `ρ × 12 / k` a year: a plain multiple, not compounded, since each charge is a share of the value at that time (F-14, F-15). A sum `a` every `k` months is `a × 12 / k` a year (F-17). *What adds.* Shares of the same thing add. The yearly shares of what is held (custody, a manager's fee on the savings, the management fee inside a fund that is held) sum to one yearly rate. The shares of one action sum to the action's rate: for a purchase or sale of a holding whose currency is not the shekel, the commission's rate and the conversion's (F-12, F-13), each read as a share of the action's amount, the value traded; the conversion charged on the commission itself on a purchase, and the commission taken off the proceeds before they are converted on a sale (F-09), each a share of a share, are left out. A sum is never added to a share, and a fee that is free in a period adds nothing in it. A sum per unit (F-12's price per share) is not a share of the action either: its share depends on what a unit costs, so it adds to nothing, as a sum per action does. Where a fee prices a sale apart from a purchase, or is charged on one of the two alone, the action's rate is given for a purchase and for a sale; otherwise once. A minimum, a maximum, a percent maximum (a cap as a share of the action) or a flat price for a small action is not part of a group's rate: F-112 says where each decides. A group's rate is given per period of F-110, and is always the sum of all the group's fees in that period: where only some of a group's fees hold throughout, the page lists them and gives no rate for them alone. A group's fees are all the fees whose price falls in the group in any period, a free price counted by its base.

**F-112 The sums a deal's own prices mark (`dealMarks`, `actionCost`, `holdingCost`).** With no plan, the only sums the page names are the ones the deal's prices create. (1) *Where each part of a price decides.* A price on an action is F-12's line, `fee(a) = flat` where `a ≤ upTo`, else `min(cap × a, max(min, min(pct × a, max)))` (F-12, F-13's conversion fee), and a share of what is held is the same without a cap or a flat price, per charging period (F-14). Its parts are a flat price for a small action (`flat_up_to`: the trade fee and the conversion, BRK-54, BRK-68), a percent maximum (`cap`, a share of the action: the trade fee, BRK-67, BRK-40, BRK-42, BRK-48), the minimum, the rate and the maximum. The cap is read on the whole fee, after the minimum, as F-12 reads it. Hapoalim's, Yahav's, First International's and Massad's words cap the fee and Mizrahi-Tefahot's and Leumi's cap the minimum (BRK-67); the two give the same fee while the rate is under the cap, as every collected line's is, and differ only on a rate typed above a Mizrahi-Tefahot or Leumi cap, where their words would charge the rate and the formula charges the cap. That the cap wins over the minimum is printed by Interactive Brokers and the six banks; at Excellence and Interactive Israel it is the site's reading (F-12). Along the amount, each stretch is decided by one part: the flat price up to `upTo`; past it, the parts in F-12's order (the rate held to the maximum, lifted to the minimum, held to the cap). The amounts where one part gives way to another are among `upTo`, `min / cap`, `min / pct`, `max / pct` and `max / cap`; between two of them one part decides throughout, so each stretch is read at its middle. The page marks each stretch of the flat price, the cap and the minimum at its end ("up to `x` the minimum decides"), from where it starts where that is above nothing ("from the flat price's sum" or "from the cap's end"), and the maximum's stretch at its start ("above `x`"). With a typed rate under the cap, as every collected line has, this is: the flat price up to `upTo`; the cap up to `min / cap` (where `cap × a` is under the minimum); the minimum from there up to `min / pct`; the rate; the maximum above `max / pct`. Example, Mizrahi-Tefahot's direct line abroad, 0.3% with at least 25 $, at most 6,750 $ and at most 25% of the action: the cap decides up to 100 $, the minimum from 100 $ to 8,333 $, the maximum above 2,250,000 $; its internet conversion, 0.133% with at least 5.4 $ and 2.85 $ for a conversion up to 50 $: the flat price decides up to 50 $ and the minimum from 50 $ to 4,060 $. A stretch a flat price covers whole leaves no mark (PEPPER's 4 ₪ up to 30,000 ₪ covers its cap's and its minimum's stretches). A price per unit (F-12's per-share line: `fee = min(cap × a, max(min, min(c × n, max)))` with `n` the units) reaches its minimum at `min / c` units and its maximum at `max / c` units, whatever a unit costs, so those marks are counts of units; its flat price and its cap are read against the minimum on the money traded, the cap up to `min / cap`, and the minimum's stretch starts where theirs ends. Where a unit costs less than `c / cap` (half a dollar at Interactive Brokers' IBKR Pro Fixed) the cap also decides over `c × n`: that depends on the unit's price, which no mark takes, and only the curve shows it. Every mark needs the part that ends the stretch typed. On a share, a minimum's or a maximum's mark also needs the rate above nothing, and a flat price's or a cap's does not. A price per unit marks nothing unless its sum per unit is above nothing; a sum typed in another currency gives its mark in that currency, with no conversion. A sum per action marks nothing, unless it is held to a percent maximum: read as F-12's line of a share of nothing whose minimum and maximum are the sum, with the cap (the cost below), its cap decides up to `sum / cap`, in the sum's currency (Excellence's 11 dollars under 2%: up to 550 dollars), where the price changes to the sum. (2) *Where a share of what is held binds* is (1) without a cap or a flat price: the minimum up to `min / pct` and the maximum above `max / pct`, per charging period. (3) *The sums per period against custody:* the yearly sums on the account, added (`A`), equal the yearly rate of the deal's custody fees, added (`ρ`, F-111), on `B = A / ρ`; on less than `B` the sums are the larger of the two. The page calls them the largest cost of holding below `B` only where every other share of what is held, priced at `B` as the holding curve prices it (its bounds included), costs no more than the sums: such a share's cost does not rise as the value falls, so it then stays below them; otherwise it says only that they are larger than custody. It is read per period of F-110, where the deal has both and neither side is free, and it is marked only where no custody minimum or maximum binds at `B` (a charge exactly at a bound does not bind): where one does, the two costs do not meet there. Like a group's rate, it is given for the whole deal only where none of those sums and custody fees changes; otherwise per period, in the reading's statements. A mark that falls on the same sum for a purchase and for a sale is one mark. The other shares of what is held are not in `ρ`: they are paid inside a fund or to a manager, not to the account's provider. Two more statements are read off the prices and name no sum: a share of what is held that is charged every `k` months takes `k / 12` of its yearly rate each time, `12 / k` times a year (F-111); and a share with no maximum typed has no upper sum, so the fee grows with the amount. *The two curves.* The cost of one action of amount `a`, the value traded, is the sum, at `a`, of the deal's fees on that action as F-12 and F-13 price them (`tradeCommission`, `fxFee`, with the flat price, the cap and a sum per unit as F-12 reads them), the conversion priced on the same `a`; a sum per action in another currency (a trade's fixed sum on each order, Altshuler Trade's 11 dollars and IBI's 12 abroad; One Zero's 50 dollars for each corporate event, F-103) is that sum at F-84's rate, as a sum per unit's sum is; a sum per action under a percent maximum (Excellence's "11$ פיקס לעסקה" under "עמלת מקסימום 2% משווי העסקה") is F-12's line of a share of nothing whose minimum and maximum are the sum, with the cap, `min(cap × a, sum at F-84's rate)`, the cap winning as it wins over a share's minimum (the list does not say which applies under its minimum). Where a minimum is typed the curve starts at it: the cost of the smallest action is the minimum, not nothing (a cap takes it down to `cap × a`, nothing at the start, and a flat price is its own start). A sum per unit is charged on `n = a / U` units, where `U` is what one unit costs in shekels: the person gives the unit's price beside the chart, one figure for the deal, in the currency of what the deal holds where it says (F-12 reads the unit's price in the fund's currency) and otherwise in the currency of the deal's first sum per unit, converted at F-84's rate. Until it is given, a curve with a sum per unit on it is not drawn and the page says it needs the unit's price; the marks in units fall on the curve at `n × U`. A holding curve starts the same way, at the custody minimum and the sums. The yearly cost of holding a value `B` is the sum of each share of what is held, priced as F-14 prices custody at `B` for each of its charging periods in a year with its own bounds, and the yearly sums of F-17 in full at every `B`. Neither a custody charge nor a sum is limited here to what the account holds: that limit belongs to a run (F-17, and the same guard in the run's custody charge), so below a custody minimum the curve shows the minimum. A bound typed in another currency enters a curve in shekels at F-84's rate on the site's latest reference date, which the page names beside the curve. `B` is a value held through the year, not a plan's balance: the curve is the price list's rule at each amount and forecasts nothing. A curve is drawn only where a mark falls on it, from 0 to the first of 1, 2 or 5 times a power of ten that is at least three times the largest such mark, one curve per period where the prices differ.

**F-113 A typed price against the legal cap (`capStanding`).** A kind's record gives each fee base its cap (`fee_caps`: a value in a unit, or none). The price judged is the price charged: a discount is taken off it as F-116 says, since a cap limits what may be charged (PF-02: a managing company "may charge up to" its figures), and a third party's fee is not the provider's and is left out (F-117). For such a price `x` in the cap's unit and a cap `c > 0`: the share is `x / c`; the price is **below** the cap where `x < c`, **at** it where `x = c` and **above** it where `x > c`. Where `c = 0` the law allows no such fee: a price that charges anything after its discount, a rate of zero with a minimum among them, is above it, and a third party's fee beside a price that charges nothing leaves it at the cap. Where the record has no value there is no cap and nothing is judged; a cap whose record is not sourced is not judged either. Each price of a fee is judged on its own, so a fee may be below the cap today and at it from a date (F-110). **Above the cap, the page keeps the typed price** and says the law allows less. The runs hold a rate at the cap (F-11, F-15), because a run prices what may lawfully be charged; this page states what the person was told. A price typed for a period other than the cap's (a monthly rate against a yearly cap) is converted by F-111's plain multiple, and the conversion is not exact: a monthly figure is typed rounded, and a pension fund's bylaw sets its monthly maximum as the compounded twelfth of the yearly cap (PEN-21: 0.04157% a month for 0.5% a year, and 0.08708% for 1.05% computed the same way). Compounding only lowers the converted figure, and rounding the plain twelfth of a cap the records hold to four decimals of a percent raises it by at most 0.08% of that cap (0.0417% a month for 0.5%). So a converted price is at the cap from 0.5% below it to 0.1% above it, as shares of the cap. A price typed in the cap's own period is compared as typed. A cap that binds only some providers says so in `binds` on its record: the price of moving securities out and the ban on custody of makam bind banks only (`banks`; BRK-18, BRK-17, BANK-18); the ban on custody of money-market units binds banks, and a fund's distributor that is not an investment marketer where it has a distribution agreement for the fund (`banks_and_fund_distributors`; MF-26 reg. 2(ד)), and which non-bank members are such distributors is not known (BRK-52). Such a cap is judged only where the deal says its provider is a bank; elsewhere the page states the cap and whom it binds, and judges nothing. A cap that holds only in a case a deal does not state says so in `caveat` on its record; the page states it with the caveat's words and judges nothing, at any provider. `closing_account`: a bank's price for moving securities out is at most the record's value only when the account is being closed and the securities move to the person's own account at another financial body. The rules name third-party costs with the value ("להעברת התיק והוצאות צד ג'"), and whether those costs are inside it or on top of it is not settled; while the account stays open the price is the supervised one (BRK-18, SW-33). So a typed price above the value may be lawful and one below it may not be. `distribution_agreement`: no cap is set on buying and redeeming money-market units in general (the record's value is empty), but a bank, or another distributor of the fund that is not an investment marketer, that has a distribution agreement for the fund may charge the holder nothing for it (MF-26 regs. 1 and 2(ד)); an investment marketer is outside those regulations (BRK-52). The caps are those of a member who is saving: the other caps the kinds' notes name (a member drawing a pension, an account with lost contact, money from before 2008) are not judged on this page.

**F-114 The case a fee is read in (`caseFor`, `stepOf`, `stepStretch`).** *What, and why.* A price list often prints one fee at several prices: by what is bought (a security on the Tel Aviv exchange, a mutual fund, a security abroad on one exchange or another), by the channel (a branch, the internet), by the track the customer chose (Interactive Brokers' Fixed and Tiered), by the currency (a conversion from or to dollars), or by a size (of the action, of the portfolio, of a month's trading, of the number of actions in a month). The page keeps such a fee as the list prints it, one flat list of cases, and reads one deal for one situation. *The rule.* A fee that changes names one to three things it changes by, in order. Each case gives, for each of them, a value, several values, or any value; for a range, one of its steps: the range's tops `t_1 < … < t_n` cut it into `n + 1` steps, step `k` above `t_k` (0 for the first) and at most `t_{k+1}`, the last with no top, so the steps leave no gap and an amount `x` is in the first step whose top it does not pass. Each case has its own prices over time (F-110), and its own sale's. The reading picks one value for every thing the deal's fees change by, except a size read across sizes (F-115): it starts on the person's own track (the track a case is marked as theirs) and, for every other thing, on the first case's value; what is bought and what is held are one pick. The currency of a conversion is not picked: where what is bought is foreign it is that holding's currency (dollars, euros or pounds, else another currency); where what is bought is in shekels, or not said, there is none, and a conversion priced by currency is read only in a case for any currency. Where no case names the holding's currency the list prints no price for it and the fee is not read, never at another currency's price (Psagot prices a conversion to or from dollars per dollar, which the deal cannot hold, so a holding in dollars reads no conversion there, not the 0.5% it prints for other currencies). A fee is read in the case whose every condition holds the picks, any value holding every pick; where several do, the one with the fewest "any", and of those the first. Where none does, the list prints no price for the situation picked and the fee is not read: the page names it. What is bought is what puts a conversion on a purchase (F-111's `foreign`): each value of what is bought knows where it is bought and its currency, and a value the person names gives them once; it is foreign where its currency is not the shekel. A deal from before cases, which said what it held, picks that holding with the place and the currency it said, so a fund on the exchange in dollars still carries a conversion and one abroad in shekels does not, as before. A price-list template fills the whole list this way (§10.6, the site's price lists only fill a deal's fields). Each fee changes by what is bought (by what is held, for custody and the account's fee; by the currency, for the conversion) where the list prints different prices by it or prices only some of its values, by the track where it prints several ways to buy one thing and no standard line, and by the channel where it prints several; any fee may change by the channel. For each value the template reads the line F-63 reads: the standard line (no band) wins over a banded one, and of two standard lines the one that names fewer classes (where F-63 finds the list ambiguous unless one names a sub-class). A trade line that is one sum on every trade, a share of nothing whose minimum and maximum are the same sum in one currency with no flat price (F-12 gives `max(min, min(0, max)) = min`), fills a sum per action in that currency (Altshuler Trade's "11$ לעסקה", IBI's "פיקס 12$"), under the line's cap where it prints one (Excellence's "11$ פיקס לעסקה" under its 2% ceiling). A fee read from one line that prices only some values names them in its one case and the others have none (One Zero's 50 dollars for each corporate event is read abroad alone). A value the site does not name is the line's own, named by its band: a sub-class of the exchange that another line prices above (Psagot's bonds), a band for traditional mutual funds beside a standard line for every mutual fund (Excellence's 16 ₪ a trade; a mutual fund there reads 0.4% with at least 10 ₪), and a place abroad that no value of the site's is (IBI's line "מחוץ לארה״ב, לקנדה ולישראל" prices Europe and that value, not the rest of the world, which holds Canada).

**F-115 A size read across sizes (`inStep`, `actionCost`, `holdingCost`, `dealMarks`).** *What, and why.* A price that changes by the size of the action is not one price for the deal: a small conversion may be free and a large one charged in full. The cost curve and the marks of F-112 read every size, so they read every step. *The rule.* The size of the action is never picked, and neither is the size of the portfolio for a fee charged on what is held or on the account (a share of what is held, a sum per period); every other range is picked. Each step of such a range, in the case the other picks give for it (F-114), is a fee of its own for F-110 to F-112, charged on an action of more than its bottom and at most its top (the first step on an action of nothing too), or while the value held is in that stretch, the amount read in the range's currency at F-84's rate. Its price is read as F-110 reads any price, so a step may change at a date while the others hold. A step adds to no group of F-111: its share holds on some sizes only. Its marks of F-112 are those that fall inside its stretch, a stretch that starts below its bottom starting at its bottom; a mark in another currency than the range's, or counted in units, cannot be set against the stretch and is kept as it is. Each step's top is a mark: above it the next step's price takes over, so the curve jumps there. A step with no case is a price the list does not print, and the fee is not read (F-114). Example, a conversion of up to 10 $ free, up to 100 $ at half the price and its minimum (F-116), and above that 0.19% with at least 6.8 $ and at most 1,075 $: the marks are the two tops, 10 $ and 100 $, the full price's minimum from 100 $ to 3,579 $, and its maximum above 565,789 $; the half price's minimum would decide up to 3,579 $, past its step, so it marks nothing.

**F-116 A discount (`discounted`).** *What, and why.* A list may print a price and a share off it (Discount's conversion: "הנחה 50%, לרבות על המינימום"). *The rule.* A share `d` off a price lowers its rate, its sum per unit or its sum per action to `(1 − d)` of it, and its minimum the same way where the list says the discount is on the minimum too. The maximum, the percent maximum, the flat price for a small action and a third party's fee stay as typed: the list names the minimum alone ("לרבות על המינימום", BRK-68), so leaving the others is the site's reading of it. The discounted price is the one F-110 to F-113 read: a discount that starts or ends at a date changes the price there, and F-113 judges the price charged, after the discount, since a cap limits what may be charged.

**F-117 A third party's fee with a figure (`actionShare`, `actionCost`).** *What, and why.* A list may add another body's fee to its own (a broker's, an exchange's and clearing house's), priced on the same action. *The rule.* A third party's fee with a figure is a price of its own on the action its fee is on: a share of the action, a sum for each unit, or a sum on each action, with its own minimum and maximum, in its own currency. The cost of an action (F-112) adds it to the fee's own cost, each priced on its own as F-12 prices it; a share of the action adds to the fee's share in F-111, where the fee's own price is a share. Its bounds mark nothing (F-112 marks the fee's own parts), so only the curve shows them. It is not the provider's fee, and F-113 does not judge it.

**What the page states and does not compute.** Some parts of a price name no sum the page can add, and the reading says each in words beside its fee: a third party's fee where the list gives no figure ("בלי סכום בתעריפון"), so the action costs more than the curve shows; a fee that is offset against another fee's charges in the same month, up to its own sum (IBI's 15 ₪ a month against that month's trade fees), which costs nothing in a month whose other charges reach it, so the page shows it at its full sum; a refund; a fee charged on an order that was not carried out too, where a house's own list says so (Excellence), and at a bank only as the fee rules say, an order on the Tel Aviv exchange other than a mutual fund's placed through a clerk and not carried out for a reason on the client's side paying the minimum, on the branch's lines and never on direct ones, whatever the bank's heading says (BRK-16); and each partial fill of an order charged on its own, so an order filled in parts may pay a minimum more than once. None of them enters F-110 to F-113.

## 11. Ranking, ties and sensitivity

**F-80 Ranking and ties.** Rank eligible wrappers (section 8, B3) by `V_T^{real}`. Options `i` and `j` are a tie when `|V_i − V_j| / max(V_i, V_j) < θ` (P-01). Every option within θ of the top is in the tie set, compared pairwise with the top, not transitively. The "X is best because Y" line names the formula that produces the largest difference between the top two (deposit-side relief, fee drag, tax during and at exit (the engine's tax component sums the tax paid during the holding and the tax at exit), or lock-up), found by running F-61-style decompositions on both. An entered fee level for the representative instrument (D4) replaces its fee in F-80 and F-81 as an entered value: the track's balance fee, or the fund's management line with its variable and trustee lines at zero; the tariff and the fund's other costs stay. An entered balance fee below what the run would otherwise charge holds for PF-13's period and then returns to that rate (A-19, D86, 2026-11-07: a lower fee a company agrees to is a discount, and no binding document promises it for longer); at or above it, it is one rate for the whole run. The record's deposit fee and its discount stay. A provider the person chose for the brokerage wrapper replaces the representative tariff in F-80 and F-81; the tariff lines stay sourced figures, the choice is the person's. **Representative tariff of the brokerage wrapper (Doron's case).** When the person chose no provider, the wrapper runs in F-80 and F-81 on the collected list tariff that gives this goal's run the highest real value, found by running the wrapper once on every tariff the providers workbench ranks (F-63's snapshots) before F-81's moves; the card names that provider as the site's pick, the picker marks it, and a saved comparison's re-run (F-82) makes the same pick. The fund inside the account follows the same rule (the cheapest record collected). P-05's tariff entry serves the fee page and the comparison's start. **On the guide pages:** the securities account, makam and the government bond run on the collected tariff that leaves P-05's example plan the most, among those with a buy and sell line for the class (`mutual_fund_units` for the account's Israeli tracking fund; `makam`; `tase_bonds` or `tase_shares_bonds`), with a foreign broker left out of the two securities (a bond reads a line of its own where the tariff prints one, in the guide path as in F-63: Psagot's 0.06%); the terms block names the house as the site's pick and says how many were compared. It is the comparison's pick at the same plan (tested), and the pick is fixed on the example plan, so an amount the person enters changes the figures and not the house. Tariffs within θ of the top are a tie: the terms block names them, says they tie and that the site does not choose between them, and the example runs on the first. **The provident fund for investing (2026-11-07):** with twelve managers' list fees collected, the guide page and the comparison run on the manager P-05 names, else on the manager whose fees are the lowest on both bases (balance and deposit) in every month of any plan, which leaves any plan the most (`providentPick`). Each manager's fee is priced as A-19 says (`pricedLine`): a printed rate below the most its bylaws allow is a discount, kept for its printed period and no less than PF-13's five years, then that most (D56: the most the binding documents let it charge an ordinary customer); a printed rate conditional on a document that is not published is an offer, priced at that most from the start. The prices are steps, so they are compared at month 1 and at the month after each discount ends. Managers priced the same in every month tie: the example runs on the first by id and the terms block names them all, choosing none. The terms block names the pick, how many were compared, its fees in the discount period and after it, and the period; and the regulator's track of that manager on the account's index is the visual's holding (by the catalogue, else by the company name the regulator gives). When no list is the lowest on both bases, no manager is picked and the page has no run, so one manager never stands for the product by default. On the lists of 2026-11-07 the pick is Harel, of twelve: 0.7% of the balance and 0% of deposits printed on its joining request (form 8207, version 11/2025, p.2), a discount under s.36.1 of its bylaws (edition 07/2024), so 1.05% and 4% after five years. Yalin Lapidot (0.9% and 0%, a discount under its bylaws of 16.10.2025) is never cheaper in any month; Mor's 0.8% and 0% rest on a discount letter that is not published, so Mor is priced at 1.05% and 4% (`provident-lists.test.ts` checks every manager at months 1, 60, 61, 120 and 240). The fee table shows the range over all of them (F-105). The other provident-family wrappers keep their one collected list fee (D4) or the regulator's average until a second list price is collected. **Securities in the account (D6).** Makam and a government bond are held in the securities account, so the same pick prices them: every collected Israeli bank and member tariff with a trade line for the security's class (`makam`, or `tase_bonds` with `tase_shares_bonds`), its commission, custody and monthly-fee lines (F-12, F-14, F-17), with `memberKind` from the provider's class; a foreign broker is left out, since it does not sell either on TASE. **F-81 Sensitivity panel.** No page reads this formula; the engine keeps it with its tests. For each assumption `a` in P-02 with bounds `[a_lo, a_hi]`: move `a` from its current value towards each bound by bisection, re-running F-80, until the top option changes or the bound is reached; report the smallest change that flips the result ("flips if `a` moves from `x` to `y`") or "no flip within bounds". The value shown for `y` is rounded as a reader means it (years whole, halves under 3; a fee's rate in tenths of a percent; a return or inflation by its size), away from `x`, so it is still past the point, never past the bound tested, and finer rather than up across the point when the step would round it to zero; the sheet keeps the exact point (2026-11-07). A move that only breaks a tie is reported as that ("the tie breaks if … then X first alone"), and a fee row names the option and the fee it moves, never "the fund" (Doron's case). Shared by 3.2, 3.4, 3.5 and 3.6. **On the fee page.** The result that can flip is not the top option but the page's conclusion: the signature of the F-62 verdict of every fee plus which fee is the largest. `topOf` re-runs F-61 and F-62 with one assumption moved (A-01, A-02, the horizon, and each percentage fee from half to double, one at a time, P-02) and returns that signature; a flip is reported as the fee whose verdict changed and the new verdict, or the new largest fee, else "does not flip within the range tested". The comparison that decides "removes the fee" is held as it is, and each verdict is F-62's as the card gives it: a discount that ends within the moved horizon is judged by the rate after it (D88, D90), and the negotiate test reads the worth of bringing the moved fee to the base reference on the moved plan (D90 revised), two more runs per line with a reference below its rate (`valueOfReduction` runs the reduced plan and the plan as it is), at each probe of the search. A flip is said as where the moved value lands against where it is, under a heading that names what moves ("ב־0.42% במקום 0.7%, המסקנה על דמי ניהול מחיסכון עוברת מ״…״ ל״…״", the verdict it leaves and the one it reaches), so no verb has to agree with the heading's noun (2026-11-07). The holding period moves P-02's years from the chosen one but never below the shortest period the pages offer (P-05's slider minimum, one year), so no row says "0 years"; a chosen period already that short is not moved lower. A discounted fee (A-19) moves in both of its periods, the discounted rate and the rate it reverts to alike, in the comparison and on the fee page; the run holds each at its legal cap (F-11, F-15 with PF-02), and the page shows the rate after the discount beside the first and never a rate above the cap. The in-fund cost axis is searched down to P-02's A-06 floor (0 %), not only to half the record's cost, and its flip point is reported in fee terms (the cost below which the answer changes) beside the lowest cost among the fund records collected for the exposure.

**F-82 Revisit (3.9).** A saved comparison stores every `A-nn`, `P-nn`, the rule figures and data versions used (by file and `checked` date). On revisit the engine re-runs with current versions, lists the entries whose values differ, and reports whether F-80's top option and tie set are unchanged. Engine: the saved record keeps the inputs, every A-nn (A-01, A-02 and each candidate's cash rate), every P-nn, each rule figure read (rule, figure, value, `checked`), each data file (path, `checked`) and the result (top option, tie set, each ranked value). A rule figure or parameter differs when its value differs, a data file when its `checked` date differs or it is gone, an assumption when its value or state differs; a re-check with the same value is not a difference. The re-run uses the saved goal with the current wrapper records, terms, parameters and rules. The tie set is compared as a set.

**F-83 Candidate set and coverage line (product D17).** Every comparison starts from its full candidate set and accounts for each candidate exactly once. An option whose minimum-amount or other eligibility dimension is an open question in its wrapper record is listed, not ranked (outcome 4), with the reason "minimum amount: open question {code}". **Accepted fall below the fit table:** when the fall the person accepts (A-29) is below the fall P-04 judges the option's own classification to take (`max_fall_pct` of its row), the option is "listed, not ranked" (not every option that rests on the entered return A-01 is equity). The row is the one for the representative track's regulator classification for a provident or pension wrapper (a general track, such as P-05's 880, 2158 and 923, is judged at the "כללי" row, 20 %), the equity row for a fund-based wrapper whose fund holds an `equity_index` exposure, and the `equity_index` exposure's equity row in exposure mode; an option with no classification is not demoted. The option is "listed, not ranked" with that reason, unless the person entered a forecast fall for it that passes F-71 (the forecast does not exceed the accepted fall). No accepted fall entered: no change. P-04 stays a site parameter; D11 is unchanged (the site still holds no forecast fall).

- **Candidate sets.** 3.2 wrapper mode and its presets: every tier-1 product record, the wrappers and the securities held in them that a person chooses between (the product IDs in data-schema §2; D2, D6: makam, a government bond and the money-market funds are securities held in the securities account, each a candidate of its own, so the coverage count is unchanged), and every tier-2 instrument on the "not covered yet" list (D2). 3.2 exposure mode: every exposure record. 3.4 providers: every provider in the provider list for the wrapper (F-63). 3.4 tracks and the catalogue fit mark: every track for the wrapper in the latest CMA period, at every managing company (F-64, F-70). 3.4 fund variants: as F-65. The tracks, providers and fund variants inside a wrapper are not candidates of 3.2; its coverage line links to 3.4 for them.
- **Outcome.** Each candidate gets one outcome, with its reason as a code (rule ID, G-ID, constraint or missing data item). When more than one applies, the first in this order is used:
  1. **not eligible**: a rule bars this person or this money (section 8);
  2. **excluded by the person's constraint**: section 8's constraint filters, or the loss filter (F-71);
  3. **not evaluated**: the run lacks an input (no tariff, no product terms, no fund record), the instrument is tier 2 (G-24), or the method has no run for it yet (a gap whose effect says "not evaluated", for example G-28 or G-30). A gap that blocks one rule inside a run that otherwise works, such as an exit tax, is not this outcome but B3 (item 4);
  4. **listed, not ranked**: B3 (locked at the horizon, or a rule in its run blocked by a gap), D15, or a ceiling that keeps the wrapper from taking the same money (F-05);
  5. **ranked**: F-80. In the idle-cash preset (F-60) this reads "compared against the current account", and the current account is the baseline.
- **Output.** `considered = N`, the count per outcome, and every candidate that is not ranked with its outcome and reason. A group the site knows exists but does not hold one by one (G-29) is one line with the count held. Invariant: every candidate appears exactly once and the counts sum to `N`.
- **Tests.** A golden scenario may restrict the candidate set to exercise a formula (S39 compares four wrappers); the site never restricts it. S40 and S41 test the full coverage line.
- **Track workbench outcomes (F-64).** The workbench ranks nothing, so its outcomes are: shown with a mark (fits, does not fit), shown without a rule (the classification is not in P-04), and not evaluated (no saved goal, or no classification in the data); each track has exactly one, the counts equal the rows, and the page's coverage line names them. **Engine:** `trackWorkbench` gives each row exactly one outcome in that order. A track is not evaluated when no goal was given or both its specialization and sub-specialization are empty; it is shown without a rule when P-04 has no row for it; otherwise it is shown with the mark. The coverage line carries the four counts and the finer split (fits, does not fit, no rule, not eligible, no goal, no classification), and both add up to the tracks considered.

## 13. Conventions for the engine and the tests

- Compute in full precision; round only for display. Display rule: whole shekels from 1,000 ₪ for computed, entered and parameter amounts, and no `.00` on a whole sourced amount from 1,000 ₪; two decimals for tariffs and fees under 1,000 ₪, a source's own agorot, fee rates and table cells, and within 0.50 ₪ of a P-03 threshold; percentages to two decimals.
- The golden tests compare against the independent check (`tests/golden/expected_calc.py`, worked in `tests/golden/workings.md`) within P-06.
- Every figure the engine reads carries its tax year or effective date; a run in tax year `y` reads the figure for `y`, and refuses to run with a figure whose `effective_to` has passed, showing "figure not updated" (D3).
- A rule of strength `open` never enters a formula. A rule of strength `secondary` or `institution` enters and labels the number. The dimension checklist is derived from the rules used in the run, not hand-marked (product.md 3.3).
- Every comparison test checks F-83's invariant: each candidate appears exactly once, with one outcome.
- The golden scenarios live in `tests/golden/scenarios.md`, the test file the founder named.
