## What it said along the way

My son asked how it went. This is what I told him.

"I'm not changing anything. I'll keep the deposits at the bank and ask you, and then the clerk, two things. I'm fairly sure of that decision, but not sure I understood the page.

What stayed with me is the example. I typed nothing; the site filled it in: 10,000 now, 500 a month, 10 years, 1.8% interest. The big number was **68,270 ₪**, and under it, 'what you put in' was **70,000 ₪**. So after ten years I'd have less than I put in? There was a button **"בכסף של היום"**, which I think means it's counted in today's prices, because the 'inflation' box says 2%, more than the interest. Even so, I never found one sentence that says it plainly. It frightened me.

Then **"אין ביטוח פיקדונות"**, no deposit insurance. Further down it says that if a bank fails, no law gives you your money back. I always thought a deposit was the safe thing. It also says there's only a proposal for a 250,000 ₪ guarantee, and only at small banks. We have 400,000 at one bank. That's the first thing I'll ask about.

The second thing is breaking a deposit early. There was a red box saying the site doesn't calculate the penalty and that it depends on the bank. So the one thing I came to learn, what I lose by locking the money away, I didn't really get. In another place it says I might lose the interest 'and even part of the amount deposited'.

Some things did help. A deposit is money for a fixed period, with fixed or variable interest. At the end it's renewed automatically unless you ask to withdraw it. Some deposits have 'exit points', dates when you can take the money out without a fine. The tax is 15% of the interest on a regular deposit, and the bank takes it off by itself.

Under 'how much it costs' it only said 'according to the provider's price list', with no number. So I don't know if the bank takes too much. It does say there's a lower 'preferred' interest for good customers. I'll ask the clerk whether I get it after forty years."
