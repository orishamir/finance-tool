# s07: careful-retiree-pc

shots mode, pc; 4 steps, 42 s, $0.20

## Answer

A fixed deposit is money you give the bank for an agreed time. It pays fixed or changing interest, and 15% of the interest goes to tax. If you need the money before the end, the bank charges a fine that the site doesn't calculate, unless the deposit has an "exit point" (תחנת יציאה). It also doesn't keep up with prices, which is why their example ended at 68,270 after putting in 70,000. I'm not moving anything: I'll ask the clerk how big the fine is and whether there are exit points, and ask my son about that 68,270.

Confidence: fairly sure; gave up: false
