## Answer

A פיקדון locks your money in the bank for a set time at about 1.8% a year, minus 15% tax on the interest, and if you pull it out early you pay a bank fine the page doesn't calculate. In the example you end up with 68,270 ₪ "in today's money" against 70,000 ₪ put in, so with 2% inflation it doesn't really grow, and I don't think it's for my 30,000.

## What it said along the way

**What I'd tell my friend:** A פיקדון means you lock your money in the bank for a set time, from a day to a few years, and they pay you interest. The example pays 1.8% a year and then takes 15% of the interest as tax. What you give up is getting your money when you want it. If you take it out early you pay a fine (קנס), and the page says straight out that it doesn't calculate how big. Pretty sure the deposit isn't for my 30,000. What decided it was the big number. Putting in 10,000 plus 500 a month for 10 years shows **68,270 ₪ "נשאר לכם"**, and right under it **"ההפקדות שלכם 70,000 ₪"**. So I get back less than I put in? I think that's because of the "בכסף של היום" button and the 2% "אינפלציה", which is more than the 1.8% interest. Basically prices rise faster than the interest, so the money buys less.
