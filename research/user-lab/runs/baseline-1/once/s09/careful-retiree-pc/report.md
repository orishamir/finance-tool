## Answer

A fixed deposit is money locked in the bank for an agreed time for interest (example 1.8% a year) minus 15% tax on the interest. What I give up is getting the money out early: breaking it needs the bank's permission and can cost a fine that wipes out the interest and even part of the money. The page doesn't say how much the fine is, so I'll ask the bank before I sign anything.

Confidence: fairly sure; gave up: false

## What it said along the way

I told my son: it's still a fixed deposit, and I'll ask at the bank how much they charge if I take the money out early.

What I learned:
- **How it works:** you put money in the bank for an agreed time, "מיום אחד ועד כמה שנים", and the bank pays you interest. The interest can be "קבועה", fixed on the day you sign for the whole time, or "משתנה", which can change. At the end the money comes back to the עו"ש or starts a new deposit by itself.
- **What it pays:** the page's example uses the interest of "בנק לאומי", 1.8% a year, for 12 months.
- **Tax:** the bank takes 15% of the interest at the end of each deposit. For a deposit "צמוד למדד" it's 25%.
- **What I give up:** I can't take the money out before the end unless there is a "תחנת יציאה" (a fixed date when you can leave). Otherwise "שבירת פיקדון" needs the bank's permission, a form and a cost. Near the end it says the "קנס שבירה" can wipe out the interest "ואף חלק מהסכום שהופקד". The page doesn't calculate that fine and doesn't say how much it is. So that's what I'll ask the bank.

I'm fairly sure about how it works. I'm not sure what it really pays in the end or how much breaking it costs.
