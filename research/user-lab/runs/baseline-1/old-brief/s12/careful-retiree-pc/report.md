# Keren hishtalmut: what I took from the page

## 1. My answer

I'll join, because my employer puts money in that I don't get otherwise. I'm fairly sure about joining, about 8 out of 10. I'm less sure about the details.

What settled it was one quiet sentence in the grey box: **"המעסיק יכול להפקיד עד 7.5% מהמשכורת הקבועת בלי שזה ייחשב לכם כהכנסה"**. On the page's example salary of 12,000 ₪, that's up to **900 ₪ a month** from my employer, and I only add 2.5%. The page then says **"הכסף של המעסיק לא עומד לבחירה ולכן הוא מחוץ להשוואה"**. So the big green-versus-yellow comparison leaves out the best part, the employer's money. Even without it, the fund still came out slightly ahead.

What I would do:
- Join and put in my 2.5%.
- Not put in more than the tax-free limit. The example uses 500 ₪ a month, but **"רק 300 ₪ נחשבים הפקדה מוטבת"**. The other 200 ₪ gets taxed like a normal investment account.
- Look hard at the management fee before signing. The page says the fee can be **"עד 2% בשנה"**, but the example fund charges 0.54%. The bullet **"ההפרש נע בין −342.02 ₪ לבין 2,106 ₪"** tells me a fund with high fees could leave me worse off than an ordinary investment account.
- Not take the money out early. The orange box says **"משיכה לפני הזמן אפשרית, אבל יקרה"**.

## 2. The parts I actually used

- The headline and **"מה זה"**: what it is, the 6 years, the 7.5% and 2.5%.
- **"למה משתמשים בזה"**: the trade-off, money locked for a while in return for tax-free profits.
- **"מה מייחד קרן השתלמות"**: the green and yellow bars, the costs-and-tax table under them, and above all the grey sentences beneath them.
- **"כסף נכנס ויוצא"**: the four boxes (money free after 6 years, paid within 4 business days, switching without tax).
- **"כמה זה עולה"**: the fees table.
- **"מיסוי"**: the three cards and the orange warning box.
- **"סיכונים ומה לא מוגן"**.
- A few rows of **"מונחים"**: ותק, רווח ריאלי, הפקדה מוטבת.

## 6. What I wanted to know and couldn't find

- **What is retirement age?** Does the 3-year rule apply to me at 61? If I'm close, the wait drops from 6 years to 3. That's the most important question for me, and the page doesn't answer it.
- How much would I have after 6 years if you count the employer's money too? That's the real number I care about.
- If I leave my job or retire, what happens to the fund? Can I keep the money in it after 6 years and stay tax-free?
- Which investment track is sensible for a 61-year-old. I'd want something less risky than an index.
- What a normal management fee is. Is 0.54% good? Can I negotiate it?
