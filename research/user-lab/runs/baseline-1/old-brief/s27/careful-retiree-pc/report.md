# What I made of the page, as a careful 61-year-old investor

## 1. What I would do, and how sure I am

I would open an account at **פסגות** (Psagot) and buy every month into **"ילין לפידות מחקה S&P 500 (4D)"**, fund number 5139282.

The page's own example is 10,000 ₪ today plus 500 ₪ a month for 10 years. That puts in **"70,000 ₪"** and leaves **"86,492 ₪ … נשאר לכם בעוד 10 שנים, אחרי דמי ניהול ומס, בכסף של היום"** (what's left after fees and tax, in today's money). The fund's fee is **"0.02%"**, which is about as low as it gets. Psagot's total account cost is **"1,057 ₪"** over 10 years, against thousands at the other places.

**How sure I am:** about 70% on the account and 60% on the fund.

- **Why less sure about the fund:** the runner-up, Altshuler, is only **"‎−43 ₪"** worse over ten years. That's nothing. So the "best fund" question hardly matters for my money; any of the top three or four would do.
- **Why less sure about the account:** further down, the page says Psagot's advert promises **"פטור מלא מדמי ניהול ללא הגבלת זמן"** (no management fees, ever). But the official price list says **"דמי משמרת 0.12% גבייה רבעונית 0.03%"** (a 0.12% custody fee, charged 0.03% each quarter). I'd phone Psagot and ask which is true before I signed anything.
- **What really matters:** the chart that starts from **"ירושלים"** (Bank of Jerusalem) says changing the account is worth **"+4,972 ₪"**, and changing the fund only **"+2,344 ₪"**. So the account is the bigger decision.

## 3. Going down the page block by block

- **Title and short explanation:** read every word. It told me what the page is for.
- **Index buttons (S&P 500, ת"א-125 and so on):** used. S&P 500 was already chosen.
- **"מדד S&P 500" description:** read it. **"אפשר להחזיק אותו גם דרך קרן חוץ"** (you can also hold it through a foreign fund) made me wonder whether I should.
- **The four sliders:** used. I'd put in my own numbers. I didn't touch the **"אינפלציה"** (inflation) button because I wasn't sure what it would change.
- **The three result boxes:** used, and they're the heart of it. Seeing **"−43 ₪"** next to **"הזוג הבא בתור"** (the next pair in line) was reassuring: the choice barely matters at the top.
- **The cost bars:** used, and they surprised me. The big pink **"5,703"** is **"מס"** (tax). So most of my "cost" is the state, which I can't change anyway.
- **"העלות אצלכם, לעומת הזוג הבא"** (your cost compared with the next pair): skimmed it. **"מס 14 ₪ יותר כי נשאר לכם יותר רווח"** (14 ₪ more tax because you're left with more profit) made sense.
- **The two cards (Psagot / Yelin Lapidot):** used.
- **The fund list:** I read the top ten or so and skipped the foreign funds marked **"אין מחיר כאן"** (no price here). The grey pop-up **"מס רק במכירה"** (tax only on sale) lay on top of other text and I couldn't read what was under it.
- **The account list:** used it down to number 15. I noticed **"לא בחישוב: מחיר אחר… לגמלאים"** (not in the calculation: a different price for pensioners). Hapoalim mentions **"לנשים מגיל 60 וגברים מגיל 65"** (women from 60, men from 65), but it says **"סגור למצטרפים"** (closed to new customers). That's a shame at my age.
- **"למה חלק מהקרנות בלי מחיר בפסגות"** (why some funds have no price at Psagot): skimmed it. Too technical.
- **Advert versus price list:** read it carefully. It was the most useful warning on the page.
- **"מה הכי משנה את מה שיישאר לכם"** (what changes your result most): read the sentence. **"אם תגדילו כל מספר ב־20%, הכי ישנה מספר השנים: 19,116 ₪ יותר"** (raise each number by 20% and the years change the most: 19,116 ₪ more). I glanced at the table and ignored the line chart.

- **The two tables below it** (**"חשבון אחר, אותה קרן"** = another account, same fund; **"סוג קרן אחר"** = another type of fund): skimmed them for the green "עוד" (more) and red "פחות" (less).
- **The footnotes:** read them. **"התשואה שבחישוב: תשואה לדוגמה, לא תחזית"** (the return used is an example, not a forecast). Fair enough.
