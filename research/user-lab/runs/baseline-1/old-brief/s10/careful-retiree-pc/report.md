# What I made of this page, as a 61-year-old saver

## 1. What I would do, and how sure I am

I would click into four things: **קרן כספית** (money market fund), **פיקדון** (fixed-term bank deposit), **איגרות חוב ממשלתיות** (government bonds) and **קופת גמל להשקעה** (investment savings fund). I'm 61 and retirement is close, so I want things that don't fall much and that I can get at.

- The bonds card says **"המדינה מחזירה את הסכום במועד שנקבע"** (the state pays the money back on a set date). That is the most reassuring sentence on the page.
- The money market fund card says **"סיכון נמוך"** (low risk).
- The investment savings fund card says **"אפשר למשוך בכל עת"** (you can withdraw at any time).

I would not touch the Nasdaq or S&P 500 cards. I'd also check whether I'm still in an employer's **קרן השתלמות** (advanced study fund, a tax-free savings fund the employer pays into). If I am, I'd find out when it can be withdrawn without tax.

How sure am I? I'm fairly sure which four to read about, maybe 60%. I'm not sure at all which one to actually choose, because the page tells me nothing about fees, returns or how much tax I'd pay. So what does this mean for my money? I don't know yet.

## 2. The parts I actually used

- The top section **"שני חלקים בכל חיסכון"** (two parts in every saving). This was the one idea I learned: the "box" the money sits in, and what it's invested in.
- **"איפה הכסף מוחזק"** (where the money is kept), especially the cards **"כסף שוטף וקצר"** (everyday and short-term money) and **"חיסכון עם הטבת מס"** (savings with a tax benefit).
- From **"במה הכסף מושקע"** (what the money is invested in): the cards for money market funds, government bonds, **מק"מ** (short-term government loans) and **מזומן שקלי** (shekel cash).
- The footer line **"אתר אישי, לא מסחרי, בלי קישורי שיווק"** (a personal, non-commercial site with no marketing links). That made me trust it more.

## 3. Going down the page, block by block

- **Top menu.** I skimmed it. **"השוואה למטרה"** (comparison by goal) caught my eye for later.
- **"מדריך" heading and its intro line.** I read it. It's fine, but it doesn't tell me what to do.
- **"שני חלקים בכל חיסכון".** I used it, and it's useful. One sentence lost me: **"בכל עמוד של מוצר יש שורה..."** (every product page has a line...). Which line?
- **"מאיפה להתחיל"** (where to start). I read it, but it's written for working people: **"שכירים שהמעסיק מפקיד להם"** (employees whose employer pays in for them). At 61, is that me? There's nothing for someone near retirement.
- **"כסף שוטף וקצר".** I used it. Clear enough.
- **"חיסכון עם הטבת מס".** I used it, but it raised questions. **"עד תקרה שנתית"** (up to an annual ceiling): how much? **"אחרי תקופה שקבע החוק"** (after a period set by law): how long?
- **"פרישה"** (retirement) cards. I read them carefully, because retirement is my age, but they confused me. What is the difference between **"מקיפה"** (comprehensive) and **"כללית"** (general)? And **"תגמולים ואישית לפיצויים"** (provident payments and personal severance) means nothing to me.
- **"לילדים"** (for children). I skipped it. Maybe for grandchildren one day.
- **"לבחירה עצמית"** (self-directed), meaning managing it myself. I skimmed it. I'm not picking stocks at 61.
- **"במה הכסף מושקע"** (what the money is invested in). I used the safe cards. I skimmed **"מסלול כללי"** (general track) and skipped most of the index cards.
- **"מה האתר עוד לא מכסה"** (what the site doesn't cover yet). I noticed it and would click it, so I know what's missing.
- **Footer.** I read the trust line and **"נתונים נכון ל־30.10.2026"** (data correct as of 30.10.2026). What data, though? I saw no numbers anywhere.

## 4. Lists and cards: what I looked at and what I ignored

- I looked at the bold card titles first, then read the description lines.
- I used the **"בשפה פשוטה:"** (in plain language) lines and wanted more of them. But they mostly just repeat the name: **"קרן פנסיה מקיפה חדשה"** (new comprehensive pension fund) doesn't explain anything.
- The two small buttons **"יומית"** (daily) and **"מתחדשת"** (renewing) under the money market fund confused me. Are they kinds of fund? Can I click them?
- I ignored the little arrows, though I assume clicking a card opens more.
- I ignored the index cards: **MSCI**, **נאסד"ק 100**, **S&P 500**, **ת"א-35/90/125** (Tel Aviv stock indices). There are too many of them and they all look alike.

## 5. What I did not understand

- **"חשיפה לדולר או מנוטרלת ממנו"** (exposed to the dollar or neutralised from it). Does this mean I could lose money if the dollar falls?
- **"קרן שעוקבת אחריו"** (a fund that tracks it), on every index card.
- **"תקרה למניה אחת"** (a cap per single share).
- **"ועדת ההשקעות של החברה המנהלת"** (the managing company's investment committee). Who are these people, and do I pay them?
- **"אג"ח"** and **"מק"מ"** in the subtitles before they were explained.
- The difference between the comprehensive and general pension funds, and what **"תגמולים"** (provident payments) means.

## 6. What I wanted to know and couldn't find

- **Fees.** How much does each option cost me per year?
- **Tax amounts.** It says **"משלמים מס על הרווח"** (you pay tax on the profit). How much?
- **Real numbers:** typical returns, and how much each option could fall in a bad year.
- **Someone my age.** What changes at 60? At what age can I withdraw, and is it tax-free?
- **The ceiling and the waiting period.** What is the annual ceiling, and how long is the waiting period?
- **Insurance.** Are my deposits or bonds insured or guaranteed if the bank fails?
- **A simple starting point:** "I have X shekels and need it in Y years, so look at these."

## 7. If half the page had to go, what goes first

1. **"לילדים"** (for children).
2. Most of the index cards. Keep one example with an explanation of what an index is, and drop the Nasdaq, TA-90, MSCI ACWI and the rest.
3. **"לבחירה עצמית"** (self-directed accounts).
4. The **"יומית/מתחדשת"** (daily/renewing) buttons.

I'd keep the two-parts explanation, the short-term and tax-benefit cards, the retirement section (with better explanations), the safe investment cards and the trust line in the footer.
