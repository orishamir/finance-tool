## What it said along the way

I'm going with a Pesagot (פסגות) trading account and the "ילין לפידות מחקה S&P 500 (4D)" fund. That's what the page marks as "הזול" ("the cheapest") and has already selected. It says I'd end up with 86,492 ₪ after 10 years. I'm fairly sure. What decided it was that Pesagot is first in the list, its account costs only 1,057 ₪ over 10 years, and the big banks cost 4,500 to 8,300 ₪. Costs in shekels I take seriously.

There's a catch on the amount. The 86,492 ₪ is for the page's example of 10,000 ₪ now plus 500 ₪ a month, not my 30,000 ₪. I'd type 30,000 in the "סכום היום" ("amount today") box and check again. Also, 70,000 ₪ in and only about 86,000 ₪ out after ten years at 7% feels low. I'd have guessed something like 70% more. It says "נטו, בערכים ריאליים" ("net, in real terms") and I don't know what "ריאליים" means, so maybe that's why.

**What I looked at, in order**
1. The heading "איפה ואיך הכי זול להשקיע במדד" ("where and how to invest in an index most cheaply"). S&P 500 was already picked, and there were boxes for the amount today, monthly deposit (500) and yearly return (7%).
2. A big "70,000 ₪", then two cards: "הבחירה שלכם · הזול" ("your choice · the cheapest") at 86,492 ₪, and "הזוג הבא בתור" ("the next pair") at 86,449 ₪.
3. A coloured bar with a legend. I only got "דמי ניהול" (management fees) and "מס" (tax).
4. The list of accounts. Pesagot is first, then Leumi, One Zero, Hapoalim and the other banks, all with smaller numbers. Near the bottom, Altshuler, Excellence and Meitav are much lower, around 63–66 thousand, with a lot of red in their bars.
5. The fund box for Yalin Lapidot: "דמי ניהול 0% … 0%" (management fee 0%). That's good enough for me.
6. The list of all the funds. Theirs is on top, and the others show minus amounts like "−43 ₪" and "−447 ₪". The minus signs push me away from them.
7. A chart with "20% יותר" ("20% more") buttons, then a section on "נטרול מט"ח" (currency hedging) with "−21.8%" in big letters. That scared me a bit, and this is where I stopped scrolling.
