# Choosing an investment savings fund from my friend's screenshots

## 1. My answer

I'd go with **הפניקס, עוקב מדד S&P500**. That's the Phoenix track that follows the S&P 500. I'm about 60% sure.

- **I knew which kind of track I wanted before I opened the page.** The only thing I know is "invest in the S&P 500", so I searched for every card titled **"עוקב מדד S&P500"** (a track that follows the S&P 500 index). I found one at most of the companies.
- **Phoenix looked like the best of those.** It takes **"0.58% מהחיסכון"** (0.58% of my savings) a year. It earned **"13.31%" a year over 3 years and "12.33%" a year over 5 years**. As far as I could see, that 5-year number was the highest of any S&P card. It also manages **"3.4 מיליארד ₪"**, the most of any S&P track, and that made me feel it's safe and popular.
- **The runners-up were close.** מגדל (Migdal) had 0.57%, 12.07% over 5 years and 984 million. מיטב (Meitav) had 0.6%, 11.94% and 1.3 billion. I'd be fine with either of them.
- **The cheapest ones were too new.** אלטשולר שחם (Altshuler Shaham) charges only 0.54%, but it shows **"פחות מ־3 שנים"** (less than 3 years) instead of returns, so I can't tell how it did.
- **Why I'm not more sure:**
  - The cards called **"מניות"** (stocks) and **"עוקב מדדי מניות"** (follows stock indexes) show much bigger numbers. Klal's stocks track has 22.48% over 3 years. Meitav's stock-index track has 25.86% over 3 years and 17.25% over 5. I don't know why those beat the S&P, or whether I'm being dumb by not picking them.
  - Fees differ by only about 0.1% between companies, and I have no idea whether that's a big deal in shekels.

## 2. The parts I actually used

- **The top tabs** **"לפי מוצר"** (by product) and **"קופת גמל להשקעה 143"** (investment savings fund, 143 tracks). They told me I was looking at the right product.

- **The track cards titled "עוקב מדד S&P500" / "עוקב מדד S&P 500"**, at Mor, Analyst, Meitav, Altshuler, Phoenix, Yelin Lapidot, Migdal, Klal, Menora and one more.
- **The "מניות" and "עוקב מדדי מניות" cards.** I only noticed them because their numbers were big.

## 4. Inside each card

**What I looked at:**
- **The track name.** This was my main filter.
- **"X% מהחיסכון"**, the fee taken from my savings. I compared these.
- **"תשואה שנתית ממוצעת — 3 שנים / 5 שנים"** (average yearly return over 3 and 5 years). I looked at these most. Mostly I used 5 years, since it's longer.
- **"כסף מנוהל"** (money managed). I used it as a "lots of people trust this" sign.
- **"מניות X%"** (stocks), with the teal dot. I took it as "how much is in stocks".

**What I glanced at but didn't really get:**
- **"0% מההפקדות"**, the fee on deposits. It was almost always 0%, so I stopped looking at it. A few cards had 0.01%, 0.05% or 0.16%.
- **"מט"ח X%"**, with the yellow dot. I think it means foreign currency, but S&P cards say 99% and stock cards say 26%. I don't know whether that's good or bad.

**What I ignored:**
- **The donut rings.** The teal and yellow lines didn't tell me anything the numbers didn't.
- **"קופה 15287"** and the arrow icon. It's just an ID number to me.
- **The dotted purple underlines.** I didn't know they were tappable or what they'd show.

## 5. What I didn't understand

- **"מניות 101%"**: how can more than 100% of the money be in stocks?
- **"מט"ח"**: why it's 99% on S&P tracks and around 20–30% on stock tracks, and whether I should care.
- **"סחיר"**, **"גמיש"**, **"אשראי ואג"ח"**, **"משולב"**, **"כספי (שקלי)"**: I don't know these words in this context.
- **"מהחיסכון" versus "מההפקדות"**: the two kinds of fee.
- **"123 ממקור / 123 מחושב"**.
- **"פחות מ־3 שנים"**: I figured out it means the track is too new to have returns, but at first I thought it was some kind of rule.
- **Why "מניות" earned about 22% a year when the S&P earned about 13%**, even though both are almost all stocks.
- **Whether 13% a year is "real"**, or whether it's before fees or adjusted somehow.

## 6. What I wanted to know but couldn't find

- **What 0.58% versus 0.71% means in shekels** if I put in, say, 1,000 ₪ a month for 10 years.
- **Whether the S&P tracks are basically the same product at every company**, so the cheapest one wins, or whether they're really different.
- **How risky each track is**: how much it could drop in a bad year.
- **How to actually open one**: minimum deposit, monthly deposits, whether I can do it in an app.
- **When I can take the money out and what the tax is.** I've heard there's a tax benefit at age 60, but nothing on this page says so.
- **A simple "if you're young and don't know, pick this" hint.**
- **A way to see only the S&P tracks side by side**, instead of scrolling through every company.
