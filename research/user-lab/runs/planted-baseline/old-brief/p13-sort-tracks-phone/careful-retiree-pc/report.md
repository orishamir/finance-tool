# Choosing a kupat gemel lehashkaa from my friend's screenshots

## 1. My answer

**I would open it with Harel, in the general track called "כללי", fund number 8211 (the page writes it "קופה 8211").**

I compared the "כללי" track from each company and wrote the numbers on a piece of paper. Harel's came out best on both things I can understand:

- **It charges the lowest fee of the general tracks:** "0.55% מהחיסכון". Mor charges 0.72%, Yelin Lapidot 0.68%, Clal 0.65% and Altshuler Shaham 0.63%. I can feel that in shekels. On 200,000 ₪, 0.55% is about 1,100 ₪ a year and 0.72% is about 1,440 ₪. That's roughly 340 ₪ a year more for Mor's general track, which also earned less.
- **It has the highest yearly return over the past 5 years:** "8.26%" next to "5 שנים". The others were between 6.43% (Altshuler) and 8.13% (Mor and Analyst). Over 3 years it shows 13.5%, which is also among the highest.
- **About half is in shares:** "מניות 49%". At 61 I don't want everything in shares, like the "מניות 100%" tracks. I also don't want the "כספי (שקלי)" money-market tracks earning about 3% a year, because I'd lose against prices going up.
- **Harel is the biggest company on the list:** "כסף מנוהל 19.1 מיליארד ₪". This track alone manages "6.7 מיליארד ₪". A big company makes me feel safer, though I know that's not a promise.

**How sure am I? About 6 out of 10.** I'm fairly sure about the company and the fee. I'm much less sure that "half in shares" is right for my age. The page never tells me how much this track lost in a bad year, and that's what I really want to know before I put my savings in it.

If I lose my nerve, my second choice is a more careful track, "אשראי ואג"ח עם מניות (עד 25% מניות)". At Harel that would be fund 8521 (about 4.73% a year over 5 years). But that card was cut between two of my screenshots, so I never saw its fee.

- **The summary line:** "143 מסלולים של 12 חברות · דמי ניהול מהחיסכון: מ־0.5% עד 0.73%". Now I know the full range of fees.
- **The sentence about the order:** "החברות מסודרות מהגדולה לקטנה לפי הכסף המנוהל". It told me why Harel comes first.
- **The grey header for each company:** its name, how much money it manages, and its fee range.
- **The "כללי" card in every company.** This is where I spent most of my time.
- **The "אשראי ואג"ח עם מניות (עד 25% מניות)" card in every company,** as my careful backup.

## 4. Inside each card: what I looked at and what I ignored

**Looked at:**
- "מהחיסכון", the fee. This was the first number I read on every card.
- "5 שנים" under "תשואה שנתית ממוצעת". I trust 5 years more than 3.
- "מניות". It tells me how risky the track is.
- "כסף מנוהל". Bigger feels safer to me.
- "קופה" with its number. I'll need it when I phone the company.

**Glanced at:**
- "3 שנים". The 3-year returns all look big (13% to 26%), and I'm afraid they just reflect a few good years.
- "ההפקדות". It's almost always 0%. At Phoenix I saw 0.04% and 0.16%. It's small, but I noticed it.

**Ignored:**
- **The round coloured ring** (teal for "מניות", yellow for "מט"ח"). The numbers next to it were enough. I couldn't tell what the ring added.
- **The dotted purple underlines and the small arrows (‹).** I can't tap a screenshot.
- **The little tags** "הלכה יהודית", "הלכה איסלאמית" and "עוקב מדדי אג"ח".

## 5. What I did not understand

- **"מט"ח".** I assume it means foreign currency. How can a track be "מניות 99%" and also "מט"ח 99%"? That adds up to almost 200%. Is my money in dollars? Does that mean extra risk for me?
- **"מניות 101%".** How can a track have more than 100% in shares? Does it borrow money? That frightens me.
- **"תשואה שנתית ממוצעת".** Is it before or after the fee comes off? If it's before, every number I compared is a bit too high.
- **"סחיר", "גמיש" and "עוקב מדדים".** I can guess, but I'm not sure what each one means for my money.
- **"123 ממקור / 123 מחושב".** No idea.
- **Fees "מ־0.5% עד 0.73%".** The 0.5% is the "הממוצע שנגבה בפועל". Does that mean some people pay less? Can I ask for less?

## 6. What I wanted to know and couldn't find

- **How much a track lost in its worst year, in plain numbers.** For example: "if you put in 100,000 ₪, in the worst year you'd have had X". This matters most to me at 61.
- **Which track suits someone my age, or someone who needs the money in 5 to 10 years.**
- **What it means in shekels:** fees and returns on, say, 200,000 ₪.
- **Whether the fee can be negotiated**, and how to join (minimum deposit, a phone number).
- **Tax.** I've heard that after 60 this fund has an advantage if you turn it into a monthly pension. The page says nothing about it.
- **How fast I can take money out if I need it.**
- **A simple "best value" marker,** so I didn't have to compare ten companies across 40 screens with a pen and paper.

## 7. If half the page had to go, what goes first

1. **The "חדש" tracks** with "עוד לא דווח", "פחות מ־3 שנים" and "0 מיליון ₪". They give me nothing to compare.
2. **The religious tracks** ("הלכה", "שריעה"). They're not relevant for me.
3. **The many near-duplicate tracks:** "עוקב מדדי מניות", "S&P500", "מניות סחיר" and "משולב סחיר" inside each company.
4. **The coloured ring and the "123 ממקור / מחושב" line.**
5. **The buttons for other products** (קרן השתלמות, חיסכון לכל ילד and so on).

What I'd keep: the date sentence, the company headers, and one line per track with the fee, % in shares, the 5-year return and the fund number. Above all I'd keep the "כללי" tracks side by side, which is exactly the comparison I ended up doing by hand.
