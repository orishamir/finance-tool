# My answer after going through all 15 screenshots

## 1. What I would do, and how sure I am

I would go with **Harel (הראל), the track "אשראי ואג"ח עם מניות (עד 25% מניות)", fund number 8521.** That is a track mostly in loans and bonds, with stocks capped at 25%.

My reasons, in the order I thought of them:

- **I'm 61.** I don't want most of my savings in the stock market. I passed over "מניות" (stocks) and "עוקב מדד S&P 500" even though their numbers looked wonderful, like "24.04%" for 3 years. Those are the ones that can drop badly in a bad year. The 25% track looked like the middle road.
- **Fees.** Harel charges "0.58% מההיסכון" (from savings) and "0% מההפקדות" (from deposits). Mor's same track is "0.72%" and Yelin Lapidot's is "0.68%". I worked out that 0.58% on 200,000 ₪ is about 1,160 ₪ a year, and 0.72% is about 1,440 ₪. That's a few hundred shekels a year for nothing, every year.
- **Size.** Harel's company line says "16 מסלולים · כסף מנוהל 19.1 מיליארד ₪", which is 16 tracks and 19.1 billion shekels managed. It's the biggest on the page and a name I know. This track alone holds "431 מיליון ₪", so it isn't some tiny new thing.
- **Track record.** It has "3 שנים 8.6%" and "5 שנים 4.73%". That's similar to the other companies' versions of the same track. It's not the best, but it's not bad.

**How sure am I? About 6 out of 10.**

- Infinity (אינפיניטי) has the same kind of track (9639) even cheaper, at "0.54%", with slightly better past numbers. But the whole company manages "910 מיליון ₪". I've never heard of them, and I'd want to ask someone before giving them my money.
- I also went back and forth on Harel's "כללי" (general) track, 8211. It has the best 5-year figure of all the general tracks, "8.26%", at only "0.55%". But it's "49%" stocks, and I'm not sure I want half my money in stocks at my age.
- Before I sign anything, I'd phone Harel and ask whether that 0.58% is fixed or whether I can get it lower.

## 2. Which parts of the page I actually used

- **The track-type buttons near the top, under "במה הכסף מושקע".** I didn't click them, because I only had screenshots. But they told me the kinds of track that exist, and that "אשראי ואג"ח" has 23 options.
- **The grey company lines** ("הראל · 16 מסלולים · כסף מנוהל… · דמי ניהול מהחיסכון: מ־0.54% עד 0.61%"). These were the most useful thing on the page. They let me compare companies quickly by fee range and size.
- **The table rows for one track type.** I followed "אשראי ואג"ח עם מניות (עד 25% מניות)" and then "כללי" from company to company.
- **The note at the very bottom:** "התשואה היא תשואת עבר… היא לא אומרת מה יקרה בהמשך, ולכן אין מיון לפיה". It means past returns don't promise anything, which is why the page won't sort by them. I appreciated that honesty, and it's partly why I leaned on fees rather than returns.

## 4. In the table: what I looked at and what I ignored

**What I looked at:**

- **"המסלול" (the track name):** always. It's how I found my type.
- **"דמי ניהול, ממוצע 2025" (management fees, 2025 average):** this is the column I trusted most. I compared the "מההיסכון" (from savings) numbers. I checked the "מההפקדות" (from deposits) line too and was glad to see it was "0%" almost everywhere. I did notice a few like "0.16%" (Meitav's "משולב סחיר") and avoided them.
- **"תשואה שנתית ממוצעת, לפני דמי ניהול" (average yearly return, before management fees):** I looked at both "3 שנים" and "5 שנים". I trusted the 5-year number more because it's a longer stretch. Where it said "פחות מ־3 שנים" (less than 3 years), I treated the track as too new.
- **"כסף מנוהל" (money managed):** looked at it. I don't want a track holding "0 מיליון ₪" or "2 מיליון ₪".
- **"חשיפה למניות ולמט"ח" (exposure to stocks and foreign currency):** I used the **"מניות" (stocks) percentage** to judge risk. That's how I saw the general track is about 50% stocks.

**What I ignored:**

- **The "קופה" number** (8521 and so on): I only wrote it down for the phone call.
- **The little round charts with teal and yellow:** they meant nothing to me. The numbers next to them were enough.
- **The "מט"ח" (foreign currency) percentage:** ignored, because I don't know what it means for me.
- **The small arrows** next to the column headings, the **"?"** circles, the **dotted underlines** and the **">"** at the end of each row: ignored. With screenshots I couldn't click any of them.
- **The grey tags** like "הלכה יהודית", "אג"ח סחיר" and "עוקב מדדי אג"ח": ignored, because they mostly repeat the track name.

## 5. What I did not understand

- **"לפני דמי ניהול" (before management fees).** So the return I would actually get is lower. By how much? Do I just subtract 0.58%? Nobody says.
- **"מט"ח 19%".** Is my money in dollars? Is that good or bad for me? The page doesn't say.
- **"מניות 101%".** How can more than 100% of the money be in stocks? That worried me.
- **"סחיר"**, as in "אג"ח סחיר" and "מניות סחיר". I think it means "tradable", but how is that different from the plain "מניות" track?
- **"עוקב מדד" vs "מניות".** Both are stocks. What's the difference for me?
- **"123 מקומם 123 מחושב"**, the line I mentioned in section 3.
- **"עוד לא דווח" (not reported yet).** The page says to click to find out why, which I couldn't do. Does it mean the fee could turn out to be high?

## 6. What I wanted to know and could not find

- **What the fees and returns mean in shekels.** For example: "on 100,000 ₪ you'd pay X a year and might have Y after 5 years."
- **Which track suits someone my age**, or someone who might need the money in 3 to 5 years. There's no word about age or time horizon at all.
- **The worst year.** How much did each track lose in its worst year, for example 2022? That matters more to me than the average.
- **Whether the fee can be negotiated.** It's an "average", so are some people paying less?
- **Taxes, and when I can take the money out without penalty**, especially after 60. As far as I know that's the main reason to choose a kupat gemel lehashkaa, and it's not mentioned.
- **Customer service and reputation.** Are these companies easy to deal with when you want your money back?
- **A plain "cheapest few for my track type" summary**, so I didn't have to scroll through 13 screens.
