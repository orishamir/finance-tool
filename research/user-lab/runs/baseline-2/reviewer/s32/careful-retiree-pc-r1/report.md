## What it said along the way

I'd choose the third drawing, but I'm not sure.

Look, son, I'll be honest. The moment I saw the dark page with "קרנות סל" (that's how they're labelled) and "S&P" and "MSCI" in English letters, my stomach turned. This isn't my deposits. On a normal day I'd have closed it there. But I was asked to choose, so I compared the three pictures.

**What decided it**
- In the first and third drawings the fund names, the numbers and the fees each sit in their own column. I can go across from the right without getting lost.
- The third has fewer numbers. Under "חשיפה מרבית למט"ח" (the most exposure to foreign currency) there's only one line per fund.
- In the first, every fund shows "מניות עד 120%" (shares up to 120%). That 120% is the number that stayed with me, and it frightened me. More than everything in shares?
- In the third, the two Tel Aviv funds just say "מט"ח עד 10%" (foreign currency up to 10%). That I can live with.
- The second drawing squeezes "פרופיל 4A: מניות עד 120%, מט"ח עד 10%" into a smaller, paler line under the green writing. I lost it in the middle, and "פרופיל 4A" means nothing to me.

**What I looked at, in order**
1. The heading "קרנות סל 4 קרנות" and the line under it about "דמי הניהול לפי הדוח" (the fees according to the report).
2. The fund names, starting from the right.
3. The green lines "עוקבת אחרי מדד ת"א-125" (follows the Tel Aviv 125 index). To me "מדד" is the price index on the news, so I wasn't sure what it follows.
4. The fees: "קבוע" (fixed) and "משתנים" (variable). The Tel Aviv 90 one looked cheapest, 0.1% and 0.1%. The American ones are 0.8% fixed, which looked like a lot.

**What I skipped**
