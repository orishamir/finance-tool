## What it said along the way

I'll put it simply: if the money stays in the עו"ש, it shrinks. The page's example was 10,000 ₪ sitting there for 10 years, which comes to "בעו"ש שווים ₪8,203 בכסף של היום", so "₪1,797 פחות". Mine is 30,000, so I lose more, probably about three times that. The page didn't show me my own figure. I'm fairly sure of it, and the one thing that decided it was that 8,203 under 10,000.

**Where I looked, in order**
- **Top:** the heading "חשבון עובר ושב" and the line under it, "כסף שיושב בו לא תמיד מקבל ריבית". OK, that's my situation.
- **"מה זה":** I read the first lines. Not every bank pays interest. I noticed 15,000 ₪, which I think is the amount where they have to send you a notice. The rest I didn't read.
- **"למה משתמשים בזה":** I scrolled past it.
- **"כמה עולה להשאיר כסף בעו"ש?":** this is the part I came for. 10,000 ₪, 10 years, 8,203, 1,797 less. There's a "יתרה בעו"ש" field set to 10,000. I'd want to put 30,000 in it, but in what I have it stays at 10,000, so I never saw my own number.
- **The chart:** a yellow line going up for "בקרן כספית", a light line going down for "בעו"ש", and a brown area marked "המחיר של להשאיר ₪2,576". The small text says prices go up 2% a year (that part I understood) and the fund "3.25%".
