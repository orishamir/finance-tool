# s09: first-job-phone (old brief)

pc; 53 s, $0.23

## 1. What I would do, and how sure I am

I'd put part of my idle money, not all of it, into a 12-month deposit at my own bank. It would be a non-indexed one with a fixed rate (**"ריבית קבועה"**), so I know from day one what I'll get. Money I might need soon would stay out of it.

I'm fairly sure I now understand how a deposit works. Here's what I took from the page:

- **"כסף שמפקידים בבנק לתקופה מוסכמת, ובתמורה הבנק משלם עליו ריבית"**: I give the bank money for an agreed time, and it pays me interest.
- **"בסוף התקופה שנקבעה בהסכם"**: I get the money back at the end of the period.
- **"שבירת פיקדון לפני הזמן... הקנס הוא תנאי של הבנק"**: if I want it earlier, I pay a fine.

I'm only about 50% sure it's actually a good idea. The example worried me. I put in **"ההפקדות שלכם 70,000 ₪"** (my deposits) and was left with **"נשאר לכם 68,270 ₪"** (what remains) **"בכסף של היום"** (in today's money). So in real terms I end up with less than I put in. As far as I can tell, that's because prices go up 2% a year (**"אינפלציה 2"**) and the interest is only 1.8%. The page says it outright: **"פיקדון לא צמוד לא מגן מעליית מחירים"** (a non-indexed deposit doesn't protect against rising prices). Nobody explained that to me. I just worked it out from the numbers. So maybe a deposit only makes sense as somewhere safe to park money, not as a way to grow it. I'd want to compare it with the **"קרן כספית"** the page mentions, since you can apparently take money out of that any day.
