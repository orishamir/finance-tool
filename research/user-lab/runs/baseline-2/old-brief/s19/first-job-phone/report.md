## 1. What I would do, and how sure I am

I'd pick an **S&P 500 track** (the rows called **"עוקב מדד S&P 500"**, "follows the S&P 500 index"), from whichever company charges the lowest **"מהחיסכון"** ("from the savings") fee.

The S&P rows I found were all pretty close to each other:

| Track (as written) | Fee from savings | Fee from deposits | 3 yrs | 5 yrs |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| "עוקב מדד s&p 500" 13343 (company name cut off in my screenshots) | 0.47% | 0.01% | 13.39% | 12.01% |
| "תגמולים ופיצויים עוקב מדד S&P500" 13946 (מנורה מבטחים) | 0.48% | 0.02% | 13.76% | "פחות מ־5 שנים" (less than 5 years) |

| "גמל עוקב מדד s&p500" 13263 (הפניקס) | 0.56% | 0% | 13.34% | 12.33% |

I'd lean toward **Menora 13946** or the **0.47% one**. At the bottom of the page it says past returns don't tell you what will happen next ("היא לא אומרת מה יקרה בהמשך"), so I decided the fee was the only fair thing to compare.

**How sure: about 5 out of 10.** I'm fairly sure "S&P 500" is the right kind of track for me. I'm not sure whether 0.47% versus 0.56% matters much, or whether I'm even allowed to move my money to any company I want.

I also noticed the **"לבני 50 ומטה"** ("for people aged 50 and under") tracks, which sound like they're made for me. Their 3-year numbers were even higher (for example 14.85%), but their 5-year numbers were lower (about 8.5%). Nothing on the page told me which of the two to choose, so I stayed with S&P.
