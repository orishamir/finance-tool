**In one line.** Neither bank advertises a securities price to the ordinary customer, and neither has a live securities campaign. - Discount's young-investor page;
- Discount's **Digital Premium**, with a salary or portfolio condition and no end date (issue 145);

**No price moves.** One new line per bank (transfer out, 5 ₪) is not read by the calculation. Mercantile's record now cites the bank's newer price list (01/10/2026) instead of the April 2025 file, which the tariff page no longer links. Its securities and conversion figures are the same; only the pages moved.

## 4. The ordinary customer's price (the most the documents allow)

The two banks print the same securities prices. The only differences are the conversion minimum and the current account.

**Case 1: an S&P 500 tracking fund or ETF in Tel Aviv, online**
- Buying and selling: **0.4%, at least 25 ₪, at most 7,150 ₪** (4.1.1 note 4). Footnote (1) takes in "קרן מחקה" and ETFs. At a branch: 0.54%, at least 35 ₪.
- Monthly standing order: no line and no offer in either tariff.
- Custody: **0.2% a quarter (0.8% a year)**, at most 7,500 ₪ per security per quarter, no minimum. It is collected at quarter end and pro rata on every sale or transfer (notes 3 and 4). Not charged on makam or a money-market fund.
- Distribution fee (4.9): charged only on a fund whose manager has no distribution agreement with the bank: 0.2%, 0.35% or 0.1% a year.
- Dividends, inactivity, data and platform: nothing printed is needed. The trading-system licence and data (11.20–11.22) are optional.
- Transfer out: **5 ₪ per transfer** (4.6.1), plus the exchange's costs.
- Current account: up to **5.90 ₪** a month (Discount) or **6.40 ₪** (Mercantile) as a minimum. The alternatives:
  - per direct action: 1.85 ₪ or 2.00 ₪;
  - tracks: 9 or 25 ₪ at Discount, 8.50 or 26.50 ₪ at Mercantile.

  At Discount the digital branch (Digital Classic) waives both the actions and the minimum, for anyone who joins it.

Illustration, inferred: a 67,000 ₪ account pays 536 ₪ a year in custody.

**Case 2: an ETF in the US, online**
- Trading: **0.59%, at least 23.5 $, at most 10,680 $**. On top: "עמלת סוכן, Sec fee, מס מלכה, Option regulatory fee, הוצאות סוויפט". These are **not limited**: 11.9 "משתנה", 11.17 "בהתאם להוצאה הממשית".
- Conversion: **0.17%, at most 1,055 $**, with a minimum of **6 $** (Discount) or **6.30 $** (Mercantile). Spread: **not limited by the documents**.
- Custody: **0.25% a quarter (1% a year)**, at most 7,500 ₪ per security, plus "הוצאות הקסטודיאן בחו"ל" (note 6, 11.24), **not limited**.
- Transfer out: 5 ₪ plus the agent's fee.

## 6. Issue 145: what Digital Premium's binding terms say (decision left to the founder)

- **Binding.** It is a row of the price list itself (annex A p.40, dated 22/06/2026), not a campaign page. It prints custody "פטור" in Israel and abroad on "דמי ניהול פיקדון ניירות ערך (לרבות יחידות השתתפות בקרנות נאמנות)", and the conversion fee "פטור".
- **Conditions.** Each is printed:
  - a private shekel account in the digital branch;
  - a monthly salary above 8,000 ₪ (14,000 ₪ for a couple), or a portfolio held and managed at the bank above 500,000 ₪ (the page counts deposits and savings plans in it);
  - kept "בכפוף להתמדה".

  The customer leaves after two consecutive months below the condition within half a year, with notice. Annex p.39 moves a Digital Classic customer up after two consecutive months of salary. So a new joiner may wait two months; the page says new joiners choose the plan through a banker at a branch. The benefit applies to one single-owner account. An INVEST or portfolio-management account does not get it.
- **End.** No end date is printed. Like every tariff line, it can be changed with notice. The page reserves "לשנותם... ואף לבטלם, בכפוף למתן הודעה כנדרש לפי הדין". The annex itself is "בכפוף לתעריפון המלא".
- **What it does not cover.** Its trade rows name "מניות ואגרות חוב" only. So for a tracking fund the trade price stays 0.4% with a 25 ₪ minimum (D42), and only custody and the conversion fee change. The conversion spread stays the bank's own.
- **Verdict under D56:** an **offer**: binding, but conditional on salary or wealth. It stays under `groups` and out of the ranking.
- **New for the question in the issue.** Mercantile, of the same group, has salary plans too (Top, Deluxe) with the custody waiver. They differ from Digital Premium: they are a **promotion for new joiners with a three-year end**, not a tariff row. So "salary plans" are not one kind of thing, and collecting them at every bank would have to record both kinds.
