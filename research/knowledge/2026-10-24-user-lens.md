# The user lens on a browsable rule base (2026-10-24)

Written 2026-10-24 by the user-research agent for the founder's brief: make the 500 sourced rules browsable by friends and family "in a way that intrigues rather than overwhelms", and let a sceptic verify a surprising claim at the source. His example: "maybe he didn't know he can switch kupat gemel track without getting taxed, and he was able to easily get verification from a 3rd-party source."

Status: research synthesis, not a product decision. Nothing here adds a feature to `docs/product.md`. No number was verified; no rule is restated from memory. Where a rule is named, its id is given and its record's own `text_he` is quoted or pointed to.

## How to read this file

- **Observed** means it is written in the cited project file (and that file cites its own source). **Inferred** is my reading, always labelled.
- Rule ids (`TXH-17`) are records in `data/rules/<id>.json`; the fuller write-up with the source list is the matching section of `.claude/skills/il-finance/references/<file>.md`.
- "Strength" is the record's own `strength` field: `primary` (law, regulator, official publication), `primary-mirror` (the law read on Wikisource or Nevo, not the official site), `institution` (the provider's own document), `secondary`. "Link in record" says whether the JSON carries at least one `source.url`; the audit of 2026-10-24 found that 286 of 500 cards had none (`research/audit/2026-10-24-user-audit.md`, "Trust" theme), and the site now prints "הקישור למקור עוד לא צורף לכלל הזה." on those (`site/app/copy.ts`, key `sourceMissing`).

## 1. Who browses, and why: four personas from the research

Each persona is built only from people the research already describes. "Moment" is when they would open a rules or sources page; "must find in under a minute" is the test for that page.

### P1. The friend whose deposit is maturing (the "where does this money live" job)

- **Who (observed).** The founder's friend: 24, medical student, 67,000 ₪ landing next week, house in 7 to 8 years, "is it enough time for the S&P 500?" (`2026-10-24-scenario-audit.md`, scenario 1). The 52-year-old whose bank offers to renew 100,000 ₪ at 3.8% for a year (scenario 7). The couple saving for a wedding in two years (scenario 3). Behind them, the forum's most frequent capital-market questions: "where do I keep liquid cash" and "money-market yields are falling, where now" (`ux-landscape.md` 3.1, Q1, Q2, Q11).
- **Moment.** The bank calls, or the deposit auto-renews (BANK-05 describes the renewal notice). On the site, the idle-cash verdict and the comparison already show the tax rules "as chips" at the point of decision; the scenario audit found that "tax reasons appear as codes where the person decides" (cross-cutting finding 11) and that "the cash ranking rests on a 3.5% site parameter disclosed 5,000 px down" (scenario 1). This person opens a rule because a chip stands between him and the answer.
- **Must find in under a minute.** Why an after-tax fund rate can compete with a deposit rate (TXI-03 on the deposit side, TXS-09 and TXS-12 on the fund side); that neither is insured (REG-04, BANK-23); that the bank may not charge custody on a money-market fund or makam (BANK-18); that the bank is obliged to show the fund and makam next to its deposit (BANK-15). One sentence per rule, the law section, and a link that lands on the section.
- **Inferred.** He will not read a group of 40 cards. He reads the one card the chip opened, then wants out.

### P2. The salaried 22-year-old who fears the missing dimension (the "am I set up right" job)

- **Who (observed).** The founder himself: 22, salaried, pension and study fund through the employer, parental provident funds and child savings, cash in the current account, no self-directed investment, "I can't be sure if I know enough" (`docs/discovery.md` section 1). Noa, 23, 15,000 ₪ in checking, never invested; Dan, 29, 120,000 ₪ and a 7-year apartment goal (`2026-10-24-user-audit.md`, Method table). The 21-year-old student asking "is it even worth it?" (scenario 8).
- **Moment (observed).** A conclusion flips when a rule appears: the founder concluded an ETF in a brokerage beats a provident fund, then reversed after learning about tax-free track switching and an age-60 treatment; the tax track then found the exemption he described applies to an annuity, not a lump sum after 60 (`docs/discovery.md` 1.1). "I can't know what else I'm not considering" is problem D, the stated reason he has not invested (section 2, D).
- **Must find in under a minute.** The rule that flips his conclusion, stated as the record states it (TXK-05: "בסעיף אין תנאי של גיל, של תקופת החזקה או של מטרה"), the rule that confirms the half he had right (TXK-04, TXH-17), and the complete dimension list for the product he is comparing, "visible at the point of comparison rather than in a separate article" (`docs/discovery.md` 2, D "Missing").
- **Inferred.** For him a rules page is a checklist of what he has not thought of, not a library. The most-viewed beginner resource on the forum is exactly such a sequence of dimensions (`ux-landscape.md` 3.1, Q8: 364K views).

### P3. The sceptic who distrusts industry-funded comparison (the "who says so" job)

- **Who (observed).** Trust in pension funds is middling to low (10% "not at all", 23% "a little", 49% "moderately"); 61% think the state should mediate between savers and funds (`ux-landscape.md` 4.3, BGU 2019 pp. 24 to 25). Comparison engines are owned or funded by the industry compared: The Phoenix buying Wobi, Wobi's earlier per-referral fee, iGemel-Net's "no commercial interests" next to lead forms, even הסולידית's disclosed referral boxes (4.3). The forum member whose Migdal quote was withdrawn after a clearing-house consent, and whose agent called the next day with figures he never authorised (4.5). On the site: auditor 13 ("sceptical user: the number marks, the 'where is this from' sheet on 15 numbers, 40 source links followed") and auditor 4, the licensed adviser asking "would I let a client read this" (`2026-10-24-user-audit.md`, Method table).
- **Moment.** A sentence on the site contradicts what the bank, the agent or a comparison site told him. He opens the source to catch the site out.
- **Must find in under a minute.** Who says it (law or regulator versus "the company says so"), the section or page, the date it was checked, and that the link lands on that section rather than a home page. The audit recorded the failure modes: 286 cards with no link, "some source links landed on home pages", "מוסדי" read as more authoritative than it meant, "English rule titles and working notes in the provenance sheets", "a raw CSV path as a source" (Trust and English-codes themes). He also needs the funding statement; the footer now carries "אתר אישי, לא מסחרי, בלי קישורי שיווק" (What changed, Shell and home).
- **Inferred.** This persona is the one the founder's example describes ("verification from a 3rd-party source"). He does not need intrigue; he needs the link to be one tap and to land on the sentence.

### P4. The parent with child savings and parental defaults (the "review what I hold" job)

- **Who (observed).** Rina, 54, teacher, four paper statements, "do I pay too much?" (`2026-10-24-user-audit.md`, auditor 3). The father with a 3-year-old daughter and 500 ₪ a month (scenario 5). About a third of child-savings plans (33.1%) sit in a track the National Insurance chose; 77% of first-child parents chose a track against 63% for later children (`ux-landscape.md` 3.1, Q10). The founder's own kupot gemel and child savings are parental defaults (`docs/discovery.md` section 1). After the 2016 default-fund reform, 41 to 43% of inactive savers still paid the maximum fee in 2021 (`ux-landscape.md` 4.6).
- **Moment.** The annual statement arrives (CS-27 says a short report once a year until 21, by early March), or the question "where is my child's account?" which the scenario audit found the site cannot answer yet (scenario 5; "Decisions for the founder" 4).
- **Must find in under a minute.** Who pays the fees and until when (CS-18); whether the track was a default (CS-20 says no source shows a parent this, an open question); what can and cannot move (CS-12, CS-25); what the parent can add (CS-04). For the parental provident funds: the fee cap (PF-02), the regulator's average actually charged (PF-39), and that a transfer carries no fee but may end a discount (SW-15, PF-13).
- **Inferred.** She browses by product she holds, not by topic. "Older people may find that very important" is the founder's own guess about her (`docs/discovery.md` 1.1).

### The eight to put first, and why

Selection rule (inferred from the research): a fact earns the first screen only if (a) the research documents it as a measured misconception or a recurring question, (b) the site already has a decision point where it changes the answer, and (c) its record carries a primary source with a link, so persona P3 can check it in one tap. Where (c) fails the fact still ranks, but the link must be attached first.

1. **TXH-17 and TXK-04** (track switch is not a tax event), with **TXS-21** as the contrast. The founder's own example; Q4 is recurring; the holdings decision block for a liquid study fund already lists "switch track" as one of four options (`2026-10-24-scenario-audit.md`, "What was built"). **Blocker:** neither record carries a link; the sources (the Chief Economist paper and Income Tax Rules rule 33ב for TXH-17; the Ordinance wording for TXK-04) sit only in `references/tax-hishtalmut.md` and `references/tax-gemel-lehashkaa-ira-child.md`. The founder's headline example cannot be verified at the source on the site today.
2. **REG-04 with BANK-23** (no deposit insurance; a 2026 law orders an examination). A domain key finding, primary, linked, and the one fact that changes how P1 reads "a deposit is safe". REG-04's `likely_to_change` is "yes" with a recheck date, which is itself a teaching moment about dated rules.
3. **BANK-20** (the bank must write to you when a large balance sits idle for a quarter). Primary, linked, in force since late 2025, and it speaks straight to the founder's own state and to problem F (`docs/discovery.md` 2, F). For P2 it turns "idle money" from a nag into a legal fact.
4. **PEN-23 with PEN-06 and SW-17** (default funds, employee choice, no agent needed). Q5 had 94 replies; the default-fund reform is the one documented case where a rule moved outcomes (`ux-landscape.md` 4.6). PEN-23 is primary and linked; SW-17 needs its link.
5. **TXH-09 with KH-15** (six years from the first payment of that account; a deposit after a withdrawal opens a new account). Scenario 2 showed the liquid study fund is one of the two most common dilemmas the site could not answer. TXH-09 is linked.
6. **TXS-24 with MF-37** (not all money-market funds have the same tax status; the expected yield is after fees, before tax, not guaranteed). Directly contradicts the forum's "all similar" and gives P1 something to check on his own fund. TXS-24 is primary and linked.
7. **PF-13 with PF-14 and SW-15** (a discount must last a minimum period, but a transfer or track switch may end it early). The teaser-fee and withdrawn-quote stories (4.2, 4.5) and the 58% who do not know their fees (4.2). PF-13 is linked; the other two are not.
8. **CS-18 with CS-20 and CS-12** (who pays child-savings fees and until when; a third of plans were defaults and no statement tells a parent that; bank money cannot move). P4's whole first minute, all primary, all linked.

Runners-up, in order: TXP-15 (severance reduces the annuity exemption; the BGU 60%), TXI-03 and TXI-25 (tax on nominal interest; the BGU 29%), BRK-06 (foreign broker not supervised), TXS-19 (losses across banks need a return), TXF-27 (US estate tax). Each is documented, but either sits further from a decision the site makes today or (BRK-06) rests on institution statements only.

## 3. How Israelis read sources, and what it implies for the "verify" link

### What the research says (observed)

- **Trust is low and the state is the trusted party.** 61% think the state should mediate between savers and funds; trust in funds is mostly "moderately" or less (`ux-landscape.md` 4.3). The regulator's tools (Gemel-Net, Pensia-Net, the fee calculator, the Bank of Israel rate comparison, the TASE calculator) are "authoritative data; used as the data source by most private sites", with a dated interface and low use (5.1; Bank of Israel: "only a small share of depositors use its comparison tools", 4.6).
- **Every private comparison has a funding question.** 4.3 lists the ownership and referral models; the inference drawn there is that a personal tool's edge is "the best option that pays nobody", the MoneySavingExpert principle (6).
- **Information alone did not change behaviour.** A 2021 study found that giving employees pension information did not make them manage pensions more actively (4.3; 7 "Education first" failure mode).
- **The community's own practice.** The forum's most valued assets are crowd-maintained, continuously updated fee threads (486K and 208K views) and a pinned beginner sequence (5.5, 3.1 Q8); answers "disagree within the same thread" (8, hypothesis 1). The rule base records four forum threads as `experience` rules (SW-X01 to SW-X04), each ending "זהו סיפור של אדם אחד, לא כלל, ולא אומת".
- **Gap.** The brief asks about "the forum culture of citing law sections". The discovery research does not document that practice as such; it documents the fee threads, the beginner wiki and peer review with diverging opinions. What the project itself does is cite law sections: the rule records name "פקודת מס הכנסה, סעיף 9(16א)" and read the law on Wikisource or Nevo mirrors (the `primary-mirror` strength; `site/app/copy.ts` `strength["primary-mirror"]`: "נוסח החוק נקרא באתר שמעתיק אותו, ועוד לא באתר הרשמי."). Whether friends and family recognise a section number as a credential is **not evidenced** and would need two or three short sessions.
- **What the auditors said about the site's sources.** The trust auditor followed 40 links; "some source links landed on home pages"; 286 of 500 cards had no link; the strength word "מוסדי" "in Israeli finance reads as more authoritative, the opposite of its meaning here" and was renamed "לפי החברה"; English rule titles, working notes and a raw CSV path appeared in provenance sheets; there was no "about" or data-date line (`2026-10-24-user-audit.md`, themes "Trust", "English and developer codes", "The strength mark"). The adviser's test was "would I let a client read this". The audit also lists what worked: "the sheet skeleton (value, state, source, publisher, checked, rules, strength)" and "honest negatives everywhere" ("What worked well").
- **Rules at the decision point, not on a page.** "The decisive sentences exist on four pages and are never assembled" (scenario 2); "tax reasons appear as codes where the person decides" (cross-cutting 11); the playbook's "summary box of verbatim rule sentences" was built in response and tested (`2026-10-24-user-audit.md`, "Fees and switching").

### Implications (inferred)

1. **The link's authority is the regulator's or the law's, not the site's.** Name the publisher before the site's own title: the sceptic reads "רשות שוק ההון" or "פקודת מס הכנסה" as the credential; "לפי החברה" is the warning. The existing legend already does this; the gap is that 286 cards cannot show it.
2. **Land on the sentence.** A link to a home page is worse than no link for P3, because it reads as evasion. Where the record has a section or page ("s.9(16A)", "p.12 row ..."), print it beside the link; where the official site is unreadable and a mirror was used, say so in the existing words ("המקור הרשמי נקרא בעותק שמתארח ב…", `copy.ts` playbook `chipCopy`).
3. **Date is part of the source.** The research's "rules most likely to change or be misremembered" list (`tax-landscape.md` 10) is a list of things websites still show wrong. "נבדק ב־22.10.2026" and, where set, the recheck date, are what separate the site from those websites.
4. **Experience is labelled experience.** The four SW-X rules show how to carry a forum story without passing it off as a rule. Keep that label on anything from a thread.
5. **Place the verify link where the decision is, first; the rules page second.** The research is consistent that people act at a decision point and that education pages alone move nothing (4.3, 7). The "why" sheet, the holdings decision block, the idle-cash verdict and the playbook are where a chip already stands; the rules page is for the person who followed a chip or who browses on purpose.

## 4. The ordering question

The founder says "the 25% tax is the most known and important". What the research says about what people ask (observed):

- **By volume, capital-market and consumer questions dwarf tax, and tax roughly equals pensions.** Forum sub-forums: capital market 14.3K topics, financial consumerism 4K, real estate 3.4K, tax 2.3K, pension/gemel/hishtalmut 2.2K (`ux-landscape.md` 3, table).
- **The catalogue clusters into three jobs**, in this order of frequency on the forum's front pages: "where should this money live" (Q1, Q2, Q9, Q10, Q11), "am I paying too much and how do I move" (Q3, Q5, Q6, Q7), "am I set up right" (Q4, Q8, Q12, Q13, Q14) (3.1, closing inference). The crowd-maintained fee threads are the most-viewed pages (486K, 208K); the beginner sequence is next (364K) (5.5, 3.1 Q8).
- **The tax questions people actually ask are not "what is the rate".** They are real versus nominal, fund domicile, US estate tax, accumulating funds (4.7; 3.1 Q13; `tax-landscape.md` 2.4, 12). The tax track's own list of rules "most likely to change or be misremembered" (`tax-landscape.md` 10) has ten items; the headline 25% is not one of them. The things that are: brackets, the two-part surtax, the annuity exemption share, frozen versus wage-linked figures, 15% versus 25% on interest, the study-fund exemption proposals.
- **The measured misconceptions are about fees, ownership, severance and insurance components**, not about the capital-gains rate (4.1, 4.2).
- **The founder's own flip was a tax rule**, but a conditional one (switching; annuity exemption), not the rate (`docs/discovery.md` 1.1).

**Inferred ordering for a browsable rule base.** The founder is right that the 25% is the most *known*; the research says that is exactly why it should not lead. A known fact is not a "wait, really", and the question catalogue says people arrive with a liquidity or fee question and meet tax as a dimension inside it. So:

1. **Lead with the job, not the law.** Three top-level groups in the forum's order: "איפה הכסף יכול לשבת" (liquidity, lock-ups, exit rules, ceilings; the Q1/Q2/Q11 cluster), "כמה זה עולה ואיך עוברים" (fee caps, averages, discounts, switching, agents, the clearing house; the Q3/Q5/Q6/Q7 cluster), "מה חל על הכסף" (tax at deposit, during and at exit; protection; regulators; the Q4/Q8/Q12/Q13 cluster). The dimension vocabulary already in the rule base (`liquidity-lockup`, `fee-*`, `tax-at-exit`, `protection`, `switching-tax-event`; `references/README.md`) is the index for this, so it is a grouping change, not a data change.
2. **Within each group, product first.** P4 and P1 browse by what they hold; the regulator's tools and every private site are product-first (5.1, 5.2), and the audit's guide readers wanted the product's rules on the product's page ("'all the rules' lands on the product's section", `2026-10-24-user-audit.md`, Guides).
3. **Tax is the third group, and inside it the conditional rules lead the rate.** Switching (TXH-17, TXK-04, TXS-21), real versus nominal (TXS-02, TXI-03, TXI-25), what counts as linked (TXI-04), fund status (TXS-24, TXS-22), then the rate records (TXS-09, TXI-03) as the floor everyone already knows.
4. **The current rules page orders groups alphabetically by Hebrew topic name** (`site/app/routes/rules.tsx`, loader: `localeCompare(..., "he")`), which puts "אג״ח ממשלתיות ומק״מ" first and the tax groups together under "מס:". That order serves nobody in the research.

**Where the research is silent.** There is no usage data for Gemel-Net, Pensia-Net or the TASE calculator beyond the Bank of Israel's "small share" sentence (`ux-landscape.md` 10.1 item 3); no evidence on whether anyone reads a rules page voluntarily; no content from Facebook groups, Telegram or Reddit (2.3), so whether friends' questions differ from the forum's is unknown (`docs/discovery.md` "Research gaps carried over"). The audit sampled the rules page but did not test browsing it for a purpose ("Gaps of the audit"). The "25% is most important" ranking is the founder's judgement and is not contradicted by the research; it is simply a different axis (importance to the outcome) from the one the research measures (what people ask and get wrong).

## 5. What would make it spam, and the one-line test

What the research and the audits say turns information into noise (observed):

- Up to 40 rule chips and 38 company pills ahead of "why people use it" on every guide; fixed by putting them behind toggles (`2026-10-24-user-audit.md`, "Guides as teaching"). A rules page of 1.9 MB and 295,000 px (Mobile and speed). A fee-only table read as "broken" (Decisions, 1).
- English codes and ids in Hebrew text; "F-20" as a section; decision ids after sentences (English-codes theme). Section 10.7's test: "אמא שלכם לא תבין".
- A verdict without its assumptions ("the leader's rate is a site parameter and the headline does not say so", scenario cross-cutting 2); a hedge in a small line above a confident headline (cross-cutting 1).
- Information delivered away from the decision: education alone did not change behaviour (`ux-landscape.md` 4.3); "the decisive sentences exist on four pages and are never assembled" (scenario 2).
- Marketing register: the style guide forbids "אל תפספסו", "הטיפ שלנו", promises and exclamation marks (10.1), and any light tone on facts and sources (10.7). For P3, whose whole frame is "who pays you", a teaser tone is itself the evidence against the site.
- Past-return badges with unexplained selection and gross, nominal returns (iGemel-Net, 5.2); "sucker index" framing (RAK, 5.2) is the commercial version of intrigue.

Per persona (inferred): for P1, any fact not about the sum in front of him this week; for P2, a fact without the "and this changes your comparison by..." line (the site's "what would change the answer" flip points were the behavioural auditor's favourite device, "What worked well"); for P3, any claim whose source is more than one tap away or lands on a home page, and any tone; for P4, anything about products she does not hold.

**The one-line test for every element of the intrigue layer:**

> Does this sentence change a decision this person is making now, and can they tap once to the section of the law or the regulator's page that says so? If either answer is no, it is spam.

Operationally: one fact, in the record's own words, with its chip, its publisher and its link; shown where the decision is, and on the rules page only as the top of its group; never more than three per screen; nothing without a `source.url` in the record.
