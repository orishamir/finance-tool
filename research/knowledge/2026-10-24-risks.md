# Opening `data/` to the site's users: what goes wrong, and the guard for each

Written 2026-10-24 (night) by the red-team reviewer, read-only. Scope: the founder's idea of the same evening (see `README.md` in this folder): a browsable, importance-ordered page of every rule, each verifiable through a link to a third-party document; tariffs, fund records, deposit terms, series and parameters browsable; search over all of it. No number was verified; no code or data was changed. Everything below was measured on the repository as it stood at commit 57d3944 plus the uncommitted README, and on the build in `site/build/client` produced at 15:05.

## 1. Overload and misreading

**What the record holds today.** A rule record has `text_he` (two to four sentences, median 419 characters), `strength`, `dimensions`, `checked`, optional `recheck_date` and `figures` (214 records). It does not hold the markdown's "Conditions / exceptions", the "Likely to change: yes, with the bands (TXG-02)" cross-reference, or any list of the rules it depends on. The card renders the text, the figures, one source link (the first figure's), topic, dates, dimensions.

**How a friend acts on a half-rule.**

- *Derivations.* TXG-08 is "taken together, TXG-03, TXG-04 and TXG-07 give three cases". The markdown says in its conditions "this is a derivation from the three rules cited, not a sentence found in one source", that withholding at the bank is at the standard rate (TXG-15) and the difference comes back only through a return (TXG-25). A card showing "60 or over: real gains can fall in the 10%, 14% or 20% bands" with none of that sends someone to argue with the bank clerk. 31 records are derivations or pointers of this kind (SW-X04, TXS-12, MKT-01, CS-09, PF-03, ETF-43, BRK-39, CAT-43, ETF-14, ETF-09, ETF-01, MF-55, CAT-38, TXG-02, TXG-28, TXS-22, PF-36, PF-38, PEN-23, TXK-25 and others).
- *Tax-year figures.* 141 figures carry `tax_year: 2026`; 3 carry 2012 (the management-fee regulations' year), 3 carry 2025, one each 2027 and 2028 (the bracket indexation). The card shows the year only when the record uses `by_tax_year`; a flat figure shows a number and no year. TXG-02's bands print as "≤ 84,120 → 10%" with no "לשנת המס 2026" and no "frozen through 2027" (that is in the English `notes`, which must not be shown).
- *Institution-strength figures.* KGL-03's 83,641 comes from Harel, Phoenix, Meitav and the Hebrew University fund; the official notice was not found. The card does say so, because the Hebrew text was written to say it, and the label "לפי החברה" is on it. This is the model to copy: the text names the weakness. 70 records are `institution`; not every text is as careful. The browse page cannot fix that; the guard is that the figure keeps its caution mark and the text is reviewed for the 70 before they are promoted to a browsable list.
- *Open questions.* The flows (6.14) promise "each group lists its open questions at the end, in the blocked style". The rules page does not render them (one `open` record exists). A browsable base that shows only what is known hides the lock the product promised. Render the group's open-question ids from the rule base (the validator already reads them).
- *Experience entries.* SW-X01 to SW-X04 are forum accounts. In the playbook they are drawn as experience cards; in a rules list they appear as rules with the "משני" tag.

**Structural guards, cheapest first.**

1. `derived_from: [ids]` on the 31 records, rendered as chips "נגזר מ־" with the sentence "זו הסקה משלושה כללים, לא משפט שנמצא במקור" when the markdown says so. One day.
2. `conditions_he` on the record, shown under the text with the heading "מתי זה לא חל". The markdown has the English for all but 20 rules; writing Hebrew for 500 is about 140,000 characters, a Hebrew-expert agent's day plus review. Do the rules that carry figures first (214), since those are the ones a friend quotes.
3. Show the tax year and the update rule on every figure: the data has `update_rule` strings ("cpi-linked 1 jan", "frozen 2025-2027", "fixed in law", "provider-set") in English; they need a Hebrew mapping in `copy.ts`. "לשנת המס 2026 · מתעדכן ב־1 בינואר" answers the question before it is asked.
4. Keep the card as the unit. No table view of figures without their text, no "copy the figure" affordance that strips the text. The audit's clean copy-paste of amounts is for the person's own numbers, not for rules.
5. A one-paragraph lede on how to read the page: what the strength words mean, that a copy is a mirror of the law, that a date is when a person read the source. The legend component exists; the sentence about mirrors does not.
6. "Importance" is a judgement, and the site's rule is that judgements are parameters. If the founder wants an order, define its basis in `docs/method.md` (for example, how many formulas and pages cite the rule, which is computable from `rule_ids` across `data/` and the engine) or record a hand-picked list as a parameter with a sign-off date, and show it as "סדר של האתר". Otherwise the strength word gets read as importance and vice versa.

## 3. Staleness

**What the page will show in spring.** 452 cards saying "נבדק 22.10.2026". 117 rules whose "ייבדק שוב" date, 1 January 2027, is in the past; 58 more on 1 April. 141 figures labelled tax year 2026 in tax year 2027. Today's code has a future-date caution (a figure not yet in force) and nothing for the opposite.

**What the page should say.**

- Per rule: the checked date (never hidden, never rounded to "recently"), the recheck date when there is one, and for the 259 rules with `likely_to_change: no` the words "לא משתנה מעצמו; נבדק 22.10.2026", so an old date on a stable rule reads as stable, not abandoned.
- Per figure: "לשנת המס 2026" and the update rule in Hebrew. When today's year is past the tax year and no newer figure exists: a caution "סכום של שנת המס 2026; השנה הנוכחית עוד לא נבדקה". That is a presentation of a fact in the data, not a rule from memory.
- Per page: one line "הבדיקה המלאה האחרונה של הכללים: <date>", from a small data file the recheck pass writes (not from git, which the site does not read at build).

**What to automate.**

- Validator warnings (not failures, or the January cliff blocks every deploy): a rule whose `recheck_date` is before today; a figure whose `tax_year` is before today's year with no successor; a fee snapshot older than N days where N is a parameter.
- A weekly workflow on the `refresh-watchdog.yml` pattern that opens or updates one issue listing the rules past their recheck date, grouped by topic file. That turns the cliff into a to-do list.
- Nothing that touches `checked` or `recheck_date` by script. A checked date is a claim that someone read the source; the recheck is browser work under the `data-update` and `il-finance` conventions.

**What must never be hidden.** The checked date, the strength word, the tax year, the "source link not yet attached" row, and the open-question lock. The temptation when 500 dates look old is to drop the date to the popover; the audit's trust reviewer and the standing rule both say the opposite.

## 4. Mirrors versus the official text

- Today: 144 rules are `primary-mirror` ("ראשי (עותק)", popover names the host). 113 figure sources cite Wikisource, 19 Nevo, 9 the Knesset gazette on fs.knesset.gov.il, 95 gov.il, 32 National Insurance. Zero mirror records carry an official URL.
- The honest label is the one in use, with one more sentence in the lede or popover: "the law as consolidated on a public mirror; the project read it there on <date>; the official text is the gazette, linked where it was read". Do not call a mirror "the government's PDF", and do not call a consolidated text "official" unless the project recorded reading an official consolidated text.
- Two failure modes specific to Wikisource: the page can change after the checked date, and the project read it through a tool that paraphrases (coverage.md §0.1 asks to re-read the exact wording before encoding). A friend who clicks and finds different words, or a section renumbered, loses trust in all 500 cards. Guard: cite the section in the link title (done for many) and store permanent links (`index.php?title=...&oldid=<revision>`) so the link shows the revision that was checked.
- Data task, yes: add `official` {url, title_he, read: true|false} beside each mirror source, filled from what the research actually opened (the gazette PDFs on fs.knesset.gov.il for amendments; the Tax Authority's own PDFs for the ordinance figures already come from gov.il). Where no official consolidated text was found, the field says so and the page says "נוסח רשמי מאוחד לא נמצא". Order by materiality: the rules whose figures the engine reads first (the resolver's `rule_ids`), then the rest. Not all 144 at once.

**Per-rule pages.** 500 routes at about 85 KB of shell each plus a `.data` file: roughly 45 MB and a build that pre-renders 1,162 pages instead of 662. GitHub Pages' 1 GB site limit is far away; the cost is build minutes and 500 near-empty pages that duplicate the anchors `/rules#TXG-08` already provide. Not worth it. What is worth it: the landing on an anchor today requires parsing 2.5 MB before the scroll. Split the rules page by topic (25 topic files, about 20 cards each, about 100 KB a page), keep `/rules` as the index with counts, and make `/rules#ID` resolve to the topic page. The section index and collapsible groups already exist, so the split is mostly routing.

**One page for everything.** Fees (165 lines), funds (12), deposits (3), series and parameters on the rules page would push the HTML past 3 MB; the MoF series alone is 4,223 observations. One page per collection, each pre-rendered with what it has, and an index page "מאחורי הקלעים" that lists the collections with counts, dates and links (most of those pages exist: rules, parameters, companies, workbench providers and funds).

**Search.** Adding 500 rules as entries with id, `title_he` and `figure_names_he` is about +60 KB raw, a few KB gzipped. Indexing the 420-character `text_he` is about +300 KB raw, perhaps +70 KB gzipped (Hebrew prose compresses less than repeated fund names), and the matcher is substring-based, so "מס" would match hundreds of texts. Index names, not texts; add a `rule` kind ranked after `term`; show the id and topic in the result line.

**Phone.** The search index is fetched once on first use (47 KB gzipped today), fine. The rules page is the problem page on a weak signal already (audit: 8 to 14 seconds of "טוען…" on the heavy pages); the topic split is the fix.

## 6. Maintenance: two copies of every rule

**Which is the truth.** `SKILL.md` says the skill folder is the maintained copy and `research/knowledge/` the snapshot. The JSON under `data/rules/` was written from it, by hand, with `text_he` and `title_he` that exist nowhere in the markdown. So: the markdown is the truth for title, source, strength, checked date, conditions and open questions; the JSON is the truth for `text_he`, `title_he`, `figure_names_he` and `figures`. Write that down in `docs/data-schema.md` §3.3's rule section; today it is implicit.

**What has drifted already.** 117 of 500 `title` fields differ from the markdown heading (for example ETF-33 "TA-125: TASE's own description: its most important benchmark..." against "TA-125: what TASE says it is"; TXG-17 lost "(closes Q17)"). File assignment and checked dates match everywhere, for now. Links exist for every rule in the markdown and for 214 in the JSON. Conditions exist in the markdown only. Ten markdown blocks carry correction or supersession notes (MF-31, CAT-43, CS-27, GOV-03, PF-36, MF-03, BRK-18, CS-10, IRA-28, PEN-36) and 25 carry "browser pass" annotations with later findings; none of that reaches the JSON unless someone re-edits it.

**What the validator should add** (`packages/pipeline/src/references.ts` already parses the headings; extend it to parse the Source line and the Conditions line):

1. For every record, the markdown block exists (done) and its heading title equals `title`, or the record declares `title_override: true`. Warn, not fail.
2. The strength word on the markdown Source line maps to the record's `strength` ("primary (mirror)" → `primary-mirror`, "institution" → `institution`). Fail on mismatch: the strength drives what computes.
3. The latest checked date in the markdown block is not later than the record's `checked`. Fail: a newer read in the markdown means the JSON was not updated.
4. Every URL on the markdown Source line (resolving reference-style links) appears in the record's sources or figures, once the `sources[]` field exists. Warn.
5. A record whose markdown block contains a correction or "browser pass" annotation dated after the record's `checked` is reported.

**Direction of generation.** Better than checking is generating: a script that rebuilds `title`, `sources[]`, `strength`, `checked`, `conditions_en` from the markdown on each validation run and diffs them against the committed JSON. `text_he` and the Hebrew fields stay hand-written. One direction, declared, keeps the browsable base from showing text the research has already corrected.

## 7. Scope: a day, or a release

**About a day each, no product decision needed:**

- `derived_from` on 31 records and the "נגזר מ־" chips.
- The `experience` strength (or excluding SW-X01 to SW-X04 from the rules list).
- Validator checks 1 to 3 of section 6.
- Search entries of kind `rule` (id, `title_he`, figure names), ranked after terms.
- A "מאחורי הקלעים" index page that lists the collections with counts, dates and links to the pages that exist.
- Hebrew for `update_rule` and the tax-year line on every figure.
- Rendering each group's open questions at the end (promised in flows 6.14).
- The staleness warnings in the validator and the weekly issue.

**A release in itself (needs a product decision first, then days to weeks):**

## 8. Other things seen

- **The repository is already public.** Everything under `data/` is on GitHub (architecture 3.3 applies the terms gate to the repository for that reason). Opening `data/` to users changes the reader, not the exposure: friends instead of developers. The guard is the field whitelist of risk 8, because text written for developers (English titles and notes, "open question 7", "not opened; Magna returns 403 to scripts") was never meant to be read by them.
- **Hebrew source titles are missing.** The card shows the source as its Hebrew title or falls back to the link's host. Most rule sources have English or mixed titles ("Tax Authority deductions booklet..."), so a browse page would show "gov.il" 95 times. The "government paper" promise needs `title_he` and `publisher_he` on sources, which the audit already listed as a data follow-up.
- **Deep links into PDFs.** The record titles carry page numbers in prose ("p.10", "עמ' 30"). A `page` field would allow `#page=N` fragments, which desktop PDF viewers honour and phones often do not. Cheap for new records, a pass for old ones; say on the page "עמ' 30" so a phone reader can find it.
- **Tariff dates.** 7 of 16 found tariffs print no date; Bank of Jerusalem's part 4 prints "06/02/20217" as written. A browse page must show "ללא תאריך מודפס" and the as-printed typo in quotation, not a corrected date.
- **The trust paradox.** Showing 500 strength words at once, 144 of them "copy", 70 "לפי החברה", makes the site look weaker than a blog that cites nothing. That is the honest cost of the standing rule. The guard is the lede and the legend, not softer labels; the audit's trust reviewer called the provenance skeleton the thing that worked.
- **Register.** "Intrigue" pulls toward the television-adviser voice the style guide (copy.md §10.1) forbids. The copy for a transparency page is the plainest on the site: "זה הכלל, זה המקור, זה התאריך".
- **The open decisions queue.** Sixteen founder decisions from the user audit and the scenario audit's hand-offs are still open. This idea adds three (capability, importance, tariff terms). Record them in the same place so they are not lost.
- **No personal data is involved**, so nothing here touches D1.
