Who: You are 33, on a computer. You have savings in a bank deposit and want to start investing in the S&P 500. You have never opened a securities account and do not know which bank or investment house to choose.

Shots: research/audit/2026-11-06-indices-pricing/readers/round2/shots/A-open-pc-00.png, research/audit/2026-11-06-indices-pricing/readers/round2/shots/A-open-pc-01.png, research/audit/2026-11-06-indices-pricing/readers/round2/shots/A-open-pc-02.png, research/audit/2026-11-06-indices-pricing/readers/round2/shots/A-open-pc-03.png, research/audit/2026-11-06-indices-pricing/readers/round2/shots/A-open-pc-04.png, research/audit/2026-11-06-indices-pricing/readers/round2/shots/A-open-pc-05.png, research/audit/2026-11-06-indices-pricing/readers/round2/shots/A-open-pc-06.png, research/audit/2026-11-06-indices-pricing/readers/round2/shots/B-leumi-pc.png, research/audit/2026-11-06-indices-pricing/readers/round2/shots/C-fold-open-pc.png

I read all nine screenshots. Here is my reaction as a 33-year-old about to invest for the first time.

## 1. My decision

**Account:** פסגות ניירות ערך בע״מ (an investment house, not a bank).
**Fund:** ילין לפידות מחקה S&P 500 (4D), number 5139282.

**How sure:** I'm fairly sure about the account and less sure about the fund.
- **Account.** פסגות is first in the list whatever I pick in the bank box. It's marked "★ הזול", and its whole account cost over 10 years is "₪ 1,057". Even after I chose לאומי, the next account, לאומי, costs "₪ 4,541". That gap is bigger than the gaps between the funds.
- **Fund.** The page says itself: "ההפרשים בין 3 הזוגות הזולים ביותר קטנים מכדי לקבוע איזה מהם זול יותר". אלטשולר שחם מחקה S&P 500 (4D) is only "‎−43 ₪" behind. So I'm choosing between near-equals.

The fund's management fee looks tiny ("דמי ניהול: 0% קבוע, 0% משתנה, 0.02% נאמן"). It also beat the index by "+0.37%", but under that it says "בשנה · שנה אחת", which is only one year of history. That makes me a bit nervous.

**What I used to decide:**
- The top summary: "‏86,492 ₪", the "אחד מ־3 הזוגות הזולים ביותר לתוכנית שלכם" card and the cost breakdown bars.
- The two lists: "כל החשבונות" and "כל הקרנות על מדד S&P 500".
- The step-by-step chart further down (start with ירושלים + MTF, then switch account, then switch fund).

Before I acted on it, I'd change "סכום היום" from the default ₪10,000 to my real savings. I'd also set "הפקדה חודשית" to what I can actually save each month.

## 2. The lines

**a. "הבנק שבו כבר יש לכם חשבון עו״ש", set to "לא באחד מהבנקים האלה"**
- **What it does:** it tells the calculator which bank I already have a checking account (עו״ש) at, so that bank isn't charged for an extra checking account.
- **Would I change it?** Yes. I have savings in a bank, so I'd pick my real bank. Leaving the default would make my own bank look more expensive than it really is for me.
- **What's unclear:** I wasn't sure at first whether "לא באחד מהבנקים האלה" meant "I don't have a bank account at all" or "my bank isn't on this list". I also couldn't tell whether I'd still pay my current bank's checking fees if I go with פסגות. I would, but the page never says so.

**b. "חשבון ניירות ערך בבנק צריך חשבון עו״ש באותו בנק, ולכן השורה של כל בנק סופרת גם דמי ניהול עו״ש. בית השקעות לא דורש עו״ש אצלו."**
- **What it means:** if I open a securities account at a bank, I must also have a checking account at that same bank. So each bank's cost includes checking-account fees. An investment house like פסגות doesn't need one, so its row doesn't include them.
- **What's unclear:** the comparison isn't quite like-for-like. With פסגות I still keep, and pay for, a checking account somewhere else. That isn't counted in פסגות's "₪ 1,057". It doesn't change my decision because my bank fees exist anyway, but the page could say this.
- **Another question:** how does money get from my bank to פסגות, and does that cost anything? I don't know.

**c. Set B, "השורה של לאומי לא סופרת את העו״ש, כי משלמים עליו בכל מקרה. השורות של שאר הבנקים סופרות אותו."**
- **What it means:** since I already pay for my לאומי checking account, the calculator stops charging it to לאומי. Other banks still carry it, because I'd have to open a new checking account with them.
- **What changed in the list:**
  - לאומי rose from #3 to #2.
  - Its result went from ₪82,723 to ₪83,396.
  - Its cost went from ₪5,283 to ₪4,541, about ₪742 less.
  - The red part of its bar disappeared.
  - ואן זירו dropped to #3.
  - Nothing else seemed to move, and פסגות is still #1.
- **What's unclear:** "כי משלמים עליו בכל מקרה" made sense to me. What I can't tell is whether the red part vanishing completely means לאומי charges no securities-account fee at all.

**d. "מה לא נכנס למחיר של קרן שנקנית בחו״ל", first point "מעבר לעמלת המרת המטבע…"**
- **What it means:** on top of the currency-conversion fee, a bank or investment house turns my shekels into dollars at a rate it sets, which can be worse than the market rate. Most don't cap that gap in their price list, so it isn't counted. Only אי.בי.אי., אלטשולר טרייד and מיטב טרייד publish a "תקרה לפער" (a maximum gap). In practice, funds bought abroad (the iShares/Vanguard ones) may cost more than shown.
- **What's unclear:**
  - "מעבר לעמלת המרת המטבע": is the conversion fee itself included in the numbers? The colour key has "המרת מטבע", so I think yes, but I'm not sure.
  - "תקרה לפער": how big is the ceiling? It isn't given.
  - Does this matter for my fund at all? My fund is an Israeli fund ("קרן מחקה") bought in shekels, and its tag says "חשיפה לדולר". I'm guessing this point doesn't apply to me, but I'm not sure.

**e. Second point, "גם גופים בחו״ל גובים עמלות: ברוקר, הגוף ששומר את ניירות הערך, הבורסה או רשויות…"**
- **What it means:** foreign brokers, the firms that hold the securities, stock exchanges and regulators also charge fees. Most Israeli providers pass these to me "בלי תקרה" (with no upper limit), and they aren't in the calculation. So a fund bought abroad "יכולה לעלות יותר ממה שמחושב כאן".
- **What's unclear:** "בלי תקרה" sounds scary, but there's no rough idea of how much this usually is. I can't weigh it against the shekel difference shown. It pushes me away from buying abroad without telling me by how much.

**Red in the bars:** red is labelled "דמי ניהול חשבון ניירות ערך ועו״ש", so for a bank it's the securities-account fee and the checking-account fee together in one colour. I can't tell how much of the red is each. After choosing לאומי the red disappeared completely, which suggests that for לאומי it was all checking fees. I'm guessing there. In פסגות's bar I see no red, which fits "בית השקעות לא דורש עו״ש".

## 3. Other things that confused or bothered me

- **Terms nobody explains:** "(4D)" in the fund names, and the tags "עסקת החלף" (swap) and "מס גם על שינויי המטבע" (tax also on currency changes). They sound important, and maybe there's a tooltip, but I don't know what they mean for me or whether they're risky.
- **Discounts:** לאומי says "לא בחישוב: מחיר אחר לצעירים, לחיילים משוחררים, לסטודנטים…". I'm 33. Does "צעירים" include me? I don't know.
- **The standing-order assumption:** "קנייה חודשית מחושבת כהוראת קבע" means I'd have to set up a standing order to get that price. I'd want to remember that.
- **Missing brokers:** "אינטראקטיב ישראל" and "Interactive Brokers" show "אין נתון" and aren't ranked at all, so the list isn't complete.
- **Bank vs investment house:** this is what I expected and didn't find. Is my money as safe at פסגות as at a bank? If פסגות goes bankrupt, what happens to my funds? The page assumes I know the difference.
- **Practical steps:** there's no word on how to open the account, whether there's a minimum, or what a transfer from my bank costs.
- **Negotiated prices:** "קיבלתם מחיר אחר? הזינו אותו" suggests prices can be negotiated. That's useful, but it makes me wonder whether the listed prices are what I'd actually pay.
- **The tax line:** "המס ₪14 יותר כי נשאר לכם יותר רווח" is a little odd to read as a downside, but it makes sense once you see it.
