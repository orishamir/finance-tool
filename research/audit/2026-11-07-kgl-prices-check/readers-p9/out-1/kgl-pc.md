Who: You are 41 and have about 60,000 shekels in a kupat gemel lehashkaa (a provident fund for investing) that you opened a couple of years ago. You want to know whether it is worth asking the fund for a lower fee. You read Hebrew natively and do not work in finance. You are reading on a computer.

Shots: research/audit/2026-11-07-kgl-prices-check/readers-p9/shots-kgl-1440/page-0.png, research/audit/2026-11-07-kgl-prices-check/readers-p9/shots-kgl-1440/page-1.png, research/audit/2026-11-07-kgl-prices-check/readers-p9/shots-kgl-1440/page-2.png

I read all three screenshots. The quotes below are in the page's Hebrew.

**(1) Would I call?** Probably not, at least not for the money. What decided it was this line near the bottom: "הספים שהאתר קבע: שווה להתקדם כשהדרדרת העמלה לממוצע שווה ₪1,000 או יותר". In plain words, it's worth acting when getting down to the average fee saves ₪1,000 or more. Here it saves only ₪108.03. The deposit-fee box says "לא שווה עכשיו" ("not worth it now"). The management-fee box never says whether asking is worth it. Its label, "מוצר אחר במקום גבוה יותר", is about another product ranking higher, not about asking for a discount. Also, the page uses ₪10,000 plus ₪500 a month, not my ₪60,000, so its numbers aren't really mine.

**(2) How much is it worth?** About ₪108.03 over 10 years, after tax, if the fee goes from 0.7% down to the 0.62% average. The calculation assumes the lower fee lasts 5 years and then goes back to 1.05%.

**(3) When could it change?** The "מה היה משנה את המסקנה" section ("what would change the conclusion") lists two things:
- If I hold the money 8 years instead of 10.
- If my fee is 0.4% instead of 0.7%.

In either case the management-fee verdict changes from "another product ranks higher" to "not worth the bother". The annual return (0–10%), inflation (0–5%) and the later deposit fee (2–4%) don't change it. Separately, my discount lasts 5 years. After that the fee can rise to 1.05%, "אז כדאי לבדוק שוב" ("then check again").
