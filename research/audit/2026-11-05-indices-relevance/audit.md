# The indices page: what stays, what goes (decision audit, 2026-11-05)

For the founder. The S&P 500 page as it will be after the PC fixes, at the site's example plan: 10,000 ₪ now,
500 ₪ a month, 10 years, 7% a year, 2% inflation, the dollar flat, collected list prices for an ordinary customer.
Every shekel figure is what is left after 10 years, after fees and tax, in today's prices.

Two kinds of evidence sit behind every verdict:

- **Money.** How much each element can change the reader's choice, from the site's own calculation
  (`elements.md` in this folder has the full working, heights measured at 1440 px).
- **Use.** Three fresh readers who knew nothing of the project decided with the page's screenshots
  (`readers/`): a 24-year-old on a phone, a 35-year-old who knows fees from pension statements, and a careful
  61-year-old. All three chose the same thing: פסגות with ילין לפידות מחקה, about 60 to 70% sure.

What was read and what was inferred: shekels, heights and counts were read from the build and the calculation;
what the readers did is quoted from their reports; each verdict is a judgement over both. A figure marked
*inferred* was worked out here from two read figures.

## 5. The method ("decision audit"), to repeat on every page

1. **Decisions ranked by money.** List every choice the page's reader makes and compute, with the site's own
   functions at the page's default plan, how much each moves the result; also whether the plan numbers flip the answer.
2. **Every element tied to a decision with numbers.** Measure each element's height and record which decision it
   serves, how much it can move it, what else on the page already carries it, and which decision or rule requires it.
3. **Fresh readers' use.** Two or three readers who know nothing of the project (different ages and devices) decide
   from screenshots alone and report what they used, skimmed, skipped or misread, and what they could not find.
4. **Verdicts.** Keep, cut, fold, move, merge or rewrite each element on both kinds of evidence; name each
   disagreement and decide it; mark "needs the founder" wherever a verdict would reverse a decision.
5. **A skeptic.** Before the verdicts stand, ask where they could be wrong (below), and say what was read and what was
   inferred.

**The skeptic's notes for this round.** The readers saw screenshots, so nothing they could only reach by a tap was
tested, and folding cannot be judged from their reports. Their plan was exactly the site's example, so the plan fields
look less useful than they are. Only S&P 500 was audited; on an Israeli index there are no hedged funds and no funds
abroad, and the hedge and fold verdicts do not apply. Three readers chose the same pair, so the page's answer was
clear; what they missed (the second-best house, the hedge break-even) they could not know they missed. The heights
after are estimates until built.
