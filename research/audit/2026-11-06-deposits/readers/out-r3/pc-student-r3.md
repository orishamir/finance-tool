# pc-student-r3

Who: You are 27 and have 25,000 shekels saved for a master's degree that may start in six months or in a year. You are deciding between a one-year bank deposit and leaving the money in your current account. You read Hebrew natively and do not work in finance.

Shots: research/audit/2026-11-06-deposits/readers/shots-pc3/page-1.png, research/audit/2026-11-06-deposits/readers/shots-pc3/page-2.png, research/audit/2026-11-06-deposits/readers/shots-pc3/page-3.png, research/audit/2026-11-06-deposits/readers/shots-pc3/page-4.png, research/audit/2026-11-06-deposits/readers/shots-pc3/page-5.png, research/audit/2026-11-06-deposits/readers/shots-pc3/page-6.png, research/audit/2026-11-06-deposits/shots/fix-tax-1440.png

**1. Can I take the money out after 6 months? At which banks?**
Only at Bank Discount, according to the page. The site collected one-year deposits from 4 banks. At 3 of them, none of the one-year deposits it found can be broken. The money comes back only at the end of the term, and some of those deposits also have a "תחנת יציאה" (exit point). At Discount, all the one-year deposits it collected can be broken, but there is a penalty. The page doesn't name the other 3 banks; it only links to "הרשימה". The example in the chart is a Hapoalim deposit, so Hapoalim is apparently one of the 3.

**2. How much would breaking early cost?**
The page gives no amount in shekels. It says:
- At Discount, breaking needs the bank's approval.
- The penalty is set only on the day you break, according to Discount's "טבלת ריבית לווים", which changes every day. So there is no amount you can know in advance.
- Breaking can wipe out the interest and even part of the money you deposited.

**3. Do "3 of 4" in the chart and in the tax section agree?**
They mostly agree, but the wording trips you up. The chart box doesn't actually say "3 of 4". It says the example is the middle one of 3 one-year deposits, one from each bank, all of which can't be broken. That fits the tax section: 3 banks with unbreakable deposits, plus a fourth bank (Discount) where you can break. You only see this once you link the two places. The chart never mentions that a fourth, breakable option exists.

**4. What the red box says, in my words**
If breaking the deposit costs me part of my principal, nobody knows how that is taxed. Can the loss reduce the tax, or does it count for nothing? No source answers this. The only known fact is that the bank deducts tax from the interest it actually pays.

**5. All deposits in Israel, or something narrower?**
Much narrower. The numbers cover only the one-year deposits the site collected, from 4 banks. The top of the page lists about 10 banks, and the data is as of 26.10.2026. It also says the terms depend on the bank.

**6. Wording that confused me**
- **"ובחלק מהפיקדונות גם בתחנת יציאה"** (and in some deposits, also at an exit point): this comes right after "the money comes back only at the end of the term". So can I get out of these unbreakable deposits or not? When is the exit point (could it be at 6 months)? Which deposits have one?
- **"טבלת ריבית לווים"** (borrower interest-rate table): this is in quotation marks and never explained. Why would a borrowers' table set my penalty?
- **"פיקדון לשנה ומעלה אי אפשר לשבור בחלקו בשנה הראשונה"** (a deposit of a year or more can't be broken partly in the first year): does "בחלקו" mean I can't take out just part of the money? Does this apply only to Discount?
- **"קנס"** (penalty): the word is underlined as if there is an explanation, but the text gives no amount or example.

**7. What's missing for my decision**
- The names of the 3 banks, and whether my own bank is one of the 4.
- When the exit points fall, and what you get when you leave at one.
- An example of the penalty in shekels, or at least a typical range.
- Any comparison with leaving the money in my current account. The page compares deposits to money market funds, not to a current account.

So, going only by this page: if there's a real chance you'll need the money in 6 months, a one-year deposit locks it up at 3 of the 4 banks. At Discount, breaking early has a cost you can't know in advance.
