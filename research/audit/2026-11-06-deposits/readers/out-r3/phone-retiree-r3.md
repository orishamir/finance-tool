I read all nine screenshots. My answers use only what the page says.

**1. Can I take the money out after 6 months? At which banks?**
Mostly no. The page checked one-year deposits at only 4 banks. At 3 of them you can't break a one-year deposit at all. The money comes back only at the end of the year, or for some deposits on a "תחנת יציאה" (exit date). At Discount Bank (בנק דיסקונט) you can break it, but only with the bank's approval and a fine (קנס). The page doesn't name the other 3 banks. It only shows a link, "(הרשימה)".

**2. How much would it cost?**
The page doesn't give a shekel amount. It says this outright: "האתר לא מציג את הקנס בשקלים" (the site doesn't show the fine in shekels). At Discount the fine is set only on the day you break the deposit, using a table that changes every day. Breaking it can wipe out all the interest and even part of the money you put in. You also can't break just part of a deposit of one year or longer, so it's all or nothing.

**3. Do the chart and the tax section agree?**
I couldn't find a chart near the top that says "3 of 4 deposits". The chart at the top is a calculator for 10,000 ₪ over 10 years, and it says nothing about locking. "3 מתוך 4 הבנקים" (3 of 4 banks) appears only in the section on breaking a deposit, and again in the tax close-up. So the two don't contradict each other, but I can't confirm the chart you remember.

**4. The red box in my own words**
If breaking the deposit eats into the money I put in, nobody knows how the tax works in that case. The page calls it an open question. The only known fact is that the bank takes tax from the interest it actually pays.

**5. Is this about every deposit in Israel?**
No, it's much narrower. It covers only one-year deposits, at only the 4 banks the site collected data from. The page lists 12 banks at the top.

- "טבלת ריבית לווים" (borrowers' interest table): what is it, and where can I see it?
- "הפיקדונות לשנה שנאספו" (the one-year deposits that were collected): collected by whom? Is my bank's deposit among them?
