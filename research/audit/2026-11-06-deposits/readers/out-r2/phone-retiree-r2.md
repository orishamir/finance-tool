1. **Can I take it out after 6 months, and where?** Sometimes. The page says that of the 28 deposits it collected from the rate tables of 4 banks, 18 can't be broken at all. With those, the money comes back only at the end of the term. Of those 18, 4 let you withdraw at a set date during the term (a "תחנת יציאה", an exit point) without a penalty. The other 10 can be broken early with a penalty, and all 10 are at Bank Discount. The page doesn't say which 4 banks it checked. At those banks, only Discount appears as a place where you can break a deposit.

2. **What would it cost?** The page gives no amount in shekels. At Discount the penalty is set only on the day you break the deposit, based on rates that change every day, so the amount isn't known in advance. Breaking can wipe out the interest, and even part of the money you put in.

3. **The line "הקנס הוא תנאי של כל בנק: בנק דיסקונט":** each bank sets its own penalty. Here, the penalty terms are on Bank Discount's own page, so I'd have to look there or ask the bank.

4. **The red box in the tax section:** if breaking the deposit eats into the money I put in, nobody knows what happens with the tax. No source covers it. The only known fact is that the bank takes tax from the interest it actually pays.

5. **Do 28, 18, 4 and 10 cover every deposit in Israel?** No, it's narrower. They cover only the 28 deposits the site collected from the rate tables of 4 banks.

6. **What confused me:**
   - "תחנות יציאה" (exit points): there's an explanation further down, but in the paragraph itself I didn't know what it meant.
   - "ריביות שמתעדכנות כל יום" (rates that update every day): which rates? My deposit's rate, or something else?
   - "וכולם של בנק דיסקונט" (and all of them are Discount's): does that mean other banks don't allow breaking at all, or only that the site didn't find any?
   - "4 בנקים" (4 banks): which banks? It doesn't say.

7. **What's missing to decide:**
   - A shekel amount or a formula for the penalty.
   - Which deposits have exit points, and when the exit dates are (after 6 months?).
   - Whether my own bank allows breaking at all.
   - How tax works when I lose part of the money I put in (it says outright that this is unknown).

   The page does mention a few things that may help: a daily money market fund ("קרן כספית יומית") or a deposit with an exit point if the date isn't certain. It also says there's no deposit insurance.
