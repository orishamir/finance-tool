## Short answers

1. **Prime is defined in law, and the law gives no number.** The Bank of Israel itself writes it as its rate plus 1.5%, in an explanation page in a directive. No text read obliges a bank to keep that 1.5. Of the nine banks read, two state it.
2. **No regulator or law writes "P−x".** The letter form belongs to the banks (six of nine). The plain wording is the better form on the site.
3. **Two glossary entries are missing, three need rewording, and four cite a source that does not say what the entry says.**

The earlier reading in `research/knowledge/2026-10-26-cash-rates/report.md`, "prime is not set by law or by a Bank of Israel rule" and "a Knesset paper defines it", is now out of date on two points. The Interest Order defines the term (closed 2026-10-31), and directive 451 gives a regulator's own wording for the plus 1.5, which the 2026-10-26 report had not found.

### Inferred

- **Each bank has its own prime, and the law leaves the level to the bank.** No text says this in so many words. It follows from the law taking an average over banks in two places, and from the Bank of Israel page averaging three banks' reports. The rule base had this reasoning from the Interest Order alone. It now rests on three texts.
- **The 1.5 is a convention, described by the regulator and not imposed.** The Order has no such number, the Knesset says the banks set it, and directive 451 prints it inside an explanation of mortgage tracks. "No text read imposes it" is not "no such rule exists", so this stays an open question (BANK OQ19).
- **The 1.5 has held across the dates read.** Nov 2009, 2019, 2023, 2024, 2025 and today's figures all agree. That is a set of points, not a series. I found no machine-readable prime series to test every month: EDGE flows BR, BIR, WEB_M and EZER carry none under that name, and the chart on the Bank of Israel page has no values in its HTML.

The Accountant General's rate is also the Bank of Israel rate plus 1.5% ([gov.il, read 2026-10-31](https://www.gov.il/he/pages/credits-info)). That is a different rate, so it is no evidence about prime.

"פריים פחות" appears in the library only in a broker's tariff ([IBI, p.2](https://campaign.ibi.co.il/PDF/TRADE/TAARIFON_IBI.pdf#page=2)) and a press comparison site ([TheMarker](https://supermarker.themarker.com/Savings/CompareSavings.aspx)). I searched the library's text only, so this is not a claim about every document that exists.

**Recommendation: the site writes the words.** Draft: "פריים פחות 2.7 נקודות אחוז". My reasons:

- The founder's rule asks for full words, and no authority gives the letter form standing.
- The unit matters. Inferred: x is percentage points off prime, not x percent of prime. The arithmetic agrees at the banks read: Mizrahi-Tefahot prints P=4.75%, P-3.55%, 1.20%. The words carry that unit and the letter form does not.
- Minus signs in right-to-left text misread. Hapoalim's table prints "2.19% = 2.56% - פריים".
- "מינוס" is the law's word and "בניכוי" the banks'. "פחות" is the plain Hebrew and neither of theirs, so the Hebrew editor should choose. I recommend "פחות" because it is the plain one.

People will still meet "P-2.7" on their bank's page, so the glossary should list the letter form as something they will see (entry below). A figure a bank prints can be quoted as printed, named as that bank's.

**One caveat on "fixed".** The Bank of Israel says the spread "נשאר קבוע לכל אורכה של תקופת הפיקדון" (review, p.59), and directive 421 s.2א says the same reduction or addition applies at each change and renewal, unless the customer withdrew part (s.2ב). But First International and Massad describe a deposit named "גולן" in which "המרווח המנוכה מריבית ה"פריים" משתפר מדי תקופה" ([FIBI](https://www.fibi.co.il/private/depositsandsavings/savingtypes/depositsteam/), [Massad](https://www.bankmassad.co.il/private/depositsandsavings/savingtypes/golan/)). So the drafts below say "בדרך כלל". I did not read that deposit's terms or test it against directive 421. A one-deposit question, too small to open on its own.
